## Hunter education: the CORE course

| CORE (Conservation and Outdoor Recreation Education) | |
|---|---|
| Study time | About 21 hours (99%) |
| Exams | Practical firearm handling and written (99%) |
| Graduate fee | $30.00 to BCWF (B.C. Wildlife Federation) (99%) |
| Exam fees | VERIFY with BCWF |

```more the full page 5 detail
- Since 1974 the [[CORE]] (Conservation and Outdoor Recreation Education) course has set the standard of knowledge and skill for safe and ethical hunting (99%, page 5, 2 October 2026 edition).
- The BCWF (B.C. Wildlife Federation) delivers the program and keeps the records (99%).
- A CORE graduate who is not a BCWF or affiliated club member can apply for a BCWF membership at half price within the first year of the CORE certificate, with the Outdoor Edge magazine and liability insurance (99%).
- Classroom instruction is not compulsory but is recommended: adult education, community colleges, rod and gun clubs, or advertised courses (99%).
- The written exam is based on the CORE manual chapters: Outdoor Ethics, Firearm Handling, Hunting Regulations, Animal and Bird Identification, Outdoor Survival, First Aid and Safety, and Hunter Heritage, Conservation and Wildlife Management (99%).
- There is a fee for the practical firearms handling exam and a fee for the written exam (amounts are not printed in the synopsis: VERIFY with BCWF), plus a $30.00 graduate fee payable to BCWF when you finish (99%).
- Plan on about 21 hours of self study and firearm handling practice (99%).
- The CORE manual and the list of certified examiners: BCWF at 1 888 881 2293 or [bcwf.bc.ca](https://www.bcwf.bc.ca) (99%).
- Bowhunter education: the recognized North American Bowhunter Education Program runs through the BC Archery Association, [bcarchery.ca](https://bcarchery.ca/) (99%).
```

## WILD and your FWID

| FWID (Fish and Wildlife ID) | |
|---|---|
| Cost | Free (99%) |
| Age | 10 or older (99%) |
| How many | One per hunter, for life (99%) |
| Where | WILD online, Service BC or FrontCounter BC (99%) |

- WILD: Wildlife Information and Licensing Data.

```more the full page 5 detail
- WILD (Wildlife Information and Licensing Data) is the online system for hunter services in BC: hunting licences, a permit to accompany, guide outfitter licences and LEH (Limited Entry Hunting) applications. You also submit and view hunting and trapping reports there (99%, page 5, 2 October 2026 edition). Address: [www.gov.bc.ca/WILD](https://www.gov.bc.ca/WILD).
- Not online? Any Service BC or FrontCounter BC office, or the FrontCounter BC Contact Centre at 1 877 855 3222 (99%). Once you hold an FWID you can also buy hunting licences and apply for LEH at participating licence vendors (99%).
- Your FWID (Fish and Wildlife ID) is a permanent number tied to your personal profile. It replaces the BC Hunter Number. You must be 10 or older to get one. It costs nothing (99%).
- Already had a Hunter Number? Your FWID is your old Hunter Number with three extra digits. Find it by logging into WILD with your Hunter Number, last name and date of birth, or ask at Service BC, FrontCounter BC, a participating vendor, or the Contact Centre (99%).
- No Hunter Number or FWID? Register online, or in person at Service BC or FrontCounter BC (99%).
- After the profile exists you must prove your residency and your identity (99%).
- Proof of hunter safety training is needed to buy most hunting licences: a CORE certificate, a letter from BCWF or the Province of BC confirming CORE, or a document from another province, territory or country showing you passed a hunter safety course (99%).
- One hunter, one FWID. Update the profile when your residency or your legal name changes (99%).
```

## Prove BC residency every three years

| BC residency proof | |
|---|---|
| How often | Every three years, in WILD (Wildlife Information and Licensing Data) (99%) |
| Help | FrontCounter BC, 1 877 855 3222 (99%) |
| Exemptions | Some military, students and youth (99%) |

```more the rule and the exemptions
- The province requires hunters to prove BC residency every three years, so that only eligible hunters get the licences, permits and authorizations reserved for residents (99%, page 5, 2 October 2026 edition).
- Renew the BC resident credential in WILD (Wildlife Information and Licensing Data). If the online service will not take it, call FrontCounter BC at 1 877 855 3222 (99%).
- Residency exemptions can apply to a hunter who is (99%):
  - Serving full time in the Canadian military and has made a permanent residence in BC for 30 consecutive days before applying, with military identification and posting documents, or
  - A Canadian citizen or permanent resident who attended a recognized educational institution outside BC and can show a certificate, diploma, degree or a school letter confirming the program, or
  - Under 18, lived with a parent or guardian in another province, and returns to BC to live with another parent or guardian who is a resident.
```

## Aboriginal hunting

| Who | Hunting and species licence? |
|---|---|
| Registered under the Indian Act, living in BC | Not required. Federal firearms licence and safety, health and conservation rules still apply (99%) |
| Métis | Required (99%) |

```more the page 6 words
- The Ministry of Water, Land and Resource Stewardship puts the long term conservation of wildlife and habitat first (99%, page 6, 2 October 2026 edition).
- It recognizes that First Nations may have or establish Aboriginal or treaty rights protected by the Constitution Act, 1982 (the synopsis cites section 35(2)), including rights to harvest wildlife for food, social and ceremonial purposes in their traditional territories (99%).
- Harvest under those rights must be sustainable, and the methods must not put safety or the use and enjoyment of property at risk (99%).
- Hunting wildlife for sale or barter, in whole or in part, is not legal unless a regulation allows it or there is a demonstrated Aboriginal or treaty right (99%).

> [!law]
> "Persons registered under the Indian Act (Canada) residing in British Columbia are not required to have a hunting licence or species licences, although they are required to have a federal Firearms Licence and comply with hunting regulations related to public health, public safety, and conservation."

- Where a wildlife population is a conservation concern and rights holders must follow the hunting regulations, which may include LEH (Limited Entry Hunting) authorizations, the Ministry consults the affected First Nations first (99%).
- A First Nation member in doubt about a traditional area, practice or rule can contact their Nation's officials and the Ministry regional office (99%).
```

## Aboriginal hunting, continued

| Situation | Rule (99%) |
|---|---|
| First Nation member outside own territory | Follows the regulations, licence exemption aside |
| LEH (Limited Entry Hunting) draw | FWID (Fish and Wildlife ID) with BC resident credential |
| Tsilhqot'in title area | Not open for public hunting at this time |

```more the page 6 detail
- Outside their traditional territory or treaty area, First Nation members must follow the hunting regulations in both synopses, apart from the licence exemption above. That includes applying through the LEH (Limited Entry Hunting) draw (99%, page 6, 2 October 2026 edition).
- To apply for LEH they need an FWID (Fish and Wildlife ID) with an active BC resident credential, plus either a hunting credential or a licence exemption WA S.11(9) credential (99%).
- Proof of status for those credentials: a Secure Certificate of Indian Status from the Government of Canada, a TCRD (Temporary Confirmation of Registration Document), or a letter from the Band Office or First Nation Government. LEH fees apply (99%).
- First Nations may take responsibility for managing their members' harvest (99%).
- Aboriginal title: on 26 June 2014 the Supreme Court of Canada declared Tsilhqot’in Nation title over an area in the South Chilcotin, Cariboo Region. As title holder the Nation controls access, and it has announced the title area is not publicly available for hunting at this time (99%).

> [!law]
> "All Métis individuals intending to hunt in B.C. are required, under the Wildlife Act, to hold a valid hunting licence and comply with all hunting regulations."

- That includes species licences and the LEH rules (99%).
- The BC government encourages mutual courtesy and respect between Indigenous and non Indigenous hunters (99%).
```

## Youth Hunting Licence

| Youth licence | |
|---|---|
| Age | 10 to under 18 (99%) |
| Bag limit | None of its own (99%) |
| Supervision | Always, by a qualified adult (99%) |
| Under 10 | May not hunt at all (99%) |

```more the page 6 words
- Youth aged 10 or older and under 18 who have an FWID (Fish and Wildlife ID) may get a Hunting Licence or a [[Youth Hunting Licence]]. A youth licence gives no personal bag limit (99%, page 6, 2 October 2026 edition).
- It is issued only to a parent or guardian, on behalf of their child or ward aged 10 to under 18. No hunter safety training is needed. The parent or guardian needs an FWID to hold it (99%).
- A youth hunter, trained or not, on either licence, must be accompanied and under the close personal supervision of an adult who carries the proper licences and qualifies as a supervising hunter (99%).
- Non resident youth: same rules, except they may not hunt wildlife that needs an LEH (Limited Entry Hunting) authorization, and for big game they must be accompanied by a guide outfitter, assistant guide outfitter or a permit to accompany holder (99%).
- Fee: $7.00 (99%, page 8).

> [!law]
> "No one under the age of 10 may hunt. A person who causes or allows a person under the age of 10 to hunt commits an offence."

- A parent or legal guardian applies for the hunting or youth licence on the youth's behalf and signs a written undertaking not to let the child hunt or carry firearms unless accompanied and closely supervised by a hunter who meets the qualifications (99%, pages 6 and 8).
```

## Supervising youth and initiation hunters

```diagram rb-supervise
```

```more every supervisor rule
- Wildlife taken under a youth or initiation licence counts in the bag limit of the supervising licensed adult (99%, page 6, 2 October 2026 edition).
- No species licences may be bought with a youth or initiation licence (99%). The one exception: in an LEH (Limited Entry Hunting) season a resident licensed youth may hunt with an adult holding a valid LEH authorization and species licence (99%).
- A [[Supervising hunter]] must meet every one of these (99%):
  - Hold an FWID (Fish and Wildlife ID) with active residency and hunting credentials.
  - For an initiation hunter: be a resident and hold a basic hunting licence (unless exempt).
  - Have held a hunting licence, other than an initiation licence, in BC or in another jurisdiction, in at least 3 of the licence years before this one. This does not apply if a guide outfitter also accompanies the initiation hunter.
  - If residing in BC and registered under the Indian Act, have received training in hunting and have hunted lawfully without supervision before.
  - Not be prohibited from carrying or possessing a firearm in any jurisdiction.
  - Supervise no more than two youth licence hunters, two initiation licence hunters, or one of each at a time. Youth who hold a basic hunting licence do not count toward that maximum.
```

## Deer licences and Haida Gwaii

| Deer licences you may hold | Max |
|---|---|
| Mule deer | 3 (99%) |
| White tailed | 3 (99%) |
| All deer licences together | 15 (99%) |
| Deer you may take, province wide | 3 (99%) |

```more the page 7 words and Haida Gwaii
> [!law]
> "The provincial bag limit for deer is 3."

- You may not hold more than 3 mule deer species licences, nor more than 3 white tailed deer licences (99%, page 7, 2 October 2026 edition).
- You may not hold more than 15 deer licences in total: mule deer, white tailed deer and Haida Gwaii deer combined (99%).
- Haida Gwaii deer seasons are more liberal and the limits higher. The possession limit for Haida Gwaii deer is 5 (99%).
- Haida Gwaii deer can be taken under a mule deer licence (still a maximum of 3) or a special Haida Gwaii deer licence, maximum 15 (99%).
- Up to 3 mule deer licences can be used on Haida Gwaii, but those deer count toward the Skeena region and provincial mule deer bag limits (99%).
- Up to 12 Haida Gwaii deer licences can be used without touching regional or provincial bag limits. Any Haida Gwaii deer beyond 12 counts toward them (99%).
- Haida Gwaii deer licences cannot be used for mule deer anywhere else in the province (99%).
- Fees: deer $15.00 resident; Haida Gwaii deer $10.00 resident, $25.00 non resident (99%, page 8).
```

|---|---|
| BC hunting licence | Yes (99%) |
| Federal MGBH (Migratory Game Bird Hunting) permit | Yes, $17.00 (99%) |

```more the page 7 words and banded birds
> [!law]

- Migratory game birds are ducks, geese (brant included), American coot, common snipe, band tailed pigeon and mourning dove (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition).
- The permit is the [[MGBHP]] (Migratory Game Bird Hunting Permit). The synopsis fee table prints it at $17.00 (99%, page 8).

- Youth hunters and their mentors or supervising hunters must also carry the proper provincial hunting licences at all times while hunting migratory birds, on top of the federal permits (99%).
- Federal rules are being modernized. The synopsis points to the Government of Canada status page for updates (99%).
- Found a banded bird? Report it to the Bird Banding Laboratory in Maryland, which collates all North American banding records: [pwrc.usgs.gov/BBL/bblretrv](https://www.pwrc.usgs.gov/BBL/bblretrv/) (99%).
```

## Non resident basics

| Non resident | Rule (99%) |
|---|---|
| Licence fee | $75.00, or $180.00 for a non resident alien |
| Small game alone | Needs proof of hunter safety |
| Big game | Always with a guide or permit to accompany holder |

```more the page 7 words and permits to accompany
- Non residents of BC must follow all hunting regulations and need an FWID (Fish and Wildlife ID) to get licences (99%, page 7, 2 October 2026 edition).
- Small game alone needs an unrestricted licence, which needs proof of hunter safety training: upload the certificate in WILD (Wildlife Information and Licensing Data) or show it at Service BC or FrontCounter BC. A certificate not in English or French needs the original plus a notarized translation (99%).
- With no hunting credential you can only get a restricted licence and must be accompanied by a guide outfitter, assistant guide outfitter or a permit to accompany holder whenever you hunt (99%).
- Restricted and unrestricted cost the same. Fees differ by residency: $75.00 non resident, $180.00 non resident alien (99%, pages 7 and 8).

> [!law]
> "Non-resident and non-resident alien hunters are required to be accompanied by a guide outfitter, assistant guide outfitter or a holder of a permit to accompany at all times when hunting big game, regardless of the type of licence (restricted or unrestricted) they have."

- Guided hunt: give the guide outfitter's licence number when buying big game species licences, and get a completed Guide Outfitter Report after the hunt. Missing it is an offence for hunter and guide. A report filed in WILD posts to your profile and counts (99%).
- Permit to accompany: a BC resident who is a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, 19 or older, and held a BC hunting licence and a big game species licence in 3 of the last 5 years (2 of 5 with CORE, Conservation and Outdoor Recreation Education) can take one non resident big game hunting. One permit per resident per licence year (99%).
- A non resident alien qualifies only in a listed family relationship: parent, sibling, child, parent's sibling, sibling's child, grandchild, grandparent, spouse, spouse's parent, child's parent, spouse's sibling or sibling's spouse. Cousins are excluded (99%).
- Permits are not available for every species and area, mountain sheep for example. The permit holder files a report within 10 days after the hunt, whether or not it took place, and pays royalties within 30 days if there is a harvest (99%).
```

## Replacement licences and where the money goes

```diagram rb-fee-split
```

```more the page 8 detail
- Lost, stolen or destroyed species licence, cancelled or not? You must replace it if you plan to keep hunting this licence year (99%, page 8, 2 October 2026 edition).
- Go to a FrontCounter BC or Service BC office. If the original was cancelled you must declare the harvest details from it (99%).
- Replacement fee: $10.00 (99%).
- The $32 resident hunting licence splits into a $25 fee to general government revenue and a $7 conservation surcharge to the HCTF (Habitat Conservation Trust Foundation) (99%).
- Other licence types split differently, averaging 80% fee to 20% surcharge. Surcharge revenue averages around $2.5 million a year (99%).
- HCTF is a non profit charitable foundation. It spends the surcharges from hunting, angling, trapping and guide outfitting licences on conservation projects, education programs and buying wildlife habitat. ## Buying licences
```

```anim rb-mail-vs-counter Online species licences come by mail. In person: right away.
```

```more the page 8 detail
- Online at [www.gov.bc.ca/hunting](https://www.gov.bc.ca/hunting), or at Service BC, FrontCounter BC and participating commercial outlets and sporting goods stores (99%, page 8, 2 October 2026 edition).
- A species licence bought online is mailed and depends on Canada Post delivery times (99%).
- Not in the mail yet? You may qualify for a free replacement if you report the loss to FrontCounter BC or Service BC between 15 and 30 business days after the purchase date (99%).
- Electronic only, registered on your FWID (Fish and Wildlife ID) profile: the resident, non resident and non resident alien hunting licences, initiation licence, youth licence, upland game bird licence, and the Fraser Valley and Gulf Islands special area licences. No paper copy is needed while hunting (99%).
- A parent or legal guardian applies for a youth's hunting or youth licence and signs the written undertaking about supervision (99%).
- Disabled hunting permit: a hunter with a permanent physical disability who cannot exit a vehicle to discharge a firearm, or cannot walk 100 m carrying a firearm, may apply through FrontCounter BC at the [disabled hunting permit page](https://www.gov.bc.ca/disabled-hunting-permit) (99%).
```

> [!rule]
> Buy the species licences in person, two weeks before you need them. The mail does not care about your season dates.

> [!mistake]
> - Treating an LEH authorization as the licence. You still need the hunting licence and the species licence.
> - Leaving cancelled species licences at home. You carry every one bought this year.
> - Assuming a cougar or goat licence works the day you buy it. It is not valid for 2 days.

> [!field]
> Pocket check at the truck: photo ID, every species licence, the federal permit if birds are on the menu, and your FWID number in your head.
