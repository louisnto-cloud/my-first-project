> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Source: BC Hunting and Trapping Regulations Synopsis 2026 to 2028, edition dated 2 October 2026.

> [!why]
> The law gives everyday words exact meanings. "Loaded", "hunting" and "4 point" decide whether you are legal before you ever pull a trigger.

- This session carries every definition from the Definitions section, pages 3 to 5 of the synopsis, in alphabetical order (99%).
- Each entry: the term in bold, the official words in quotes, then one plain English line.
- Printed page numbers are cited. Page 3 and 4 hold nearly all of them; the last four spill onto page 5 (99%).
- The pictures in the synopsis (caribou, elk, moose, sheep, mule deer, tine) are described in words under the term they belong to.
- Terms with the same word are grouped as the synopsis groups them: three moose classes together, four mountain sheep classes together.

## Definitions: Accompany to Antlerless animal

- **Accompany** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "ACCOMPANY - means to remain in the company of the other person, able to see the other person without the aid of any device other than ordinary corrective lenses and able to communicate by unamplified voice with that person."

- Plain English: stay with the person, close enough to see them with your own eyes (glasses are fine, binoculars are not) and to talk without a radio or a shout (99%). This is the test for supervising a youth or initiation hunter and for a permit to accompany a non resident (95%, my reading).

- **All terrain vehicle, [[ATV]]** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "ALL TERRAIN VEHICLE (ATV) - means a wheeled vehicle or tracked vehicle propelled by motorized power, and capable of travel on or off a highway, including motorcycles but not including a snowmobile or motor vehicle that is licensed for highway travel under the Motor Vehicle Act."

- Plain English: a quad, side by side or dirt bike is an ATV (All Terrain Vehicle). A snowmobile is not, and neither is a truck or bike with highway plates (99%).

- **Antlered animal** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "ANTLERED ANIMAL - means a member of the deer family over one year of age bearing visible bony antlers."

- Plain English: a deer, moose, elk or caribou older than 1 year with antlers you can see (99%).

- **[[Antlerless]] animal** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "ANTLERLESS ANIMAL - means a member of the deer family bearing no visible antlers. The small skin or hair covered protuberances of male fawns and calves do not constitute antlers."

- Plain English: no visible antlers means antlerless. A button buck, a male fawn with skin covered bumps, counts as antlerless (99%).

## Definitions: Bait to Big game

- **Bait** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "BAIT - means anything, including meat, cereals, cultivated crops, restrained animal or any manufactured product or material, that may attract wildlife and includes plastic or other imitation foods, but does not include a decoy as described under these regulations."

- Plain English: anything put out to draw animals in, fake food included. A decoy is not bait (99%).

- **B.C. resident** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "B.C. RESIDENT - means a person who is a Canadian citizen or permanent resident of Canada, and whose only or primary residence is in British Columbia and who has been physically present in B.C. for the greater portion of each of 6 calendar months out of the 12 calendar months immediately before doing a thing under the Wildlife Act, or if not a Canadian citizen or permanent resident of Canada, but whose only or primary residence is in British Columbia, and has been physically present in B.C. for the greater portion of each of the 12 calendar months immediately before doing a thing under the Wildlife Act."

- Plain English: a citizen or permanent resident whose main home is in BC and who was physically in BC for more than half of each of 6 of the last 12 months (99%). Not a citizen or permanent resident? Then your main home must be BC and you must have been here more than half of each of the last 12 months (99%).

- **Bearded turkey** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "BEARDED TURKEY - means a turkey with a tuft of hair-like feathers on the chest that grow larger with age."

- Plain English: any turkey showing the beard tuft on its chest. The definition goes by the tuft, not the sex of the bird (99%).

- **Big game** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "BIG GAME - means any mountain sheep, mountain goat, bison, caribou, elk, moose, deer, black bear, cougar, wolf, bobcat, lynx, wolverine or other animal designated by regulation."

- Plain English: 13 named animals, plus any other the regulations add. Wolf, bobcat, lynx and wolverine are big game too, not only the hoofed animals (99%).

## Definitions: Bolt to Buck or bull

- **Bolt** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "BOLT - means a shaft or missile designed to be shot from a crossbow or catapult."

- Plain English: a crossbow arrow (99%).

- **Bow** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "BOW - means a longbow or crossbow."

- Plain English: in BC law a crossbow is a bow (99%). Compound and recurve bows fall under longbow (90%, my reading).

- **[[Brow tine]]** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "BROW TINE - means the first tine (point) projecting forward or upward in the lower 1/3 of the antler of a moose, caribou, elk, or deer."

- Plain English: the first point that sticks forward or up in the bottom third of the antler (99%). It does not count toward a 4 point mule deer buck, and it does count for elk and moose (99%).

- **Buck or bull** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "BUCK OR BULL - with reference to deer, moose or elk means one bearing visible bony antlers but does not include a calf; with reference to caribou means a male bearing visible bony antlers but does not include a calf."

- Plain English: for deer, moose and elk the law goes by visible antlers, not by sex. For caribou it must be a male with antlers, because cow caribou grow antlers too (99%).

## Definitions: Calf to Caribou

- **[[Calf]]** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "CALF - means a moose, elk, or caribou less than twelve months of age."

- Plain English: under 1 year old (99%).

- **Calf moose** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "CALF MOOSE - a moose less than twelve months of age usually less than 1.5 m at shoulder height weighing 135-180 kg."

- Plain English: a first year moose, usually under 1.5 m at the shoulder and 135 to 180 kg (99%).
- What the picture shows: a cow moose standing with a calf at her side, the calf much shorter and with a short, blunt face. The caption says calves are much smaller and show a distinctly stubby face, that not every moose walking with a larger moose is a calf, and to be sure of your target (99%).

- **Cancelled species licence** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "CANCELLED SPECIES LICENCE - means a Species Licence that has been cancelled as indicated on the licence."

- Plain English: a [[Species licence]] (tag) you have cut or notched the way the licence itself shows, after a kill (99%).

- **Caribou, 5 point bull** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "CARIBOU - 5 POINT BULL - has one antler which bears at least 5 tines (points), including the tip of the main beam above the rear point. If rear point is missing, the first rear-facing point will be used as the rear point."

- Plain English: on one antler, count the points above the rear point. The tip of the main beam counts as one (99%).
- What the picture shows: two caribou antlers drawn side by side, each with an arrow to its rear point, the tine that sticks backward low on the main beam. Everything above that is what you count (99%).

## Definitions: Compound crossbow to Crossbow

- **Compound crossbow** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "COMPOUND CROSSBOW - means a crossbow on which the bow string runs through pulleys."

- Plain English: a crossbow with cams or pulleys (99%).

- **Cougar kitten** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "COUGAR KITTEN - means any cougar with spots or any cougar under one year of age."

- Plain English: spots or under 1 year old, either one makes it a kitten (99%).

- **Cow moose** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "COW MOOSE - a female moose recognizable by having no antlers and being 1.8 m at shoulder height weighing 340-420 kg."

- Plain English: an adult female, no antlers, about 1.8 m at the shoulder and 340 to 420 kg (99%).

- **Crossbow** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "CROSSBOW - means a bow fixed across a stock with a groove for the arrow or bolt and a mechanism for holding and releasing the string. (NOTE: The use of crossbows is permitted during special bow only seasons.)"

- Plain English: a crossbow is legal in bow only seasons (99%).

## Definitions: Decoy to Electric bicycle

- **Decoy** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "DECOY - means any material or manufactured product that simulates the appearance or has the form of wildlife."

- Plain English: anything made to look like an animal or bird (99%).

- **Deer family** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "DEER FAMILY - moose, caribou, deer, and elk."

- Plain English: the four antlered species. Rules that say "deer family" cover all four (99%).

- **Drone** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "DRONE - means an aerial vehicle that (a) is not occupied or operated by a person within the vehicle, (b) is operated by (i) remote control, (ii) pre-programmed software, (iii) onboard computer autonomy or artificial intelligence, or (iv) any combination of (i) to (iii) and is capable of attracting, searching for, chasing, pursuing, following after or on the trail of, stalking, spotting or monitoring wildlife."

- Plain English: any pilotless aircraft, remote controlled or self flying, that could be used to find, follow or watch wildlife (99%).

- **[[Edible portions]]** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "EDIBLE PORTIONS - with respect to big game, excluding wolf, lynx, bobcat and wolverine, means the edible portions of the neck, ribs, four quarters and the loins of the animal and with respect to game birds, means the edible portions of both breasts of the bird."

- Plain English: the meat you must not waste. Big game: neck, ribs, all four quarters and the loins. Game birds: both breasts. Wolf, lynx, bobcat and wolverine are exempt (99%).

- **Electric bicycle** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "ELECTRIC BICYCLE - a wheeled device (a) on which a person may ride, (b) with pedals or hand cranks that allow it to be propelled with human power, and (c) with an electric motor."

- Plain English: a bike you can pedal that also has an electric motor (99%).

## Definitions: Elk and Existing road or trail

- **Elk, six points or greater bull** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "ELK - SIX POINTS OR GREATER BULL - means any bull having at least six tines on one antler."

- Plain English: count one antler. Six or more tines, brow tine included, and he is a [[6 point bull]] (99%).
- What the picture shows: two bull elk heads side by side. The left one is labelled 6 point antler, the right one 3 point antler. The caption under them reads, in capitals, that antler tines must be at least 2.5 cm in length (99%).

- **Elk, three points or greater bull** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "ELK - THREE POINTS OR GREATER BULL - means any bull having at least three tines on one antler."

- Plain English: three or more tines on one antler (99%).

- **Existing road or trail** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "EXISTING ROAD OR TRAIL - means (a) a road or trail with a paved surface, (b) a cross-country or downhill ski route marked in a ski area by the owner of the ski area, (c) a road used for logging or mining, (d) a road or trail used for access to fences, power lines, wind generators or communication towers, or (e) a trail on which there is no vegetation except on a strip that, if present, can be straddled by a 4-wheel vehicle."

- Plain English: this matters in motor vehicle closed areas where you may only drive on existing roads and trails. A paved road, a logging or mining road, a service road to a power line or tower, a marked ski run, or a bare two track with at most a grass strip down the middle all count (99%). A game trail or an overgrown track does not (90%, my reading).

## Definitions: Feral pig to Game bird

- **Feral pig** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "FERAL PIG - means a pig of the genus Sus that is not in captivity or is not otherwise under a person’s control."

- Plain English: a loose pig nobody controls (99%).

- **Firearm** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "FIREARM - includes a device that propels a projectile by means of an explosion, compressed gas or spring and includes a rifle, shotgun, handgun, pellet gun, "BB" gun or spring gun but does not include a bow."

- Plain English: rifles, shotguns, handguns, air guns and spring guns are all firearms under the Wildlife Act. A bow is not (99%).

- **Furbearing animal** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "FURBEARING ANIMAL - means a fox, beaver, black bear, marten, fisher, lynx, bobcat, mink, muskrat, river otter, raccoon, striped and spotted skunk, northern flying squirrel, red and Douglas’ squirrel, weasel (least, short-tailed, long-tailed), wolverine, wolf, or coyote."

- Plain English: the trapping list. Black bear, wolf, lynx and bobcat sit on both the big game list and this one (99%). A [[Furbearer]] can be trapped under a trapping licence; this session covers hunting only.

- **Game** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "GAME - includes all big game, small game, game birds and furbearing animals."

- Plain English: when a rule says "game", it means all four groups at once (99%).

- **Game bird** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "GAME BIRD - means any grouse, partridge, quail, pheasant, ptarmigan, migratory game bird, or wild turkey."

- Plain English: upland birds plus the migratory game birds, ducks and geese included (99%).

## Definitions: Handgun to Licence year

- **Handgun** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "HANDGUN - is a firearm that is designed, altered, or intended to be aimed and fired by the action of one hand or that has a barrel less than 305 mm (12") in length."

- Plain English: a one handed firearm, or any firearm with a barrel under 305 mm (99%).

- **Hunt and hunting** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "HUNT AND HUNTING - includes shooting at, attracting, searching for, chasing, pursuing, following after or on the trail of, stalking, or lying in wait for wildlife or attempting to do any of those things, whether or not the wildlife is then or subsequently wounded, killed or captured: (a) with intention to capture the wildlife, or (b) while in possession of a firearm or other weapon."

- Plain English: walking the bush with a rifle while looking for deer is hunting, even if you never fire (99%). So the licence, the open season and every other rule apply from the moment you start looking with a weapon in hand (95%, my reading).

- **Infrared optics** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "INFRARED OPTICS - means an optical device with the capacity to detect infrared radiation."

- Plain English: thermal scopes and thermal monoculars (99%).

- **Licence year** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "LICENCE YEAR - Hunting and Guide Licences - means the period from April 1 - March 31 of the following year. Trapping and Fur Trader's Licences - means the period from July 1 - June 30 of the following year."

- Plain English: hunting licences run 1 April to 31 March. Trapping licences run 1 July to 30 June (99%).

## Definitions: Loaded firearm and Migratory game birds

- **[[Loaded]] firearm** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "LOADED FIREARM - means any firearm containing ammunition in any of the following; the breach or firing chamber of the firearm, a non-detachable magazine that is inside the firearm, a magazine attached to a hinged floor plate, or a detachable magazine that is inside or attached to the firearm. A firearm is loaded even if the firearm is temporarily incapable of being discharged."

- Plain English: a round in the chamber, in a fixed magazine, in a hinged floor plate magazine, or in a detachable magazine that is clipped in or inside the gun. Any one of those and the firearm is loaded, even with the safety on, the bolt open or the action broken (99%).
- An empty chamber does not make it unloaded if the clipped in magazine holds rounds (99%). A loaded magazine in your pocket, out of the gun, is not a loaded firearm (95%, my reading).
- The definition ends by pointing to muzzle loaders on page 12. That page says a muzzle loader with powder and shot in the barrel but unprimed, meaning no powder in the pan of a flint lock and no cap on the nipple of a percussion lock, is not considered a loaded firearm under the Criminal Code (Canada) (99%, page 12, 2 October 2026 edition).

- **Migratory game birds** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "MIGRATORY GAME BIRDS - for which there may be an open season in B.C. and for which a Canadian Migratory Game Bird hunting permit is required are: waterfowl (ducks and geese, including Brant); American Coot; Common Snipe; Band-tailed Pigeon, and Mourning Dove."

- Plain English: ducks, geese, brant, coots, snipe, band tailed pigeon and mourning dove. For all of these you need the federal permit, the [[MGBHP]] (Migratory Game Bird Hunting Permit), on top of your BC licence (99%).

## Definitions: Moose (three classes)

- **Moose, spike fork bull** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "MOOSE - SPIKE-FORK BULL - means a bull moose having no more than two tines (points) on one antler. (Includes tines on main antler and brow palms.) Does not include a calf. See diagram on next page."

- Plain English: one antler must carry 2 points or fewer, counting every point including any on the brow palm (99%). Only one side needs to qualify (90%, my reading). A calf never counts as a [[Spike fork bull]] (99%).
- What the picture shows (page 4): a panel titled Spike Fork Bull Moose with four bull heads drawn from the front. Each wears small antlers with one or two points a side, some a single spike, some a simple fork. All four are legal spike fork bulls (99%).

- **Moose, 10 point bull** (99%, page 3, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "MOOSE - 10 POINT BULL - means a bull moose having at least one antler with a minimum of ten tines (points), including the tines on the brow palm. (See diagram on next page and tine definition.)"

- Plain English: ten or more points on one antler, brow palm points included, each one a true [[Tine]] (99%). That makes him a [[10 point bull]].
- What the picture shows (page 4): one wide moose antler drawn from the front. An arrow from the words 10 Point Bull points at the main palm, with the note that the minimum is a total of ten points, including brow palm, on one antler. A second arrow from the words Tripalm Bull points at the brow palm, with the note that the minimum is a total of three points on one brow palm. Under the drawing a note says hunters must refer to the definition of a tine, because the rounded protuberances at the top of the main palm on some moose may not constitute a point (99%).

- **Moose, tripalm bull** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "MOOSE - TRIPALM BULL - means a bull moose having at least one antler with a brow palm bearing three or more tines (points). The brow palm is separated from the main palm by the deepest antler bay. The deepest bay is the bay whose vertex (deepest location) is the shortest distance from the antler base, when measured along the surface of the antler. See diagram."

- Plain English: find the deepest notch (bay) in the antler, the one whose bottom sits closest to the antler base. The palm in front of that notch is the brow palm. Three or more true tines on that [[Brow palm]] and he is a [[Tripalm bull]] (99%).
- What the picture shows: a full moose rack drawn from the front, labelled Tripalm Bull Moose. Labels mark the Main Palm (the big upper palm), the Brow Palm (the smaller forward palm low on each side), the Antler Base where the antler meets the skull, and the Vertex of the deepest bay, the bottom of the notch between brow palm and main palm. The caption reads: at least 3 points on brow palm (99%).

## Definitions: Motorcycle to Mountain goat kid

- **Motorcycle** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "MOTORCYCLE - means a motor vehicle that runs on 2 or 3 wheels and has a saddle or seat for the driver to sit astride."

- Plain English: a dirt bike or trike you straddle (99%).

- **Motor vehicle** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "MOTOR VEHICLE - means a device in, on or by which a person or thing is being or may be transported or drawn, and which is designed to be self-propelled, and includes an ATV or snowmobile but does not include a device designed to be moved by human, animal or wind power; a device designed to be used exclusively on stationary rails or stationary tracks; or a boat propelled by motorized power."

- Plain English: trucks, cars, quads, snowmobiles and dirt bikes are all motor vehicles under the Wildlife Act. A canoe, a bicycle, a horse, a sailboat, a train and a motorboat are not, for these rules (99%).
- Note: the synopsis has no definition of "off road vehicle" in this edition (99%). The Off Road Vehicle Act is a separate law. Page 11 says it applies to ORVs (Off Road Vehicles) on Crown land and prescribed private land, and that ORVs include ATVs, motorcycles, snowmobiles, side by sides and on highway vehicles under 4,536 kg (99%, page 11, 2 October 2026 edition). See the glossary entry [[ORV]].

- **Mountain goat kid** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "MOUNTAIN GOAT KID - means a mountain goat with horns less than 10 cm in length."

- Plain English: horns shorter than 10 cm make it a kid (99%).

## Definitions: Mountain sheep (four classes)

- **Mountain sheep, full curl bighorn ram** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "MOUNTAIN SHEEP - Full Curl Bighorn Ram - means any male bighorn mountain sheep, the head of which, when viewed squarely from the side, has at least one horn tip extending upwards beyond a straight line drawn through the centre of the nostril and the lowest hindmost portion of the horn base. If the skull and horns are presented for examination, when viewed squarely from the side with both horns in alignment, at least one horn tip extends upward beyond a straight line drawn through the lowest hindmost portion of the horn base and the lowermost edge of the eye socket."

- Plain English: look square at the side of the head. Draw a line from the centre of the nostril through the lowest back part of the horn base. If either horn tip rises above that line, he is full curl (99%). On a bare skull the line runs from the lowest back edge of the horn base through the bottom of the eye socket (99%).
- What the picture shows: a panel titled Full Curl Bighorn with two ram heads in profile. On the LEGAL ram the horn sweeps round and its tip climbs past the dotted line through the nostril and horn base. On the ILLEGAL ram the tip stops short, below the line. Each horn shows an arrow to an annulus, a growth ring. The caption says the reference points are the centre of the nostril and the lowest hindmost portion of the base of the horn (99%).

- **Mountain sheep, 3/4 curl bighorn ram** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "MOUNTAIN SHEEP - 3/4 CURL BIGHORN RAM - means any male bighorn mountain sheep, the head of which, when viewed squarely from the side, has at least one horn tip extending beyond a straight line drawn through the back of the eye opening and at right angles to a line drawn between the centre of the nostril and the lowest hindmost portion of the horn base. If the skull and horns are presented for examination, when viewed squarely from the side with both horns in alignment, at least one horn tip extends beyond a straight line through the back edge of the eye socket and at right angles to a line drawn through the lowest hindmost portion of the horn base and the lowermost edge of the eye socket."

- Plain English: use the same nostril to horn base line, then draw a second line through the back of the eye at right angles to it. A horn tip that reaches past that second line makes him 3/4 curl (99%).
- What the picture shows: a panel titled 3/4 Curl Bighorn. Two ram heads in profile, each with the dotted nostril to horn base line and a second dotted line drawn through the back of the eye, square to the first. LEGAL: the horn tip crosses the eye line. ILLEGAL: it does not reach it. Annuli are marked with arrows (99%).

- **Mountain sheep, mature bighorn ram** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "MOUNTAIN SHEEP - MATURE BIGHORN RAM - means any bighorn ram mountain sheep whose horn tip, when viewed squarely from the side at right angles to the sagittal plane of the skull, extends dorsally beyond the nose bridge plane"

- Plain English: from square on the side, the horn tip rises above the line of the bridge of the nose (99%). The synopsis prints this one with no closing full stop (99%).

- **Mountain sheep, full curl thinhorn ram** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "MOUNTAIN SHEEP - FULL CURL THINHORN RAM - means any thinhorn ram mountain sheep that has attained the age of 8 years as evidenced by true horn annuli as determined by the regional manager or his designate, or whose horn tip, when viewed squarely from the side at right angles to the sagittal plane of the skull, extends dorsally beyond the nose bridge plane. Do not use yearly horn growth annuli to determine the age of a ram in the field, because “false” annuli may be present."

- Plain English: a thinhorn ram is full curl if he is 8 years old by true horn rings, as judged by the regional manager, or if a horn tip rises above the nose bridge line (99%). In the field, judge the curl, not the rings, because false rings fool you (99%).
- What the picture shows: a panel titled Full Curl Thinhorn / Mature Bighorn. Two ram heads in profile with a dotted line along the bridge of the nose. LEGAL: the horn tip climbs above the nose bridge line. ILLEGAL: it stays below (99%).

## Definitions: Mule deer to No shooting area

- **Mule (black tailed) deer, four points or greater buck** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "MULE (BLACK-TAILED) DEER - FOUR POINTS OR GREATER BUCK - means any buck having at least four tines, excluding the brow tine, on one antler."

- Plain English: count one antler. Four or more true tines, not counting the brow tine, and he is a [[4 point buck]] (99%). Black tailed deer are mule deer under this definition (99%).
- What the picture shows: a mule deer buck drawn from the side, labelled Mule (Black tailed) Deer, 4 Point antler. The near antler forks, then each fork forks again, giving four points on that side (99%).

- **No hunting area** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "NO HUNTING AREA - means a designated area in which hunting (see definition) is prohibited."

- Plain English: hunting itself is banned there, and hunting includes searching for wildlife with a weapon (99%).

- **[[No Shooting Area]]** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "NO SHOOTING AREA - means a designated area in which the discharge of firearms is prohibited."

- Plain English: firing a firearm is banned there. Compare the two: a no shooting area bans the shot, a no hunting area bans the hunt (99%). A bow is not a firearm under these definitions, but other closures may still apply, so treat both areas as closed unless the regional pages say otherwise (85%, my reading).

## Definitions: Non resident to Non toxic shot

- **Non resident** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "NON-RESIDENT - means a person who is not a B.C. resident, but who is a Canadian citizen or a permanent resident of Canada, or a person who is not a B.C. resident, but whose only or primary residence is in Canada and has resided in Canada for the 12 month period immediately before making an application under the Wildlife Act or doing another thing relevant to the operation of the Wildlife Act."

- Plain English: a Canadian from another province or territory, or someone whose main home has been in Canada for the last 12 months but not in BC (99%).

- **Non resident alien** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "NON-RESIDENT ALIEN - means a person who is neither a B.C. resident nor a non-resident."

- Plain English: everyone else, mostly visitors from outside Canada (99%).

- **Non resident or alien restricted licence** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "NON-RESIDENT/ALIEN RESTRICTED LICENCE - means a licence issued to a non-resident or a non-resident alien who has not successfully completed examinations, approved by the director, about conservation, outdoor recreation, and safety."

- Plain English: the licence for a visitor with no proof of hunter safety training. Holders must be accompanied even for small game (99%).

- **Non resident or alien unrestricted licence** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "NON-RESIDENT/ALIEN UNRESTRICTED LICENCE - means a licence issued to a non-resident or a non-resident alien who has successfully completed examinations, approved by the director, about conservation, outdoor recreation, and safety."

- Plain English: the licence for a visitor who has passed an approved hunter safety course. They may hunt small game alone, but big game still needs a guide or a permit to accompany (99%).

- **[[Non toxic shot]]** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "NON-TOXIC SHOT - means shotgun pellets consisting of, by weight, not more than one percent lead."

- Plain English: steel, bismuth, tungsten and the like. Lead must be 1 percent or less by weight (99%).

## Definitions: Raptor to Snowmobile

- **Raptor** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "RAPTOR - means birds of the order Falconiformes (falcons), Accipitriformes (vultures, eagles, and hawks), or Strigiformes (owls) and includes the eggs of these birds."

- Plain English: falcons, hawks, eagles, vultures and owls, eggs included. Raptors are wildlife and are never game (99%).

- **Shot** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "SHOT - means a cartridge manufactured so that it contains 8 or more roughly spherical projectiles."

- Plain English: a shell with 8 or more pellets. Birdshot and buckshot are shot. A slug is not (99%).

- **Small game** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "SMALL GAME - includes fox, raccoon, coyote, skunk, snowshoe hare and game birds."

- Plain English: the small animals plus all game birds. No species licence is needed for small game in BC for a resident (99%, page 8).

- **Snowmobile** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "SNOWMOBILE - means a vehicle designed primarily for travel on snow or ice, having one or more steering skis, self-propelled and using one or more endless belts or tracks driven in contact with the ground."

- Plain English: a sled with skis up front and a track behind. It is a motor vehicle but not an ATV (99%).

## Definitions: Tine or point, and Traffic

- **[[Tine]] or point** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "TINE OR "POINT" - means a branch of an antler which is longer than its breadth and is at least 2.5 cm (1 inch) in length, and for the purpose of determining the length of a tine (a) the breadth of the tine is measured (if extending from a palmation of an antler, then in the plane of the palm) at a location at least 2.5 cm (1 inch) from the tip of the tine, and (b) the length of the tine is measured from its tip end, following the midline of the profile of the tine, and following the natural curvature of the tine, to the midpoint of the straight line along which the breadth is measured. (See How to Measure a Tine diagram below)."

- Plain English: a point must be at least 2.5 cm long and longer than it is wide (99%). Measure the width at least 2.5 cm down from the tip, then measure the length from the tip, along the curve of the point, to the middle of that width line (99%).
- What the picture shows: a box titled How to Measure a Tine. Top left, labelled NOT A TINE: a low wide bump marked A inside a square 2.5 cm on each side; it is wider than it is long, so it fails. Top right, labelled TINE: a tall narrow spike marked B inside the same 2.5 cm square; it is at least 2.5 cm long and longer than wide, so it passes (99%).
- Below those, two antler sketches labelled PALMATED (a moose palm with small bumps A and B along its top edge) and NON PALMATED (a branched deer or elk antler with A and B marked), showing where the measuring square sits on a real antler. The caption says that on larger tines the measurements can be taken at any point at least 2.5 cm from the tip (99%).

- **Traffic or trafficking** (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "TRAFFIC OR TRAFFICKING - means to buy, sell, trade or distribute for gain or consideration or to offer to do so."

- Plain English: selling, trading or handing out wildlife or its parts for anything of value, or offering to (99%).

## Definitions: Upland game bird to Wireless camera

- **Upland game bird** (99%, page 5, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "UPLAND GAME BIRD - includes the gallinaceous birds, such as wild turkey, grouse, partridge, ptarmigan, pheasant, and quail."

- Plain English: the chicken like land birds. Ducks and geese are not upland birds (99%).

- **Vehicle** (99%, page 5, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "VEHICLE - means a wheeled or tracked device in, on or by which a person or thing is or may be transported or drawn on a highway."

- Plain English: anything on wheels or tracks that carries people or things on a highway, motor or not. A trailer is a vehicle (95%, my reading).

- **Wildlife** (99%, page 5, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "WILDLIFE - means raptors, threatened species, endangered species, game and other species of vertebrates prescribed as wildlife by regulation."

- Plain English: game is wildlife, but wildlife is bigger than game. Raptors and listed species count too (99%).

- **Wireless camera** (99%, page 5, 2 October 2026 edition)

> [!law] Official definition
> "WIRELESS CAMERA - means a camera that obtains an image by means of a remote or infrared sensor and that is capable of sending an image remotely to an electronic device."

- Plain English: a cell trail camera, one that sends pictures to your phone (99%).

## What this edition no longer defines

- **[[Power boat]]**: gone. The July 2026 edition defined it on page 4 as a boat, canoe or yacht powered by electric, gasoline, oil, steam or other mechanical means, not counting a manually powered boat or one with an outboard tilted or disengaged so it was not ready for immediate use (99%, July 2026 edition, page 4).
- The 2 October 2026 edition has no such entry anywhere in the Definitions section, pages 3 to 5 (99%).
- The words "power boat" still appear once, in item 25 on page 11, which makes it unlawful to use a power boat, aircraft, motor vehicle or other mechanical device to herd or harass wildlife (99%, page 11, 2 October 2026 edition).
- The boat rules for hunting now read differently: item 23 covers hunting wildlife other than migratory game birds from a boat that is not being propelled by a motor, or held stationary by its motor, and item 24 bans hunting migratory birds from or by using a moving boat that is equipped with a motor or sail (99%, page 11, 2 October 2026 edition).
- **Off road vehicle**: never a defined term in this section. The vehicle terms here are ATV, motorcycle, motor vehicle, snowmobile, vehicle and electric bicycle (99%).

> [!rule]
> If a word in a season table looks ordinary, it probably is not. Flip to the definition before you decide an animal is legal.

> [!mistake]
> - Counting the brow tine on a mule deer buck. It is excluded. Count it on elk and moose.
> - Calling a rifle unloaded because the chamber is empty while a loaded magazine is clipped in.
> - Thinking "hunting" starts at the shot. It starts when you search with a weapon in hand.

> [!field]
> Before you count points, ask: is each one at least 2.5 cm and longer than it is wide? If not, it is not a point.
