## CWD Management Zone: the law on transport

> [!law] Carcass Transport Restrictions - Prevent Spread of Chronic Wasting Disease, page 15
> "A person who kills a deer, elk, or moose within M.U.s 4-1 to 4-8 and 4-20 to 4-25 (the CWD Management Zone, see page 36) must not ship or transport, or engage another person to ship or transport, outside of the CWD Management Zone, the brain tissue or the spinal column, including the vertebrae and central nervous system tissue but not including the tail."
>
> "There is a 24-hour exemption after harvest for deer, elk or moose harvested in M.U. 4-7, M.U. 4-8, and M.U. 4-25 that require travel outside of the CWD Management Zone to immediately access a designated CWD freezer. This exemption applies only to the routes below."

- Killed a deer, elk or moose in the zone? The brain and the spinal column, vertebrae included, may not leave the zone. The tail may (99%, page 15).
- You may not get someone else to carry them out either (99%).
- Exception: from MUs 4-7, 4-8 and 4-25, you have 24 hours to drive the whole animal to a CWD freezer, on the listed routes only (99%).

## CWD Management Zone: testing and the biologists' reminders

> [!law] Page 37 box
> "Chronic Wasting Disease was confirmed in white-tailed deer and mule deer populations in the Kootenay Region in January 2024."
>
> "An emergency response has been initiated to prevent spread and access samples to better understand the extent of the outbreak."
>
> "A high risk boundary (the CWD Management Zone) that includes MUs 4-1 to 4-8 and 4-20 to 4-25 has been defined based on animal movement and proximity to CWD cases in BC, Alberta and Montana."
>
> "Mandatory testing is expanded to include deer, elk and moose harvested within the CWD Management Zone and samples must be submitted to a designated freezer before leaving this area. For freezer locations see www.gov.bc.ca/CWDdropoff"
>
> "There is a 24-hour exemption after harvest on specified routes for deer, elk or moose harvested in M.U. 4-7, M.U. 4-8, and M.U. 4-25 that require travel outside of the CWD Management Zone to immediately access a designated CWD freezer. See page 15 for specific routes."
>
> "Brain tissue and spinal column (except for the tail) from deer, elk and moose harvested within the CWD Management Zone cannot be transport outside of this boundary. It is recommended to leave these parts at or near the kill site or dispose of them at a landfill before leaving the area."
>
> "We encourage hunters to continue hunting in the affected areas; hunting can help to suppress transmission and spread by removing infected animals from the landscape. It is estimated that less than 1% of cervids currently have CWD in these areas."
>
> "Hunting is the most important tool for managing CWD as it helps to suppress disease prevalence and provides essential information to support science-based management of CWD."
>
> "For more information visit: www.gov.bc.ca/chronicwastingdisease"

- Testing is mandatory for every deer, elk and moose taken in the zone (99%).
- Drop the head sample at a designated freezer before you leave the zone (99%). Freezer list: www.gov.bc.ca/CWDdropoff (99%).
- Leave the brain and spine at the kill site or at a landfill inside the zone (99%, a recommendation).
- Under 1% of deer family animals in the zone are thought to carry CWD. Keep hunting there (99%).
- The season tables repeat it: "Mandatory submission of deer heads for CWD testing in highest risk areas", and the same for elk and moose (99%, page 39).

## Regional bag limits

> [!law] Regional Bag Limits, page 37
> "Bobcat: The bag limit for Bobcat is 1."
>
> "Cougar: The bag limit for Cougar is 2, except in MUs 4-1 to 4-5, 4-9 to 4-27, 4-32 to 4-37 and 4-40 where the bag limit is 1."
>
> "Coyote: No bag limit (NBL)."
>
> "Deer: The aggregate bag limit in the Kootenay Region is 2. The bag limit for mule (black-tailed) deer is 1. The bag limit for white-tailed is 2; one may be antlerless and one may be a buck. One additional deer (white-tailed or mule deer, antlered or antlerless) may be taken during the January 5-31 Cranbrook Deer Hunt (see map D27). Hunters cannot exceed the provincial bag limit of 3 deer."
>
> "Grouse: The daily aggregate bag limit for grouse: blue (dusky), spruce (Franklin), and ruffed is 5."
>
> "Turkey: The regional aggregate bag limit for turkey is 3. Only one turkey may be taken in the spring and only two turkey may be taken in the fall."

- Deer: 2 in Region 4 all together. Mule deer 1. White tails 2: one buck, one antlerless (99%, page 37).
- The page 14 table: Region 4 mule deer 1 buck, 0 antlerless; white tails 1 buck, 1 antlerless; regional 2 (99%, page 14).
- Cranbrook Deer Hunt: one more deer of either species and either sex, 5 to 31 January, inside the Map D27 area. Never more than 3 deer province wide (99%, pages 14, 37 and 41).
- A Kootenay mule deer buck uses up your one mule deer buck from Regions 3 to 8 (99%, page 14).
- Cougar: 2 in 4-6 to 4-8, 4-28 to 4-31, 4-38 and 4-39; 1 everywhere else (99%).
- Grouse: 5 a day all three species together (99%). Turkey: 3 a year, 1 spring, 2 fall (99%).

> [!rule]
> In the Kootenay, the deer is not done when it is down. Head to the freezer, spine stays in the zone.

> [!mistake]
> - Driving a Cranbrook buck home to Heffley Creek with the head on. The brain and spine may not leave the CWD (Chronic Wasting Disease) zone.
> - Shooting a mule deer buck on 20 November because white tails run to 30 November. Mule deer close 10 November.
> - Putting out a salt block for deer anywhere in Region 4. Feeding or baiting ungulates is unlawful region wide.

> [!field]
> Hunting the zone: find the nearest CWD freezer before you go, pack a saw, and plan to leave spine and skull at the kill site or landfill.
