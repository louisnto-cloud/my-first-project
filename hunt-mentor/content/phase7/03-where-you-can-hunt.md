> [!why]
> A legal animal in the wrong place is an illegal animal. Parks, roads, buildings and fences decide where your season actually applies.

- This session carries printed pages 9 and 10 of the synopsis in full: site and access restrictions, motor vehicle prohibitions, the federal firearms lines, the 100 m rule, No Shooting Areas, the road allowance rule and every highway closure (99%).
- Exact official words sit in quotes. The plain English line under each is this app's.
- Text the synopsis prints in green is new or changed in this edition. The page 2 key says: "New information and regulation changes are highlighted as green" (99%, page 2, 2 October 2026 edition). Those lines are marked "new in this edition" below.
- Regional closures (more No Hunting and No Shooting Areas, more motor vehicle closures) sit in each region's own pages and are not part of pages 9 and 10 (99%).

## National Parks

> [!law] National Parks, page 9
> "Hunting is prohibited in all National Parks. All firearms transported in National Parks must be dismantled, carried in a closed case, or wrapped and tied securely so as not to expose any part of the firearm."
>
> "Firearms are also not permitted outside of a vehicle, vessel or aircraft in a National Park unless they are being moved into, or out of, a person’s premises, or with a permit issued by the park superintendent."

- No hunting at all in any National Park (99%, page 9, 2 October 2026 edition).
- Driving through one with a firearm: take it apart, case it, or wrap and tie it so no part shows (99%).
- The firearm stays in the vehicle, boat or plane. Out of it only when moving it into or out of your own premises, or with a superintendent's permit (99%).

## Regional District Parks

> [!law] Regional District Parks, page 9
> "The discharge of firearms and bows is prohibited in most regional district parks. Regional district bylaws are NOT included in this synopsis. Contact the Regional District for further information."
>
> "Hunting and the discharge of firearms is prohibited within 100 m of all Regional District Parks in Regions 1 and 2."

- Most regional district parks ban firing a firearm or a bow (99%, page 9, 2 October 2026 edition).
- The local bylaw is the rule there, and the synopsis does not print it. Ask the regional district (99%).
- In Region 1 (Vancouver Island) and Region 2 (Lower Mainland) you may not hunt or shoot within 100 m (109 yd) of any regional district park (99%). Your Mission base is in Region 2 (95%).
- Province wide, the 100 m rule on page 10 also names "regional district park" (99%). That rule is quoted later in this session.

## National Wildlife Areas and Migratory Bird Sanctuaries

> [!law] Page 9
> "Hunting is prohibited in National Wildlife Areas and Migratory Bird Sanctuaries unless a special federal permit is granted or notices to the contrary are posted. Blue signs depicting a loon mark the boundaries. Contact Canadian Wildlife Service in Delta for more information (604) 350-1950."

- Federal bird lands are closed to hunting by default (99%, page 9, 2 October 2026 edition).
- Open only with a special federal permit, or where posted notices say hunting is allowed (99%).
- Look for blue signs with a loon on them. They mark the boundary (99%).
- Questions: the Canadian Wildlife Service office in Delta, 604 350 1950 (99%).

## BC Parks: is hunting allowed?

> [!law] BC Parks, page 9
> "Individuals visiting BC Parks, either a Park, Conservancy, Recreation Area, or Protected Area should be aware that the Park Act and its regulations apply, as do the hunting regulations in this synopsis. Visit www.bcparks.ca for more information on activities allowed or prohibited in specific parks."
>
> "Hunting and the discharge of firearms, or bows is prohibited in many BC Parks. Before hunting in a BC Park, hunters must verify that hunting is permitted. To see if the park you are planning to visit allows hunting, check the park's webpage: bcparks.ca/find-a-park/. Hunting in BC Parks is only permitted during a lawful open season."

- Parks, conservancies, recreation areas and protected areas all count as BC Parks. Two sets of rules apply at once: the Park Act and the hunting regulations (99%, page 9, 2 October 2026 edition).
- Many BC Parks ban hunting and firing a firearm or bow (99%).
- The rule puts the check on you: before you hunt a park, confirm on its own page, through the [BC Parks find a park page](https://bcparks.ca/find-a-park/), that hunting is allowed (99%).
- Even where allowed, only in a lawful open season (99%).

## BC Parks: firearms, vehicles and park roads

> [!law] BC Parks, page 9
> "When a BC Park is closed to hunting or there is no open season for any species, both the possession and discharge of a firearm or bow are prohibited except when authorized by a park officer. Possession is only allowed when such weapons are carried within a vehicle or when authorized by a park officer."

- Park closed to hunting, or no season open for anything: you may not even carry a firearm or bow. It stays in the vehicle unless a park officer authorizes it (99%, page 9, 2 October 2026 edition).

> [!law]
> "The use of horses and motor vehicles (including motorcycles, snowmobiles, ATVs and other self-propelled vehicles or cycles) is generally prohibited in BC Parks except where specifically authorized. All motor vehicles on park roads must be licensed. Use of aircraft to arrive at or depart from some parks is restricted."

- No horses, trucks, dirt bikes, snowmobiles or ATVs (All Terrain Vehicles) off authorized routes. Anything on a park road must be licensed (99%).

> [!law]
> "It is prohibited to hunt or discharge a firearm or bow in a BC Park within 400 m of either side of the centre line of any park road or highway except as authorized by a park officer, unless otherwise stated in this synopsis for a specific road. A park road is a road in a park or recreation area that is designated and developed for licensed motor vehicles."

- Inside a BC Park: no hunting and no shooting within 400 m (437 yd) of the centre line of any park road or highway (99%).
- A park road is one built and designated for licensed vehicles (99%).
- Gut piles: move them away from hiking trails and busy spots, or tell park staff where the kill was (99%, page 9).

## Recreation sites and trails

> [!law] Recreation Sites and Trails, page 9
> "Provincial Recreation Orders prohibiting motorized use."
>
> "Unless authorized by a recreation officer; it is unlawful to trap or discharge a firearm, bow or crossbow on or into: The developed portion of a Recreation Site or Interpretive Forest. The “developed portion”, refers to that area that extends 100m in all directions from a structure, day use area, camping area, trail staging area, or similar area. Any portion of a Recreation Trail where a prohibition has been posted."
>
> "It is unlawful to dispose of game residue in the developed portion of a recreation site."

- Provincial Recreation Orders can ban motorized use. The list: [provincial Recreation Orders](https://www.gov.bc.ca/recreation-orders) (99%, page 9, 2 October 2026 edition).
- These rules come from the Forest Recreation Regulation (99%).
- No shooting a firearm, bow or crossbow on or into the developed part of a recreation site or interpretive forest (99%).
- Developed part: 100 m (109 yd) in every direction from a structure, a day use area, a campsite, a trail staging area or similar (99%).
- On a recreation trail, the ban applies only where it is posted (99%).
- No game residue, such as a gut pile or scraps, in the developed part of a recreation site (99%).
- Map of sites: [www.sitesandtrailsbc.ca](https://www.sitesandtrailsbc.ca) (99%).

## Ecological reserves and conservation lands

> [!law] Ecological Reserves, page 9
> "Hunting, trapping, and angling are prohibited in Ecological Reserves in B.C. The discharge of a firearm, or bow is prohibited. A complete list of Ecological Reserves, maps and legal descriptions is available at www.bcparks.ca/eco_reserve."

- An [[Ecological reserve]] is closed to hunting, trapping and fishing, and to firing any firearm or bow (99%, page 9, 2 October 2026 edition).

> [!law] Conservation Lands, page 9
> "B.C. has different types of Conservation Lands, including Wildlife Management Areas, Crown Reserves, and lands acquired for fish and wildlife management; sometimes in cooperation with other agencies (e.g., Ducks Unlimited, The Nature Trust of BC)."
>
> "Hunting, the discharge of firearms or bows, motor vehicles, and access are restricted or prohibited in some Conservation Lands."
>
> "Before hunting in a Conservation Land, please contact your regional office."

- Conservation lands include Wildlife Management Areas, Crown reserves, and land bought for fish and wildlife, often with partners such as Ducks Unlimited or The Nature Trust of BC (99%).
- Some of them limit or ban hunting, shooting, vehicles or entry itself (99%).
- The synopsis does not list which ones on these pages. Before you hunt one, call the regional office (99%).

## Towns, reserves and cabins

> [!law] Municipal Restrictions, page 9
> "Most municipalities have bylaws restricting and controlling the use of weapons, firearms and bows within their boundaries. Municipal bylaws are NOT included in this synopsis. Consult municipal clerks for details."
>
> "Most municipalities have local bylaws restricting and controlling wildlife attractants and illegal dumping, so hunters are reminded to dispose of carcasses and animal parts responsibly. Carcasses are considered wildlife attractants and can attract large predators. Do not dispose of them within municipal boundaries or in areas frequented by the public."

- Inside a city or district, the local bylaw decides where you may shoot a firearm or bow. The synopsis does not print bylaws (99%, page 9, 2 October 2026 edition).
- A carcass is a wildlife attractant. Never dump one inside town limits or where people go (99%).

> [!law] Indian Reserves, page 9
> "Indian Reserves are private land. Permission must be obtained from the local Indian business office in order to hunt on or across these lands."

- Treat a reserve like private land. Ask the band office before you hunt on it or cross it (99%).

> [!law] Unauthorized Use of Cabins, page 9
> "While in the field you may encounter cabins. These belong to someone engaged in a legitimate business such as trapping or guiding. They are private and are not to be entered, damaged, or disturbed"

- A trapper's or guide's cabin is private. Do not go in, damage it or disturb it (99%).

## Access: the Trespass Act

- "Several laws govern access" (99%, page 9, 2 October 2026 edition). The synopsis names four: the Trespass Act, the Forest and Range Practices Act, the Off Road Vehicle Act and the Wildlife Act (99%).

> [!law] Trespass Act, page 9
> "The provincial Trespass Act limits public access to enclosed private or leased land. Private or leased property is considered enclosed if any one of the following conditions are met: there are clearly visible signs prohibiting trespassing posted at each ordinary access point; or the property is surrounded by a lawful fence; or the property is surrounded by a natural boundary such as a riverbank or a 4 1/2 foot hedge."
>
> "In some areas local clubs may have arrangements with landowners to exclude other hunters and may post No Hunting or No Trespassing signs."

- Private or leased land is [[Enclosed land]] if any one of these is true (99%):
  - No trespassing signs are clearly visible at each normal way in.
  - A lawful fence goes all the way round.
  - A natural boundary goes all the way round, such as a riverbank or a hedge about 1.4 m (4 1/2 ft) high.
- Enclosed land is closed to the public without the owner's permission (90%, my reading).
- Some clubs lease hunting rights and post No Hunting or No Trespassing signs (99%).

## Access: Forest and Range Practices Act

> [!law] Forest and Range Practices Act, page 9
> "It is illegal for individuals to cause environmental damage. Irresponsible off-road vehicle use can disturb soil and destroy plants, risk watershed and water source quality, threaten or kill wildlife, introduce invasive plants, and reduce forage. Environmental damage includes any change to soil that adversely alters an ecosystem. Individuals found to have caused environmental damage may be fined $575. More serious cases of damage could lead to penalties of up to $100,000 and/or a year in jail."
>
> "While travelling on a Forest Service Road, operators of ATVs are required to hold a valid driver’s licence and carry a minimum of $200,000 third-party liability insurance."

- Rutting up a meadow or a creek bank with a quad is environmental damage (90%, my reading). The fine starts at $575 (99%, page 9, 2 October 2026 edition).
- Serious cases: up to $100,000, a year in jail, or both (99%).
- Riding an ATV (All Terrain Vehicle) on a Forest Service Road: you need a valid driver's licence and at least $200,000 of third party liability insurance (99%).

## Access: Off Road Vehicle Act

> [!law] Off Road Vehicle Act, page 9
> "The Off Road Vehicle Act (ORV) applies to ORVs on Crown land and prescribed private land. ORVs include ATVs, motorcycles, snowmobiles, side-by-sides, and on-highway vehicles (under 4,536 kg)."
>
> "Mandatory registration applies to ORVs. The plate or sticker must be clearly displayed on the front, back or left side of the ORV."
>
> "Mandatory safety laws apply to all ORVs; this includes helmets for ride-astride ORVs and seatbelts (if installed). People under 16 must be supervised by an adult at least 19 years."

- The Act covers an [[ORV]] (Off Road Vehicle) on [[Crown land]] and on private land named in its regulations (99%, page 9, 2 October 2026 edition).
- ORVs: ATVs, motorcycles, snowmobiles, side by sides, and on highway vehicles under 4,536 kg (99%).
- Every ORV must be registered, with the plate or sticker clearly shown on the front, back or left side (99%).
- Helmet on any ORV you sit astride. Seatbelt on if one is fitted (99%).
- Riders under 16 must be supervised by an adult aged 19 or older (99%).
- More: [www.gov.bc.ca/offroadvehicles](https://www.gov.bc.ca/offroadvehicles). The synopsis prints the BC Laws address as www.bclaws.gov.ca; the working address is [www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca) (99%).
- Saw a breach? Call the [[RAPP]] (Report All Poachers and Polluters) line, 1 877 952 7277 (99%).

## Access: cultivated land and grazing leases

> [!law] Wildlife Act, page 9
> "A person is not permitted to hunt on cultivated land or on Crown land which is subject to a grazing lease while the land is occupied by livestock, without the consent of the owner, lessee, or occupant of the land."
>
> "Note: leased land is subject to the Trespass Act, regardless of whether livestock are present."

- [[Cultivated land]]: no hunting without the owner's, lessee's or occupant's consent (99%, page 9, 2 October 2026 edition).
- [[Grazing lease]] on Crown land: no hunting while livestock are on it, without the lessee's or occupant's consent (99%).
- New in this edition: leased land falls under the Trespass Act whether or not cattle are present (99%). So a signed or fenced lease is closed without permission even when empty (90%, my reading).
- The same cultivated land and grazing lease rule is item 53 of the "It is unlawful" list on page 12 (99%).

## Motor vehicle prohibitions

> [!law] Motor Vehicle Prohibitions and Restrictions, page 10
> "There are 2 types of motor vehicle prohibitions under the Wildlife Act, and all types can be seasonal or year-round."
>
> "1. Motor Vehicle Closed Area: Prohibits the use or operation of a motor vehicle and e-bike. These prohibitions can be for all motor vehicles, or specific to ATVs/e-bikes or snowmobiles."
>
> "2. Motor Vehicle for Hunting Closed Area: The operation of motor vehicles and e-bikes to hunt, transport wildlife, transport equipment and supplies which are intended for or in support of hunting, or transport hunters to and from the location of wildlife is prohibited. These prohibitions can apply to all motor vehicles or be specific to ATVs/e-bikes or snowmobiles."

- Type 1, a [[Motor Vehicle Closed Area]]: no driving the vehicles it covers at all, hunter or not (99%, page 10, 2 October 2026 edition).
- Type 2, a Motor Vehicle for Hunting Closed Area: you may drive, but not to hunt, not to haul game, gear or hunters, and not to get hunters to the animals (99%).
- Either type can cover all motor vehicles, or only ATVs and electric bicycles, or only snowmobiles. Either can be seasonal or all year (99%).
- Electric bicycles are named in both types (99%). A closure for all motor vehicles, or for ATVs and electric bicycles, covers your e bike too (95%, my reading).

## Motor vehicle prohibitions: where they apply

> [!law] Page 10
> "Unless specifically exempted, all motor vehicle prohibitions include private land. Unless specifically included, motor vehicle prohibitions do not include highways (as defined in the Motor Vehicle Act). See definitions section for vehicle types."
>
> "Information signs may be posted at the entry of closure areas. Where there are inconsistencies with signs and the regulation, the regulation is the final authority."

- A closure covers private land inside it unless that land is exempted (99%, page 10, 2 October 2026 edition).
- Highways are outside closures unless a closure names them (99%).
- If a sign and the regulation disagree, the regulation wins (99%). A missing or wrong sign is no defence (90%, my reading).
- The rules sit in the Wildlife Act Motor Vehicle Prohibition Regulation. The closures themselves are in the regional pages and on detailed online maps at [www.gov.bc.ca/motorvehicleprohibitions](https://www.gov.bc.ca/motorvehicleprohibitions) (99%).
- Vehicle words (motor vehicle, ATV, snowmobile, electric bicycle) are defined on pages 3 and 4, carried in full in the Rule Book definitions session (99%).

## Federal firearms and the 100 m rule

> [!law] Federal Firearms Legislation, page 10
> "A BC hunting licence does not give authority to carry a firearm."
>
> "For information on the Canadian Firearm Licence contact Canadian Firearm Centre at 1-800-731-4000 or visit the website at www.rcmp-grc.gc.ca/cfp-pcaf/."

- Your hunting licence is not a firearms licence (99%, page 10, 2 October 2026 edition). The federal licence is the [[PAL]] (Possession and Acquisition Licence) (95%, my reading).
- Canadian Firearm Centre, as the synopsis names it: 1 800 731 4000 (99%).

> [!law] No Shooting or Hunting Areas, page 10
> "It is unlawful to hunt or discharge a firearm within 100 m of a church, school building, school yard, playground, regional district park, dwelling house, or farm or ranch building that is occupied by persons or domestic animals. An owner or occupier of land, or an employee of an owner or occupier are exempted near a dwelling house or farm or ranch building that is occupied by persons or domestic animals, for the purposes of slaughtering livestock."

- No hunting and no shooting within 100 m (109 yd) of a church, a school building or school yard, a playground, a regional district park, a dwelling house, or a farm or ranch building with people or domestic animals in it (99%).
- The only exemption: an owner, occupier or their employee slaughtering livestock near their own house or farm buildings (99%). It is not a hunting exemption (95%, my reading).

## No Shooting Areas

> [!law] No Shooting Areas, page 10
> "It is unlawful to discharge a firearm in a No Shooting Area (see Definitions section). No Shooting Areas are open to the use of bows (including crossbows) unless specifically restricted (see regional sections)."

- A [[No Shooting Area]] bans firing a firearm (99%, page 10, 2 October 2026 edition).
- Bows and crossbows are allowed there unless the region restricts them (99%).
- A No Hunting Area is different: hunting itself is banned there, bow or firearm (95%, my reading of the page 4 definition).
- The No Shooting Areas themselves are listed in each region's pages (99%).

## Highways: the road allowance rule

> [!law] Highway No Shooting or Hunting Areas, page 10
> "It is unlawful to discharge a firearm across or to discharge a firearm or hunt within the road allowances of all numbered highways and any two lane or greater public road in B.C. that is maintained by the Ministry of Transportation and Infrastructure (or their contractors), the federal government or another province or territory. The road allowance extends:"
>
> "(a) 15 m on either side of the middle of a road with less than three lanes, or"
>
> "(b) 15 m from the edge of the paved surface of a highway with three lanes or more, or"
>
> "(c) to the boundary of private or cultivated land,"
>
> "whichever comes first."

- Which roads: every numbered highway, and every public road of 2 lanes or more kept up by the Ministry of Transportation and Infrastructure or its contractors, the federal government, or another province or territory (99%, page 10, 2 October 2026 edition).
- Three bans: no shooting across the [[Road allowance]], no shooting within it, no hunting within it (99%).
- How wide, whichever comes first (99%):
  - Under 3 lanes: 15 m (16 yd) each side of the middle of the road.
  - 3 lanes or more: 15 m from the edge of the pavement.
  - Or to the line of private or cultivated land, if that is closer.
- More restrictive closures on some roads come next, and more sit in the regional pages (99%).

## Regional closures

> [!law] Regional No Hunting or Shooting Areas, page 10
> "The No Hunting or Shooting areas described above are provincial or multi-regional in area."

- Every closure on this page crosses regions or covers the province (99%, page 10, 2 October 2026 edition).
- Each region adds its own No Hunting and No Shooting Areas in its regional section (99%). Those are not part of this session.

> [!rule]
> Before the first shot of the day, ask three things: whose land is this, how far is the road, how far is the nearest building.

> [!mistake]
> - Thinking shot only means anywhere near the road. The road allowance itself is closed to every shot and every hunt.
> - Trusting a missing closure sign. The regulation, not the sign, is the final authority.
> - Hunting an empty pasture on a grazing lease without asking. Leased land is under the Trespass Act even with no cattle on it.

> [!field]
> Pace 20 steps off the road before you load. On the routes listed above, carry the rifle 400 m in before you chamber a round.
