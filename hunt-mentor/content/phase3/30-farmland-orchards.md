## Crops and who eats them

| Crop or field | Who comes | Certainty |
|---|---|---|
| Alfalfa, hay, clover | deer, elk, geese | 85% |
| Corn and grain stubble | geese, ducks, deer | 75% |
| Grass and forage fields, Fraser delta | wigeon, geese, mallards | 70% |
| Orchards | deer, elk, quail, bears in fruit season | 80% |
| Vineyards | deer, quail | 70% |
| Berry fields, Fraser Valley | black tailed deer, bears | 65% |
| Pasture | geese; deer at the edges | 70% |

- Fields are feed. Edges and hedgerows are cover (Tip).

```more sources for each row
- Elk relish cultivated forage crops such as alfalfa and clover (85%, BC Ministry of Environment, Elk in BC).
- Hayfields and alfalfa in the North Thompson pull white tails every evening and mule deer in hard weather (80%, from [Food, water and cover](#/s/food-water-cover/0)).
- Agricultural areas in the BG (Bunchgrass), PP and IDF list elk, mule deer, white tailed deer, coyote, pocket gopher, Canada goose, kestrel and magpie (95%, zone book Tables 18, 20, 22).
- Fraser Valley: corn stubble, berry fields, pasture and hay draw black tailed deer at dusk, and ducks and geese in winter (65%, from Food, water and cover).
- Deer and elk chew buds, spurs, shoots and leaves of all fruit tree varieties (85%, BC Tree Fruit Production Guide).
- Bears are drawn to fruit trees in fall; BC conservation programs ask residents to pick ripe and fallen fruit daily (70%, WildSafeBC via news).
```

## Read a farm field

```diagram rp-farm-field
```

- Deer enter at the corner or gap nearest cover (Tip).
- Hedgerows and ditches are travel lanes (Tip).
- 100 m ring around occupied houses and barns: no hunting, no shooting (99%).

```more field reading in detail
- Field deer come out the same corner most nights, from the nearest thick cover, with the wind in their favour (hunter lore, 60%).
- Look for trails worn into the field edge, fence crossings with hair, and droppings at the corners (Tip). See [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0) and [Trails and funnels](#/s/funnels/0).
- Creek strips through farmland link timber to fields (Tip). See [Wetlands and riparian strips](#/s/wetland-country/0).
- Glass fields from public roads at last light. Never shine lights or shoot from the road (Tip; road rules in [Where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt/0)).
```

## The 100 m ring and cultivated land

```diagram rb-100m-ring
```

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- No hunting or shooting within 100 m of an occupied house or farm building (99%).
- Cultivated land: always ask (99%).

```more the official words
> [!law] No Shooting or Hunting Areas, page 10, 2 October 2026 edition
> "It is unlawful to hunt or discharge a firearm within 100 m of a church, school building, school yard, playground, regional district park, dwelling house, or farm or ranch building that is occupied by persons or domestic animals."

> [!law] Wildlife Act, page 9
> "A person is not permitted to hunt on cultivated land or on Crown land which is subject to a grazing lease while the land is occupied by livestock, without the consent of the owner, lessee, or occupant of the land."

- Orchards and vineyards are cultivated land: get the owner's consent (90%, my reading, from [Region 8 Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-8/0)).
- Many valley towns and districts add discharge bylaws and No Shooting areas. Check [Region 8](#/s/rb-region-8/0), [Region 2](#/s/rb-region-2/0) and the local sessions before every new farm (Tip).
- Full rules: [Where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt/0).
```

## Okanagan orchards and deer

```anim rp-orchard-deer A deer slips through a fence gap into the orchard rows.
```

- Deer and elk eat fruit tree buds and shoots (85%).
- Many orchards are deer fenced (80%).
- Gaps and gates are funnels (Tip).

```more orchard deer facts
- Deer and elk chew buds, spurs, shoots and leaves of all fruit trees. Small damaged trees may never become productive. Antler rubbing breaks branches and strips bark (85%, BC Tree Fruit Production Guide).
- The guide calls a properly built fence the only long term solution (85%).
- South Okanagan orchardists have asked for deer culls because of damage (70%, BC Fruit Growers' Association via news).
- An orchard owner with deer damage may welcome a polite, safe, local hunter. Ask in person, early (Tip). See [Landowner permission](#/s/landowner-permission/0).
```

```diagram rp-fence-specs
```

```more fence numbers
- Woven wire: at least 2.4 m high with 15 cm mesh, secured close to the ground so deer cannot crawl under (85%).
- Electric: 1.5 to 2.1 m high, 7 to 9 high tensile wires spaced 20 to 30 cm apart. Okanagan research found vertical designs work better than slanted ones (85%).
- Tip: a fence pushes deer along it to the end, a gate or a washout. That is where to look for tracks.
```

## Fraser Valley winter fields

```diagram rp-fv-fields
```

- About 5 million migratory birds winter in the Fraser estuary (70%).
- Ducks and geese graze winter fields hard (70%).
- Mallard, wigeon, snow goose most common (70%).

```more the Delta survey
- Delta Farmland and Wildlife Trust, 2020 to 2021 survey of about 130 fields: by March 2021, 60% were grazed down to the soil surface; only 21 fields showed no grazing (70%, Delta Optimist reporting). The "some grazing" share is my maths from those numbers.
- 2021 to 2022 surveys: mallard, American wigeon and snow goose were the most common in Delta and south Richmond, with northern pintail, trumpeter swan and gadwall (70%).
- The trust's winter cover crop program has planted 33,210 ha since it began (70%).
- About 5 million migratory birds use the Fraser River estuary as wintering habitat (70%).
- Fraser Valley goose and duck rules and areas: [Region 2](#/s/rb-region-2/0), [Migratory birds](#/s/rb-migratory-birds/0). Field goose tactics: [Geese](#/s/geese/0).
```

## Scout a farm in 4 steps

```steps Scout and ask in 4 steps
rp-farm-1 | From public roads, watch fields at last light.
rp-farm-2 | Mark the corner or gap where deer step out.
rp-farm-3 | Find who owns or works the land.
rp-farm-4 | Ask, then hunt only where they say.
```

- A yes from one farmer is worth 10 public spots (Tip).

```more asking well
- Knock in person, in daylight, off season if you can, no firearm in sight (Tip). Full method and a permission form: [Landowner permission](#/s/landowner-permission/0).
- Offer your phone number and plate, walk not drive, shoot only where they say, help with chores, share meat if they want it (Tip).
- Leased farmland may be signed by a hunting club with exclusive rights (99%, page 9: "local clubs may have arrangements with landowners to exclude other hunters").
```

```steps Ask permission in 6 steps
lc-ask-1 | Knock in person, in daylight, no firearm in sight.
lc-ask-2 | Say who you are and where you live first.
lc-ask-3 | Ask one short question. Then listen.
lc-ask-4 | Offer your number, your plate and your help.
lc-ask-5 | Get it in writing.
lc-ask-6 | Thank them, every time.
```

## Farm safety

```photo hab-woods-rose-slope Brushy farm edges: quail cover and hidden workers.
```

- Workers, ladders, irrigation and livestock are everywhere (Tip).
- Know every building and road before you load (Tip).

```more farm safety checklist
- Ask the owner where workers, pickers, ladders and machinery will be that day (Tip).
- Shot falls a long way. Never shoot toward roofs, roads, livestock or greenhouses (Tip). See [Shotgun skills](#/s/shotgun-skills/0).
- Sprayed orchards may have re entry signs. Respect them and ask (Tip).
- Close every gate you open. Never drive on wet fields (Tip).
- 100 m rule around occupied buildings (99%, above).
```

## Common mistakes

```diagram rb-100m-ring
```

> [!mistake]
> - Hunting a field because "nobody was there". Cultivated land always needs consent.
> - Shooting toward a house, barn or road you forgot was there.
> - Showing up in the season to ask, with a rifle in the truck window.

```more more mistakes
- Assuming a farm outside town is outside all bylaws. Check No Shooting areas first (Tip).
- Treating a farmer's yes as a forever yes. Call before every visit (Tip).
```
