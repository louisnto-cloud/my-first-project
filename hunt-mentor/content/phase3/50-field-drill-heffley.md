> Reading land is a skill you only learn by doing. This drill walks you through one full day, stop by stop, with a question at each stop. Do it on paper first, then on the ground.

## The drill day

```diagram rq-dr-timeline
```

- Legal deer hunting about 6:37 to 18:52 on 24 October (85% times, 99% rule).
- Glass early, read sign mid morning, sit late (Tip).

```more the times and the moon
- Heffley Creek, 24 October 2026: sunrise about 7:37, sunset about 17:52, Pacific Daylight Time (85%, my calculation with the NOAA solar method; check the NRC calculator).
- Big game: from 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (99%, synopsis page 11). So about 6:37 to 18:52.
- Moon: full moon falls about 26 October 2026 (70%, my calculation, about a day either way). It barely matters for deer movement: see [Moon and daylight](#/s/moon-and-daylight/0).
- Deer pattern: Penn State October collars moved most at dawn and dusk, 120 to 140 m an hour (75%). The bedded block midday is a tip.
```

## Stop 0: the night before

```diagram rq-map-stack
```

- Question: what is legal in MU 3-27 on 24 October?
- Question: where can you not shoot near here?

```more the answers
- Mule deer: any buck, 1 to 31 October, in 3-12 to 3-20, 3-26 to 3-44 and 3-46 (99%, synopsis page 34). One mule deer a year, Regions 3 to 8 combined (99%).
- White tailed deer: bucks 10 September to 10 December; antlerless 10 to 31 October (99%).
- Grouse (dusky, ruffed, spruce): 10 September to 30 November, 5 of each, 10 in total a day (99%). Black bear: 1 September to 30 November, with a bear species licence (99%).
- No shooting: City of Kamloops (no firearm discharge at all), Map C11 Sun Peaks (400 m from any lift, run, facility or development), Map C7 Kamloops band (99%). Paul Lake Park lists no hunting: treat as closed (95%).
- Recreation sites: no firearm "on or into the developed portion", which is the structures plus a 100 m buffer (99%, Forest Recreation Regulation s. 11, via research notes). No game offal, hides or bones in the developed portion (99%, s. 10).
- 100 m from any lived in house or occupied barn; 15 m from the centre of a 2 lane public road (99%, page 10).
- Score: 4 points if you got all of these before opening the answer.
```

## Stop 1 and 2: park and walk in

```diagram rq-dr-step-2
```

- Question: where do you park, and when do you load the rifle?

```more the answers
- Park in a wide pullout or a signed parking area, never on a blind corner or blocking a gate (Tip).
- Not inside the developed part of the recreation site if you will shoot nearby: walk well past its 100 m buffer before you load (99% rule; Tip for where you load).
- Load only at legal light and only once you are walking in to hunt (Tip). No loaded firearm in or on a vehicle (99%, page 11).
- Walk in on the downwind side of where you expect deer (Tip). With a north wind, that is the south side.
- A headlamp to walk in before light: point it at your feet and switch it off before you start looking. Using any light to hunt is unlawful (99%, page 11, item 38). Whether walking in with one is an issue: VERIFY with the Conservation Officer Service.
```

## Stop 3: glass the slope

```diagram rq-dr-glass
```

- Question: how many deer can you find? Which is the buck?
- Grid it in 3 strips. Take 3 minutes before you open the answer.

```more the answer
- Three deer (made up picture):
  - One at the timber edge, top centre, just leaving the open slope (antlers not visible).
  - One bedded under the tree on the right: a body shape and an ear, no legs showing.
  - One standing by a shrub, lower left, with antlers: the buck.
- Real glassing: look for parts, not whole deer: an ear, a leg, a flicking tail, the white rump, a horizontal line in vertical timber (Tip). See [Glassing](#/s/glassing/0).
- Order: the app shows near first and far first as two options; pick one and stick to it (Tip).
- Score: 1 point per deer found, 3 maximum.
```

## Stop 4: read the sign

```diagram rq-dr-sign
```

- Question: who made it, which way did it go, how old is it?
- Question: what does the rub add?

```more the answers
- A: a deer track line. The pointed tips lead, so the deer went up and to the right, toward the timber (85% for direction rule). Edges crisp in cold ground can last days, so do not over age it (65%).
- B: shiny pellets: fresh, likely hours, not days (Tip; see [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0)).
- C: a fresh rub: a buck has been here (85% that rubs are made by bucks). Mule deer rub less than white tails (60%).
- Story (Tip): a buck fed on the bench at night and walked up into the timber at dawn to bed. He may come back down at last light.
- Score: 4 points if you told the who, which way, how old and the story.
```

## Stop 5: pick the seat

```diagram rq-dr-wind
```

- North wind blows down from the ridge; the morning thermal rises up the slope.
- Question: route A from below, or route B from the side?

```more the answer
- Morning thermals rise as the slope warms; evening thermals sink (80%).
- Here the forecast wind (from the north, downhill) and the morning thermal (uphill) fight. Near the ground the thermal can win on a sunny slope (Tip).
- Route B, across the slope from the side, keeps your scent off the bench either way (Tip). Check with a puff bottle every 100 m.
- For the evening sit, the thermal sinks: sit below or across from the bench, with the bench above and upwind of you (Tip).
- Range: use the Hunt Map range rings from your seat. Know 100, 200 and 300 m marks before you sit (Tip).
- Score: 4 points for route B and a sound evening plan.
```

## Stop 6: walk out, write it down

```diagram rq-dr-score
```

- Leave the way you came, quietly. Write the Journal the same night (Tip).
- Add your total. 16 or more: you are reading land (Tip).

```more the debrief
- Journal: date, place, weather, wind by hour, every animal with the time, every sign with a pin, what you would do differently (Tip). See [Hunt journal guide](#/s/hunt-journal-guide/0).
- Update map layer 6 with the pins (Tip).
- Score 5 points for a full Journal entry the same night.
```

## Common mistakes

```diagram rq-dr-wind
```

> [!mistake]
> - Skipping the night before checks because "it is close to home".
> - Walking straight up the slope at the bench with the morning thermal rising.
> - Leaving the Journal until next week.

```more why each one hurts
- Close to home is where the city line and the Sun Peaks map sit (99% rules).
- Morning thermals carry scent uphill (80%).
- Memory fades fast. A same night entry is worth ten later ones (Tip).
```

```checklist rq-50-drill
Night before: season, class, closures, land status, legal times written
Parked legally, outside any developed recreation site area
Glassed for at least 45 minutes at first light, in a grid
Read 3 pieces of sign: who, which way, how old, the story
Seat chosen for the wind and thermal, range rings set
Walked out the same way, Journal written the same night
Score out of 20 noted in the Journal
```
