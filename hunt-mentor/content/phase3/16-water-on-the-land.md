> [!why]
> Every animal drinks. In a dry October, a little water in a big dry hill tells you where they will be.

```anim rl-wa-spring A spring: rain in, tight layer, water out the side.
```

- Water shapes trails, beds, browse and crossings (75%).
- The smaller and rarer the water, the more it is worth (Tip).

```more what this lesson covers
- The water types you will meet: rivers, creeks, dry creeks, springs, seeps, wallows, dugouts and ponds, beaver ponds, marsh and lake edges.
- How each looks on a topo map, on the Hunt Map and Satellite base, and on the ground.
- Why animals use each one, and when water matters most.
- Safety and the law near water.
- For the plants and birds of wet places see [Wetlands, marshes and riparian strips](#/s/wetland-country/0) and [Habitat tour: ducks](#/s/habitat-ducks/0). For drinking it yourself see [Safe drinking water](#/s/water-safe-drinking/0).
```

## Eight kinds of water

```diagram rl-wa-types
```

| Water | What to look for | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|
| Creek | Blue line, brush strip | Everything (75%) |
| Dry creek | Dashed line, pools in bends | Deer in dry spells (Tip) |
| Spring | Wet spot, green patch | Deer, elk, birds (Tip) |
| Seep | Damp ground, aspen, sedge | Deer, grouse, bears (Tip) |
| Wallow | Muddy pit, rank smell | Elk, moose in the rut (75%) |
| Dugout or pond | Round, on ranch land | Deer, ducks, geese (Tip) |
| Beaver pond | Dam, flooded willow | Moose, ducks, deer (75%) |
| Marsh, lake edge | Cattail, reeds | Ducks, moose (85%) |

```more definitions
- **Spring:** a place where groundwater comes out of the ground on its own and flows (90%).
- **Seep:** groundwater that oozes out slowly over a patch, keeping it damp but rarely making a flowing stream (85%).
- **Intermittent creek:** flows only part of the year, after snowmelt or rain (90%).
- **Dugout:** a pond dug for cattle on ranch land (85%). Most are on private land or grazing leases: permission and the rules apply. See [Rule Book: where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt/0).
- **Wallow:** a muddy pit dug and rolled in by bull elk in the rut; bull moose dig rut pits too (75%). See [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0).
```

## How water shows on the Hunt Map

```diagram rl-wa-hm-key
```

- Dashed blue: the creek may be dry (99%, app).
- Springs and seeps are often missing from maps (Tip).

```more reading water on maps
- On the Hunt Map Topo base: solid blue lines for creeks, dashed blue for intermittent ones, a pale green grey fill for wetlands, blue fill for lakes and ponds (99%, app map style).
- The map data comes from OpenStreetMap; small springs, seeps and dugouts may not be drawn (Tip). Check the Satellite base.
- Layers: Wetlands shows mapped wetlands; Duck staging and winter waters shows key duck water (99%, app layer list).
- Water always runs in the V of a draw, never on a ridge (95%). If a draw has no blue line, the bottom may still hold a seep (Tip).
- Paper map symbols vary by series: check the legend on your sheet (VERIFY).
```

## Green in a brown hill

```diagram rl-wa-green
```

- In a dry fall, the greenest spot is usually the wettest (Tip).
- Aspen, willow, red osier dogwood and cottonwood mean water near (75%).

```gallery
hab-dogwood-stems | Red osier dogwood: a wet draw.
hab-willow-shrub | Willow: wet ground and moose browse.
hab-aspen-grove-snow | Aspen clump: often a seep.
hab-cattail-head | Cattail: marsh from 500 m.
```

```more plants that point to water
- Willows, red osier dogwood, cottonwood and alder need wet roots and line creeks and seeps (75%). See [Habitat tour: deer](#/s/habitat-deer/0).
- On open grassland, a lone clump of aspen often marks a seep or high water table (65%, Tip).
- Sedges and rushes in a patch of grassland mean damp soil (70%).
- On the Hunt Map Satellite base, these show as dark or bright green spots in pale grass. Mark them and walk to check (Tip).
```

## Springs and seeps

```steps Find a spring
rl-lf-bench-3d | Look at the toe of a bench or slope break.
rl-wa-green | Look for green in a brown slope.
photo:hab-elk-tracks-shore | Look for tracks pounded into mud.
```

- Springs often appear where a slope eases: the foot of a bench or a draw head (Tip).
- Fresh tracks in mud tell you who drinks here (75%).

```more why springs are gold
- Rain soaks in, meets a tight layer of rock or clay, and runs along it until it comes out of the slope (85%).
- In the dry Interior, springs and seeps are small and scattered. One spring can serve a whole slope (Tip).
- Deer in desert studies gathered near permanent water as other sources dried (60%, desert data). Thompson grassland deer also get water from green feed, dew and snow (Tip).
- Read the mud: count species, aim the tracks, age them. See [Tracks](#/s/tracks/0) and [Aging tracks and sign](#/s/rs-track-aging/0).
- Never sit right on a small spring: sit downwind where you can see the trails coming in (Tip).
```

## Creeks: travel and crossings

```diagram rl-wa-crossing
```

- Trails follow creek bottoms and gather at easy crossings (65%).
- Evening air drains down creek bottoms (85%).

```more creek details
- Brushy creek bottoms are travel lanes with cover, food and water in one strip (70%).
- Animals cross where banks are low and the bottom is firm: one crossing may serve a whole slope (Tip). See [Pinch points](#/s/pinch-points/0).
- Cold air flows down creek bottoms at dusk and before dawn (85%). Your scent goes with it.
- A creek is also noise: running water hides your footsteps, but hides an animal's too (Tip).
- Crossing yourself: unload first, use a pole, unbuckle the pack belt, never cross fast deep water (Tip). See [Water safety](#/s/water-safety/0).
```

## Wallows

```photo hab-elk-wallow A wallow: a muddy pit rolled in by bull elk in the rut.
```

- Bull elk dig and roll in wallows in the rut (85%).
- A fresh wallow is a classic afternoon sit (Tip).

```more wallow details
- Fresh wallow signs: muddy water, fresh tracks, mud splashed on nearby trees, a strong musky smell (75%).
- Wallows sit in wet meadows, seeps and spring heads, often in timber near feed (70%).
- Bull moose dig shallow rut pits and urinate in them; cows are drawn to them (75%). See [Moose](#/s/moose/0).
- In October the elk rut is mostly over in the Interior; old wallows still mark where elk live (Tip). See [Elk](#/s/elk/0).
```

## Ponds and dugouts

```photo hab-kamloops-pond-farm Pond, hay fields and cottonwoods near Kamloops.
```

- Ranch ponds pull deer, ducks and geese (Tip).
- Most are on private land: ask first (99%, Rule Book).

```more ponds and the rules
- On private land you need permission to enter and hunt (99%, Trespass Act, as carried in the Rule Book).
- No hunting or shooting within 100 m of a dwelling or an occupied farm building (99%, Rule Book).
- Ducks: for puddle ducks on small ponds see [Habitat tour: ducks](#/s/habitat-ducks/0) and [Ducks](#/s/ducks/0).
- Dugout banks are often steep and slippery. Retrieving a duck from one needs a plan (Tip).
```

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

## Beaver ponds

```diagram rl-wa-beaver
```

- Water, browse and cover in one place (75%).
- The dam is often a crossing (Tip).

```more beaver pond details
- Beaver ponds flood timber and grow willow, a moose favourite (85%). See [Habitat tour: moose, elk and bears](#/s/habitat-big-game/0).
- Ducks use the quiet water and flooded edges (80%).
- Dams look solid but can collapse. Never stand on a dam with a pack and rifle in deep water (Tip).
- Old dried beaver meadows grow lush grass and sedge: good feed (70%).
```

## Marsh and lake edges

```photo hab-osoyoos-wetland Haynes Point wetland on Osoyoos Lake: reeds and calm water.
```

- Reeds and cattails: duck cover and feed (85%).
- Moose feed in shallow lakes and marsh edges (85%).

```more marsh edge details
- Moose live by water: willow swamps, lake edges, beaver ponds (85%).
- In the south Okanagan, marsh edges also hold quail cover in the thorny brush nearby (Tip). See [Habitat tour: quail and grouse](#/s/habitat-upland/0).
- Soft marsh bottoms can trap a wader. Test every step (Tip).
- Many valley wetlands are private, parks or bird sanctuaries. Check the land status layers and the Rule Book (99%).
```

## When water matters most

```diagram rl-wa-dry-wet
```

- Dry spell: water is a magnet. Sit it at dawn and dusk (Tip).
- After rain or snow: water is everywhere. Hunt food and cover (Tip).

```more timing water
- Deer drink at dawn and dusk (70%).
- Deer get much of their water from green feed; when feed dries, they need surface water (75%).
- The first big fall rain refills every puddle and ends the water stand season (Tip).
- In freezing weather, open water at springs and fast creeks becomes valuable again (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

```photo hab-elk-tracks-shore Read the mud before you read the map.
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): In a dry year, the smallest water on the mountain is the biggest thing on it.

> [!mistake]
> - Trusting the map to show every spring.
> - Sitting right on top of the water instead of the trail in.
> - Sitting a water hole after the rain filled every puddle.

```more the mistakes in full
- Maps miss most springs and seeps. Use the satellite, the plants and your boots (Tip).
- Sit downwind of the trails coming in, with a view of the water, not at the edge (Tip).
- Water stands lose value after a big rain (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

- **Green in brown, water's found.**
- **Dashed line, maybe dry.**
- **Dry year: sit the drink. Wet year: sit the food.**
- **Dam is a bridge** (but test it).
- **SWAMP check:** Springs, Wallows, Aspen clumps, Marsh edges, Ponds.

```more using the hooks
- SWAMP is a scan list for any new map: find one of each within walking distance of the truck (Tip).
```
