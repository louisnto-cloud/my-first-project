> [!why]
> Big animals leave big sign. Learn their food, their water and their marks, and you can tell from one walk whether a moose, an elk or a bear lives there.

- This is a photo walk: moose country, elk country, bear food, then the tracks and sign of each.
- Moose: willow swamps, lake edges, beaver ponds, burns and cut blocks (85%). The Bonaparte Plateau north of Kamloops is a provincial moose study area (90%).
- Elk: few near our bases; about 1,350 in the Thompson Okanagan area in 2000 (85%). Roosevelt elk were released near Chehalis, not far from Mission (85%).
- Black bears: everywhere near our bases, following the food from spring greens to fall berries and fish (85%).
- Carry bear spray on every walk in this country (Tip).

## Moose: willows, wetlands and beaver ponds

```gallery
hab-moose-willows | Cow moose browsing willows in winter. Twig ends bitten off at chest height.
hab-willow-shrub | Willow shrub in a wet mountain meadow. Long narrow leaves, many stems.
hab-dogwood-stems | Red osier dogwood. Red stems, a top moose browse in winter.
hab-beaver-lodge | Beaver pond and lodge. Beaver ponds grow the willow and water plants moose need.
```

- Winter moose browse: red osier dogwood, cottonwood, birch, aspen, willow and subalpine fir (85%).
- On plateaus, summer moose spread out near lake shores, swamps and beaver ponds; in winter they gather in river valleys, burns, logged areas and wetland complexes (85%).
- Moose move easily in snow up to 40 cm deep and often winter where the snow is 50 cm or more (85%).
- Willow: long narrow leaves, grows in wet ground and cut blocks (80%). Look for broken twig tops up to 2 m high (Tip).

## Moose tracks and trails

```gallery
hab-moose-tracks-mud | Moose tracks in mud. Much bigger than deer, with long pointed toes.
hab-moose-trail-snow | A moose trail ploughed through deep snow among small spruce.
```

- A moose track is about 13 to 15 cm long, more than twice a deer track (75%).
- Moose step over deep snow and logs; the trail is a trough with deep holes, not a line of prints (Tip).
- Moose pellets are big, oval, about 2.5 to 3 cm long, and piles are large (75%).
- Fresh tracks at a beaver pond edge at dawn: sit there the next evening (Tip).

## Elk: meadows, wallows and rubs

```gallery
hab-elk-wallow | Elk wallow: a muddy pit in a wet meadow, dug and rolled in by bulls in the rut.
hab-elk-rub | Subalpine fir rubbed raw by a bull elk. Bark stripped high up the trunk.
hab-elk-tracks-shore | Elk tracks churning a lake shore where a herd came to drink.
hab-elk-pellets | Elk pellets in a meadow. Bigger than deer pellets, with a boot for scale.
hab-elk-trail-snow | Elk trail in spring snow: many animals walking in single file.
```

- The elk rut is mid September; most cows are bred mid to late September (85%).
- Bull elk dig and roll in wallows and rub trees with their antlers in the rut (80%). Fresh mud and bright wood mean a bull is near (Tip).
- Elk tracks are about 9 to 11 cm long, rounder than a deer track (75%).
- Interior elk spend summer in subalpine basins and avalanche tracks and move down with autumn snow (85%).

## Bear food: berries and greens

```gallery
hab-huckleberry-berry | Black huckleberry. Bears fatten on these in August and September.
hab-soopolallie | Soopolallie (soapberry) with red berries, North Thompson River Park.
hab-skunk-cabbage | Skunk cabbage in a wet spring forest near Aldergrove. Early spring bear food.
hab-bear-berry-patch | Black bear standing in a fall berry patch. Bears follow the ripening fruit.
```

- Spring bear food: meadows, estuaries, stream sides, skunk cabbage, avalanche chutes, grassy south slopes and burns (85%).
- Fall bear food: berries and spawning fish (85%).
- Soopolallie berries ripen in July and August; huckleberries in August and September (70%).
- Huckleberry: shrub to about 1.5 m, thin oval leaves, dark purple berries, in mountain forest and old burns (80%). Source: E Flora BC.

## Bear sign

```gallery
hab-bear-track-front | Black bear front track in the Pasayten, next to the Okanagan. Wide pad, 5 toes.
hab-bear-scat-okanagan | Black bear scat on a trail in Mission Park, Kelowna. Full of seeds and skins.
hab-bear-scat-berries | Black bear scat full of berries, with a pen for scale.
hab-bear-claw-aspen | Claw marks on an aspen trunk where a bear climbed.
hab-bear-track-mud | Black bear track in mud. The hind foot looks a little like a human foot.
```

- Bear tracks show 5 toes and a wide pad; the hind track looks like a short, wide human foot (80%).
- Berry filled scat on a trail means a berry patch is close and in use (Tip).
- Black bears den in mid October to late November in the Interior and come out in April and May (75%).
- Fresh sign, a carcass smell or ravens circling: back out, talk loudly, and keep bear spray ready (Tip).

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Find the groceries and you find the big animals. Willow for moose, wet meadows for elk, berries for bears.

> [!mistake]
> - Calling every big track a moose. Check the size: elk tracks are smaller and rounder.
> - Walking into a berry patch with the wind at your back. The bear smells you; you do not see it.
> - Hunting dry timber for moose. They want water and willow.

> [!field]
> Walk the edge of one wetland or beaver pond. Find browsed willow or dogwood, one big track and one scat. Measure the track and photograph everything with a ruler. Decide: moose, elk, deer or bear?
