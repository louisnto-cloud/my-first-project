> Fruit ripens in a wave, valley first and mountain last, and bears, grouse and deer follow it. Know what is ripe this month and you know where to look this week.

- Mast: the fruit and nuts of trees and shrubs (70%).
- In the Interior, mast is berries, hips and conifer seed (70%).
- Fruit ripens later the higher and shadier you go (70%).

```more what this session covers
- In the Interior there are no acorns; Garry oak grows only in the coastal CDF (Coastal Douglas fir) zone on southeast Vancouver Island and the Gulf Islands (95% for Garry oak in the CDF; 70% for "no acorns" in the Interior).
```

## The fruit and seed calendar

```diagram rp-berry-months
```

- July: saskatoon and soopolallie (70%).
- August and September: huckleberry, blackberry, chokecherry (65 to 70%).
- October on: hips, snowberry, kinnikinnick hang on (65 to 85%).

```more month by month table
| Month | Ripe now (southern BC) | Certainty |
|---|---|---|
| June | salmonberry (coast), early strawberries | Tip |
| July | saskatoon in valleys (mid to late July), soopolallie | 70% |
| August | saskatoon up high (70%); huckleberry (70%); Himalayan blackberry, Fraser Valley (70%); thimbleberry (Tip) | 70% |
| September | huckleberry at high elevation (70%); chokecherry, Oregon grape, elderberry | Tip |
| October | rose hips, hawthorn, snowberry, kinnikinnick, orchard apples; conifer seed falls | Tip |
| November to March | what hangs on: snowberry (85%); rose hips, kinnikinnick, high bush cranberry (Tip) | 85% or Tip |

- Saskatoon ripens in valleys mid to late July and on ski hills in August; berries that need sun are delayed by cool weather (70%, Whistler bear researcher via news).
- A high Interior huckleberry patch may not peak until August or September (70%, BC Magazine).
- Nlaka'pamux knowledge: soapberries (soopolallie) are ready when the wild rose blooms (65%, traditional knowledge review). See [Indigenous respect](#/s/indigenous-respect/0).
- Snowberry fruits persist through the winter (85%, E Flora BC).
- Rows without a source are Tips from the general pattern: check the fruit yourself.
```

## Ripe fruit climbs the mountain

```anim rp-ripe-wave Ripe fruit moves uphill from July to September. Bears follow.
```

- Valley fruit first, mountain fruit last (70%).
- Bears move uphill with the ripe band (Tip).
- A cold or dry summer shifts and shrinks the crop (70%).

```more why the wave matters
- One Whistler season: valley berries ripened a month late; huckleberries failed in patches from about 600 to 1,100 m; above about 1,400 m ripening was 2 months late (70%, Whistler bear researcher via Pique News).
- Warm, exposed sites fruit earlier than shaded forests and high slopes (70%, BC Magazine).
- A poor berry year puts more pressure on bears to find other food, including near towns (70%, Whistler).
- Tip: on an early October hunt, ripe fruit near you is probably on cool north slopes or high ground, not the valley.
```

## Bears: eating for winter

```diagram rp-bear-calories
```

- Fall bears eat up to 20,000 calories a day (70%).
- Berries are low in calories, so bears eat a lot of them (65%).
- That is about 55 kg of huckleberries a day (my maths).

```more black bear food year
- With only about 6 months to build fat for hibernation, black bears seek foods that are abundant, high in energy and easy to get (85%, BC Ministry of Environment, Black Bear in BC).
- Spring: succulent plants in meadows, estuaries, riparian areas, skunk cabbage swamps, avalanche chutes, grassy south slopes and burns; newborn deer, elk and moose calves (85%).
- Summer: insects and larvae, fruits, berries, salmon, carrion (85%).
- Autumn: berries and spawning fish (85%).
- They seek concentrated food: spawning fish and dense berry patches (85%).
- Fall eating phase ([[Hyperphagia]]): up to 20,000 calories a day (70%, WildSafeBC figure via news). Calories per pound: huckleberry 166, blackberry 195, chokecherry 249 (65%, People and Carnivores via news). Per kilogram that is about 366, 430 and 549 (my maths). 20,000 divided by 366 is about 55 kg (my maths).
- Bear droppings spread the seeds of many berry shrubs (85%).
- Bear hunting: [Black bear](#/s/black-bear/0).
```

## Grouse: the fall menu

```diagram rp-grouse-menu
```

- Fall grouse: berries, clover, leaves, seeds (85%).
- Winter: buds (ruffed) or needles (spruce, dusky) (70 to 85%).
- Road edges with clover and berries at dawn (Tip).

```more grouse food details
- Ruffed grouse: early forest of aspen, poplar, birch and alder, with buds and catkins as winter food; in fall, berries, clover, leaves and seeds (85%, from [Grouse](#/s/grouse/0)).
- Spruce grouse: on the ground late spring to early autumn, then up in trees from late autumn through spring eating pine and spruce needles (80%).
- Dusky grouse: in autumn they walk uphill into dense conifers and eat needles all winter (70 to 75%, from [How animals move](#/s/migration/0)).
- Grouse eat rose hips, snowberry, kinnikinnick berries and clover along roads in October (65%, from Food, water and cover).
- Young birds feed on berries and clover along road edges and cutblocks in late September and October (hunter lore, 65%).
```

## Deer and fruit

```photo hab-saskatoon-berries Saskatoon: browse all year, berries in July.
```

- Deer eat fallen fruit and leaves in fall (Tip).
- But twigs, not berries, carry deer through winter (85%).

```more deer, fruit and seed
- Mule deer winter on shrubs and Douglas fir (85%). See [Browse plants](#/s/browse-plants/0).
- Cottonwood, aspen and alder leaves on the ground in October are a quick deer snack (Tip, from Food, water and cover).
- Old orchard apples draw deer and bears in the Fraser Valley and around Kamloops (60%). Orchards are cultivated land: ask first. See [Farmland and orchards](#/s/farm-country/0).
- Feeding or baiting wildlife has rules. Check [Legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods/0) before you put anything out. Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.
```

## Seeds and nuts

```gallery
hab-douglas-fir-tree | Douglas fir: cones drop seed in fall.
hab-subalpine-forest | Spruce and pine: seed for squirrels and birds.
```

- Conifer seed feeds nutcrackers, crossbills and squirrels (95%).
- Beaked hazelnut: the Interior's wild nut (85%).

```more seed and nut details
- Pine parkland birds that forage on large conifer seeds: Clark's nutcracker, pygmy nuthatch, and the yellow pine chipmunk (95%, zone book).
- Red crossbills depend on conifer seeds (95%).
- Beaked hazelnut: shrub 1 to 3 m; edible hard shelled nuts in bristly husks, in 2s or 3s at branch tips; common south of 57 degrees east of the Coast Mountains (85%, E Flora BC).
- Squirrel middens (piles of cone scales) mark a good cone tree (Tip). See [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0).
```

## Fruits to know

```gallery
hab-huckleberry-berry | Black huckleberry: purple, 7 to 10 mm.
hab-soopolallie | Soopolallie: bright red, bitter, soapy.
hab-chokecherry | Chokecherry: shiny red to black.
hab-woods-rose-hips | Rose hips: scarlet, 1 to 2 cm.
hab-snowberry | Snowberry: waxy white, lasts all winter.
hab-kinnikinnick | Kinnikinnick: bright red on a ground mat.
```

- Learn the 6 fruits above first (Tip).
- Never eat a berry you cannot name (Tip).

```more fruit ID details (E Flora BC, 85%)
- Black huckleberry: shrub to 1.5 m; finely toothed leaves 1.5 to 6 cm; purple or dark red berries 7 to 10 mm, sweet. Main zone ESSF (Engelmann spruce and subalpine fir).
- Soopolallie: shrub 1 to 2 m; leaves with brown and white scales underneath; bright red berries 6 to 8 mm, bitter and soapy when crushed. Main zone IDF.
- Chokecherry: 1 to 6 m; cherries 6 to 12 mm, shiny red, purple or black, one stone. Main zone IDF.
- Prickly rose: scarlet to purplish hips 1 to 2 cm.
- Snowberry: waxy white berries 5 to 15 mm; some persist through the winter. Main zone IDF.
- Kinnikinnick: bright red berries 5 to 10 mm on a ground mat. Main zone IDF.
- Saskatoon: berry like pomes 5 to 14 mm, red turning purple to nearly black with a white bloom.
- Salal (coast): purplish black, hairy berries 5 to 10 mm. Red huckleberry (coast): bright red, 6 to 10 mm.
- Some red berries are poisonous to people. Treat this as a game food list, not a foraging guide (Tip).
```

## Hunt a berry patch

```steps Hunt a berry patch in 4 steps
rp-patch-1 | Find where fruit is ripe now, at your elevation.
rp-patch-2 | Read the sign: seedy scat, broken branches, tracks.
rp-patch-3 | Glass it from across at dawn and dusk, wind right.
rp-patch-4 | Confirm black bear, not grizzly. If in doubt, no shot.
```

- A patch with fresh seedy scat is a patch in use (Tip).

```more the method and the safety
1. Ripe fruit moves uphill through the season. Taste test is fine on fruits you know (Tip).
2. Fresh bear scat is moist and full of whole berries and seeds (Tip). See [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0).
3. Bears feeding in a patch keep their heads down; glass for a black shape that moves (Tip). See [Glassing](#/s/glassing/0).
4. There is no open season for grizzly bears in BC (99%, synopsis notice page 19, from the [Region 1 Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-1/0)). Grizzly ID: [Black bear](#/s/black-bear/0). Bear safety: [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0).
- Grouse in a berry patch flush close. Shotgun ready, safe angles (Tip). See [Shotgun skills](#/s/shotgun-skills/0).
```

## Grandpa's rule

```anim rp-ripe-wave Follow the ripe band uphill.
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom, not fact): Taste the berries before you pick the slope. Where they are sweet today, the bears were yesterday.

## Common mistakes

```photo hab-bear-berry-patch
```

> [!mistake]
> - Hunting last month's berry patch. The fruit and the bears moved uphill.
> - Walking into a dense berry patch without making noise in grizzly country.
> - Calling every bear in a berry patch a black bear. Check for the hump.

```more more mistakes
- Assuming one good berry year means the next will be the same (70%).
- Eating berries you cannot name (Tip).
```
