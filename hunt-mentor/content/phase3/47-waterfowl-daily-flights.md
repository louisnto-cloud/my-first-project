## The roost and the feed

```diagram rq-wf-roost-feed
```

- Ducks commute between a safe roost and a feeding area (65 to 85%).
- Louisiana mallards: 3.1 to 15 km between day and night sites (85%).

```more the commute in full
- Dutch GPS (Global Positioning System) mallards made "regular commuting flights at dusk and dawn between a fixed day roost and one or several fixed nocturnal foraging sites", visiting 2 to 4 feeding areas a night (65%, search preview).
- Louisiana: 126 radio tagged female mallards, 2,455 paired day and night locations, 2004 to 2006. Moves between day and night sites ranged from 3.1 to 15.0 km by land type (85%, Link and others 2011, Waterbirds, abstract read directly).
- Distances were longer from some refuges as the season went on (85%, same).
- Flights got longer when fresh water was scarce and as temperatures fell toward 0 °C (65%, Dutch study, search preview).
- Roost: calm, safe water with little disturbance (Tip). Loafing: sunny, sheltered shore, sandbars, logs (Tip).
```

## How far they fly

```diagram rq-wf-dist
```

- Missouri mallards: about 3 km a day on average, most under 13 km (65%).
- So the field they feed in is usually within sight of a map's reach (Tip).

```more details
- Ducks Unlimited satellite tracking, Missouri and Mississippi corridors: "local movements of satellite-marked mallards averaged only about two miles, and most were less than eight miles" (65%, quoted with its hyphen).
- On your phone map, draw a 15 km circle around a roost lake. The feeding fields are almost certainly inside it (Tip, based on the 3 to 15 km range).
- Canada geese: roost on a big lake, feed on crop and pasture land nearby (70%, Illinois study).
```

## Geese: two meals a day

```photo hab-geese-stubble Geese feed in stubble and pasture, rest on water.
```

- Morning flight to fields, back to water, out again late afternoon (70%).
- Stormy days: flights all day (70%).

```more the goose study and BC places
- Crab Orchard refuge, Illinois, about 40,000 geese, two winters: geese "usually fly to feeding areas in the early morning and late afternoons but undertake increased flight activity throughout the day when it is stormy" (70%, Raveling, Crews and Klimstra, Wilson Bulletin, search preview).
- Fraser Valley: Bert Brink WMA (Wildlife Management Area) "open fields are partly flooded in the winter and attract foraging wigeon and Canada geese" (90%, BC WMA page). Its hunting status: VERIFY with the regional office.
- Thompson: Tranquille is goose and swan staging, and a No Shooting area (90%).
- Canada and cackling geese, Region 3: 1 October to 23 December, 10 a day (99%, synopsis page 34).
- Field hunting needs the landowner's yes. Cultivated land: consent always (99%, page 9).
```

## Dabblers and divers feed differently

| | Dabblers ([[Puddle duck]]) | [[Diver]]s |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | Mallard, wigeon, teal, pintail | Scaup, goldeneye, bufflehead |
| Feed | Tip up in shallows, fields | Dive in deeper water |
| Take off | Jump straight up | Run along the water |
| Where you find them | Marsh edges, flooded fields, ponds | Open lakes, rivers |

- Shallow water and fields: dabblers. Open deep water: divers (80%).

```photo divers-taking-off Divers patter across the water to take off.
```

```more details and sources
- Dabblers feed at the surface or tip up and often feed in fields; divers dive for food and patter across the water to take off (80%, app duck session, Ducks Unlimited ID material).
- Night feeding varies by species: in one southeastern US study buffleheads, lesser scaup and ring necked ducks fed more by day than at night (65%, Ducks Unlimited "Ducks after dark", search preview).
- Pintails in Louisiana and Texas feed at night in flooded rice fields and loaf by day (65%, same).
```

## Watch them land

```anim rq-wf-landing Into the wind, into the hole.
```

- Ducks swing around and land facing the wind (70%).
- Put the wind at your back so they come at you (70%).

```more landing and decoy setup
- Ducks land into the wind, turning to fly against it as they drop (70%, onX and Huntwise guides, search preview).
- Ducks Unlimited: choose a blind so the wind blows from your back, directly or quartering (70%).
- Put decoys where you do not want birds to land; leave an open hole. About 20 yards (18 m) out is a common landing zone advice (65%, Ducks Unlimited Source).
- Crosswind: birds land short; move the spread upwind (60%, Outdoor Life).
- More: [[Decoy spread]], [[Landing zone]] and [[J hook]] in the glossary.
```

## Wind at your back

```diagram rq-wf-wind
```

- Wind blows from the blind out over the water (70%).
- Birds come in facing you. Shots are in front, not over your head (Tip).

```more why it works and when it does not
- A bird landing into the wind slows down and drops; with the wind at your back, that drop happens in front of you (70%, landing into wind; Tip, the shot angle).
- Quartering wind (from behind one shoulder) also works: birds slide across in front (65%, Ducks Unlimited).
- Wind straight in your face: birds finish behind you or land short. Move the blind or the spread (Tip).
- Safety: never swing on a bird past your hunting partner. Agree on zones of fire first: see [[Zone of fire]].
```

## Weather changes the timetable

```diagram rq-wf-weather
```

- Storms keep geese flying (70%). Freeze up moves ducks (75%).
- Wind and calm: tips from waterfowlers, not studies.

```more weather in full
- Cold: a cumulative run of freezing days and snow cover best explained dabbling duck numbers in Missouri (75%, Schummer 2010). See [Weather fronts](#/s/fronts-and-movement/0).
- BC freeze up ends most Interior migration by late November (75%).
- Wind: birds look for calm, sheltered water and lee shores; a windy day can keep birds moving (Tip).
- Bluebird calm: short dawn flights, then quiet (Tip).
- Fog: birds can fly low and late (Tip).
- Your safety on cold water comes first: [Water safety](#/s/water-safety/0).
```

## Scout the spot

```steps Scout a duck spot in 5 steps
rq-wf-step-1 | Watch the evening flight from far away.
rq-wf-step-2 | Mark where they land.
rq-wf-step-3 | Check it is legal water.
rq-wf-step-4 | Set up with the wind at your back.
rq-wf-step-5 | Hunt the feed, rest the roost.
```

- Scout the evening, hunt the morning (Tip).

```more the 5 steps in full
1. Glass the last 30 minutes of light from a road or ridge far off. Do not walk to the water (Tip).
2. Pin exactly where they set down. Birds that fed there tonight often come back at dawn (Tip).
3. Land status in iMapBC or the Hunt Map; closures; 100 m from any lived in house or barn (99%, page 10). Lac du Bois potholes outside the city line, Roche Lake and Bonaparte parks allow hunting in season (99%).
4. Arrive in the dark. Be set and still 30 minutes before legal light (Tip). Wind at your back (70%).
5. Shooting a roost every day drives the birds off (Tip). Hunt it every few days at most.
```

## Legal hours on the water

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

| Place, 15 October 2026 | Sunrise | Legal start | Sunset | Legal end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heffley Creek | 7:22 | 6:52 | 18:11 | 18:41 |
| Mission | 7:28 | 6:58 | 18:21 | 18:51 |

- Ducks and geese: 30 minutes before sunrise to 30 minutes after sunset (99%).
- Times: my calculation, about 2 minutes either way (85%). Check the NRC calculator.

```more the rule and the boat
> [!law] Item 37, page 11
> "To hunt migratory game birds from 1/2 hour after sunset to 1/2 hour before sunrise."

- Times use Pacific Daylight Time; my calculation with the NOAA (US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration) solar method (85%). The synopsis names the [National Research Council sunrise calculator](https://nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/eng/services/sunrise/index.html) (99%).
- Deer and grouse get 30 more minutes each end: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (99%).
- No hunting ducks or geese "from or by using a moving boat that is equipped with a motor or sail" (99%, page 11, item 24). Beached or anchored is fine (95%).
- [[Non toxic shot]] only, and a [[Magazine plug]] limiting the gun to 3 shells (99%, pages 12 and 13).
- Region 3 ducks: 8 September to 23 December, 8 a day (99%, page 34). Full rules: [Migratory birds in BC](#/s/rb-migratory-birds/0).
```

## Common mistakes

```diagram rq-wf-wind
```

> [!mistake]
> - Setting up with the wind in your face.
> - Shooting the roost every morning until it is empty.
> - Guessing legal light instead of writing it down.

```more why each one hurts
- Wind in your face: birds land short and behind you, facing away (70% for landing into wind).
- A shot out roost pushes birds to other water (Tip).
- Thirty minutes before sunrise is the line (99%). Write it on your hand.
```

## Do this in the field

```diagram rq-wf-step-1
```

> [!field]
> One evening this week: sit a ridge above Lac du Bois potholes or a ranch pond you have permission for. From far away, log each flock: time, size, direction, where it landed.

```checklist rq-47-ducks
Evening flight watched from far away, landing spot pinned
Land status, closures and the 100 m rule checked for that spot
Legal start and end times written down for the hunt day
Wind forecast: blind placed with the wind at your back
Non toxic shot, plug in, boat beached or anchored before shooting
Roost rested at least a few days between hunts
```
