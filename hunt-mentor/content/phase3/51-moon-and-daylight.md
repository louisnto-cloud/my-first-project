> [!why]
> The moon gets blamed for empty days. Daylight is what really runs the deer year, and the clock of legal light runs your hunt. Spend your planning on the right one.

## What the collars say about the moon

```anim rq-mn-cycle Hunt the calendar, not the moon.
```

- Mississippi bucks: moon effect about 4 m an hour, inside GPS error (75%).
- Penn State does: about 6 m an hour more at new moon than full moon (75%).

```more the moon studies in full
- Mississippi State University Deer Lab, 48 GPS (Global Positioning System) collared bucks, September to February: moon effects of about 4 m (4 yd) an hour, within the collar error. "The moon has no effect on buck movement." (75%, read through a MeatEater article)
- Same data: buck travel nearly doubled in the rut (75%). Rut beats moon.
- Penn State Deer Forest blog, 9 August 2017: adult does on three state forests, October 2015 and 2016. Deer were about 6 m an hour more active at new moon than full moon; the author calls it neither statistically nor biologically meaningful (75%, read directly).
- A 2016 paper in the Journal of the Southeastern Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies: peer reviewed work has "failed to document any association between lunar phase and white-tailed deer activity" (65%, search preview, quoted with its hyphen).
- A review found only four peer reviewed papers on big game and moon phase, all on white tailed deer (60%, search preview).
- One North Carolina State study reported a dawn movement peak around the new moon (55%, search preview). Treat it as a Tip until you can read it.
- No mule deer or elk moon study was found for this session (VERIFY if you find one).
```

## Moon versus time of day

```diagram rq-mn-moon-bars
```

- Dawn and dusk: the real peaks (75%).
- Midday under 50 m an hour; night about 60 m an hour (75%).

```more the Penn State hourly numbers
- October does: about 60 m an hour at night, under 50 m an hour in daylight, rising to 120 to 140 m an hour at dawn and dusk; single hours ranged from 0 to 2,748 m (75%, read directly).
- An Oregon study of mule deer and elk found activity peaks at about 4 to 9 am and 5 to 9 pm (85%, Stewart 2002, via deer research notes).
- So: be on a glassing point at first light whatever the moon (Tip).
```

## Daylight runs the deer calendar

```diagram rq-mn-photoperiod
```

- Longer spring days: melatonin falls, antlers grow (80%).
- Shorter fall days set the breeding season (70 to 80%).

```more photoperiod in full
- University of Georgia extension: "In whitetail deer, the increase in photoperiod length causes a decline in melatonin levels." "As melatonin decreases, antler growth is initiated." Then testosterone rises and "The velvet is sloughed off". "Shortly after whitetail breeding season, testosterone levels begin to decline in bucks." (80%, read directly)
- Mississippi State Deer Lab: white tailed deer are "short-day breeders"; the rut builds as days shorten (65%, via a hunting site summary, quoted with its hyphen).
- Weather changes how hard the rut shows, not when it starts (60%, extension style summary). Tip: the rut window in an area is about the same each year.
- Mule deer rut in BC: November and early December (85%, BC deer account); peak mid November (80%).
- See [The rut](#/s/the-rut/0).
```

## Moons and the rut in 2026

```diagram rq-mn-moons-rut
```

- Full moons about 26 October, 24 November, 24 December (70%).
- The rut window does not follow them (Tip, on the photoperiod evidence).

```more how the dates were made
- New and full moon dates: my calculation from the mean lunar cycle (29.53 days from the new moon of 6 January 2000), about a day either way (70%). The Hunt Map's Insights panel shows today's phase with the same method.
- Mule deer rut mid November to mid December (80%).
- So in 2026 the rut overlaps a full moon around 24 November. Hunt it anyway: the rut nearly doubled buck travel in the Mississippi data (75%).
```

## Daylight shrinks fast in October

```diagram rq-mn-daylength
```

- 11.7 hours on 1 October; 9.8 hours on 31 October (85%).
- About 3.8 minutes less light every day in mid October (85%).

```more the numbers and what they mean
- Heffley Creek (50.86 N, 120.27 W): 1 October 11.7 h, 15 October 10.8 h, 31 October 9.8 h, 30 November 8.3 h, 21 December 7.9 h (85%, my calculation with the NOAA solar method).
- Less light means less time to glass, stalk, gut and pack out. Plan the pack out before the shot (Tip). See [Packing out](#/s/packing-out/0).
- Cold mornings come with the short days. Dress for the 2 hours you sit still at dawn (Tip). See [What to wear](#/s/what-to-wear/0).
```

## Legal light is the only light law

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

```diagram rq-mn-legal
```

- Game: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (99%).
- Migratory birds: 30 minutes before sunrise to 30 minutes after sunset (99%).

```more the rule, the clock change and the calculator
> [!law] Items 37 and 38, page 11
> "To hunt migratory game birds from 1/2 hour after sunset to 1/2 hour before sunrise."
>
> "To hunt game, except migratory game birds from one hour after sunset to one hour before sunrise."

- The moon changes nothing: no hunting outside these hours, and no light of any kind to hunt by (99%, page 11, items 37 and 38).
- Sunrise and sunset: the synopsis names the [National Research Council calculator](https://nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/eng/services/sunrise/index.html) or a local newspaper (99%).
- Assumption: clocks fall back 1 hour on 1 November 2026. VERIFY. Legal hours follow the sun, so a clock change moves the numbers on your watch, not the rule.
- Heffley Creek windows (my calculation, 85%): 15 October 6:22 to 19:11; 31 October 6:49 to 18:39; 1 November 5:51 to 17:38 if clocks fall back; 30 November 6:39 to 17:00. Ducks: 30 minutes less at each end.
```

## Plan around light, not the moon

```steps Plan the day in 4 steps
rq-mn-step-1 | Write the legal times down.
rq-mn-step-2 | Be in place before legal light.
rq-mn-step-3 | Glass the first two hours hard.
rq-mn-step-4 | Sit the last hour, identify every shot.
```

- Legal light is not always enough light to identify (Tip).

```more why each step
1. Legal times change daily; write them on your hand or the Journal (99% rule).
2. Moving in the dark spooks less than moving at first light (Tip).
3. Dawn is the movement peak (75%).
4. Before every shot: positive ID, target, what is beyond. If you cannot see it clearly, do not shoot (Tip; see [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety/0)). Legal light ending does not change that.
```

## Ducks, birds and the moon

```diagram rq-wf-day
```

- Many ducks feed at night anyway; species differ (65%).
- No study found that isolates the full moon for ducks (VERIFY).

```more the bird evidence
- Ducks Unlimited: research shows "a variety of nocturnal feeding behaviors in ducks"; some species feed mostly after dark, others do not (65%, search preview).
- Hunter belief: ducks feed all night under a full moon and fly late in the morning. No controlled study found for this session (Tip, VERIFY).
- Upland birds are active in daylight: California quail have "yearlong, diurnal activity" (75%).
- See [Waterfowl daily flights](#/s/waterfowl-daily-flights/0).
```

## Common mistakes

```diagram rq-mn-moon-bars
```

> [!mistake]
> - Skipping a free weekend for a full moon.
> - Guessing legal light from how bright the morning looks.
> - Planning a long stalk at 4 pm in late November.

```more why each one hurts
- The measured moon effect is about 4 to 6 m an hour (75%). A free weekend is worth more (Tip).
- A bright moon does not move legal light (99%).
- On 30 November the day is about 8.3 hours long and legal light ends at about 17:00 (85%). Leave time to recover the animal (Tip).
```
