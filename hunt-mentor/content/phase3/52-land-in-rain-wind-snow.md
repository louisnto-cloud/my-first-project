> [!why]
> Your October days will not all be dry and calm. Rain dates tracks, wind shows you shelter, snow shows you everything. Bad weather is a reading lesson if you dress for it.

- Rain dates tracks. Wind shows shelter. Snow shows everything (Tip, on the tracking rules).
- Dress for it first: cold and wet is the real danger (75%).


## Rain: it dates every track

```diagram rq-rw-raintrack
```

- Rain pits inside a track: made before the rain (70%).
- Clean track on spattered ground: made after it (70%).

```more rain and tracks in full
- "Rain pits inside the print mean it was made before the rain. A clean print on rain spattered ground is after the rain." (70%, Tracks session)
- So the time the rain stopped is your clock. Check the overnight record or ask your brother what time it quit (Tip).
- Mud after rain: fresh prints have shiny wet walls (Tip). Soft ground also shows prints that dry ground hides (Tip).
- Blood trails wash out in heavy rain: if you hit an animal and rain is coming, follow up sooner rather than later, within the rules in [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing/0) (Tip).
```

## Rain: how game and you behave

```photo hab-deer-trail-reeds Wet trails show up darker and quieter.
```

- Deer moved less during storms (80%). Bucks moved less in rain than does (65%).
- Rain softens your steps and leaves (Tip).

```more details
- Penn State: collared does moved less during storms (80%); preliminary notes: bucks moved less in rain than does (65%).
- Light rain: wet leaves and grass are quieter underfoot. A good still hunting day (Tip). See [Moving quietly](#/s/still-hunting/0).
- Scent: hunters say rain knocks scent down and humid air carries it close to the ground. No study found for this session (Tip).
- Optics fog and rain spots blur glass: keep lens caps on and a soft cloth in a dry pocket (Tip).
- After a long rain, glass the first dry, calm hours (Tip).
```

## Wind: find the sheltered side

```diagram rq-rw-lee
```

- Strong wind: glass sheltered lee slopes and timber (Tip).
- A swirl forms just past a ridge top (Tip).

```more wind and shelter
- Lee slopes and thick timber break the wind. Hunters find deer bedded there in gales (Tip).
- Swirls and eddies near ridge tops and in draws carry your scent in odd directions: see [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals/0).
- Thermals still run early and late on light wind days: up in the morning, down in the evening (80%).
- Noise: strong wind hides your steps but also hides an animal's steps from you. Look more, listen less (Tip).
- Safety: dead trees and big limbs fall in strong wind. Do not sit under a dead snag in a windstorm (Tip).
```

## Wind: what the studies say

```diagram rq-rw-wind-evidence
```

- Three studies, three answers: mixed evidence (55 to 65%).
- Do not stay home for wind. Change where you look (Tip).

```more the three studies
- Oklahoma, 7 years of GPS (Global Positioning System) collars (Noble Foundation): very little change in distance travelled with wind speed; temperature mattered more (60%, magazine summary, search preview).
- South Carolina GPS thesis (Goethlich, Auburn): wind speed and other weather affected activity in some seasons and hours, less than the daily rhythm (65%, search preview).
- South Texas radio collars, 25 bucks, 1980s: movement dropped at 15 to 19 mph (about 24 to 31 km/h) and came back up above 20 mph (32 km/h) (55%, magazine summary). Treat it as a Tip.
- Shooting in wind: bullets drift. Know your drift at 200 m before the season: see [.308 ballistics](#/s/ballistics-308/0).
```

## Snow: the slate is clean

```anim rq-rw-snowfall Fresh snow: everything on top is new.
```

- A track on top of new snow was made since the snow stopped (70%).
- Old tracks under it are older than the snowfall (Tip).

```more reading fresh snow
- Fresh snow tells you who moved since it stopped, how many, which way and where they bedded (Tip, built on the tracking rules).
- Cold, still nights keep tracks crisp for days, so crisp does not always mean fresh (65%).
- Snow falling while you track fills prints from the edges: watch them soften (Tip). See [Snow tracking](#/s/snow-tracking/0).
```

## Snow: what to look for

```diagram rq-rw-snow
```

- Trails, beds, urine, grouse snow roosts (65 to 80%).
- Snow moves mule deer down: trouble near 30 cm, gone near 50 cm (75%).

```more snow sign and snow depth
- Beds melt an oval to the ground or to a crust (Tip). Count beds to count deer (Tip).
- Ruffed grouse dive into soft snow to roost and burst out leaving wing marks (80%).
- Mule deer: snow and cold trigger the move to winter range (85%); trouble near 30 cm, leave near 50 cm (75%).
- Moose: little trouble to 40 cm; often winter in 50 cm or more (85%).
- Chukar move lower and onto south slopes in heavy snow (75%).
- Avalanche terrain is real on steep open slopes with snow. See the avalanche screen in [Snow tracking](#/s/snow-tracking/0).
```

## Read any weather morning

```steps Read a weather morning in 4 steps
rq-rw-step-1 | Note when the weather changed.
rq-rw-step-2 | Find sign newer than the change.
rq-rw-step-3 | In wind, glass the sheltered side.
rq-rw-step-4 | On snow, glass for dark shapes.
```

- Fresh sign since the change is your best clue (Tip).

```more the 4 steps in full
1. When did rain stop, snow end, or wind swing? That is your clock (Tip).
2. Sign made after the change is fresh: clean tracks on spattered mud, tracks on top of new snow (70%).
3. In wind, lee slopes and timber edges (Tip).
4. On snow, deer bodies and dark legs stand out; so do you. Wear white or break your outline in the open, with blaze orange where others hunt (Tip).
```

## Fog and low cloud

```diagram rq-rw-reveals
```

- Fog: close range only. Never shoot at a shape (Tip).
- Wait for it to lift, then glass hard (Tip).

```more fog details (Tip)
- Valley fog often burns off by mid morning on clear fall days in the Interior (Tip). Use the time to walk to a higher glassing point.
- Fog plays tricks on distance and colour. Positive ID of a legal animal and a safe backstop come first (Tip). See [Legal to shoot](#/s/legal-to-shoot/0).
```

## Cold, wet, windy: when to stop

```diagram rq-rw-cold
```

- You do not need frost to get hypothermia; wind and rain raise the risk (75%).
- Fumbles, mumbles, stumbles, grumbles: turn back (75%).

```more hypothermia and the stop rules
- Fraser Health: it does not need to be below freezing to get hypothermia; wind chill and rain raise the odds (75%, health authority handout, search preview).
- Mild signs: shivering, cold, and the "umbles": fumbles, mumbles, stumbles, grumbles (75%, Bush safety session).
- Shivering that stops with confusion and stiffness is severe hypothermia, an emergency (Bush safety session).
- Keep a dry warm layer in a dry bag in your pack, eat and drink every hour, and turn back early (Tip). Full detail: [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0).
- On the water, cold water is a bigger danger again: [Water safety](#/s/water-safety/0).
- Snow and ice on Forest Service roads: check road reports and carry chains or traction aids (Tip). Highways: [DriveBC](https://www.drivebc.ca/).
```

> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Bad weather writes the freshest news. Dress warm enough to sit and read it.

## Common mistakes

```diagram rq-rw-lee
```

> [!mistake]
> - Calling every crisp snow track fresh on a cold, still morning.
> - Glassing the windy open face in a gale.
> - Pushing on while cold and fumbling.

```more why each one hurts
- Cold, still air keeps tracks crisp for days (65%).
- In a gale, deer use sheltered ground (Tip).
- Fumbling hands mean mild hypothermia has started (75%). Turn back together.
```

> The first rainy or snowy morning this fall: write down when it stopped, then find 3 tracks made after and 3 made before. Photo each with your knife for scale.
