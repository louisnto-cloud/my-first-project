> [!why]
> Grassland is where you can see deer a kilometre away, and they can see you. Learn its belts, draws and edges and you hunt it instead of just looking at it.

```photo hab-lac-du-bois-grassland
```

- BC grassland is tiny but packed with game (70%).
- Deer use the draws and edges, not the open middle (Tip).
- This is the country right outside Kamloops and Heffley Creek.

```more what this session covers
- Grasslands cover less than 1% of BC but hold about 30% of the province's species at risk (70%, conservation groups, search preview).
- Most Interior grassland sits in the BG (Bunchgrass) zone and in the grassland phases of the IDF (Interior Douglas fir) zone (95%). See [BC habitat zones](#/s/bec-zones/0).
- This session: the three belts, healthy versus worn out range, where deer are in October, stalking open country, hazards, and the access rules on leased range.
- Grassland plants for deer: [Browse plants](#/s/browse-plants/0). Water and fall food: [Food, water and cover](#/s/food-water-cover/0).
```

## Three grassland belts

```diagram rp-grass-belts
```

- Lower grassland: sage on the valley floor (95%).
- Middle grassland: no sage, thicker grass (95%).
- Upper grassland: fescue, inside the fir zone (95%).

```more the belts in the zone book
- BGxh (Bunchgrass, very dry hot), the lower grassland: valley bottoms to about 700 m. Widely spaced bluebunch wheatgrass, big sagebrush, lichen crust on the soil (95%).
- BGxw (Bunchgrass, very dry warm), the middle grassland: about 700 to 1,000 m. Generally no big sagebrush; bluebunch wheatgrass grows closer together (95%).
- Upper grasslands above about 900 to 1,000 m are mapped as grassland phases of the IDF (95%). Rough fescue dominates in the central part of the IDF, Idaho fescue in the south (95%).
- Undisturbed climax grassland is about 60% bunchgrass, 10 to 15% shrubs and 25 to 35% soil crust (mosses, lichens) (95%).
- Moisture comes mostly in winter. Plants dry out as summer goes on (95%). By October the grass is cured brown (Tip).
- Source: Meidinger and Pojar 1991, Bunchgrass zone and Interior Douglas fir zone chapters.
```

## Bunchgrass: what you are looking at

```photo hab-sagebrush-bush Big sagebrush: silver grey, strong smell when crushed.
```

- Bunchgrass grows in clumps with bare soil between (95%).
- Sagebrush marks the hottest, driest ground (95%).
- Deer eat little sage but use sage country in winter (70%).

```more grassland plants to know
| Plant | How to know it | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Bluebunch wheatgrass | tall clumps, the main grass of the zone (95%) | top forage for elk, sheep and cattle (95%) |
| Rough fescue | dense clumps on north slopes and higher (95%) | upper grassland feed (90%) |
| Big sagebrush | silver grey shrub, 3 blunt teeth at the leaf tip (90%) | marks the lowest, driest belt (95%); a mule deer winter food (85%) |
| Rabbitbrush | grey woolly shrub, yellow flowers in fall (85%) | low value to cattle (95%); winter browse for mule deer (85%) |
| Antelope brush (bitterbrush) | wedge leaves with 3 teeth (90%) | key mule deer browse, south Okanagan (90%) |
| Arrowleaf balsamroot | big arrow leaves, yellow spring flowers (90%) | spring deer food (85%) |

- Sources: Bunchgrass zone chapter (95%); BC Ministry of Environment, Mule and Black tailed Deer in BC, winter food list (85%); E Flora BC (90%).
```

## Healthy range or worn out?

```diagram rp-grass-condition
```

- Tall bunchgrass: good range, good feed (95%).
- Sage, cheatgrass, cactus, knapweed: overgrazed (95%).
- Elk and sheep follow good grass (Tip).

```more range condition in the zone book
- Overgrazing raises unpalatable or weedy plants at the expense of bunchgrass, especially bluebunch wheatgrass (95%).
- Sites in fair to poor range condition often have more big sagebrush, low pussytoes, needle and thread grass, brittle prickly pear cactus and cheatgrass (95%).
- Extreme disturbance brings knapweeds and cheatgrass (95%).
- Bighorn sheep and elk use grassland in good condition; overgrazing and weeds have hurt them (90%).
- Tip: cheatgrass seeds work into dog paws, ears and noses. Check your quail dog after every hunt.
```

| Animal | When on the grass | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Mule deer | early spring green up; shrub steppe in winter | (95%) |
| White tailed deer | edges, fields, shrub steppe | (90%) |
| Elk | grassland and farm fields | (95%) |
| California bighorn sheep | grass near cliffs | (95%) |
| Sharp tailed grouse | bunchgrass grassland | (90%) |
| Coyote, badger | all year | (95%) |
| California quail | shrub steppe, farm edges | (90%) |

- Grass plus cliffs: sheep. Grass plus brush: deer and quail (Tip).

```more more on grassland wildlife
- The Bunchgrass zone is one of the smallest in BC but supports a tremendous diversity and density of wildlife, because grassland, shrub steppe, riparian strips and forest sit side by side (95%).
- Mule and white tailed deer graze IDF grasslands in early spring (95%).
- Shrub steppe adds mule and white tailed deer to the grassland species (95%).
- California quail, ring necked pheasant and chukar were introduced; gray partridge came in from Washington (95%).
- Sage grouse once lived in the Bunchgrass zone and are gone from BC (95%).
- Bighorn sheep use low cliffs next to grassy forage as lambing grounds (95%).
- Source: Meidinger and Pojar 1991, Tables 18, 20 and 22.
```

## Where grassland deer are in October

```diagram rp-grass-oct
```

- Draws, timber edges and water. Not the open middle (Tip).
- Dawn and dusk on the grass, midday in cover (80%).

```more why the draws and edges
- Draws, moist depressions and north slopes keep green feed longest. Ponderosa pine and Douglas fir grow in moist draws (95%).
- Brushy draws hold saskatoon, chokecherry and rose: browse plus cover (Tip). See [Browse plants](#/s/browse-plants/0).
- Water: when feed dries, deer need surface water. Studies elsewhere found deer prefer water within about 1 km (65%). See [Food, water and cover](#/s/food-water-cover/0).
- Grassland lakes and potholes are first to thaw in spring and are duck and goose staging water (95%).
- Tip: on a calm cold morning, check the sunny side of a draw. On a warm afternoon, check the shady side.
```

## Stalk the open: use the draw

```anim rp-grass-draw Stay low in the draw, wind in your face, and close the gap unseen.
```

- In the open, your route is the hunt (Tip).
- Stay below the skyline in a draw or fold (Tip).
- Wind from the deer to you, always (75%).

```more stalking open country
- Deer rely heavily on smell (70%). See [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals/0).
- On grassland slopes, morning air rises uphill and evening air sinks (75%). Approach from above in the evening, from below in the morning, when the wind allows (Tip).
- A skylined hunter is seen from far away (Tip). Cross ridges where a tree, rock or saddle breaks your outline.
- Move when the deer has its head down feeding; freeze when it lifts its head (Tip).
- Full method: [Moving quietly](#/s/still-hunting/0) and [Glassing](#/s/glassing/0).
```

## Hunt a grassland basin

```steps Hunt a grassland basin in 5 steps
rp-gh-1 | Sit high at first light and glass the basin.
rp-gh-2 | Find deer in the draws and on the edges.
rp-gh-3 | Plan a route below the skyline, wind in your face.
rp-gh-4 | Move only when the deer feeds. Use folds.
rp-gh-5 | Shoot only with hillside, not sky, behind it.
```

- Most of the work is the glassing (Tip).

```more the 5 steps in detail
1. Glass from a high seat at first light, binoculars braced (Tip). Scan order (near first or far first) is your choice; both are covered in [Glassing](#/s/glassing/0).
2. Look in the draws, at timber edges, under lone trees and on north slopes (Tip).
3. Plan with the wind and thermals. Pick 2 landmarks to find the deer again from below (Tip).
4. Close in using draws, folds, rocks and cattle trails. Head down: move. Head up: freeze (Tip).
5. A bullet that misses on open grass can travel a long way. Shoot only with hillside behind the animal (Tip). See [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety/0).
```

## Range country: cattle, leases and gates

```photo hab-lac-du-bois-potholes Range land: rolling grass, potholes and fir patches.
```

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Leased range with cattle on it: no hunting without consent (99%).
- Leased land is under the Trespass Act even with no cattle (99%).
- Leave every gate as you found it (Tip).

```more the rule words and courtesy
> [!law] BC Hunting and Trapping Regulations Synopsis, page 9, 2 October 2026 edition
> "A person is not permitted to hunt on cultivated land or on Crown land which is subject to a grazing lease while the land is occupied by livestock, without the consent of the owner, lessee, or occupant of the land."

- Plain English: cultivated land always needs consent. A [[Grazing lease]] needs consent while livestock are on it (99%).
- Note on page 9: leased land is subject to the Trespass Act whether or not livestock are present (99%).
- Full rules: [Where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt/0).
- Lac du Bois Grasslands near Kamloops is a no shooting area inside the city boundary (95%, from [Habitat tour: deer](#/s/habitat-deer/0)). Use it as a classroom.
- Courtesy (Tip): leave gates as found, never drive on wet range, never shoot near cattle, and tell the rancher what you saw. Ranchers remember hunters.
```

## Grassland hazards in fall

```diagram rp-grass-hazards
```

- Nearly all rattlesnakes are in their dens by mid October (85%).
- Badger holes, grass fire, cattle, long shots (Tip).

```more hazard details
- Western rattlesnakes live in the dry valleys of the Fraser Canyon, Similkameen, Thompson, Okanagan and Kettle (80%).
- They spend about 180 days a year in communal dens. They come out in March and April. Virtually all are back in the dens by mid October (85%, BC Identified Wildlife account).
- Dens are often near the base of warm, sunny slopes in rock. Snakes seldom move more than about 1.5 km from a den (85%). On a warm October day, watch your hands and feet near rock piles (Tip).
- Badger holes and ground squirrel burrows twist ankles and catch ATV wheels (Tip).
- Hot exhaust on dry grass can start a fire. Park on bare ground or gravel (Tip). See [ATV rules](#/s/atv-rules/0).
- Give cattle and bulls room. Never get between a cow and calf (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule

```photo hab-grass-fir-edge
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom, not fact): On the grass, the deer is never in the open you are looking at. It is in the fold you are not looking at.

## Common mistakes

```diagram rp-grass-oct
```

> [!mistake]
> - Walking the open ridge top in plain sight. Every deer in the basin sees you.
> - Glassing the open grass at noon. Glass the draws and the timber edge.
> - Taking a shot with sky behind the deer.

```more more mistakes
- Driving or parking on dry grass with a hot exhaust (Tip).
- Hunting leased range with cattle on it without asking (99%, rule above).
- Ignoring the wind because "it is open". Open country moves scent a long way (Tip).
```

## Do this in the field

```steps Your grassland drill
rp-gh-1 | Glass one basin for 30 minutes at first light.
rp-gh-2 | Mark every deer you see and where it stood.
rp-gh-3 | Plan one stalk route on paper. No shooting.
```

> [!field]
> Pick one grassland basin. Glass it at first light, mark every deer, then plan a stalk route with the wind. Walk it at midday to learn the folds.

```checklist rp-grass-field
Glass one basin at first light for 30 minutes
Mark every deer: draw, edge, open or water
Find the nearest water to where they fed
Note the wind and thermal direction
Walk the stalk route at midday to learn the folds
Ask the rancher before entering any lease with cattle
```
