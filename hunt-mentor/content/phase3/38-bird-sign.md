## Bird tracks

```diagram rs-bs-tracks
```

- Grouse: 3 toes forward, 1 short back, about 5 cm (85%).
- Duck 5 to 7.6 cm, goose 7.6 to 10 cm, webbed (85%).

```more tracks in full
- Ruffed grouse track about 5 cm (2 inches) (85%, Maine Inland Fisheries and Wildlife pocket card).
- Grouse: 3 toes forward, 1 short toe back, about 5 to 7 cm long; the bird walks in a line, often zig zag (65%, from [Tracks](#/s/tracks/0)).
- In winter grouse grow comb like fringes on their toes, so the snow track looks wider (65%).
- Waterfowl: "Duck: 2 to 3 inches long; Goose: 3 to 4 inches long; Swan: 6 to 7 inches long" (85%, North Dakota Game and Fish). That is about 5 to 7.6 cm, 7.6 to 10 cm, and 15 to 18 cm.
- Quail prints are smaller than grouse prints; when running, prints space further apart (65%, Bear Tracker via search preview).
- Ground birds walk, one print ahead of the next. Many songbirds hop, leaving prints side by side in pairs (65%).
```

## Dust bowls

```diagram rs-bs-dust
```

- Grouse: one round scrape about 20 cm, a feather or two (65%).
- Quail: many small 7 to 15 cm bowls together (65%).

```more dust bowls in full
- A grouse dusting bowl is a round to oval scrape in dry fine soil, about 20 cm across, often on a trail or road edge, with a few feathers in it (65%, from [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0)).
- Quail: "circular indentations left behind in the soft dirt, some 7 to 15 cm (3 to 6 in) in diameter"; birds pick sunny spots with newly turned or soft soil (65%, Vancouver Island University biology blog and Wikipedia via search preview).
- Upland birds dust "to help minimize body parasites"; dust bowls are found where soil is exposed (85%, North Dakota Game and Fish).
- A cluster of small fresh bowls near rose or chokecherry thickets in the South Okanagan: a covey uses this spot (Tip). See [Habitat tour: quail and grouse](#/s/habitat-upland/0).
```

## Droppings and roosts

```diagram rs-dr-bird
```

- A pile of curved white capped droppings: a night roost (70%).
- Snow roost: a hole in soft snow with droppings inside (70%).

```more droppings and roosts in full
- Upland bird droppings are "often found in large groups where birds have roosted overnight" (85%, North Dakota Game and Fish).
- Grouse: curved cylinders about 2 to 3 cm long, pencil thick, brown with a white tip (70%).
- Grouse that snow roost for a long time defecate in one spot, making a small latrine (65%, Ruffed Grouse Society biologist via search preview).
- In deep soft snow ruffed grouse dive in and roost under the snow for warmth (80%, from [Habitat tour: quail and grouse](#/s/habitat-upland/0)).
- Quail roost in trees or thick cover at night (75%, from [Quail, chukar, partridge, pheasant](#/s/upland-birds/0)).
- A drum log with a pile of droppings on it: a ruffed grouse displays there (Tip).
```

## Feeding sign

| Bird | Feeding sign | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Ruffed grouse | Clipped buds and catkins, winter (75%) | Aspen, birch, willow |
| Grouse, fall | Berries, rose hips, leaves (75%) | Edges, burns, cut blocks |
| Quail | Scratched soil and litter patches (65%) | Under shrubs near cover |
| Geese | Grazed shoots, droppings, feathers (75%) | Fields, pasture, lawns |
| Ducks | Dabbled mud, floating feathers (Tip) | Shallow marsh edges |

- Fresh sign plus food plus cover: come back with the shotgun (Tip).

```more feeding sign in full
- Quail feed "on the ground, often scratching at the soil" (65%, search preview). Look for scratched patches near cover.
- Goose grazing can look very similar to deer feeding in a field. Check for droppings and tracks (65%, North Carolina State University extension via search preview).
- Ruffed grouse eat aspen buds in winter (75%, from the photo caption of the aspen grove).
- Before you carry the shotgun: grouse, quail and waterfowl all have their own seasons and limits. See [BC rules](#/s/bc-rules/0) and the Rule Book.
```

## Moult feather or kill feather?

```diagram rs-bs-feather
```

- One or two feathers on a trail: often moult or preening (Tip).
- A pile of feathers: something died here (75%).

```more feathers in full
- "It's sometimes possible to narrow it down to an avian or mammalian predator by looking at the method of feather removal, with plucked feathers pointing to a bird of prey and chewed off feathers to a mammal" (60%, bushcraft forum via search preview).
- Mammal: teeth leave holes in the vanes, blunt or cut shafts, saliva, feathers stuck together (60%).
- Raptor: bills leave cut or crushed shafts near the base, or whole quills pulled clean (60%).
- "Carcasses are scavenged too, so if you come across older remains then more than one type of sign may be seen" (60%). Read the whole site.
- A cat grabs a mouthful of feathers and shakes its head, scattering them (55%: Tip).
```

## Bird kill sites

```diagram rs-bs-kill
```

- Ring of feathers on a stump or open spot, breastbone left: a raptor (60%).
- Scattered feathers, tracks, maybe a carcass gone: a mammal (60%).

```more kill sites in full
- A raptor often leaves the breastbone and the wing bones, often still attached (60%, forum via search preview).
- Goshawks pluck their prey; reports on great horned owls are mixed (55%: Tip).
- A coyote or fox may carry the bird away and leave only a few feathers (Tip).
- Lots of grouse kill sites in one area: lots of grouse, and lots of predators (Tip).
- Mammal predators and kill sites in detail: [Predator sign and kill sites](#/s/rs-predator-sign/0).
```

## Wings in the snow

```diagram rs-bs-snow-wings
```

- Tracks end in a fan of wing marks: it flew (80%).
- Wing marks where mouse tracks end: an owl or hawk strike (Tip).

```more snow wing marks
- Wing prints in snow mark where a grouse took off or landed (80%, from [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0)).
- A burst of wing marks beside a hole: a grouse came out of a snow roost (80%).
- Wing marks with a small hole and no bird tracks: an owl or hawk dropped on a vole (Tip).
- Snow tracking in detail: [Snow tracking](#/s/snow-tracking/0) and [Sign in snow, mud, sand and dust](#/s/rs-sign-substrates/0).
```

## Duck and goose sign

```diagram rs-bs-water
```

- Fresh green droppings in a field: geese fed there today (Tip).
- Feathers and droppings on a bar: a loafing spot (Tip).

```more waterfowl sign in full
- Geese roost on water overnight and at first light often fly out to feed on crop fields or pasture (65%, onX via search preview).
- Hunters scout the field geese use late in the afternoon and set up there the next morning (60%, hunting source via search preview).
- Geese drop about 6 times an hour while feeding, so fresh droppings pile up fast where they fed (60%, Royal Society for the Protection of Birds (RSPB) blog).
- Moulted flight feathers on a lake shore in late summer mark a moulting area (Tip).
- Fraser Valley fields near home: get landowner consent before you walk any field (95%). See [Landowner permission](#/s/landowner-permission/0).
- Ducks and geese in depth: [Ducks](#/s/ducks/0), [Geese](#/s/geese/0), [Habitat tour: ducks](#/s/habitat-ducks/0).
```

## After your shot: mark the fall

```anim rs-bs-fall Feathers puff, the bird falls. Mark it on a tree.
```

- A puff of feathers means a hit (75%).
- Line the fall up with a tree or rock. Walk straight there (Tip).

```more marking and the law
- A bird that flies on after a puff of feathers may be hit. Watch it until it is out of sight; it may drop far away (Tip).
- Line up the fall with something tall behind it. Do not shoot again until you have marked the first bird (Tip).
- Mark the fall spot with your hat or a blaze orange item, then search in small circles (Tip).

> [!law] Migratory Birds Regulations, 2022, s. 42
> "A person must not hunt migratory game birds unless they have adequate means at their immediate disposal for retrieving any such bird that they kill or injure." "An individual who wounds a migratory game bird must ensure that the bird is killed and retrieved as soon as the circumstances permit." (99%)

- BC adds for all game: make "all reasonable effort to locate, dispatch, retrieve and include it in their bag limit" (99%, synopsis page 12).
- Full text: [Migratory birds in BC](#/s/rb-migratory-birds/0). Shotgun skills: [Shotgun skills](#/s/shotgun-skills/0).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```photo hab-quail-vernon Quail on a post: the covey is in the brush below.
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom, not law): Find the dust, the droppings and the berries in one place, and the birds are within a gun shot.

> [!mistake]
> - Reading one feather on a trail as a kill site.
> - Walking a goose field the morning you want to hunt it.
> - Shooting a second bird before you marked where the first one fell.

```checklist rs-bs-check

A dust bowl found
A roost or dropping pile found
Feathers checked: plucked or bitten
```
