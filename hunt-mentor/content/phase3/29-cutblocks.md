> [!why]
> Logging has made a patchwork of cutblocks of every age across your hunting country. Each age is a different kitchen. Learn to age a block at a glance and you know who eats there.

```photo hab-wells-gray-cutblock
```

- A cutblock grows through stages, like a burn (90%).
- Young blocks feed deer. Middle aged blocks feed moose (65 to 85%).
- Old dense blocks are cover, not food (90%).

```more what this session covers
- This session: BC's forest stages, a block year by year, moose and block age, reading a logged landscape from above, roads and access, and how to hunt blocks.
- Fire does the same job in nature: [After fire](#/s/burn-country/0). Strips left along creeks: [Wetlands and riparian strips](#/s/wetland-country/0).
- Assumption: ages are years since the block was cut. Growth is faster in wet zones and slower in dry or high ones.
```

## BC forest stages

```diagram rp-cut-stages
```

- 7 stages from bare ground to old forest (90%).
- Food peaks in the shrub stages (85%).
- The pole stage is dark and bare (90%).

```more the official definitions
- Source: BC structural stages, Standards for Terrestrial Ecosystems Mapping 1998, reprinted in Accounts and Measures for Managing Identified Wildlife, Appendix 5, 2004 (90%).
- Stage 1, sparse or bryoid: mosses and lichens, under 20 years after disturbance in normal forest.
- Stage 2, herb: grasses, forbs, ferns; trees under 10% cover; under 20 years.
- Stage 3, shrub and herb: shrubs over 20% cover; 3a low shrub under 2 m (under 20 years), 3b tall shrub 2 to 10 m (under 40 years). Recent cut or burned areas regenerating to forest are stage 3.
- Stage 4, pole and sapling: trees over 10 m, densely stocked, overtopping shrubs and herbs; usually over 10 to 15 years old and under 40 years since disturbance; stagnant dense stands up to 100 years.
- Stage 5, young forest: self thinning and layering; generally 40 to 80 years.
- Stage 6, mature forest: understories well developed as the canopy opens; 80 to 140 years in group A zones (for example MS, SBPS), 80 to 250 years in the others.
- Stage 7, old forest: snags and logs of all ages, patchy understories; over 140 or over 250 years.
- [[Forb]]: a broad leaved herb that is not a grass.
```

## A block, stage by stage

```steps A cutblock from stumps to forest
rp-cut-s1 | Years 0 to 3: stumps, slash and grass.
rp-cut-s2 | Years 3 to 10: low shrubs. Deer feed.
rp-cut-s3 | Years 10 to 25: tall shrubs. Moose, grouse, hares.
rp-cut-s4 | Years 25 to 40: dense poles. Hiding cover only.
rp-cut-s5 | Years 40 to 140+: forest again, gaps reopen.
```

- Age a block from the height of the young trees (Tip).

```more who uses each stage
- Young seral forest (stages 2 to 4) in the MS: moose, mule deer, black bear, lynx, coyote, snowshoe hare; ruffed grouse, northern goshawk (95%, zone book Table 26).
- Dense young lodgepole regeneration gives moose and mule deer thermal and hiding cover (95%).
- Logging may temporarily benefit moose when willows and other shrubs become abundant in cutblocks, but removing too much forest cover can hurt them. Moose need cover to hide from predators and hunters, for shade and for shelter in blizzards (85%, BC Ministry of Environment, Moose in BC).
- Logging creates early seral forage for elk, but it disappears once the canopy closes (85%, Elk in BC).
- Coast black tailed deer make frequent use of logged habitats in spring and summer (80%).
- Snowshoe hares and grouse use the thick shrub and sapling stages (Tip, see [Small game](#/s/small-game/0)).
- Height guide (Tip): knee high seedlings about 3 to 5 years; head high about 8 to 12; twice your height about 15 to 20; closed dark canopy about 25 plus. Varies by zone.
```

## Moose and block age

```diagram rp-moose-cut-age
```

- One BC study: moose chose blocks 10 to 30 years old (65%).
- They avoided blocks under 10 and over 30 years (65%).

```more the studies
- UBC thesis using GPS data from 46 mostly adult female moose, 2004 to 2019: moose strongly selected cutblocks 10 to 30 years since cut and avoided 0 to 9 and over 30 year blocks in every season (65%, search preview; study area not confirmed).
- Near Logan Lake (Region 3), a study found plants in cutblocks had more tannins and less digestible protein than plants in forests, and summer forage was short of protein for nursing cows (65%, search preview). Good looking browse is not always good food.
- Moose basics: [Moose](#/s/moose/0).
```

## Read a logged landscape from above

```diagram rp-cut-map
```

- Look for blocks of different ages side by side (Tip).
- Stars: young feed blocks touching timber or creek strips (Tip).

```more how to use satellite images
- In a mapping app with satellite view, new blocks are brown, young blocks bright green, older blocks a softer green with texture, old timber dark green (Tip).
- Satellite images can be years old. Check the image date if the app shows it (Tip).
- BC publishes cutblock and road data; many hunting map apps show cutblock year layers (Tip). See [Map scouting](#/s/map-scouting/0) and [Land status](#/s/maps-land-status/0).
- Best spots (Tip): the edge where a 5 to 20 year block meets mature timber, narrow fingers of timber between blocks, saddles, and creek strips.
```

## Edges: where food meets cover

```diagram hf-cutblock-edge
```

- Deer feed in the block and slip back to timber (Tip).
- Most feeding is close to the edge (Tip).
- Sit the edge, downwind, at first and last light.

```more edge notes
- An [[Edge]] is where two habitats meet. Animals get food on one side and cover on the other (Tip).
- Long, wavy edges and leftover tree patches inside a block give more edge than a straight line (Tip).
- Do not cut across the middle of a feeding block at dusk. Walk the timber edge or the road (Tip).
- Thermals in blocks: open blocks heat fast in the morning sun, pulling air upslope (75%). See [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals/0).
```

## Roads, gates and access

```photo hab-wells-gray-cutblock Logging roads reach almost every block.
```

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Logging roads give access, and access means pressure (85%).
- Some roads and areas are closed to vehicles (99%).
- ATV on a Forest Service Road: licence and insurance (99%).

```more access rules and courtesy
- BC Ministry of Environment notes that logging and other access roads into moose habitat need to be controlled because they can lead to over harvesting (85%).
- Motor vehicle closures and restrictions are in the Rule Book: [Where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt/0) and [Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3/0).
- ATVs on a [[FSR]] (Forest Service Road): a valid driver's licence and at least $200,000 third party liability insurance (99%, page 9, 2 October 2026 edition).
- Environmental damage, such as rutting up wet ground with a vehicle: fine of $575, serious cases up to $100,000 and/or a year in jail (99%, page 9).
- Courtesy (Tip): pull fully off the road to park, never block a gate or a landing, give loaded logging trucks the road, and stay off active logging sites.
- ATV rules: [ATV rules](#/s/atv-rules/0).
```

## Hunt a cutblock

```steps Hunt cutblocks in 5 steps
rp-cut-map | Find blocks 5 to 25 years old beside timber.
rp-zid-1 | Glass big blocks from the road or a high corner.
rp-dh-2 | Walk the timber edge, wind in your face.
rp-cut-s3 | Check tall shrub blocks for moose sign.
rp-cut-s4 | Leave the dark pole stands. Little lives there.
```

- Glass first, walk second (Tip).

```more the method in detail
1. Scout on the map first. Mark blocks by age (Tip).
2. Big open blocks are glassing country at first and last light (Tip). See [Glassing](#/s/glassing/0).
3. Walk edges into the wind. Stop often (Tip). See [Moving quietly](#/s/still-hunting/0).
4. Moose sign in tall shrub blocks: browsed willow and aspen at chest height, big tracks, droppings (Tip). See [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0).
5. Pole stage stands are hard walking and hold little food (90%). They are escape cover; deer and moose may bed at their edges (Tip).
- Shots across a block can be long. Know your range and backstop (Tip). See [.308 ballistics](#/s/ballistics-308/0).
```

## Common mistakes

```diagram rp-cut-s4
```

> [!mistake]
> - Hunting a block because it is green on the map, without checking its age.
> - Walking across the open middle of a block at dusk.
> - Parking on a logging road where trucks need to pass.

```more more mistakes
- Thinking every new block is full of deer. Fresh blocks (years 0 to 2) are mostly stumps and slash (90%).
- Ignoring the older blocks next door. A 20 year block may hold the moose (65%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram rp-moose-cut-age
```

- **Knee, head, twice, dark:** knee high (about 3 to 5 years), head high (about 8 to 12), twice your height (about 15 to 20), dark poles (25 plus).
- **10 to 30 for moose:** blocks 10 to 30 years old were the moose favourite in one BC study.
- **"Some Hungry Shrubs Push Young Mature Oaks":** the 7 stages in order: Sparse, Herb, Shrub, Pole, Young, Mature, Old.
- **Edge = kitchen door.**
- **Rhyme:** Brown is new, bright green is food, dark green is cover.
