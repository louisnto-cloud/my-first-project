> [!why]
> A pinch point turns a whole mountain of choices into one narrow path. Find it, sit it right, and the animal walks to you.

```anim rl-pp-hourglass Four trails in, one way through.
```

- **Funnel:** land or cover that narrows the routes (65%).
- **Pinch point:** the narrowest spot in a funnel (65%).

```more what this lesson covers
- This is the deep dive after [Trails and funnels](#/s/funnels/0). That lesson defined funnels and pinch points. This one shows every kind, how to find them on the map and the Hunt Map in 3D, how to rank them, and how to sit them.
- Nearly all of this is hunter craft, not science (Tip). Treat each pinch as a guess until tracks and trails prove it.
- Builds on [Landforms 101](#/s/landforms-101/0), [Water on the land](#/s/water-on-land/0) and [Edges](#/s/edges/0).
```

## Four families of pinch

| Family | Examples |
|---|---|
| Terrain | Saddle, bench ledge, head of a draw, gap in a cliff band, narrow ridge |
| Cover | Timber strip between openings, brushy creek strip, hedge, point of timber |
| Water | Creek crossing, beaver dam, lake narrows, strip between lake and cliff |
| People | Gate, fence gap, culvert, gap between yards or roads |

- The best pinches stack two or more families in one spot (Tip).

```more why animals use pinches
- Animals take the easiest, safest route between bed, feed and water: less climbing, more cover, firm footing (65%).
- When the land or cover leaves only one easy route, most animals that pass use it (Tip).
- Use is not constant: wind, pressure, snow and season change which pinch is hot (Tip).
```

## Terrain pinches

```steps Five terrain pinches
hf-shape-saddle | Saddle: the low crossing of a ridge.
rl-pp-bench | Bench ledge: a wide bench narrows to a thin shelf.
rl-pp-draw-head | Draw head: trails go round the top.
rl-pp-cliff-gap | Cliff gap: the only way through a rock band.
rl-lf-spur-3d | Spur: trails climb the even spine.
```

- Each one shows on a topo map and pops in 3D (Tip).

```more each terrain pinch
- **Saddle:** an hourglass on the map. Animals cross ridges here (65%). In the rut, bucks cruise saddles between doe groups (60%).
- **Bench ledge:** wide gaps between contour lines that pinch to a thin gap where cliffs above and below come close (Tip).
- **Draw head:** animals walk around the top of a steep draw rather than dropping in and climbing out (Tip).
- **Cliff gap:** a break in a rock band. On 20 m contours a small band may hide: check 3D, satellite and your eyes (95%).
- **Narrow ridge or spur:** where a ridge is just a few metres wide, everything walking it passes one spot (Tip).
```

## Cover pinches

```diagram rl-pp-strip
```

- Animals cross open ground where cover connects (65%).
- A strip of timber, a brushy creek, a hedge (Tip).

```more cover pinches in detail
- A strip of trees or brush between two blocks of timber across open ground is a travel lane, especially in daylight (Tip).
- Creek strips: willow and dogwood through grassland or farmland (75%).
- Leave strips in cutblocks: narrow bands of standing timber left by logging (Tip). See Cutblocks by age on the Hunt Map.
- Points of timber: the place where cover reaches farthest into the open (Tip). See [Edges](#/s/edges/0).
```

## Water pinches

```diagram rl-wa-crossing
```

- Trails gather at the easy creek crossing (Tip).
- A lake against a cliff leaves a narrow shore strip (Tip).

```more water pinches in detail
- Low banks and firm gravel collect crossings from a whole slope (Tip). See [Water on the land](#/s/water-on-land/0).
- Beaver dams are bridges for many animals (Tip).
- Lake narrows and the strip between a lake and a cliff squeeze trails, as in the opening animation (Tip).
- Rivers: shallow riffles are crossings; deep pools are barriers (Tip).
```

## People pinches

```diagram sg-pinch-types
```

- Open gates, downed fence, fence corners, culverts (Tip).
- Usually on or near private land: permission first (99%).

```more people pinches and the rules
- Deer cross fences where they are low or broken. Look for hair on the wire (65%). See [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0).
- Most farm gates and fence lines in the valleys are on private land: you need permission (99%, Rule Book).
- No hunting or shooting within 100 m of a dwelling or an occupied farm building, and never within a road allowance (99%, Rule Book). See [Rule Book: where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt/0).
```

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

## Stacked funnels: the best spots

```diagram rl-pp-stack
```

- One feature is good. Two or three in a line are gold (Tip).
- Example: saddle above a timber strip above a creek crossing.

```more how to rank a pinch
| Score each pinch 0 to 2 | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| How narrow | Wide | Medium | One trail |
| Between bed and feed | No | Maybe | Yes |
| Fresh sign | None | Old | Fresh |
| Wind works for a seat | No | Some winds | Most winds |
| Pressure | Road side | Some | Far or awkward |

- 8 to 10: sit it. 5 to 7: test it with sign. Under 5: keep looking (Tip).
- This scorecard is a beginner aid, not science (Tip).
```

## Read the sign at a pinch

```photo hab-deer-trail-reeds A packed trail: the proof a pinch is used.
```

- Packed trails, tracks both ways, pellets, rubs (75%).
- No sign, no sit (Tip).

```more what to look for
- Trails 15 to 30 cm wide, packed and bare (65%). See [Trails and funnels](#/s/funnels/0).
- Tracks going both ways mean a daily route; one way can mean a seasonal move (Tip).
- Rubs along a pinch in late October and November mean bucks travel it in the rut (60%). See [Rubs and scrapes](#/s/rs-rubs-scrapes/0).
- In snow, a pinch tells you exactly how many animals passed and when (75%). See [Snow tracking](#/s/snow-tracking/0).
```

## Sit the pinch, not on it

```diagram rl-pp-wind
```

- Downwind or crosswind, 100 to 150 m off, a clear lane (Tip).
- Never walk through the pinch to reach your seat (Tip).

```more set up in detail
- Check the wind and the thermal for the hour you sit. Morning air rises, evening air drains (85%). Sit where both carry your scent away from the trail (Tip). See [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals/0).
- Saddles swirl: wind pours through the gap. Have a second seat for the other wind (Tip).
- Pick a seat with a rest, a backstop for every lane and a range you know. Use Range rings on the Hunt Map to see 100, 200, 300 m (99%, app).
- Be in before first light or 2 hours before dark, and come in the back way (Tip).
- If you hunt with your brother, sit two pinches on the same route or one pinch from two angles with agreed zones of fire (Tip).
```

## Pinch points for birds

```diagram sb-upland-pinch
```

- Quail and grouse run along hedges and brushy strips to the end (75%).
- Ducks fly the same lines between roost and feed (75%).

```more birds and pinches
- In upland hunting, the end of a hedge or brushy strip is where birds flush. Post a blocker there with clear zones of fire (Tip). See [Habitat tour: quail and grouse](#/s/habitat-upland/0).
- Ducks and geese follow flight lines between roosting water and feeding fields, often over low points and along rivers (75%). See [Waterfowl daily flights](#/s/waterfowl-daily-flights/0).
- Grouse in fall walk logging roads and creek edges for grit and greens (65%).
```

## Find pinches on the Hunt Map

```steps Find a pinch in 6 steps
rl-hm-3d | Tilt in 3D and rotate: look for dips and ledges.
rl-hm-boost | Raise the height boost to see benches.
rl-hm-tools | Slope steepness: find gaps in red cliff bands.
rl-hm-sat | Satellite: cover strips across the open.
rl-hm-layers | Layers: Private land, closures, Recreation trails.
rl-hm-spot | Long press: range rings and the Scent cone.
```

- Mark every candidate, then score it on the ground (Tip).

```more Hunt Map details
- Range rings show 100, 200 and 300 m from a spot (99%, app).
- The Scent cone shows where your scent goes with the forecast wind from a spot (99%, app tool name). It needs signal for the forecast (99%, app notes).
- What can I see from here shows which lanes you can see from a seat; trees are not included (99%, app).
- Recreation trails and Forest Service roads layers show where people walk and drive: human pinches and pressure (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

```photo hab-deer-trail-snow In snow, a pinch tells you everything.
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Don't hunt where the animals are. Hunt where they have to go.

> [!mistake]
> - Sitting right on the trail in the pinch.
> - Walking through the pinch to get to your seat.
> - Sitting a pinch with no fresh sign.

```more the mistakes in full
- On the trail you are in the animal's face and in its nose. Sit off it (Tip).
- Your boots and scent on the trail warn every animal that follows (Tip).
- A pinch on the map is a guess. Tracks, pellets and packed dirt make it a fact (Tip).
```

## Do this in the field

> [!field]
> Find five pinches on the Hunt Map in one MU (Management Unit). Score them. Walk the top two and read the sign.

```checklist rl-pp-field
Find one saddle, one bench ledge and one draw head on the map
Find one cover strip and one creek crossing
Score each pinch with the 5 line scorecard
Walk the best two and look for packed trails and fresh tracks
Pick a seat downwind with a backstop and range rings
```

## Memory hooks

- **Hunt where they have to go.**
- **Two in a line, that's fine. Three in a row, that's the spot.**
- **Off the trail, not on it.**
- **No sign, no sit.**
- **PINCH:** Paths join, Into cover, Narrow ground, Check the wind, Hide off the line.

```more using the hooks
- Run PINCH on every candidate before you commit a morning to it (Tip).
```
