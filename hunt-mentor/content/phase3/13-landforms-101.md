> [!why]
> Animals move, bed and feed by the shape of the land. Name the shape and you can guess the animal.

```anim rl-lf-flood Each shoreline is one contour line.
```

- Seven shapes run almost every hunt: ridge, spur, draw, saddle, bench, knob, bowl.
- You will learn each one three ways: topo map, Hunt Map in 3D, on the ground.

```more what this lesson covers and how it builds
- This lesson goes deeper than [Maps 1: reading terrain](#/s/maps-terrain/0). That lesson taught contour lines, scale and steepness. This one is a field guide to the shapes.
- For each shape: what it is, how it looks on a topo map, how it looks on the Hunt Map tilted in 3D, how it looks when you stand on it, and why animals and hunters care.
- A contour line joins points of the same height. Picture water rising around a hill: every shoreline is a contour (98%).
- On the Hunt Map in metres, contour lines are every 20 m with a thicker index line every 100 m (99%, app settings, read from the app code).
- Animal use of each shape is mostly hunter observation, not science. It is labelled Tip or given a certainty under 80%.
```

## Three views of the same ground

```diagram rl-lf-views
```

- Topo map: shape from above, in lines.
- 3D map: the same lines draped over the hills.
- Ground: what your eyes see from where you stand.

```more why three views
- A topo map is exact but abstract. The 3D view on the Hunt Map turns the lines back into hills. Your eyes on the ground see only what faces you (Tip).
- Train all three together. Every time you look at a shape on the map, tilt it in 3D, then find it on the skyline on your next drive (Tip).
- The Hunt Map 3D view is built from terrain data with about 24 m between height samples, so small cliffs, cut banks and rock bands may not show (95%, app notes).
- The drawings in this lesson labelled "3D sketch" are hand drawn to show the idea. Your screen will look like a shaded, tilted map, not a painting.
```

## The 3 families of shapes

| Family | Shapes | Contour clue |
|---|---|---|
| High | Ridge, spur, knob, top | Vs or Us point downhill; closed rings on tops |
| Low | Draw, valley, bowl | Vs or Us point uphill |
| Steps and gaps | Saddle, bench, cliff | Hourglass; wide gap; lines touching |

- Learn the families first. The names come easily after.

```more the military list and the hunter list
- Land navigation manuals use 5 major shapes (hill, saddle, valley, ridge, depression), 3 minor ones (draw, spur, cliff) and 2 made by people (cut and fill) (75%, land navigation references).
- Hunters add words the manuals do not use: bench, knob, bowl, finger, point. They are plain names for common shapes (Tip).
- A **depression** is a low spot with higher ground all around. Its contour rings have small tick marks pointing in (75%). Rare in hunting country, except sinks and potholes.
- A **cut** is a road or rail cut through high ground. A **fill** is a built up bank across low ground. Both show on maps near roads and rail (75%).
```

## Which way does the V point?

```diagram rl-lf-v-rule
```

- V points uphill: a draw. Water runs down its middle (90%).
- V points downhill: a ridge or a spur (90%).

```more the V rule in full
- Where contour lines cross a draw or valley, they bend into a V or U that points uphill, toward higher numbers (90%).
- Where they cross a ridge or spur, the V or U points downhill, toward lower numbers (90%).
- Check with water. A blue creek line sits in the V of a draw. A creek never runs along the crest of a ridge (95%).
- Check with the numbers. The top of each contour number faces uphill (98%).
- Sharp Vs mean a narrow, steep draw. Round Us mean a broad, gentle one (75%).
```

## Ridge

```steps Ridge: map, 3D, ground
hf-shape-ridge | Topo: Vs point downhill along a long spine.
rl-lf-ridge-3d | 3D: a crest with a sunny face and a shady face.
photo:hab-lac-du-bois-potholes | Ground: fir patches sit on the ridges.
```

- A long line of high ground, higher than the land on both sides (90%).
- Deer and elk travel just below the crest, not on top (Tip).

```more ridge details and how animals use it
- A ridge is a long strip of high ground. Its crest is higher than the ground on both sides (90%).
- On a topo map the contour lines make long Us or Vs that point downhill, away from the crest (90%).
- On the Hunt Map in 3D, a ridge is the line where a sunny face meets a shady face. Turn the map so the sun lights one side (Tip).
- On the ground, it is the skyline you see from the valley road.
- Animals: deer and elk often travel a ridge a little below the top, on the side away from the wind, so they are not outlined against the sky (Tip, hunter observation).
- You: a ridge is a highway for walking and a seat for [[Glassing]]. Walk just below the crest so your outline does not show (Tip).
- Morning thermals rise up both faces toward the crest (85%). A ridge top seat in the morning puts your scent above the animals. See [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals/0).
```

## Spur (finger ridge)

```steps Spur: map, 3D, ground
rl-lf-spur-topo | Topo: a tongue of Us off the main ridge.
rl-lf-spur-3d | 3D: a short finger of high ground.
rl-lf-spur-ground | Ground: lit fingers with dark draws between.
```

- A short ridge that runs down off a bigger ridge (90%).
- Each spur has a draw on both sides (90%).

```more spur details and how animals use it
- A spur is short, sloping high ground that juts out from the side of a ridge (90%). Hunters also call it a finger or a point.
- On the topo map it is a tongue of U or V shapes pointing downhill, sitting between two draws that point uphill (90%).
- On the Hunt Map in 3D, spurs look like the knuckles of a hand running off the main ridge (Tip).
- Animals: the point of a spur, just below the top, gives a bedded deer a view down both draws and the wind from below in the morning (Tip, hunter observation).
- Trails often climb a spur because the slope is even, then cross into the next draw at the top (Tip).
- You: a spur is a natural glassing seat. From the end of one spur you can see the faces of the next spur across the draw (Tip).
```

## Draw

```steps Draw: map, 3D, ground
hf-shape-draw | Topo: Vs point uphill. Water runs here.
rl-lf-draw-3d | 3D: a trough with brush and a creek.
photo:hab-dogwood-stems | Ground: red osier dogwood fills a wet draw.
```

- A small valley that runs down a slope (90%).
- Draws hold water, brush, shade and travel routes (75%).

```more draw details and how animals use it
- A draw is a small, steep sided valley with little or no flat ground in the bottom. Contours make Vs or Us that point uphill to its head (90%).
- On the Hunt Map in 3D a draw is a trough. The shady side and the sunny side meet in the bottom (Tip).
- On the ground, look for a line of darker green: shrubs, aspen, willow, red osier dogwood, chokecherry. They need the extra water (75%).
- Animals: draws give cover close to food. Mule deer, white tails, grouse and quail use brushy draws to move and hide (70%).
- Evening thermals and cold air drain down draws (85%). Do not sit at the bottom of a draw in the evening with animals below you.
- The head of a draw, where it starts near the top, is a common crossing point (Tip). See [Pinch points](#/s/pinch-points/0).
```

## Saddle

```steps Saddle: map, 3D, ground
hf-shape-saddle | Topo: an hourglass between two tops.
rl-lf-saddle-3d | 3D: a dip in the ridge. Trails cross here.
rl-lf-saddle-ground | Ground: the low notch between two tops.
```

- The low point on a ridge between two higher points (90%).
- Animals cross ridges at saddles: least climb, least skyline (65%).

```more saddle details and how animals use it
- A saddle rises on two sides and falls on two sides. Other names: col, pass, gap, notch (90%).
- On the topo map it is an hourglass: two sets of closed rings with the lines pinched between them (90%).
- A saddle can also be just a dip along a level ridge, not always between two big tops (75%).
- On the Hunt Map in 3D it is the dip in the skyline. Rotate the map to look along the ridge and it pops out (Tip).
- On the ground it is the low notch on the skyline.
- Animals: deer and elk cross a ridge at the saddle because it is the easiest climb and keeps them off the high skyline (65%, hunter observation).
- In the rut, bucks cruise saddles between groups of does (60%). See [The rut](#/s/the-rut/0).
- You: saddles are classic stand sites. Wind swirls in saddles because air pours through the gap, so check it often (Tip).
```

## Bench

```steps Bench: map, 3D, ground
hf-shape-bench | Topo: a wide gap between tight lines.
rl-lf-bench-3d | 3D: a flat step on a steep face.
photo:hab-grass-fir-edge | Ground: flat ground below timber.
```

- A flat or gentle shelf on a steep slope (90%).
- Deer bed and travel on benches (65%).

```more bench details and how animals use it
- A bench is a flat step on a hillside. On the topo map: tight lines above, a wide gap, tight lines below (90%).
- A bench can be big (a whole grassland terrace) or tiny (one contour gap). Small ones may not show on 20 m contours: use the 3D view with the height boost up (Tip).
- On the Hunt Map in 3D a bench is a pale shelf across a dark face. The Slope steepness tool shows it as an uncoloured strip between coloured slopes (95%, app tool shows slopes under 15 degrees uncoloured).
- On the ground it is the place where you suddenly stop climbing and catch your breath.
- Animals: a bench gives a bedded deer flat ground, a view down the slope and wind from below in the morning (65%, hunter observation).
- Trails run along benches because walking on the level costs less energy (65%).
- You: benches are where to look first when glassing a big face (Tip).
- Many Thompson valley sides have big flat terraces left by glacial lakes and rivers. They are benches on a giant scale (65%, VERIFY with a local geology source).
```

## Knob

```steps Knob: map, 3D, ground
rl-lf-knob-topo | Topo: a small set of closed rings.
rl-lf-knob-3d | 3D: a little top on gentler ground.
photo:rl-knoll-newnham | Ground: a rounded little hill.
```

- A small rounded hill or high point (85%).
- Your best lookout on rolling ground (Tip).

```more knob details and how animals use it
- A knob is a small top: on the map, a small set of closed contour rings, sometimes only one ring (85%).
- Hunters also say knoll, hump or high point (Tip).
- A knob smaller than 20 m tall may not show on 20 m contours at all. Look for a bump in the 3D view or a small closed ring on the satellite with hill shading (Tip).
- Animals: on rolling grassland, deer often bed on the shoulder of a knob, just below the top, where they can see and smell (Tip, hunter observation).
- You: climb the back of a knob and glass over the top. Keep your head below the skyline and use the grass or a tree as a screen (Tip).
```

## Bowl

```steps Bowl: map, 3D, ground
rl-lf-bowl-topo | Topo: big Us wrapped around a basin.
rl-lf-bowl-3d | 3D: ridges on three sides, open on one.
photo:hab-subalpine-forest | Ground: a high basin of meadow and timber.
```

- A rounded basin with high ground on three sides (85%).
- Hides animals from below. Glass it from the rim (Tip).

```more bowl details and how animals use it
- A bowl is a rounded basin at the head of a draw or valley. Contours wrap around it in big U shapes, open toward the low side (85%).
- A high mountain bowl carved by ice is called a cirque. Many hold a small lake or meadow (80%).
- On the Hunt Map in 3D a bowl looks like an amphitheatre. Turn on Slope steepness: the rim is often coloured red or purple, the floor uncoloured (Tip).
- Animals: bowls hold feed (meadows, wet seeps) and shade, and wind in a bowl swirls (Tip). Elk and mule deer use high basins in early fall before snow (70%).
- You: glass a bowl from the rim or from across the valley. Walking the floor puts you in a swirl with eyes above you (Tip).
```

## Cliffs, rock bands and depressions

```diagram hf-shape-cliff
```

- Lines touch or merge: a cliff. Plan around it (90%).
- Small rock bands may not show at all (95%).

```more cliff details
- A cliff is a vertical or nearly vertical drop. Contour lines merge or nearly touch (90%).
- On 20 m contours a 15 m rock band can hide between two lines. The 3D view and satellite base help, but trust your eyes on the ground (Tip).
- On the Hunt Map, Slope steepness colours ground over 45 degrees purple: cliffy, scrambling (99%, app tool key).
- Cliffs are escape terrain for sheep and goats and a barrier that funnels other animals (85%). See [Escape terrain](#/s/escape-terrain/0).
- Never plan a route down a gap you have not seen. Many gaps end in a drop (Tip).
```

## Read the shapes on a profile

```diagram rl-lf-profile
```

- Flat stretch on a climb: bench. Dip between tops: saddle.
- Sharp drop to a low point: draw.

```more profile tips
- An elevation profile is a side view of the ground along a line you draw (99%, app).
- Draw your line straight up a slope, across the contours, to find benches. Draw it along a ridge to find saddles (Tip).
- The profile also gives the climb in metres. Over 300 m of climb before first light is a hard morning for most beginners (Tip).
```

## Your fist is a mountain

```diagram rl-lf-fist
```

- Knuckles are tops. Dips between knuckles are saddles.
- Fingers are ridges and spurs. Gaps between fingers are draws.

```more using the fist trick
- This is an old land navigation teaching trick (Tip). Make a fist, knuckles up, and look at it from the side and from above.
- Draw contour rings on your knuckles with a pen if you like: you will see Vs point down your fingers (spurs) and up the gaps (draws).
```

## How animals use each shape

| Shape | Animals do | You do |
|---|---|---|
| Ridge | Travel just below the top | Walk below the crest, glass |
| Spur | Bed on the point | Glass the next spur |
| Draw | Hide, travel, drink | Still hunt into the wind |
| Saddle | Cross the ridge | Sit downwind of it |
| Bench | Bed, travel level | Glass benches first |
| Knob | Bed on the shoulder | Glass over the top |
| Bowl | Feed, rest in shade | Glass from the rim |

- Everything in this table is hunter observation (Tip).

```more why these patterns happen
- Animals save energy: they avoid climbing when a level route exists, which is why trails follow benches and cross at saddles (65%).
- Animals want to see danger and smell it: high beds with a view below and wind from below in the morning (Tip).
- Animals hide: draws and timbered faces give cover close to food (70%).
- These are patterns, not laws. Pressure, wind, snow and food change them every day (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

```photo hab-lac-du-bois-potholes Read the shapes, then the sign.
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): The land has a few doors. Learn where they are and you stop chasing the animal and start waiting for it.

> [!mistake]
> - Reading a draw as a ridge. Check which way the V points.
> - Trusting 20 m contours for small benches and rock bands.
> - Glassing the whole hill instead of benches, spurs and saddles first.

```more the mistakes in full
- Reading a draw as a ridge: the V points uphill for a draw, downhill for a ridge. Find the creek line to check.
- Trusting 20 m contours: a bench, cliff band or knob under 20 m can sit between two lines. Check the 3D view, the satellite base and then your eyes.
- Glassing everything evenly: start with the shapes animals favour (benches, spur points, saddles, draw edges) and work out from there (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

- **V points to the Very top: a draw.** V points down: a ridge.
- **Hourglass, cross the pass:** a saddle is where trails cross.
- **Wide gap, take a nap:** benches are beds.
- **Fist test:** knuckles tops, dips saddles, fingers spurs, gaps draws.
- **RSD SBKB** (Ridge, Spur, Draw, Saddle, Bench, Knob, Bowl): "Really Smart Deer Sneak Between Knobs and Bowls".

```more why hooks help
- Short rhymes and pictures are easier to recall under pressure than definitions (Tip).
- Say one hook every time you see the shape on a map or a skyline.
```
