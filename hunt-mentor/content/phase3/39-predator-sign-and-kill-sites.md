> [!why]
> Predator sign tells you where deer are and where danger is. A kill site can be the most dangerous spot in the woods.

## Predator sign: what to look for

```diagram rs-pr-sign
```

- Torn logs, dug anthills, fresh scat, tracks: recent bear activity (85%).
- Ravens, magpies, crows or jays: maybe a carcass that drew a bear (85%).

```more sign in full
- Alberta's hunter bear guide lists "fresh signs that indicate recent bear activity: tracks, scat, overturned logs or dug up anthills" and areas where bears may be feeding: berry patches, digging sites, near animal carcasses, grain fields (85%, Alberta Environment, Be BearSmart).
- "Watch for ravens, magpies, crows or jays. These birds could be at a carcass or gut pile that may have also attracted a bear" (85%, same source).
- Claw marks on aspen: a bear climbed (80%, photo caption). Bites and rubbed trees with hair: bear marking (65%). See the video below.
- Cougar scrapes: a small pile of needles or dirt with scat or urine on it, on a ridge trail (65%, from [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0)).
- Tracks and scat to scale: [Tracks](#/s/tracks/0), [Droppings deep dive](#/s/rs-droppings/0).
```

## Cougar kill: drag and cache

```diagram rs-pr-cougar-kill
```

- Little sign of a chase; kill dragged to cover and covered neatly (85%).
- A cougar stays near and feeds for 6 to 8 days (70%).

```more cougar kills in full
- Stalking predator: "Requires cover"; "Attacks occur over limited distance with little or no chase"; "Kills may be drug or carried considerable distance to an area of cover" (85%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife, livestock investigation guide).
- Attack zones: neck, throat, head, shoulder. "Crushed neck, windpipe, skull"; "Knife like wounds and lacerations with very clean edges" (85%).
- Feeding: "efficient, tidy"; "Hair removed by sheering or plucking"; "Entry behind shoulder or just behind ribs"; "Internal organs consumed first (heart, liver, lungs)"; "Caching of kill" (85%).
- Claw scrape marks on a cougar kill are long, about 61 cm (24 inches) or more; bobcat marks are short, 41 cm (16 inches) or less (80%, same source).
- Cats "Usually carry or drag prey item away from kill site, often to an area of dense cover. May then cache prey by carefully scraping the surrounding area and covering the prey item with ground debris" (85%).
- It stays nearby and feeds for 6 to 8 days (70%, from [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0)).
```

## Wolf kill: messy and open

```diagram rs-pr-wolf-kill
```

- Chase, bites to the rear and flanks, fed on at the kill (85%).
- Big bones cracked, remains spread out on older kills (85%).

```more wolf and coyote kills in full
- Wolf: "coursing predator"; "Potential for prolonged chase and attack"; "Kills rarely moved from attack location" (85%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife).
- Wolf attack zones: hindquarters, groin, tail, front armpits, throat (85%).
- Wolf feeding: "messy"; "Internal organs consumed first, then generally progresses from rear to head"; "Rumen often busted open"; "Large bones cracked open and marrow consumed (older carcass)"; "Prey remains spread across site (older carcass)" (85%).
- Coyote: "Typically attack at or near the throat"; "Smaller bones broken and chewed"; kills rarely moved (85%). Coyotes mostly take small prey, fawns and weak animals (Tip).
- A dog pack: "Relatively indiscriminate in attack location"; "Seldom feed on carcass" (85%).
- Wolves tend to chase and bite to bring prey down, so a struggle may show at the scene (85%).
```

## Bear cache: a raked mound

```diagram rs-pr-bear-cache
```

- Dirt and debris raked over the carcass; ground looks rototilled (85%).
- Bears defend caches (70%).

```more bears at kills in full
- Bears are "More variable than cougars": they "May or may not carry or drag prey item away from kill site. May or may not cache prey. If prey is cached, bears will use ground debris as well as dirt and ground may appear as if it was roto-tilled around the prey item" (85%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife).
- Bear attack signs: bites along the spine between the shoulder blades and to the head or neck; possible claw lacerations (85%).
- Bears may chase prey a short distance then use size and strength (85%).
- Grizzly bear sites are generally in more open habitat, black bear sites in denser habitat (80%, same guide).
- A bear may bed close to a cache. Investigators photographed a grizzly bed site near a kill (85%).
```

## Who killed it?

```diagram rs-pr-compare
```

- Cat tidy, wolf messy, bear raked (Tip, built on 85% source).

```more cautions
- The guide warns that wound patterns alone may not separate predators, and that "Figure should be used for general reference only" (85%).
- Many predators visit one carcass. A cougar kill may be fed on by bears, coyotes and ravens later (85%).
- You do not need to solve it. You need to know it is a kill site and leave (Tip).
```

## Killed here, or scavenged?

```diagram rs-pr-scav
```

- Blood, broken brush and trampled plants: killed here (85%).
- Little blood, no struggle: maybe died first, then fed on (85%).

```more predation versus scavenging
- "Wolves, coyotes, dogs, cougars, bobcats, and bears all take advantage of scavenging opportunities" (85%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife).
- Signs of a struggle: "trampled vegetation, broken branches on nearby trees or shrubs, spilled or sprayed blood on ground or nearby vegetation" (85%).
- Investigators confirm a live attack by bite marks with bruising (haemorrhage) under the hide. Bites with no bruising were made after death (85%). You will not skin a carcass you find; this is for understanding only.
- A dead deer with no predator sign may be a wounded animal another hunter lost, or a disease death. Report unusual dead deer; CWD testing matters (Tip). See [CWD](#/s/cwd/0).
```

## Found a carcass? 5 steps

```steps Found a carcass: 5 steps
rs-pr-1 | Stop. Do not walk closer.
rs-pr-2 | Look, listen, smell for the owner.
rs-pr-3 | Read it with binoculars from where you are.
rs-pr-4 | Back out the way you came, loud, spray ready.
rs-pr-5 | Tell your brother, pin it, report if needed.
```

- Never kneel over a covered carcass for photos (Tip).

```more the 5 steps in full
- A covered carcass means a cougar or bear is close and will come back. Do not touch it. Back out the way you came, talking loudly, and tell your brother where it is (75%, from [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0)).
- Bear spray in hand, safety clip off only when needed. See [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0).
- If you see a bear at the kill, leave. Do not try to haze it away (85%, Alberta guidance for hunters).
- Danger to people: call RAPP (Report All Poachers and Polluters) 1 877 952 7277, 24 hours, for wildlife conflicts where public safety is at risk (95%, synopsis page 2, from the Rule Book).
- Avoid the area for the rest of the day. Change your plan (Tip).
```

## When surprise is likely

```diagram rs-pr-risk
```

- Loud water, wind, low light and thick cover: make more noise (85%).
- Hunters move quietly, so they surprise bears more than hikers (85%).

```more why hunters are at higher risk
- "Being quiet, using animal attractants and calls and travelling alone mean that hunters place themselves at a heightened risk of a bear encounter" (85%, Alberta Environment, Be BearSmart).
- Be extra cautious when noise levels are high near rivers and streams, in wind or heavy rain, at dusk, night or dawn, and where sightlines are short because of heavy cover, bends in trails or hills (85%, same source).
- "Distress or mating calls, decoys and cover scents can attract bears as well as game" (85%).
- "Be cautious when tracking a wounded animal. Bears may also be attracted to animals that are wounded. When possible, have a partner keep watch" (85%). See [Blood, hair and tissue](#/s/rs-blood-hair-tissue/0).
- In Alberta, self defence actions by hunters have been the top cause of death for grizzly bears since 2006 (85%, same source). Prevention protects you and the bears.
```

## Your own kill: the gut pile

```photo g-bb-side Bears find gut piles first. Separate meat and guts.
```

- Bears are often drawn to the gut pile first (85%).
- Move the carcass at least 200 m from the gut pile before you leave (85%).

```more working at your kill
- "While field-dressing the carcass, be aware the scent may be attracting a bear. Make noise so nearby bears know you are in the area. If possible, have someone stand watch for bears while you handle the carcass" (85%, Alberta Environment).
- "If you need to leave the carcass and return later, separate the carcass from the gut pile. If you can, move the carcass at least 200 metres from the gut pile before you leave" (85%).
- Your brother watches with the rifle and spray while you work. Swap jobs (Tip). See [Field dressing](#/s/field-dressing/0) and [Packing out](#/s/packing-out/0).
- Do not drag the carcass back to camp: it lays a scent trail to where you sleep (85%).
```

## Hang meat in bear country

```diagram rs-pr-hang
```

- At least 3.5 m up and 1.5 m from trees, in plain view (85%).
- Store harvested animals at least 100 m from camp (85%).

```more hanging in full
- "Hang the carcass. The carcass should be suspended at least 3.5 metres off the ground and 1.5 metres from nearby trees in an area easily seen from a distance" (85%, Alberta Environment).
- "Store harvested animals at least 100 metres away from camp" (85%).
- Plain view lets you glass it from far away before you walk in (Tip).
- Meat care while it hangs: [Meat care](#/s/meat-care/0).
```

## Coming back to your kill

```anim rs-pr-return Come in from upwind, loud, and glass first.
```

- Approach from upwind, glass first, make lots of noise (85%).
- Moved, buried or partly eaten: leave right away and report (85%).

```more returning in full
- "When returning to the kill site, use caution. Approach the site from upwind, use binoculars to see if the site is clear and make as much noise as possible" (85%, Alberta Environment). Coming from upwind lets a bear smell you before you arrive, so it is not surprised.
- "If you see a bear at your kill site or if the carcass has been moved or buried, do not approach. Leave the site immediately" and report it (85%).
- If you find the carcass partly eaten, leave the area immediately: a bear may have claimed it and will defend it (85%, Alberta government page via search preview).
- In BC, report to the RAPP line (Report All Poachers and Polluters) 1 877 952 7277 (95%).
- Meat is replaceable. You are not (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```photo cougar Covered means coming back.
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom, not law): If something covered it, something is coming back for it. Be gone when it does.

> [!mistake]
> - Kneeling over a covered carcass to take photos.
> - Leaving meat beside the gut pile overnight.
> - Walking back to your kill at first light, quiet, into the wind, straight to the meat.

## Do this in the field

```diagram rs-pr-sign
```

> [!field]
> On every scouting walk, note one predator sign: track, scat, scrape, claw marks or birds circling. Pin it. Ask: what does it eat here, and where are the deer?

```checklist rs-pr-check
Game bags and rope to hang meat 3.5 m up

Return plan: from upwind, glass, loud
```
