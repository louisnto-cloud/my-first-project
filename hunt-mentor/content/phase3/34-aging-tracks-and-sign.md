> [!why]
> Old sign shows where animals were. Fresh sign shows where they are. Only one of those fills a tag.

```diagram rs-ag-ladder
```

- Five age bands: minutes, hours, today, days, a week or more.
- Always give a range and say why (Tip).

```more what this session covers
- [Tracks](#/s/tracks/0) and [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0) gave the quick aging clues. This session is the full method.
- You will learn: the control print, weather clocks (rain, frost, dew, wind, snowfall), debris and insects, broken twigs and crushed plants, how each ground type ages, scat, beds, rubs and scrapes, and how to decide if sign is fresh enough to hunt.
- Honest warning: even expert trackers sort sign into broad bands like "fresh" and "old" more easily than into hours (65%, Original Wisdom, trailing deer). Aging is a skill you calibrate, not a formula.
- Aging means judging the time since sign was made "taking into consideration vegetation damage, rain, sun, and other natural elements" (65%, tracking glossary via search preview).
```

## The control print method

```steps Age a track in 4 steps
rs-ag-1 | Press your boot beside it, same ground.
rs-ag-2 | Compare edges, colour, moisture.
rs-ag-3 | Revisit your print at 1, 6, 24, 72 h.
rs-ag-4 | Write a range and the reason.
```

- Your print is minutes old. Theirs looks the same? It is fresh (Tip).
- Every patch of ground ages differently. Test it (65%).

```more why it works
- Ground varies so much that "the only way to truly understand how a particular track has aged is to test that spot and get a feel for its nature and how it weathers" (65%, tracking source via search preview).
- Tracking schools teach aging with a tracking box or a strip of open ground: make prints each day and compare them through different weather (65%).
- Make the control print with the same pressure and in the same light. A sun print and a shade print age at different speeds (Tip).
- After a long dry spell, hard ground takes poor prints and even rain does not reset it as you expect (60%, Original Wisdom).
```

## Edges and walls

```diagram sg-track-age
```

- Crisp walls: hours. Rounded: days. Debris inside: a week (Tip).
- In fine dust a fresh print has a thin raised rim that slumps within hours (60%).

```more edge details
- As a track ages, "bits of disturbed soil will dry and the track edges will round out" (65%, Nature Mentor via search preview).
- Fresh: sharp walls, crisp toe dividers, the ridge between the two hoof halves still standing (Tip).
- A day: edges softening, loose crumbs sliding in (Tip).
- Days: walls rounded, the ridge between the toes gone (Tip).
- A week or more: debris, spider web or insect trails inside (70%).
```

## The rain clock

```anim rs-ag-rain Rain pits inside: made before the rain.
```

- Pits inside the print: made before the rain (70%).
- A clean print on pitted ground: made after (70%).

```more rain in full
- Rain pits inside a print mean it was made before the rain. A clean print on rain spattered ground is after the rain (70%, from [Tracks](#/s/tracks/0)).
- Heavy rain softens and fills prints, so old tracks look days older than they are (Tip).
- Rain also makes blood look lighter than it was (65%, Bowhunter). See [Blood, hair and tissue](#/s/rs-blood-hair-tissue/0).
- Know when it last rained. Write the time in your notebook or check the Environment Canada history for Kamloops when you get home (Tip).
```

## The frost and snow clocks

```diagram sg-snow-age
```

- Frost crystals inside: made before the frost (65%).
- Print cut through frost: made after it, often this morning (65%).

```more frost and snow in full
- Frost crystals grown inside a print mean it was made before the frost, usually last night or earlier (65%).
- A print that cuts through the frost crust was made after the frost formed (65%).
- Fresh snow inside a track: made before or during the snowfall. If it stopped snowing 4 hours ago and there is snow in the track, it is at least 4 hours old (60%, hunter source via search preview).
- No new snow in the track after an overnight snowfall: made after the snow stopped (60%).
- A frozen or icy floor usually means older, made before the freeze (60%).
- Sun melts snow prints out bigger and rounder over days (65%). Measure with care: [Measuring tracks](#/s/rs-track-measuring/0).
- Full snow method: [Snow tracking](#/s/snow-tracking/0) and [Sign in snow, mud, sand and dust](#/s/rs-sign-substrates/0).
```

## The dew clock

```diagram rs-ag-dew
```

- A dark line through silver dewy grass: made after the dew formed (Tip).
- Dew inside a print in bare ground: made before dawn (Tip).

```more dew in full
- In the morning, "look for signs of dew in the tracks to determine whether the tracks were made this morning or last night" (65%, Nature Mentor via search preview).
- "Dew knocked off vegetation" is a classic sign of passage (65%, tracking glossary).
- Dew forms on clear calm nights. A dew trail you can see from 50 m at sunrise often leads to a feeding or bedding spot (Tip).
- Glass dewy meadows at first light from above. The dark lines show where deer walked off the field (Tip). See [Glassing](#/s/glassing/0).
```

## Debris, insects and webs

| Clue | Means | Certainty |
|---|---|---|
| Leaves or needles fell in | Time passed, often a day plus | Tip |
| Insect trails across the floor | At least a night | Tip |
| Spider web over the print | At least hours, often a night | Tip |
| Wind blown sand or snow in it | It survived a wind | 65% |

- Debris inside means time has passed (70%).

```more debris in full
- Leaves, needles, insect trails or spider web inside a print mean time has passed (70%, from [Tracks](#/s/tracks/0)).
- "Broken cobwebs" across a trail are sign of passage (65%, tracking glossary). An intact web across a trail: nothing walked here since the spider spun it (Tip).
- Fall leaf drop covers tracks fast in aspen and cottonwood bottoms in October (65%, Original Wisdom).
- Wind leaves its own mark: drifted sand or snow on the downwind side of the print (Tip). Check when the last wind blew.
```

## Broken twigs and crushed plants

```diagram rs-ag-twig
```

- Fresh break: pale, moist, maybe sap. Old: grey and dry (Tip).
- Crushed grass slowly stands back up (65%).

```more plants in full
- "Healing" is the term trackers use for crushed live vegetation repairing itself, and it is used to age sign (65%, tracking glossary).
- Crushed or bruised vegetation, mud spattered on clean leaves, or broken sticks reveal passage (65%, Outdoor Life via search preview).
- A turned leaf shows its paler underside until it dries or flips back (Tip).
- Fresh browse: torn end pale green or white and moist. Old browse: grey and dry (Tip, from [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0)).
- Stones kicked out of their beds show a damp, darker underside that dries in hours in sun (Tip).
```

## Ground sets the clock

```diagram rs-ag-substrate
```

- Mud is the slowest ager: it can look fresh for days (65%).
- Sand and dust age fast at the edges (65%).

```more each ground in full
- Mud "is typically the slowest aging substrate", so tracks look fresh long after the animal has gone (65%, Nature Mentor via search preview).
- Sand: "its edges dry out fast", so tracks are "highly susceptible to drying out in sun, wind and rain" (65%, Point Reyes Light via search preview).
- Dust: shows fine detail but holds it only until the next breath of wind (60%).
- Forest duff and leaves: hardest to age. Look for crushed moss and flipped leaves instead of edges (Tip).
- In warm weather "a fresh track is hard to tell from one that is several hours old" (60%, hunter source via search preview).
- Shaded north banks stay cold and keep tracks crisp for days (65%). Cold still nights in the Interior do the same (65%).
```

## Aging scat

```diagram sg-scat-age
```

- Wet and shiny: hours. Dry crust: a day or two. Faded, mould: weeks (60%).
- Colour depends on diet. Read moisture and shine (Tip).

```more scat aging in full
- Hours: wet, shiny, strong smell, flies (60%).
- A day or two: dull, dry crust, soft inside (60%).
- Days to a week: dry, lighter, cracked (65%).
- Weeks: faded, crumbling, moss or mould, insect holes (60%).
- North Dakota Game and Fish notes deer scat "can be light brown when deposited but turn dark brown a day or two later" (80%). Other hunting sources say fresh pellets are dark and glossy (60%). Colour changes with diet, so do not age by colour alone (Tip).
- Cold slows everything. Frozen pellets can look fresh for a week (Tip).
- Break one pellet with a stick: moist and green inside is recent; dry and fibrous throughout is older (Tip).
- Full species guide: [Droppings deep dive](#/s/rs-droppings/0).
```

## Aging beds, rubs and scrapes

| Sign | Fresh | Old |
|---|---|---|
| Bed | Warm, flat, hair, frost free floor (Tip) | Grass standing back up (Tip) |
| Rub | Wet wood, curled bark, sap (65%) | Grey, dry, edges darkened (65%) |
| Scrape | Dark damp soil, urine smell (Tip) | Leaves blown back in (Tip) |
| Browse | Pale moist end (Tip) | Grey dry end (Tip) |

- Mixed ages in one place: they live here (60%).

```more sign aging in full
- In snow a used bed melts and refreezes into an icy bowl. A dry crisp floor with no refreeze is recent (65%, from [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0)).
- Fresh rub: bright wet wood, shredded bark curls on the ground, sap beading on conifers (Tip). Rubs grey and darken over weeks (65%).
- A scrape that has been reopened shows fresh pawed soil over older leaf cover (Tip). Deep dive: [Rubs and scrapes](#/s/rs-rubs-scrapes/0).
- Beds deep dive: [Beds and bedding areas](#/s/rs-beds/0).
```

## Know your last 72 hours

```diagram rs-ag-72h
```

- Every print survived some weather and not other weather (Tip).
- Note rain, frost, wind and snow times each day of your trip (Tip).

```more how to run a weather log
- Write it in the [Journal](#/s/hunt-journal-guide/0): time of last rain, last frost, last wind, last snow.
- Ask: did this print go through the frost? The wind? The rain? Each "yes" pushes its age back past that event (Tip).
- Night temperature matters most in the Interior: a hard frost at Heffley Creek in late October can hold a print crisp into the afternoon on a north slope (Tip).
- See [Weather, moon and seasons](#/s/weather-moon/0) for reading fronts.
```

## Fresh enough to hunt?

```diagram rs-ag-decide
```

- Under 24 hours and the wind is right: hunt it at dawn or dusk (Tip).
- Older: log it, keep scouting for fresher (Tip).

```more the decision in full
- Fresh sign beats a good map (Grandpa's rule from [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0)).
- Pick the freshest sign on your walk and hunt within 300 m of it at dawn or dusk (Tip).
- Lots of mixed age sign in one place means the animals live there; they did not just pass through (60%, hunter lore).
- Very fresh sign (minutes): stop moving. The animal may be watching you. Glass ahead before you take another step (Tip). See [Following a track](#/s/rs-track-following/0).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```photo hab-deer-trail-snow Old sign is history. Fresh sign is news.
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom, not law): Old sign is history. Fresh sign is news. Hunt the news.

> [!mistake]
> - Calling a crisp mud print fresh. Mud keeps a fresh look for days.
> - Aging by scat colour alone. Diet changes colour.
> - Forgetting last night's frost and rain, so every print looks the same age.
