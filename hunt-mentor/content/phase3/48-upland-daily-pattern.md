- Feed early and late, hide midday, roost at dusk: the common pattern (75%).
- Each bird does it on different ground (75 to 85%).

```more what this session covers
- Four birds you will hunt: California quail (South Okanagan), chukar (Thompson and Okanagan rock), ruffed grouse (aspen and creeks near Heffley Creek), dusky and spruce grouse (higher forest). Pheasant as a bonus (Okanagan).
- ID and limits: [Grouse](#/s/grouse/0), [Quail, chukar, partridge and pheasant](#/s/upland-birds/0). Places: [Habitat tour: quail and grouse](#/s/habitat-upland/0).
```

## Quail: never far from brush

```diagram rq-up-quail-cover
```

- Seldom more than 15 to 30 m from cover to feed (75%).
- Roost in dense trees or shrubs (75%). Need water in hot weather (75%).

```more California quail in full
- California Department of Fish and Wildlife life history (Ahlborn), read directly: "Yearlong, diurnal activity." "Seldom ventures more than 15-30 m (50-100 ft) from cover to feed." Brush and trees "provide cover for feeding, escape, movement, and roosting." (75%, quoted with its hyphens)
- Water: "In hot weather, requires free water daily." "Gathers near water in dry weather, and disperses when green vegetation and water are abundant." (75%)
- Home range: winter coveys averaged 10.5 ha (6.9 to 18 ha); "Not migratory" (75%).
- BC: Okanagan and Kettle valleys; below 750 m, most at 250 to 500 m; Ponderosa Pine and Bunchgrass zones (85%, BC Breeding Bird Atlas).
- Most valley bottom quail ground is private or reserve land: ask first (75%). See [Local plan: Okanagan](#/s/local-okanagan/0).
```

## Quail by the clock

| Time | Where (pattern) |
|---|---|
| First light to mid morning | Feeding in openings next to brush (70%) |
| Midday | In brush or shade, near water in heat (75%) |
| Late afternoon | Feeding again near cover (70%) |
| Dusk | Regroup, then roost in dense trees (70%) |

- Walk the brush edges early and late; work the thick cover midday (Tip).

```more sources for the clock
- Sunrise to mid morning is the best time to see feeding quail; late afternoon, walk feeding areas near water (65%, hunting guide, search preview).
- Fall and winter coveys of about 25 to 60 birds feed and roost together and reassemble before roosting (65%, wildlife summaries, search preview).
- One bird often acts as a lookout while the rest feed (60%, same).
- Video: watch a covey feed.
```

## Chukar: steep, rocky, water below

```diagram rq-up-chukar
```

- Feed on slopes in the morning, shade and water midday (70%).
- Back up to steep roosts late in the afternoon (70%).

```more chukar in full
- Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife, read directly: "Chukars often feed throughout the morning"; they move "to shady slopes and draws, dusting sites and water holes during mid-day"; "They'll usually begin moving back toward steeper roosting areas late in the afternoon." (70%, quoted with its hyphen)
- Cool nights: they may roost on "the warmer south-facing slopes", so check those first at dawn (70%).
- Habitat: "cliffs, bluffs, canyon walls, talus slopes" (70%).
- BC: Okanagan and Thompson Nicola, "nowhere common"; mostly 250 to 500 m, some to 1,250 m; rocky slopes with bluebunch wheatgrass, sagebrush, rabbitbrush (85%, BC Breeding Bird Atlas).
- Heavy snow pushes them lower and onto south slopes (75%, USDA Fire Effects Information System).
```

```photo g-chukar Chukar: barred flanks, black necklace, red bill.
```

## Chukar run uphill

```anim rq-up-chukar-anim From below they run up. Start above.
```

- "They usually run uphill" (70%).
- Get above them and hunt down (70%).

```more why and how
- WDFW (Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife): "they usually run uphill, and they're about as likely to run as they are to fly." "Whenever possible, approach chukars from above." (70%)
- Climb the side away from the birds, then sidehill along the top third (Tip).
- Steep rock and loose talus are a fall risk. Footing first, then the shot (Tip). Never shoot when you cannot see your footing or the ground beyond the bird (Tip). See [Shotgun skills](#/s/shotgun-skills/0).
```

## Ruffed grouse: aspen and creek bottoms

```diagram rq-up-grouse-band
```

- Ruffed grouse are most common at 750 to 1,250 m in BC (85%).
- Feed and pick grit early and late; sit in cover midday (75%).

```more ruffed grouse in full
- BC atlas: highest abundance 750 to 1,250 m; recorded 5 to 1,697 m (85%).
- App grouse session: feed early morning and late afternoon; dust and gravel on roadsides; cover midday; roost in trees or on the ground at night (75%).
- Hunting guidance: birds move out of roosts early and feed again in late afternoon, pecking grit on sandy lanes and dirt road edges (65%, search preview). Note: a diet study warned that birds collected along gravel roads may bias what we think they eat (65%).
- Fall foods: rose hips, willow buds, clover, hawthorn, chokecherry, saskatoon, red osier dogwood (65%). Winter: aspen and willow buds were 89% by weight of crops in an Alaska study (65%).
- Deep soft snow: they dive in to roost (80%).
- Adults stay in a small area; young birds disperse in fall (70%).
```

## Dusky and spruce grouse: high forest

```gallery
dusky-grouse-male | Dusky grouse male: high conifer ridges in fall.
spruce-grouse-hen | Spruce grouse: dark conifers, eats needles.
```

- Dusky grouse move uphill into dense conifers in fall (60 to 70%).
- Spruce grouse live in pine and spruce and eat needles (85%).

```more details
- Dusky grouse: in BC many breeding records at 1,500 to 2,250 m, some at 500 to 1,250 m (85%). In fall most move "from fairly open breeding areas to dense coniferous forest", in most places uphill (70%, Audubon). Winter food: conifer needles (70%).
- Spruce grouse: conifer specialist, needles of pine or spruce; recorded 293 to 1,738 m in BC (85%).
- Spruce grouse are famously tame (Tip). Shoot only on the ground or a branch if your ethics allow it, and only with a safe backstop (Tip).
- Region 3 grouse: dusky, ruffed and spruce, 10 September to 30 November, 5 of each a day, 10 in total (99%, synopsis page 34).
```

## Pheasant: the farm bird's day

```photo g-pheasant-rooster Pheasant rooster: only cocks are legal in BC seasons.
```

- First light: grit on roadsides. Then feed. Midday: thickest cover (70%).
- Late afternoon: back to feed, then roost in grass (70%).

```more the Minnesota schedule and BC notes
- Minnesota DNR (Department of Natural Resources), read directly: "Pheasants start their day before sunrise at roost sites, usually in areas of short- to medium-height grass or weeds"; "At first light, pheasants head for roadsides or similar areas where they can find gravel or grit"; "Pheasants usually begin feeding around 8 a.m."; "By mid-morning, pheasants have left the fields for the densest, thickest cover they can find"; "During the late afternoon, the birds move from their loafing spots back to the feeding areas." (70% applied to BC, quoted with its hyphens)
- Worse weather pushes birds deeper into cover (70%).
- BC: Okanagan and Georgia Depression, chiefly below 500 m, gone along the Thompson River (85%, BC atlas).
- Region 3 pheasant cocks: 1 October to 30 November, 2 a day (99%, page 34). Region 8: 2 a day, 6 in possession (99%).
```

## Sign that birds use a spot

```diagram rq-up-sign
```

- Dust bowls, droppings, tracks, feathers (80 to 85% for grouse).
- Fresh sign plus the right hour equals birds (Tip).

```more more on sign
- Grouse droppings: curved cylinders about pencil thick with a white cap, piled under roosts or on a drum log (85%).
- Dust bowls: shallow bowls in dry soil, often with a feather or two (80%).
- Chukar droppings pile under roost rocks and on ledges (Tip).
- Tracks and wing marks in snow: see [Snow tracking](#/s/snow-tracking/0) and [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0).
```

## Plan an upland day by the clock

```steps Hunt by the clock in 5 steps
rq-up-step-1 | First hours: feeding edges.
rq-up-step-2 | Midday: cover, shade, water.
rq-up-step-3 | Steep country: start high.
rq-up-step-4 | Late afternoon: feeding again.
rq-up-step-5 | Write every flush down.
```

- Same ground, different hour, different spot (Tip).

```more the plan in full (Tip)
1. First hours (from 1 hour before sunrise, legal for grouse, quail, chukar and pheasant: 99%): sunny feeding edges, grit on road edges, brush next to seed.
2. Midday: thick cover, shade, water. Go slow, stop often; birds hold tight (Tip).
3. Chukar: climb above first (70%).
4. Last 2 hours before dark: feeding edges again (75%).
5. Journal: time, place, cover, weather, how the birds flushed. After 5 trips you will have your own clock (Tip).
```

## Common mistakes

```diagram rq-up-chukar
```

> [!mistake]
> - Walking open feeding ground at noon and finding nothing.
> - Chasing chukar uphill from the bottom.
> - Knocking on no doors in quail country.

```more why each one hurts
- Midday birds sit in cover, shade or near water (70 to 75%).
- Chukar run uphill (70%). You end up below them, winded, out of range (Tip).
- Most quail ground is private or reserve land (75%). Permission first (99% rule for cultivated land and reserves).
```
