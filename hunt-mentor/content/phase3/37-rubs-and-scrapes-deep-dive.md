> [!why]
> Rubs and scrapes are bucks writing notes to other deer. Learn to read the notes and you learn the buck's route and schedule.

- Rubs show where a buck travels. Scrapes show where deer meet (65%).
- Most scrape visits happen at night (65%).


## The rub and scrape season

```diagram rs-rb-year
```

- Velvet off, then rubbing, then scrapes peak before the rut (70%).
- Bars are approximate and shift with latitude (Tip).

```more timing in full
- About 80% of scraping in the north happens from mid October to mid November (65%, from [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0)).
- In a Georgia study, bucks 2.5 years and older visited scrapes most in the week of 15 to 21 October (65%, Buckmasters reporting on University of Georgia research). Georgia is far south of BC; timing here may differ (Tip).
- BC deer rut timing: [The rut](#/s/the-rut/0) has the Region 3 timeline.
- Rubs stay visible for years. A fresh rub in October is this year's buck (Tip).
```

## Why bucks rub

```diagram rs-rb-why
```

- Forehead glands are most active in autumn (70%).
- Higher ranking mature bucks secrete more (70%).

```more the science in full
- Research by Atkeson and Marchinton found white tails have forehead skin glands that are most active in autumn, and mature, high ranking bucks tend to secrete more from them (70%, Deer and Deer Hunting, The Science of Buck Rubs, via search preview).
- Rub activity tracks testosterone, which rises with age and rank. Prime age bucks reach higher levels and make more rubs (65%, same source).
- "Yearlings make only about half as many total rubs as prime age whitetails", even with no mature bucks around (65%, same source).
- Bucks also rub a licking branch above a scrape with the gland near the eye (preorbital gland) (80%, North Dakota Game and Fish).
- Other deer smell and lick rubs. A rub is a sign and a scent post (Tip).
```

## Read one rub in 4 steps

```steps Read a rub in 4 steps
rs-rb-1 | Measure how high it goes from the ground.
rs-rb-2 | Measure the tree diameter.
rs-rb-3 | Fresh or old? Wet wood or grey.
rs-rb-4 | Which side is rubbed? That is where he came from.
```

- Photo every rub with your knife for scale, and pin it (Tip).

```more the 4 steps in full
1. **Height.** Most rubs sit about 30 to 105 cm above the ground. A rub centred 75 cm or higher suggests a mature buck (60%).
2. **Tree size.** 2.5 to 5 cm trees are often yearlings; over 10 cm is usually an older buck (60%). Rub height says more about buck size than tree size (60%).
3. **Fresh or old.** Fresh: bright wet wood, curled bark shavings on the ground below, sap on conifers (Tip). Old: grey, dry, darkened edges (65%). See [Aging tracks and sign](#/s/rs-track-aging/0).
4. **Side.** The rubbed side usually faces where the buck came from (60%).
- Tine scratches above the main rub and broken branches hint at a wide or heavy rack (Tip).
```

## A rub line is a road

```anim rs-rb-line Rubbed sides face where he came from.
```

- A line of rubs facing one way: his route between bed and feed (60%).
- Rubbed side toward the beds: he walks that way in the evening (Tip).

```more using rub lines
- A line of rubs facing one way is his route between bed and feed (60%, from [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0)).
- Rubs facing the feed: made on the morning walk back to bed. Rubs facing the beds: made in the evening heading out (Tip).
- Follow the line backward toward thick cover to find the bedding area, once, with the wind right (Tip). See [Beds and bedding areas](#/s/rs-beds/0).
- Rub lines through a funnel or saddle are prime ambush spots (Tip). See [Trails and funnels](#/s/funnels/0).
```

## Signpost rubs

```diagram rs-rb-signpost
```

- A big tree rubbed year after year (60%).
- Mature bucks keep them going; other deer visit (60%).

```more signposts in full
- "Typically, especially during the rut, mature bucks make most signposts to advertise their presence" (60%, Bowhunter via search preview).
- "The oldest bucks typically make the largest rubs, whereas younger bucks and females may visit and interact with the rub, but rarely maintain it" (60%, Outdoor Nebraska via search preview).
- Older bucks re rub traditional rubs year after year (60%, Buckmasters on research by Grant Woods, via search preview).
- A signpost rub means a buck uses this area every year. It does not mean he walks past at noon (Tip).
```

## Whose rub is it?

```diagram rs-rb-species
```

- Deer: knee to waist. Elk: over your head. Moose: thrashed brush (65%).

```more species in full
| Animal | Rub | Other sign |
|---|---|---|
| White tailed deer | Many rubs, saplings, 30 to 105 cm (65%) | Scrapes, rub lines |
| Mule deer | Fewer rubs, small conifers and shrubs (60%) | Rarely scrape (60%) |
| Elk | Bigger trees stripped, often above your head (Tip) | Wallows, bugling (65%) |
| Moose | Thrash willow and alder, strip saplings (65%) | Rut pits soaked in urine (65%) |

- Bulls thrash shrubs and small trees with their antlers many times an hour in the rut (65%, from [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0)).
- Porcupines also strip bark high in trees (85%, North Dakota Game and Fish). No antler gouges, tooth marks instead (Tip).
- Bears claw and bite trees too. Look for claw marks and hair (Tip). See [Predator sign](#/s/rs-predator-sign/0).
```

## A scrape, side view

```diagram rs-sc-anatomy
```

- Pawed bare soil under a licking branch 1.2 to 1.5 m up (65%).
- The buck chews the branch and urinates in the scrape (70%).

```more scrape anatomy in full
- A [[Scrape]] is a pawed bare oval of dirt under an overhanging licking branch about 1.2 to 1.5 m up (65%, from [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0)).
- "Scrapes are created by bucks in the early fall as a place to leave their scent so other deer know they are in the area. Scrapes are most often made below overhanging branches. To leave scent, a buck will rub on an overhanging branch with a scent gland near the eye called the preorbital gland" (85%, North Dakota Game and Fish).
- Fresh scrape: dark moist soil, pawed leaves thrown back, urine smell, chewed and broken branch tip (Tip).
- White tails scrape; mule deer rarely do (60%).
```

## Who visits scrapes, and when

```diagram rs-sc-visits
```

- 85% of buck visits and 75% of doe visits at night (65%).
- Does visited nearly twice as often as bucks (65%).

```more the camera studies
- University of Georgia trail camera study (6 traditional scrapes, about 1,375 ha managed property, 2 years): 85% of buck visits and 75% of doe visits were at night (65%, Buckmasters and Outdoor Life via search preview).
- Reported as about 562 visits: 31 per scrape per season, only about 5 in daylight (65%).
- "Nearly twice as many does visited the scrapes as bucks" (65%).
- A Missouri study by Grant Woods also found most scrape visits at night; the top daylight windows were about 8:45 to 10:15 a.m. and 3:45 to 5:15 p.m. (60%).
- What it means: sitting over a field edge scrape all day is usually a poor bet (Tip).
```

## Edge scrapes or cover scrapes?

```diagram rs-sc-map
```

- Edge scrapes: mostly night visits (Tip).
- Scrapes near bedding cover: better odds of daylight (Tip).

```more why location matters
- Bucks feel safer close to cover in daylight. Scrapes deep in or near bedding cover get a higher share of daylight visits (Tip, consistent with the camera studies at 60%).
- Four of the six Georgia scrapes were on field edges and two deeper in the woods (65%).
- A row of scrapes along a trail between cover and feed is a scrape line. Hunt the cover end at first and last light (Tip).
```

## 3 ways to hunt rubs and scrapes

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| A: Ambush a rub line near cover | Daylight movement, simple | Needs the right wind every sit |
| B: Camera on a scrape first | Learn timing without disturbing | Wireless camera rules (see below) |
| C: Mock scrape with synthetic lure | Draws local bucks | Lots of night visits; lure rules |

- My lean: A, with a non wireless camera to learn timing (Tip).

```more options in detail
> [!lean]
> My lean: find a fresh rub line between bedding cover and feed. Sit near the cover end with the wind in your face at first and last light. Put a normal memory card camera on the nearest scrape to learn which buck and when.

- Enter and leave on routes that do not cross the rub line (Tip).
- Check cameras at midday, quickly, once a week, with gloves and rubber boots (Tip).
- Do not use urine lures. See the rules below.
```

## Rules that touch this session

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

| Rule | Where |
|---|---|
| No wireless camera for hunting, 1 August to 10 December (99%) | Synopsis page 11, item 30 |
| No deer, elk, moose or caribou part or derivative from outside BC for hunting (95%) | Synopsis page 12, item 51 |
| No baiting ungulates within 200 m of homes or schools, Region 3 (95%) | Synopsis page 33 |

```more the rules in full
- Province wide: it is unlawful to use a wireless camera for the purposes of hunting from 1 August to 10 December in every region except Region 1, where wireless cameras for hunting are banned all year (99%, synopsis page 11, item 30, and page 20). A normal camera with a memory card is not a wireless camera (85%, interpretation). From [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0).
- It is unlawful to use, for the purpose of hunting wildlife, any part or derivative of a deer, elk, moose or caribou that originated outside BC (95%, synopsis page 12, item 51). Bottled deer urine is almost always from outside BC (85%, interpretation). From [The rut](#/s/the-rut/0).
- Region 3: it is unlawful to intentionally bait or feed ungulates within 200 m of a dwelling, school yard or playground (95%, synopsis page 33).
- Full rules: [BC rules](#/s/bc-rules/0) and the Rule Book in phase 7.
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```photo hab-deer-rub Rubs say where. Scrapes say who. The clock says when.
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom, not law): A rub tells you where he walked. A scrape tells you where he wants to be. Your watch tells you he did it in the dark.

> [!mistake]
> - Sitting all day over a field edge scrape.
> - Calling a big tree rub a big buck without looking at the height.
> - Hanging a bottle of store deer urine over a mock scrape.
