## How to look at a track

```steps Read a track in 5 steps
sg-trk-1 | Count the toes: 2, 4 or 5.
sg-trk-2 | Look for claws. Dogs show them, cats do not.
sg-trk-3 | Measure one clear print, length and width.
sg-trk-4 | Measure the stride, same foot to same foot.
sg-trk-5 | Measure the straddle, then read the whole trail.
```

- Photograph every good track with a coin, knife or tape for scale (Tip).

```more the 6 steps in full
1. Count the toes. 2 toes: hoofed (deer, elk, moose). 4 toes: cat or dog family. 5 toes: bear (85%).
2. Look for claws. Dogs, wolves, coyotes and bears show claws. Cats usually do not (80%).
3. Measure length and width of one clear print. Front prints are usually bigger than hind on deer and bears (75%).
4. Measure the stride: same foot to same foot, for example left front to left front (75%).
5. Measure the straddle: the width of the whole trail from outer edge to outer edge (70%).
6. Look at the whole trail, not one print. Pattern and spacing tell you the gait and the mood (Tip).

> [!tip]
> Photograph every good track with something for scale: a coin, your knife, a tape. Note date, time, weather and place in the Journal.
```

## Hoofed: deer, elk, moose

```diagram sg-hoof-grid
```

- The pointed end points the way it went (85%).
- Mule deer and white tail prints overlap; the trail tells them apart (60%).

```more sizes, dewclaws, bucks and cattle
| Animal | Front track length | Shape | Tell |
|---|---|---|---|
| White tailed deer | about 4 to 10 cm (70%) | Heart, pointed | Often narrower, tips close together (60%) |
| Mule deer | about 6 to 10 cm (70%) | Heart, pointed | Slightly wider and blockier (60%) |
| Elk | about 10 to 13 cm long, 9 to 11 cm wide (70%) | Rounder, blunt tips | A big deer track with the point rubbed off (70%) |
| Moose | about 11 to 18 cm long, 10 to 15 cm wide (70%) | Long, pointed heart | A giant deer track, often with dewclaws (70%) |

- The pointed end of the heart points the way the animal went (85%).
- Dewclaws (the two small bumps behind the hoof) show in deep mud, snow, or when the animal runs (75%).
- Mule deer and white tail tracks overlap too much to tell apart from one print (60%). Use the trail: a stotting trail is a mule deer (85%).
- A buck track is often bigger, rounder at the tips and deeper, with a wider straddle, but a big doe fools people (hunter lore) (60%).
- Cattle tracks are round, blunt and wide, and in Region 3 grassland they are everywhere. A cow print has no pointed tip (75%).
```

## Paws: bears

```diagram sg-bear-toeline
```

- Toes in a straight row, long claws far ahead: grizzly (70%).
- Grizzly track on your trail: change your plan (Tip).

> [!warn]
> Grizzly until proven otherwise: make noise, spray on your belt, do not follow.

```more black bear and grizzly sizes
- A bear track with long claw marks and toes in a straight row is a grizzly until proven otherwise. Make noise, keep bear spray on your belt, and do not follow it.
- Black bear: 5 toes in a curved row, short claws (about 3 to 5 cm) that print as dots close to the toes (70%).
- Black bear front track about 10 to 16 cm long and 10 to 14 cm wide. Hind track about 15 to 18 cm long, like a flat human foot (70%).
- Grizzly: front track about 18 cm long and 13 cm wide. Hind about 28 cm long. Claw marks sit well ahead of the toes (65%).
- The toe line test: lay a straight edge across the top of the palm pad, under the toes. Black bear: the outer, smallest toe falls below the line. Grizzly: all 5 toes sit above it (70%).
- Grizzlies live in the Thompson. A fresh grizzly track on your trail means change your plan (Tip). See [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety).
```

## Paws: cougar, wolf, coyote

```diagram sg-paw-grid
```

- No claws and a 3 lobe heel: cougar (75%).
- Coyote is about half a wolf (70%).
- Cougar tracks mean deer country (Tip).

```more the full table and dog or wild canid
| Animal | Size | Toes | Claws | Heel pad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cougar | round, about 7.5 to 10 cm wide (75%) | 4, uneven, one leads like a hand (75%) | none showing (75%) | big, 3 lobes on the back edge (75%) |
| Wolf | oval, about 9 to 11.5 cm long, some to 13 cm (70%) | 4, neat, two front toes together (70%) | show (80%) | small, triangular (70%) |
| Coyote | oval, about 6 to 7 cm long, 4.5 to 6 cm wide (70%) | 4, neat (70%) | show (80%) | small, triangular (70%) |

- A coyote track is about half the size of a wolf track (70%). A wolf track outsizes almost any dog (70%).
- Dog or wild canid? A dog trail wanders, toes splay, and the track is rounder. A wolf or coyote walks a straight, purposeful line with toes tight (tracker lore) (60%).
- A cougar track is as wide as it is long, shows no claws, and the heel pad is bigger than the 4 toes together. A dog's pad is about the size of 3 toes (70%).
- Cougar is the top killer of mule deer in the southern Interior (85%). Where you find cougar tracks, you have found deer country (Tip).
```

## Small: snowshoe hare and grouse

```diagram sg-hare-grouse
```

- Hare: a Y of prints, big hind feet in front (75%).
- Grouse: 3 toes forward, 1 back, in one line (65%).

```more hare and grouse details
- Snowshoe hare: hind track long and triangular, up to about 12 cm, in winter up to 15 cm with the fur spread. Front track round, about 4.5 cm (70%).
- Hare pattern: two big hind prints side by side, **ahead** of two smaller front prints. The group looks like a letter Y pointing the way it went (75%).
- Hare runways in brush are 10 to 15 cm wide and packed down in snow (65%).
- Grouse: 3 toes forward, 1 short toe back, about 5 to 7 cm long (65%). The bird walks in a line, often zig zag, and you may find wing marks in snow where it flushed (65%).
- In winter grouse grow comb like fringes on their toes, so the snow track looks wider and fuzzy (65%).
```

## Gaits: what the pattern tells you

```steps Watch the 3 gaits
anim:sg-gait-walk | Walk: zig zag, relaxed.
anim:sg-gait-trot | Trot: narrow line, on a mission.
anim:sg-gait-stot | Stot: groups of 4. It saw you.
```

- The pattern tells you the mood.
- Stot groups from a bed: you bumped that deer (Tip).

```more all 5 gaits and the stot tip
- **Walk:** the everyday gait. A zig zag line. The hind foot lands on or just behind the front print on the same side (75%). A relaxed animal, feeding or travelling (Tip).
- **Trot:** longer stride, narrower trail, prints nearly in one line. Hind lands on or past the front (70%). Moving with purpose, not panicked (65%).
- **Bound:** hind feet land side by side ahead of the front feet. Hares and squirrels live in this gait (75%).
- **Stot:** the mule deer bounce. All 4 feet land close together in a group, then a gap of about 2.4 to 4.6 m to the next group (60%). This deer saw or smelled you (85%).
- **Gallop:** groups of 4 prints in a line with the hind prints beyond the front, long gaps between groups (70%). White tails, elk and moose run this way (70%).
- Speed shows in stride. A walking deer step is roughly 40 to 60 cm; a running deer covers metres between groups (65%).

> [!tip]
> Stot groups leading away from a bed on a ridge mean you bumped that deer within the last hour. Mark the bed on your map. Mule deer come back to good beds (hunter lore).
```

## Aging a track

```diagram sg-track-age
```

- Crisp walls: hours. Rounded: days. Debris inside: a week (Tip).
- Cold shaded ground keeps tracks crisp for days (65%).

```more every aging clue
- **Edges:** fresh prints have crisp, sharp walls. Sun, wind and rain round them off (75%).
- **Debris:** leaves, needles, insect trails or spider web inside the print mean time has passed (70%).
- **Frost:** frost crystals grown inside the print mean it was made before the frost, usually last night or earlier (65%). A print pressed through the frost crust was made after the frost, this morning (65%).
- **Rain:** rain pits inside the print mean it was made before the rain. A clean print on rain spattered ground is after the rain (70%).
- **Dust and mud:** in fine dust a fresh print has a thin raised rim that collapses within hours (60%). In mud, fresh means the walls are still shiny and wet (Tip).
- **Snow:** see [Snow tracking](#/s/snow-tracking). Cold still nights in the Interior keep a track crisp for days, so crisp does not always mean fresh (65%).
- Best test: make your own boot print right beside it and compare (Tip).
```

## Direction of travel

```diagram sg-track-direction
```

- Hoof: the point leads (85%). Paw: toes and claws lead (80%).
- Loose soil or snow piles up at the back (65%).

```more more direction clues
- Hoofed animals: the pointed end leads (85%). Toes dig deeper than the heel when climbing or pushing off (65%).
- Paws: the toes point the way and the claws lead (80%).
- Pushed material: the animal shoves soil or snow **backward** on push off, so a lip of loose material sits at the rear of the print (65%).
- Snow: drag marks run into the print from behind. A small spray of kicked snow lies ahead of it (65%).
- Bent grass and bent twigs lie in the direction the animal moved (65%).
- Lost the line? Walk a slow circle 10 m out around the last clear print and find where the trail leaves the circle (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```photo hab-deer-trail-snow One track is a guess. A trail is a story.
```

> [!rule]
> One track is a guess. Three tracks are a direction. Ten tracks are a story. Read the story.

> [!mistake]
> - Calling every big heart shaped track a buck. Measure it, then look at the trail.
> - Judging age by one print on a shaded north bank, where tracks stay crisp for days.
> - Following a fresh grizzly track just to see.
