> [!why]
> BC is split into habitat zones by climate. Know the zone and you know the trees, the food, the snow and the game before you take a step.

```diagram rp-bec-ladder
```

- Interior zones stack up the hill like floors in a building (85%).
- Each floor has its own trees, food and game.
- Learn 6 Interior floors and 1 coast floor. That covers your hunts.

```more what this session covers
- BEC (biogeoclimatic ecosystem classification) is how BC sorts land by climate, plants and soil (90%). Bio = living things, geo = ground, climatic = climate.
- BC uses 14 zones in the classic book (Meidinger and Pojar 1991) (95%). The current provincial map legend shows 16 units because it splits the alpine into 3 kinds (80%, search preview).
- Zones are named after the main tree of mature forest on an average site (85%).
- This session: the zones you will hunt, how to read a zone code, why the coast is wet and the Interior dry, your bases by zone, and how to tell the zone in the field.
- The next sessions go deep on each kind of country: [Grasslands](#/s/grassland-country/0), [Dry forests](#/s/dry-forest-country/0), [Wet forests](#/s/wet-forest-country/0), [Wetlands](#/s/wetland-country/0).
- Assumption: elevation numbers are the ranges given for each whole zone. Your local slope can differ by a few hundred metres.
```

## The zones a hunter meets

| Code | Zone | Your game |
|---|---|---|
| BG | Bunchgrass | mule deer, elk, sheep, quail |
| PP | Ponderosa pine | mule and white tailed deer, quail |
| IDF | Interior Douglas fir | mule deer, elk, grouse |
| MS | Montane spruce | moose, mule deer in fall |
| ESSF | Engelmann spruce and subalpine fir | moose, goat, grizzly |
| ICH | Interior cedar and hemlock | moose, white tails, bears |
| CWH | Coastal Western Hemlock | black tailed deer, bears |

- Codes are just the zone name shortened (95%).
- Game lists come from the BC government zone book (80%).

```more where each zone is and what lives there
| Zone | Where | Elevation | Main trees | Wildlife named in the source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BG (Bunchgrass) | Okanagan to Summerland, Similkameen near Keremeos, Thompson from Spences Bridge to Kamloops, Nicola, middle Fraser (95%) | valley floor to about 700 to 1,000 m (95%) | none on open sites; pine and fir in draws (95%) | elk, coyote, bighorn sheep, sharp tailed grouse, mule and white tailed deer in shrub steppe (90%) |
| PP (Ponderosa pine) | thin band in the driest valleys: Fraser near Lytton and Lillooet, lower Thompson, Nicola, Similkameen, Okanagan, Kettle (95%) | 335 to 900 m (95%) | ponderosa pine, Douglas fir in draws (95%) | elk, mule deer, white tailed deer, cougar, black bear, quail, pheasant, chukar (90%) |
| IDF (Interior Douglas fir) | low to mid slopes of the south central Interior (95%) | low edge 350 to 600 m, top 900 to 1,450 m (95%) | Douglas fir, lodgepole pine higher up (95%) | mule deer, white tailed deer, elk, moose in young forest, ruffed and dusky grouse (90%) |
| MS (Montane spruce) | mid elevations from the Fraser Plateau to the US border (95%) | 1,100 to 1,500 m wet parts, 1,250 to 1,700 m dry parts (95%) | hybrid spruce, subalpine fir, lots of lodgepole pine after old fires (95%) | moose and mule deer summer and fall range (95%) |
| ESSF (Engelmann spruce and subalpine fir) | top forest zone of the southern Interior (95%) | 1,200 to 2,100 m southwest, 1,500 to 2,300 m southeast (95%) | Engelmann spruce, subalpine fir (95%) | moose, mountain goat, mule deer; one of the best grizzly zones (95%) |
| ICH (Interior cedar and hemlock) | Interior Wet Belt: Columbia Mountains, Shuswap and Quesnel highlands (95%) | 400 to 1,500 m (95%) | western redcedar, western hemlock (95%) | moose, elk, mule and white tailed deer, black bear, grizzly (90%) |
| CWH (Coastal Western Hemlock) | whole coast, up the Fraser valley (95%) | sea level to about 900 m on wet slopes in the south (95%) | western hemlock, western redcedar, coast Douglas fir (95%) | black tailed deer, black bear, grizzly, wolf, mountain goat on the mainland (95%) |

- Other zones you may pass: CDF (Coastal Douglas fir) on southeast Vancouver Island and the Gulf Islands, below about 150 m (95%). MH (Mountain Hemlock) is the coast subalpine above the CWH (95%). SBPS and SBS (sub boreal) cover the Chilcotin and central Interior (95%). AT (Alpine tundra) sits above the trees (95%).
- Source: Meidinger and Pojar, Ecosystems of British Columbia, 1991, chapters 5 to 18; wildlife lists adapted from the Wildlife Branch 1989 tables. Old but still the base description (85% to 95%).
```

## Read a zone code

```diagram rp-bec-code
```

- Zone letters, then 2 small letters, then a number (95%).
- First small letter: how wet. Second: how warm (90%).
- IDFxh2 means Interior Douglas fir, very dry, hot (95%).

```more the letter key in full
- Zone code: capitals, for example IDF (Interior Douglas fir) (95%).
- Subzone: 2 small letters (90%).
  - First letter, how much rain and snow: x very dry, d dry, m moist, w wet, v very wet (80%, BC Species and Ecosystems Explorer, search preview).
  - Second letter in the Interior, how warm: h hot, w warm, m mild, k cool, c cold (90%, read off the names in the BC zone map data: PPxh is Very Dry Hot, IDFdk is Dry Cool, IDFmw is Moist Warm, MSdm is Dry Mild, ESSFdc is Dry Cold).
  - On the coast the second letter tells how maritime the climate is. CWHdm is Dry Maritime, CWHvm is Very Wet Maritime (90%, BC zone map data).
- Variant: a number for a local version of the subzone, for example the 2 in IDFxh2 (90%).
- Why care: a dry hot subzone in late October means open slopes, little snow and deer that need water. A wet cold one means deep snow early (Tip).
```

## Why the coast is wet and the Interior dry

```anim rp-rainshadow Clouds drop their rain on the coast side of the mountains.
```

- Wet Pacific air hits the Coast Mountains and drops its rain (90%).
- The Interior sits in the rain shadow behind them (95%).
- A station near Mission gets about 9 times the rain of Kamloops (85%).

```more rain shadow details and numbers
- The rain shadow of the Coast Mountains makes the southern Interior Plateau dry. It is driest in the deepest valleys, so grassland wins there (95%, Bunchgrass zone chapter).
- Representative stations (Meidinger and Pojar 1991, Table 4) (85%):
  - Haney, UBC (University of British Columbia) Research Forest, Maple Ridge, CWH: 2,140 mm a year.
  - Kamloops, BG: 242 mm a year.
  - 2,140 divided by 242 is about 8.8. Call it 9 times.
- The CWH is on average the rainiest zone in BC, about 2,228 mm a year, ranging 1,000 to 4,400 mm (95%).
- The Bunchgrass zone gets most of its moisture in winter. Plants dry out as summer goes on (95%). So by October, green feed in the Thompson is in draws, north slopes and near water (Tip; see [Food, water and cover](#/s/food-water-cover/0)).
```

## Rain by zone

```diagram rp-bec-rain-bars
```

- Coast forest: wettest. Grassland: driest (85%).
- Wet zones grow thick brush. Dry zones are open and easy to glass (Tip).

```more the station table
| Zone | Station | Rain and snow a year | Coldest month mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| CWH (Coastal Western Hemlock) | Haney, Maple Ridge | 2,140 mm | 1.4°C |
| ESSF (Engelmann spruce and subalpine fir) | Boss Mountain | 1,177 mm | minus 10.1°C |
| ICH (Interior cedar and hemlock) | Revelstoke | 1,064 mm | minus 6.1°C |
| MS (Montane spruce) | Brenda Mines, Peachland | 638 mm | minus 7.8°C |
| IDF (Interior Douglas fir) | 150 Mile House | 414 mm | minus 10.7°C |
| PP (Ponderosa pine) | Kelowna | 332 mm | minus 3.3°C |
| BG (Bunchgrass) | Kamloops | 242 mm | minus 5.4°C |

- Precipitation numbers read directly from Table 4 (85%: the table is from 1991 and climate has warmed since).
- Coldest month means read from the second half of Table 4 (85%).
- One station is not the whole zone. Ranges inside a zone are wide: the IDF runs from about 300 to 750 mm, over 1,000 mm in its wettest parts (95%).
```

## Snow by zone

```diagram rp-bec-snow-bars
```

- High zones get the most snow: ESSF far ahead (85%).
- Deer struggle in snow deeper than about 30 cm (85%).
- Snow is what pushes deer down to the low zones (85%).

```more snow and what it does to game
- Yearly snowfall at the same stations (Table 4, 85%): Boss Mountain ESSF 782 cm, Revelstoke ICH 422 cm, Brenda Mines MS 398 cm, 150 Mile House IDF 145 cm, Kelowna PP 97 cm, Haney CWH 82 cm, Kamloops BG 67 cm.
- ESSF: 50 to 70% of precipitation falls as snow; the snowpack peaks at about 1 to nearly 4 m (95%).
- Mule and black tailed deer have difficulty moving in snow deeper than 30 cm (85%, BC Ministry of Environment, Mule and Black tailed Deer in BC).
- Moose move freely in snow to about 40 cm and with slight difficulty at 40 to 70 cm (85%, BC Ministry of Environment, Moose in BC).
- Moose sometimes winter in the drier ESSF but usually leave to escape deep snow (95%). Most ungulates in the MS move lower in winter (95%).
- Ungulates: hoofed animals (deer, elk, moose, sheep, goats).
```

## The big tree tells the zone

```diagram rp-bec-trees
```

- Look at the oldest, tallest trees. They name the zone (85%).
- Pine parks: PP. Fir: IDF. Spruce spires: ESSF. Cedar: wet zones.

```more tree clues in full
- PP (Ponderosa pine): big pines with long needles and orange plated bark in open, park like stands with grass under them (95%).
- IDF (Interior Douglas fir): Douglas fir dominates. Open stands with bunchgrass on dry sites, dense fir with pinegrass on moister sites (95%). Douglas fir cones have 3 pointed bracts sticking out like mouse tails (85%).
- MS (Montane spruce): hybrid spruce and subalpine fir at climax, but huge young lodgepole pine stands from old fires are the look you will see most (95%).
- ESSF (Engelmann spruce and subalpine fir): narrow spire shaped trees, meadows and parkland near the top (95%).
- ICH (Interior cedar and hemlock): western redcedar and western hemlock; devil's club and skunk cabbage in wet spots (95%).
- CWH (Coastal Western Hemlock): western hemlock most common, western redcedar, coast Douglas fir on drier sites, red alder where logged (95%).
- Young forest after fire or logging can fool you. Aspen and lodgepole pine pioneer in many zones (90%). Look for the old survivors (Tip).
```

## Same hill, two zones

```diagram rp-bec-aspect
```

- South slopes are hotter and drier. They act like a lower zone (85%).
- North slopes act like a higher zone (85%).
- The edge between them is prime deer country (Tip).

```more aspect in the zone book
- In the Bunchgrass zone, rough or Idaho fescue often grows on steep north and east slopes, while bluebunch wheatgrass and sage hold the hot ones (95%).
- In the IDF, ponderosa pine persists on dry, south facing slopes even in moister subzones (95%).
- Low elevation, south facing slopes in the IDF attract many animals in winter (95%). See [[Aspect]] and [[Winter range]].
- Tip: on a cold October morning, deer feed on the sunny south face. On a hot afternoon, they bed in the shade of the north face timber.
```

## How to tell the zone in the field

```steps Read the zone in 5 steps
rp-zid-1 | Note your height and which way the slope faces.
rp-zid-2 | Look at the oldest, tallest trees.
rp-zid-3 | Look at the plants at your boots.
rp-zid-4 | Check for moss, hanging lichen and lasting snow.
rp-zid-5 | Confirm on the zone map, then remember it.
```

- 2 minutes at every stop. It becomes automatic (Tip).

```more the 5 steps in detail
1. Elevation and aspect: your phone map or GPS gives metres. Aspect is the way the slope faces (Tip).
2. Big trees: ponderosa pine, Douglas fir, spruce and subalpine fir, cedar and hemlock (85%).
3. Ground plants (85%, zone book plant lists):
   - Bunchgrass, sagebrush, antelope brush (bitterbrush): BG or PP.
   - Pinegrass, kinnikinnick, soopolallie: IDF.
   - Black huckleberry, grouseberry, white rhododendron: MS or ESSF.
   - Devil's club, ferns: ICH.
   - Salal, red huckleberry, sword fern: CWH.
4. Hanging tree lichen and late snow in shade mean cold and wet (Tip).
5. Map: BC publishes the zone map free. Links in Sources.
```

## Deer move down through the zones

```anim sg-mig-snowline Snow pushes deer down, zone by zone.
```

- Summer and early fall: high zones (MS, ESSF) (85%).
- Winter: low south slopes in PP and IDF (95%).
- Some deer travel up to 80 km to winter there (95%).

```more movement through the zones
- Mule deer, white tailed deer, bighorn sheep and elk can migrate up to 80 km to winter in the PP and IDF (95%, zone book).
- In the northern IDF, mule deer need old Douglas fir for winter food (litterfall) and snow interception (95%).
- Most ungulates leave the MS for lower ground in winter; caribou and some moose stay (95%).
- Full story with timing: [How animals move through the year](#/s/migration/0).
```

## Fire shapes every zone

```diagram rp-ndt
```

- BC rates each zone by how often fire comes back (90%).
- Dry low zones: frequent small ground fires kept them open (90%).
- Coast forest: big fires, centuries apart (90%).

```more natural disturbance types
- NDT (natural disturbance type), from the Forest Practices Code Biodiversity Guidebook, 1995 (90%, BC Ministry of Environment restoration guidelines page):
  - NDT1: ecosystems with rare stand initiating events.
  - NDT2: ecosystems with infrequent stand initiating events.
  - NDT3: ecosystems with frequent stand initiating events.
  - NDT4: ecosystems with frequent stand maintaining fires.
  - NDT5: alpine tundra and subalpine parkland.
- The BC zone map data gives each subzone an NDT (90%): Heffley Creek PPxh2 is NDT4, the MSdm3 plateau and ESSFdc3 at Sun Peaks are NDT3, Mission CWHdm3 is NDT2, Harrison CWHvm2 is NDT1.
- Stand initiating means the fire kills most trees and a new forest starts. Stand maintaining means ground fire clears brush and small trees but old thick barked trees live (90%).
- More: [After fire](#/s/burn-country/0).
```

## Grandpa's rule

```diagram rp-bec-ladder
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom, not fact): Know your floor. Every 300 m up the hill is a different house with different tenants.

```more why this works
- Elevation changes zone faster than distance does. On the Heffley transect, 4 zones fit in 27 km (90%).
- When you find game in one zone, look for the same zone on the map elsewhere (Tip).
```

## Common mistakes

```diagram rp-bec-aspect
```

> [!mistake]
> - Thinking one valley is one habitat. Zones change every few hundred metres of height and with every slope turn.
> - Hunting high zones in late fall snow. The deer already moved down.
> - Reading a young pine stand as the zone. Look for the old trees.

```more more mistakes
- Trusting a 1991 number as today's weather. Use it to compare zones, not to plan a day (Tip).
- Forgetting the north slope. It acts a zone higher and holds snow longer (85%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram rp-bec-code
```

- **Big Pines In Many Elk Areas:** BG, PP, IDF, MS, ESSF, AT. The Interior floors from bottom to top.
- **X D M W V:** eXtra dry, Dry, Moist, Wet, Very wet. The first small letter.
- **Hot Warm Mild Cool Cold:** h, w, m, k, c. The second small letter. K is Kool.
- **Nine times wetter:** the coast near Mission gets about 9 times Kamloops rain.
- **30 and 40:** deer struggle past 30 cm of snow, moose shrug to 40 cm.
- **Rhyme:** South is hot and grassy, north is cool and mossy.
