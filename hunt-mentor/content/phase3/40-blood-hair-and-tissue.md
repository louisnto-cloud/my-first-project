## Search the hit site in 5 steps

```steps Search the hit site in 5 steps
rs-bl-1 | From your spot, pick a landmark by the animal.
rs-bl-2 | Flag and pin where you shot from.
rs-bl-3 | Walk in from the side, not on its path.
rs-bl-4 | Search a 3 m circle: hair, blood, bone, scuffs.
rs-bl-5 | Check the bullet path beyond for exit spray.
```

- Do this after your wait, not before, on a doubtful hit (70%).

```more the 5 steps in full
- Walk to where the animal stood and flag it. Look for blood, hair, bone chips, scuffed ground, a bullet furrow (70%, from [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing/0)).
- Pick the landmark while you are still at your shooting spot. Things look different up close (Tip).
- Dug in hoof prints show the launch spot and first direction (Tip). See [Measuring tracks](#/s/rs-track-measuring/0).
- Exit spray, hair and tissue can land metres behind the animal, on trees and brush in the bullet path (Tip).
- On a heart or lung hit you may go to the hit site after about 20 to 30 minutes; on a doubtful hit, wait longer first (65%, Deer and Deer Hunting via search preview).
```

## What you find, what it means

```diagram rs-bl-evidence
```

- Read everything together: blood, hair, tissue and the reaction (65%).
- Stomach matter beats blood colour for calling a gut hit (65%).

```more evidence in full
- Lung hit: "light coloured blood, blistered blood, and soft and light coloured lung tissue on the ground" (65%, Norma Academy via search preview).
- Bright pinkish, frothy blood is "a positive indication of a solid lung hit" (65%, Realtree via search preview).
- Gut hit: green or brownish plant matter (65%); "green digested plant material, sometimes liver, grainy, dark red blood as well as a strong smell" (65%, Norma). A paunch hit "will likely leave a sparse trail of small drops and pinpricks of blood mixed with stomach content"; back off and wait at least 8 hours (70%, Pennsylvania Game Commission, Game News, April 2016, via search preview).
- "Greenish matter smeared on the shaft, usually with white fatty tissue, means a paunch hit" (65%, Realtree, bowhunting).
- Bone chips: a bone was struck. Bone, marrow and light fur at the strike point suggest a front leg hit (65%, Norma).
- Pink blood plus bone chips can mean a shoulder or rib hit that also caught the lungs (60%).
- Full colour chart and wait times: [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing/0).
```

## Blood changes colour

| Condition | What happens | Certainty |
|---|---|---|
| Drying | Darker, duller, brown when dry | 65% |
| Freezing | Darker and dull | 65% |
| On soil | Looks darker than on leaves | 65% |
| Rain | Looks lighter, washes away | 65% |

- Read colour on fresh blood, soon after the shot (65%).

```more colour in full
- "The longer any blood is on the ground, the darker it appears, particularly after it dries" (65%, Deer and Deer Hunting via search preview).
- Blood that dries or freezes darkens and looks dull; blood on soil looks darker than on leaves; rain can make it look lighter (65%, Bowhunter via search preview).
- "Dried blood usually has a brown color and can be difficult to spot on brown grass or leaves" (70%, hunter education course, Nebraska, via search preview).
- Colour "is most accurately determined if you can inspect it soon after the shot" (65%, Bowhunter).
- Hydrogen peroxide fizzes on blood you cannot see in leaves (70%, from [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing/0)).
```

## Drops point the way

```anim rs-bl-drops Tails point the way it went.
```

- A drop that hits at an angle grows a small tail on the far side (80%).
- That tail points the way the animal was moving (Tip).

```more the physics
- Forensic research: for low impact angles, bloodstains "can include an asymmetric tail that bloodstain analysts can use qualitatively to establish directionality" (80%, Physics of Fluids 2023 via search preview).
- The tail forms in the last moments of spreading and keeps its shape as it dries (80%, same source).
- Field use: drops from a moving animal fly forward, so the tail points the direction of travel (Tip, built on 80% physics).
- Round drops with no tails: the animal was standing or moving slowly (Tip).
- On slopes and leaf litter, gravity and texture blur the tails. Use tracks too (75%).
```

## Read the brush, not just the ground

```diagram rs-bl-brush
```

- Blood high on both sides: a pass through, bleeding well (Tip).
- Low on one side: maybe no exit. Expect less blood (Tip).

```more brush in full
- Blood sprayed on brush both sides of the trail: a pass through, bleeding well (Tip, from [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing/0)).
- Blood only low and on one side: maybe no exit hole. Expect less blood (Tip, same).
- The height of blood smears on brush tells you how high on the body the wound is (Tip).
- A fine mist of tiny drops on leaves at nose height often comes from blood breathed out: a lung sign (Tip).
- Look up: blood on the underside of leaves and on twigs at chest height is easy to miss when you only look down (Tip).
```

## How much blood is a lot?

```diagram rs-bl-volume
```

- Blood volume is about 7% of body weight (60%).
- A deer may need to lose about a third before shock or death (60%).

```more volume in full
- "The blood volume of most animals is approximately 7 percent of body weight" (60%, hunting magazine via search preview). A 60 kg deer: about 4.2 L.
- "A deer must lose about a third of its blood to go into a state of shock or die" (60%, search preview); the threshold varies with how fast it is lost (60%, Buckmasters citing Texas bowhunter education).
- Hunters often guess blood loss badly; estimates in a bowhunter class were "all over the map" (60%).
- Deer often die from trauma to vital organs faster than from blood loss alone (60%).
- Practise: pour one cup (250 mL) of water with red food colour on leaves. See how big a cup looks. Then you will not panic at a "lot of blood" (Tip).
```

## Why the blood stops

```diagram rs-bl-dryup
```

- Clotting is the most common reason a trail fades (60%).
- No blood does not mean no hit. Look for tracks (70%).

```more dry trails in full
- "When you initially find blood, and then it disappears, in most cases clotting is the reason" (60%, Deer and Deer Hunting via search preview).
- Fat or hair can plug a small hole; a high hit with no exit bleeds inside (Tip).
- Gut hits bleed little outside: "much less external bleeding" and the deer often beds "in the first few hundred yards" (60%, Outdoor Life on Jeanneney's book, via search preview).
- Hair but no blood at all: back out and give it more time (70%, from [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing/0)).
- When the blood ends, switch to tracks and disturbed ground (Tip). See [Following a track](#/s/rs-track-following/0).
```

## Hair: read it, then check it

```diagram sk-hair
```

- Hair tells you how high you hit (Tip).
- After recovery, compare the hair to where you hit. Learn your own reads (Tip).

```more hair in full
- Long white fine hair: belly or brisket, a low hit. Short brown or grey with dark tips: side of the chest. Long coarse grey brown: top of the back, a high hit. White with a dark band: tail or rump (Tip, from [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing/0)).
- Cut hair at the hit site means a bullet passed close or through the hide (Tip).
- Lots of cut hair and no blood: maybe a graze. Look hard and wait (Tip).
- Keep a small bag of hair from your hit sites with notes in the Journal. Over years it becomes your reference set (Tip).
```

## Where wounded deer go

```diagram rs-bl-terrain
```

- Downhill, to water, into thick cover, often on a game trail (70%).
- Look ahead for the animal, not only down for blood (Tip).

```more terrain in full
- Wounded animals head downhill and to water, often on a game trail. Check creeks (70%, National Deer Association, from [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing/0)).
- A rifle hit in the heart or lungs: the deer is usually dead within about 137 m (150 yd) (70%, National Deer Association).
- Gut hit deer often bed early, within a few hundred metres, if not pushed (60%).
- Before you start, look at the map: where are the nearest thick patch, creek bottom and downhill trail? Plan the likely route (Tip). See [Map scouting](#/s/map-scouting/0).
- Around Heffley Creek, check the timbered draws below open grass slopes first (Tip).
- Note private land boundaries before you follow. The Trespass Act has no tracking exception (95%, from [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing/0)).
```

## Recovery plan by hit

```diagram rs-bl-plan
```

- Waits are opinion from hunter education, not law (70%).
- Rain or snow coming: go sooner (Tip).

```more the plan in full
- Wait table with sources: [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing/0). Hunter education and the National Deer Association differ on liver and gut waits; this app shows the hunter education numbers (70%).
- Lungs: often a short trail. Follow slowly, look ahead.
- Liver: dark blood, then often a bed. Back out quietly and come back after the wait (70%).
- Gut: thin blood with stomach matter. Back out at least 6 to 8 hours (70%). Pennsylvania Game Commission says at least 8 hours (70%).
- Leg or muscle: may travel far. Get help: your brother, a leashed dog (dogs on deer must be leashed in BC, 95%).
- Warm afternoon on a gut hit: the meat clock fights the wait clock. Wait less, trim more (Tip).
```

## Bears and blood trails

```photo g-griz-side Bears come to wounded animals too.
```

- Bears may be drawn to wounded animals (85%).
- One person tracks, one watches with spray and rifle (85%).

```more safety in full
- "Be cautious when tracking a wounded animal. Bears may also be attracted to animals that are wounded. When possible, have a partner keep watch" (85%, Alberta Environment, Be BearSmart).
- Talk while you track in grizzly country. A quiet tracker on a blood trail into thick cover is the classic surprise setup (Tip).
- Finding your deer covered or buried: leave and report (85%). See [Predator sign and kill sites](#/s/rs-predator-sign/0).
```

## The law on the trail

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

| Rule | Certainty |
|---|---|
| All reasonable effort to locate, dispatch, retrieve (95%) | Synopsis page 12 |
| No hunting from 1 hour after sunset to 1 hour before sunrise (95%) | Synopsis |
| No hunting with a light (95%) | Synopsis |

- Tracking with a firearm after hours or with a light is hunting. Do not (85%, my reading).

```more the law in full
- "No person shall kill, cripple or wound game without making all reasonable effort to locate, dispatch, retrieve and include it in their bag limit" (95%).
- "Hunt" includes "following after or on the trail of" wildlife "while in possession of a firearm or other weapon" (95%).
- Lost the light on a lung hit: call the RAPP line (Report All Poachers and Polluters) 1 877 952 7277 and ask for direction (lean from [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing/0)).
- Full text: [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing/0) and the Rule Book.
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram rs-bl-hitsite
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom, not law): The deer writes its own report at the hit site. Read it slowly before you go chasing the last page.

> [!mistake]
> - Walking straight down the blood line and trampling the hit site.
> - Calling it a miss because there was no blood in the first 10 m.
> - Trailing into thick cover in grizzly country alone and quiet.
