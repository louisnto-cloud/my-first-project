> [!why]
> Most animals need two things at once: food in the open and cover close by. The line where they meet is where you see them.

```anim rl-ed-dusk Out to feed at dusk, back at dawn.
```

- An [[Edge]] is where two cover types meet (75%).
- Deer, grouse, quail, bears and elk use edges every day (70%).


## What an edge is

```photo hab-grass-fir-edge The edge: grass meets Douglas fir.
```

- Edge: "the juncture of two or more different cover types" (75%).
- A wide, blended edge is called an ecotone (75%).

```more edge, ecotone, interspersion
- "Edge is defined as the juncture of two or more different cover types" (75%, Alabama Cooperative Extension, 2022).
- Where forest and open land blend through shrubs and small trees, it is an ecotone or transitional edge (75%, same source).
- Aldo Leopold wrote in 1933 that game which needs two or more cover types is most common where those types are mixed close together. Later writers say he meant interspersion (a fine mix of types), not simply more edge (70%, extension sources via search preview).
- So the real lesson: animals want food and cover within a short walk. Edges are where that happens (Tip).
```

## Hard edge, soft edge

```diagram rl-ed-hard-soft
```

- Hard edge: tall timber stops in a straight line (75%).
- Soft edge: shrubs and young trees blend the two (75%).

```more which is better
- A soft edge gives browse and hiding cover right beside the open feed (Tip).
- A hard edge is easy to see and easy to sit: animals step out of a wall of trees (Tip).
- Cutblock edges start hard and soften as shrubs and young trees grow in (Tip). See the cutblock age screen below.
```

## Six edges to look for

```diagram rl-ed-types
```

- Timber and grass, cutblock, burn, field and brush, creek strip, treeline.
- Each one is a feeding line next to a hiding line (Tip).

```gallery

hab-wells-gray-cutblock | Old cutblock: young trees and shrubs against timber.
hab-north-thompson-burn | Burn: new green feed under standing dead trees.
hab-woods-rose-slope | Rose along a dry creek: a brushy strip.
```

```more each edge type
- **Timber and grass:** the Interior classic. Mule deer feed on bunchgrass slopes and bed in the fir (75%).
- **Cutblock:** shrubs and young trees feed deer, elk and moose for years after logging (75%). See [Map scouting](#/s/map-scouting/0) for cutblock age.
- **Burn:** new grass, forbs and shrubs grow under the dead trees (75%). See [After fire](#/s/burn-country/0).
- **Field and brush:** hayfields and orchards next to brush hold white tails and quail. Mostly private land: permission first (99%, Rule Book).
- **Creek strip:** a ribbon of willow and dogwood through open ground: cover, browse and water (75%).
- **Treeline:** forest meets alpine. Mule deer and elk use it in early fall (70%).
```

## Shapes on an edge

```diagram rl-ed-shapes
```

- Inside corners, points of timber and islands of cover beat a straight line (Tip).
- These shapes bunch the travel (Tip).

```more why shapes matter
- An **inside corner** is where an edge turns back on itself. Animals cutting across the open come close to the corner (Tip).
- A **point** or finger of timber pushing into the open lets animals stay in cover longer before they step out (Tip).
- An **island** of cover in the open is a safe stop between two edges. Small islands get overlooked by hunters too. See [Islands of habitat](#/s/habitat-islands/0).
- The more turns an edge has, the more edge there is per hectare (Tip).
```

## How far from cover?

```diagram rl-ed-distance
```

- In daylight, feeding hugs the edge (Tip).
- At night animals feed farther out (Tip).

```more the numbers
- BC mule deer habitat ratings call for security and thermal cover "near forage habitat (i.e. within 200 m)" in spring (85%, BC habitat account, 2004).
- California quail "seldom ventures more than 15 to 30 m from cover" (75%, California Department of Fish and Wildlife life history).
- Mule deer security cover hides 90% of a deer at 60 m or less, in patches 180 m or more across (85%, same BC account). That is the cover they run back to.
- Hunting pressure pushes the feeding line closer to cover and later into the dark (Tip). See [Hunting pressure](#/s/pressure-push/0).
```

## Cutblocks change with age

```diagram hf-cutblock-edge
```

- Young cutblocks fill with shrubs: feed for years (75%).
- Old cutblocks close in and the edge fades (Tip).

```more cutblocks on the Hunt Map
- Layers: Cutblocks by age shows when each block was cut (99%, app layer list). Burns by year does the same for fires (99%).
- Pair the layer with the Satellite base to see what is growing there now (Tip).
- New logging roads into cutblocks bring hunters. The edge far from the road is often the one that holds game (Tip). See [Islands of habitat](#/s/habitat-islands/0).
```

## The catch: edges draw predators and hunters

```diagram sg-kill-sites
```

- Edges are busy: predators hunt them too (75%).
- Every hunter knows the field edge at dusk (Tip).

```more the downside of edge
- Edges attract predators and nest parasites; ground nesting birds near edges can lose more nests (75%, Alabama Cooperative Extension, 2022).
- Too much edge can hurt forest birds that need big blocks of forest (75%).
- For you: the obvious edge by the road gets sat by everyone. Look for the edge one bench up, one draw over, or across the creek (Tip).
```

## Where to sit on an edge

```diagram rl-ed-sit
```

- View of a corner or point, wind from the edge to you, sun at your back (Tip).
- Sit 100 to 200 m off it with a rest (Tip).

```more how to set up
- Pick the inside corner or point where trails come out (Tip).
- Check the wind and the thermal for the hour you sit: evening air drains downhill (85%). See [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals/0).
- Sit far enough off the edge that animals stepping out do not see or smell you, close enough for a sure shot: 100 to 200 m for most beginners (Tip). See [Judging distance](#/s/judging-distance/0).
- Have a [[Backstop]] behind every lane (Tip).
- Get there early: 45 minutes before legal light in the morning, 2 hours before dark in the evening (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

```photo rl-edge-grass-aspen Feed in the open, cover a few steps away.
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Don't sit in the field or in the woods. Sit where you can see both.

> [!mistake]
> - Sitting a straight edge instead of a corner or point.
> - Walking the edge you plan to hunt.
> - Sitting the road edge everyone else sits.

```more the mistakes in full
- Straight edges spread the animals out. Corners and points bunch them (Tip).
- Walking the edge leaves your scent right where animals step out. Come in from the open or the back side (Tip).
- Find the edge one step farther from the road (Tip).
```

## Do this in the field

> [!field]
> On the Satellite base, find one inside corner, one point of timber and one island of cover within 2 km of your truck. Sit the best one with the wind right.

```checklist rl-ed-field
Find an inside corner on the Satellite base
Find a point of timber and an island of cover
Turn on Cutblocks by age and Burns by year
Check the wind for the hour you will sit
Sit 100 to 200 m off the edge with a rest
```

## Memory hooks

- **See both, sit both:** a view of the open and the cover.
- **Corners collect.**
- **200 m to the bedroom:** mule deer cover within about 200 m of feed.
- **Quail stay a stone's throw** from cover: 15 to 30 m.
- **EDGE:** Eat in the open, Duck into cover, Go at dusk, Exit at dawn.

```more using the hooks
- EDGE is the daily loop of most deer: say it when you pick an evening sit (Tip).
```
