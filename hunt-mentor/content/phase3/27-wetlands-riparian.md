> [!why]
> Water is the one thing every animal needs every day. Wet ground and the green strips along creeks pull in ducks, moose, deer and bears, often in one small spot.

- Wetlands are a small share of the land with a big share of the game (Tip).
- Learn 5 kinds of wetland and the strip along every creek.

```more what this session covers
- BC wetland definition: land wet enough, or flooded often enough, to grow plants that stand out from the drier land around it (95%, Pojar in Meidinger and Pojar 1991).
- This session: the 5 wetland classes, marsh bands and who uses them, beaver ponds, riparian strips and the forestry law that leaves them, moose in wetlands, ducks, and safety.
- [[Riparian]] means the strip of land beside a stream, lake or wetland.
- Duck details: [Habitat tour: ducks](#/s/habitat-ducks/0). Moose details: [Habitat tour: moose, elk and bears](#/s/habitat-big-game/0).
```

## Five kinds of wetland

```diagram rp-wet-classes
```

- Open water, marsh, fen, swamp, bog (95%).
- Interior plateau: marshes and ponds are common (95%).
- North and coast: more fens and bogs (95%).

```more each class in plain words
| Class | What it is | Plants | Who uses it (Tip unless marked) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shallow open water | standing water under 2 m deep at midsummer, under 10% plants sticking up (95%) | pondweeds, milfoil, duckweed, water lilies (95%) | divers, puddle ducks at the edges, moose in summer |
| Marsh | flooded with nutrient rich water, over 10% cover of plants sticking up (95%) | cattail, bulrush, sedges, horsetail (95%) | ducks, geese, muskrat, deer and moose at the edge |
| Fen | peat over 40 cm, fed by groundwater (95%) | sedges, willow, scrub birch, red osier dogwood (95%) | moose browse, grouse at the edge |
| Swamp | 25% or more cover of trees or tall shrubs, moving water (95%) | coast: redcedar, alder, skunk cabbage; Interior: spruce or willow (95%) | moose, deer, bears in spring |
| Bog | moss (Sphagnum) peat, fed by rain, acid (95%) | Sphagnum moss, Labrador tea, bog cranberry, stunted black spruce or shore pine (95%) | little game; hard walking |

- Marshes are especially common on the Interior Plateau, where many poorly drained basins hold nutrient rich water. Some are alkaline or salty (95%).
- Source: Pojar, chapter 19, Non tidal Wetlands, in Meidinger and Pojar 1991.
```

## Walk into a marsh: who uses each band

```diagram rp-marsh-zones
```

- Willow edge: moose and deer (85%).
- Shallow water with pondweed: puddle ducks (80%).
- Deep open water: diving ducks (80%).

```more bands and feeding in detail
- [[Puddle duck]]s (dabblers: mallard, wigeon, teal) tip up in shallow water and strain seeds, plants and invertebrates through their bills; in fall and winter they feed mostly on seeds, grain and water plants (75%, Ducks Unlimited Source magazine, search preview).
- [[Diver]]s (scaup, goldeneye, bufflehead) dive in deeper water (80%). See [Ducks](#/s/ducks/0).
- Small lakes and potholes in Interior grasslands are breeding grounds for pintail, wigeon, mallard, blue winged teal, lesser scaup and coot (95%).
- Lakes and streams in the Bunchgrass zone stay open longest or thaw first, so they are staging water for migrating ducks (95%).
- Cattail and bulrush margins hold marsh wren, red winged blackbird, sora, bittern and muskrat (95%).
```

- Cattail and bulrush: marsh. Willow and dogwood: wet edge (95%).
- Spot them from far away and you have found water (Tip).

```more plant notes
- Cattail: tall flat leaves, brown sausage shaped head (95%).
- Red osier dogwood: opposite leaves with 5 to 7 veins curving to the tip, white berries, bright red young stems; wet to moist streamsides, lakesides and swamps (85%, E Flora BC).
- Willows: many species; long narrow leaves, many stems; moose depend mainly on willow for winter food (85%).
- Skunk cabbage grows in coast swamps with western redcedar, Sitka spruce or red alder (95%). Early spring bear food (Tip).
```

## Beaver ponds: one spot, many hunts

```diagram rp-beaver-pond
```

- Dam, lodge, drowned trees, regrowing willow (85%).
- Ducks on the water, moose on the willow (Tip).

```more why beaver ponds are gold
- Beaver dams flood the land, kill trees and create open water and marsh. Willow sprouts thick around the edges (80%, general ecology).
- Beaver are listed for lakes and streams in every Interior zone (95%).
- An old drained pond becomes a wet meadow: grass, sedge and willow (80%).
- Tip: check every beaver pond on your map for duck sign and moose tracks. Look on satellite images for dead standing trees in a flat valley.
```

## Moose live by water

```photo hab-moose-willows Cow moose browsing willow: twig ends bitten off at chest height.
```

- Summer: water plants. Winter: willow (85%).
- Moose calve in riparian strips (95%).

```more moose and wet ground
- In summer, aquatic plants like horsetail, burweed and submerged pondweeds are important moose foods (85%, BC Ministry of Environment, Moose in BC).
- In winter moose depend mainly on willows, plus red osier dogwood, cottonwood, paper birch, aspen and high bush cranberry (85%).
- Winter browse stands are mostly along rivers, around wetlands, in burns and cutblocks and on lower avalanche tracks (85%).
- In the IDF, riparian areas are often chosen by moose and mule deer as calving areas (95%).
- Moose can reach twigs up to 2.5 m above the ground (85%).
```

## Riparian strips: why trees stand along creeks

```diagram rp-riparian-widths
```

- Forestry law leaves tree strips along fish streams (95%).
- Wider stream, wider strip (95%).
- In logged country, the creek strip is the cover (Tip).

```more the law and the wetland widths
- Forest Planning and Practices Regulation, section 47: fish streams and streams in community watersheds get riparian classes by width (95%, BC Laws, read directly).
  - S1A: 100 m or wider (or flood plain 100 m or wider): 100 m management zone, no reserve unless the minister sets one.
  - S1B: over 20 m: 50 m reserve plus 20 m management zone, 70 m total.
  - S2: 5 to 20 m: 30 m reserve plus 20 m, 50 m total.
  - S3: 1.5 to under 5 m: 20 m reserve plus 20 m, 40 m total.
  - S4: under 1.5 m: no reserve, 30 m management zone.
  - Non fish streams outside community watersheds: S5 over 3 m, 30 m management zone; S6 3 m or less, 20 m.
- Section 48, wetlands: W1 over 5 ha, 10 m reserve plus 40 m management; W2 and W3 1 to 5 ha, 30 m total; W4 small wetlands, 30 m (95%).
- Widths are minimums on each side, set for forestry, not hunting (95%). Hunting rules for water and birds live in the Rule Book: [Migratory birds](#/s/rb-migratory-birds/0). Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.
- Riparian areas have high plant productivity and lots of structure, so they hold a rich mix of birds and small mammals (95%).
```

## The creek strip is a hidden highway

```anim rp-riparian-travel A deer follows the creek cover from bed to field.
```

- Deer travel along creek cover in open country (Tip).
- Sit where the strip meets the feed (Tip).

```more travel lanes and funnels
- In grassland and farm country, creek strips are often the only cover connecting timber to feed (Tip).
- Where a creek strip narrows or crosses a fence, it is a funnel. See [Trails and funnels](#/s/funnels/0).
- Wind follows drainages: in the evening cool air sinks down the creek; in the morning it rises (75%). See [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals/0).
- Riparian woodland of alder, cottonwood, aspen and willow in the Bunchgrass zone supports a rich fauna, including wood ducks (95%).
```

## Approach a pond

```steps Approach a pond in 4 steps
rp-wl-1 | Check the wind and keep the sun at your back.
rp-wl-2 | Come in low behind cattails, brush or the bank.
rp-wl-3 | Glass the edges before you show yourself.
rp-wl-4 | Plan the retrieve before you shoot.
```

- Never swim for a bird in cold water (Tip).

```more the pond approach in detail
1. Ducks usually face into the wind. Coming from downwind means they flush toward you (Tip).
2. Every cattail band, willow clump or bank fold is cover. Crawl the last part if needed (Tip).
3. Glass first: you may find a moose, a deer drinking, or a pair of mallards in the far corner (Tip).
4. Retrieve: a dog, chest waders in shallow water, or a wind that drifts the bird to shore. If none, do not shoot (Tip).
- Cold water kills. See [Water safety](#/s/water-safety/0) and the [[1 10 1 principle]].
- Jump shooting basics: [First duck hunt](#/s/first-duck-hunt/0). Shot rules for ducks: [Migratory birds](#/s/rb-migratory-birds/0).
```

```more what the mud tells you
- Webbed 3 toed tracks: ducks and geese (85%).
```

## Common mistakes

```photo hab-osoyoos-wetland
```

> [!mistake]
> - Walking straight to the water's edge in the open. Everything sees you first.
> - Shooting a duck over deep cold water with no way to get it back.
> - Ignoring the dry looking creek strip in farm country. It is the deer's road.

```more more mistakes
- Stepping on a floating mat at a fen or bog edge. It can give way (Tip).
- Leaving tracks and trash at a small pond. Small ponds are shared by everyone (Tip). See [Etiquette](#/s/etiquette/0).
```

> [!field]

```checklist rp-wetland-field

```
