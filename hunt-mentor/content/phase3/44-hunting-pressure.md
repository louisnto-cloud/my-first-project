> [!why]
> Your first hunts are in October, with everyone else. Pressure is the one force you can predict on a map. Learn where people stop, and you will know where the deer went.

```diagram rq-pr-road-bands
```

- Most hunters stay within half a kilometre of a road (90%, Pennsylvania).
- Hunted deer go thick, steep, far, or into the night (65 to 85%).

```more what this session covers
- [[Hunting pressure]] is people in the woods: trucks, quads, boots, voices, scent, shots.
- This session: where hunters actually go (a measured study), what deer, mule deer and elk do about it (collar studies), where pushed game ends up, how to use other hunters, and how not to be the pressure yourself.
- The short version is in [Weather, moon and what moves game](#/s/weather-moon/0). This is the full version.
- Most measured data comes from the United States. BC hunters face the same pattern on roaded Crown land (Tip: no BC collar study on hunter pressure was found for this session).
```

## Where hunters actually go

```diagram rq-pr-odds
```

- 73% of hunters were within 0.5 km of a road (90%).
- Each 1 km closer to a road: about 3 times more hunters (90%).
- Average farthest point from a road: 0.84 km (90%).

```more the Pennsylvania study in full
- Luloff and others, Penn State final report, August 2004: aerial counts of hunters on 45,907 ha of public forest, plus interviews (90%, read directly).
- "Hunters on our study area were not distributed uniformly (73% were ≤ 0.5 km from a road)". (90%)
- Each kilometre closer to the nearest road made an area nearly 3 times more likely to be hunted; each 10 degrees flatter, 1.25 times (90%).
- "The average maximum distance hunters reached from a road open to public vehicles was 0.84 km", and they walked an average of 5.48 km a day (90%).
- Morning: 72% sat still. Afternoon: more walked and stalked (90%).
- 16% of the area was over 1 km from a road; "there were substantial portions of the study area where little or no hunting occurred" (90%).
- Applied to BC: an assumption. Assumption: BC hunters on Crown land behave like this. Check it with your own eyes on opening weekend.
```

## What hunted deer do

```diagram rq-pr-deer-resp
```

- Move less, in smaller areas (65%).
- Bucks go to security cover away from roads (85%).
- More of their moving shifts into the dark (65%).

```more the deer studies
- Oklahoma, 1,861 ha, controlled hunter numbers: deer "reduced movements and used smaller areas more intensively"; displacement ended about 3 times lower after the season than at the start (65%, Little and others 2016, Basic and Applied Ecology, search preview).
- Same project: bucks recognized pressure quickly at both low (1 hunter per 250 acres, about 1 per 100 ha) and high (1 per 75 acres, about 1 per 30 ha) density and moved less (60%, popular summary).
- Wyoming mule deer, GPS 2016 to 2018: "Males selected security habitat away from motorized routes during hunting season"; females stayed on better feed (85%, Rodgers and others 2021, abstract read directly).
- Hunted deer shift activity to night and to thick cover; one study saw the shift within 48 hours of the opener (65 to 70%, search previews, see Weather, moon and what moves game).
- Popular claim: bucks avoid a stand for about 3 days after it is hunted. One researcher's report, secondary source (50%). Treat it as a Tip.
```

## Mule deer and roads

```diagram rq-pr-roads-deer
```

- Hunting season, Oregon: mule deer 190 m from closed roads, 1,250 m from open ones (70%).
- Closed roads are quieter. Walk them (Tip).

```more roads, traffic and the BC rules that help you
- Curtis and Du Toit 2017, Wildlife Society Bulletin, Oregon forest, GPS collared mule deer, August to October 2015 (70%, Conservation Evidence summary). Site comparison: association, not proof.
- Elk at the Starkey research site in Oregon: traffic as low as 1 vehicle every 12 hours changed where elk were (60%, review citing Starkey work).
- Oregon elk used habitat within 500 m of open roads half as much as expected (60%, same review).
- In BC Region 3, no ATV (All Terrain Vehicle) for hunting in MUs (Management Units) 3-28, 3-29 and 3-30 from 1 September to 10 December (99%, synopsis page 33). Hunters there walk from the truck. My lean: those quiet road ends are worth the walk (Tip).
- Look for gated roads and roads shown as closed on the [[Motor Vehicle Closed Area]] maps. Then walk them at first light (Tip). Check the [Region 3 Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-3/0) first.
```

## Elk: they find refuges

```diagram rq-pr-elk-public
```

- Utah: elk on public land dropped from up to 64% to 29% in rifle season (80%).
- They moved onto private land where hunting was limited (80%).

```more the elk studies
- Sergeyev and others, Journal of Wildlife Management, reported by The Wildlife Society (March 2022): 445 GPS collared elk on about 43,000 km2 in central Utah, 2015 to 2017. "As soon as the hunt opens, they all move en masse onto private lands." (80%)
- When Utah opened private land hunting in 2016, public land use rose from 29% to 41% (80%).
- Montana: elk used private land closed to hunting as refuges; limited hunting there cut both the share of the herd and the time spent (75%, Burcham, Edge and Marcum, Wildlife Society Bulletin, search preview).
- Elk are few near Heffley Creek (85% for about 1,350 in the Thompson Okanagan in 2000). The pattern teaches you about deer too (Tip).
- Private land is closed to you without permission. A refuge is a place to watch from the edge, never to cross. See [Land status](#/s/maps-land-status/0).
```

## Where pushed game goes

```diagram rq-pr-where
```

- Thick, steep, far, closed to hunting, or dark (Tip, built on the studies).
- Your job: find the nearest refuge to the road crowd (Tip).

```more the four refuges near Heffley Creek (Tip)
- Thick: north facing Douglas fir timber, dense draws with aspen and shrubs (Tip). Deer bed in hiding and thermal cover (80%).
- Steep and far: the steep side of a draw, more than 1 km from the nearest open road (Tip, from the 0.84 km figure).
- Closed to hunting or shooting: Sun Peaks within 400 m of lifts and runs (Map C11), City of Kamloops lands, private ranches (99% rules). Deer that live there can feed out onto Crown land at the edges (Tip).
- Dark: if you only see deer at last light, they may be leaving a refuge. Back off and sit the edge earlier (Tip).
- Rules for each: [Local plan: Heffley Creek](#/s/local-heffley/0).
```

## Find the pressure gap

```steps Find the pressure gap in 5 steps
rq-pr-step-1 | Mark every open road and gate.
rq-pr-step-2 | Shade about 1 km around each road.
rq-pr-step-3 | Find steep, thick ground beyond it.
rq-pr-step-4 | Pick the trail or saddle between.
rq-pr-step-5 | Be on it before the crowd.
```

- Do it on the map at home, then check it on foot (Tip).

```more the 5 steps in full
1. On the Hunt Map, turn on Forest Service roads, motor vehicle closures and private land. Mark every road a truck can use (Tip).
2. Shade about 1 km around each open road. In the Pennsylvania study, 73% of hunters were inside 0.5 km and the average farthest point was 0.84 km (90%).
3. Beyond the shade, find steep slopes, dark timber or brushy draws (Tip). Contours close together mean steep: see [Maps 1: reading terrain](#/s/maps-terrain/0).
4. Find the trail, saddle or draw that links the crowd to the refuge. That is your seat (Tip). See [Trails and funnels](#/s/funnels/0).
5. Arrive 30 minutes before legal light. Big game is legal from 1 hour before sunrise (99%, synopsis page 11). You want to be still before anyone else moves.
- Building the full map: [Build your own hunting map](#/s/build-hunting-map/0).
```

## Let other hunters move deer to you

```diagram rq-pr-crossing
```

- Opening morning: the crowd is your drive (Tip).
- Sit still between the road and the timber (Tip).

```more how to use other hunters, safely and fairly
- Many hunters still hunt and walk in the afternoon (90%, Pennsylvania). Deer that move away from them use trails (Tip).
- Sit a seat with a safe backstop, never facing a road or another hunter's route (Tip). Wear orange where others hunt (Tip; see [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety/0)).
- Do not crowd another hunter's seat. It is unlawful to "block or harass" a legal hunter (99%, synopsis page 12, item 45).
- Midweek has less pressure. Hunt Tuesday to Thursday if you can (Tip).
```

## You are pressure too

```diagram rq-pr-bumped
```

- Every walk in leaves scent and noise (Tip).
- Bumped a spot twice? Rest it (Tip).

```more keep your own pressure low (Tip)
- Walk in on the downwind side, along the bottom, and never across the feeding area (Tip). See [Moving quietly](#/s/still-hunting/0).
- Same seat every morning: deer can learn it. Rotate 3 or 4 seats by wind (Tip).
- Glass more, walk less. A glassing seat 600 m away puts no scent on the deer (Tip). See [Glassing](#/s/glassing/0).
- After a missed shot or a bump, note it in the Journal: wind, time, route. Patterns show up over a season (Tip).
```

## Rules that touch this session

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

```diagram ld-r3-atv-bar
```

- No ATV for hunting in 3-28, 3-29, 3-30, 1 September to 10 December (99%).
- Never use a vehicle or boat to herd or harass wildlife (99%).

```more the rules in full
- Page 33: "ATV for Hunting Closed Areas: from Sept 1 - Dec 10 in MUs 3-28, 3-29, 3-30, and that portion of MU 3-17 shown on Map C3." (99%)
- Page 11, item 25: unlawful "To use a power boat, aircraft, or motor vehicle or other mechanical device to herd or harass wildlife." (99%) Driving at deer to push them is that offence (95%, my reading).
- Page 12, item 45: do not block or harass a legal hunter or trapper (99%).
- Page 33: all motor vehicles except snowmobiles banned above 1,700 m in Region 3 except on existing roads and trails (99%).
```

## Common mistakes

```diagram rq-pr-roads-deer
```

> [!mistake]
> - Hunting the first 500 m from the truck on opening day.
> - Calling it "no deer here" after one pressured weekend.
> - Walking the same route into the same seat every morning.

```more why each one hurts
- The first 500 m is where 73% of hunters were (90%). Deer there have learned (Tip).
- Hunted deer move less and shift to night (65%). They are still there, just not where the trucks are (Tip).
- Same route, same scent, same time: you become the pattern (Tip).
```
