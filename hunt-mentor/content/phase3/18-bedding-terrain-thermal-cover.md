> [!why]
> Animals spend most of the day lying down. Know the ground and cover they pick, and you know where to glass at noon and where never to walk.

- A good bed gives a view below, wind from behind and cover close (Tip).
- Thermal cover keeps an animal warm or cool for less energy (85%).

## What a bedding spot needs

| Need | How the ground gives it |
|---|---|
| See danger | A view downhill or across open ground (Tip) |
| Smell danger | Wind or rising air from below, a slope or cover behind (Tip) |
| Hear danger | Quiet ground, away from roads and creeks (Tip) |
| Hide | Shrubs, young trees, rocks, tall grass (85%) |
| Stay warm or cool | Sun or shade, wind shelter, canopy (85%) |

- The best spots give all five at once (Tip).

```more why each need matters
- Bedded deer rely on sight downhill and smell from behind and above (Tip, hunter observation). See [Beds and bedding areas](#/s/rs-beds/0) for the research on deer bed choice.
- Mule deer summer day beds in an Arizona pine study were cooler, shadier spots: "Site temperature and canopy closure were the most influential attributes" (80%, Germaine and others 2004).
- Security cover and thermal cover are separate needs, often met by the same thick stand (85%, BC habitat account).
```

## Where beds sit on the land

```diagram rl-bd-terrain
```

- Below the crest of a spur or ridge, on benches, at the head of a draw, in thick cool timber (Tip).
- Mountain mule deer often bed about two thirds of the way up (60%).

```more each spot
1. **Below the crest:** on the point of a spur or just under a ridge top, where the deer sees down and the morning thermal brings scent from below (Tip). A field guide article says mountain mule deer routinely bed about two thirds of the way up slopes in front of vegetation (60%, search preview).
2. **Benches:** flat ground on a steep face with a view (65%, hunter observation).
3. **Head of a draw:** brush, shade and an escape route over the top (Tip).
4. **Thick cool timber:** north and east faces on warm days (75%).
- Plains and grassland deer often bed just below the rim of a coulee, plateau or hill (60%, same article).
- Bedding areas are rarely on the valley floor in hill country, except in thick bottomland brush for white tails (75%).
```

## Security cover: hide and watch

```diagram rl-bd-numbers
```

- Mule deer security cover hides 90% of a deer at 60 m or less (85%).
- Patches 180 m or more across (85%).

```more security cover details
- "For Mule Deer the most effective security cover hides 90% of the animal at a distance of 60 m or less, and security patches need to be 180 m or more in diameter" (85%, BC habitat account citing Nyberg and Janz 1990).
- Mature and old forest with a dense shrub or patchy conifer understory meets this (85%).
- "Areas of steep broken terrain can also be used as security habitat" (85%). See [Escape terrain](#/s/escape-terrain/0).
- Elk hiding cover in US studies: vegetation that hides 90% of a standing elk at 61 m (70%, Thomas and others 1979, as cited).
- Test it yourself: stand 60 m from a thicket. If you can see a deer sized shape through it, it is not security cover (Tip).
```

## Thermal cover: warm at night, shade by day

```diagram rl-bd-day-night
```

- Night: a closed canopy above 3 m holds heat and cuts wind (85%).
- Day: rock bluffs gather heat; canopy blocks hot sun (85%).

```more thermal cover in the BC sources
- Night thermal cover "should trap long wave radiation and maintain warmer air temperatures (occurring under a closed canopy above a deer's head or above 3 m), reduce wind at deer height ... and intercept precipitation" (85%, BC habitat account citing Bunnell and others 1985).
- "Daytime thermal requirements are met by areas that gather heat (on or near rock bluffs) or intercept excessive solar radiation (canopy closure)" (85%, same).
- Elk thermal cover in US studies: conifer stands at least 12 m (40 ft) tall with over 70% crown closure (70%, Thomas and others 1979, as cited).
- Caution: a Blue Mountains study (Cook and others 1998) found thermal cover did not improve elk condition. Animals may choose those stands for food or safety instead (70%, US Forest Service paper via search preview).
- Thermal cover for you: a tree at your back and the sun on your chest makes a long sit bearable (Tip).
```

## Snow catching cover

```anim rl-bd-snow Tall closed timber catches the snow.
```

- Tall closed conifers keep the snow up in the branches (85%).
- Deer walk and feed under them in deep snow years (85%).

```more snow interception numbers
- "Canopy closure (i.e., stands taller than 10 m with greater than 60% crown completeness) exerts the most influence on snow interception" (85%, BC habitat account citing Bunnell and others 1985).
- Mule deer winter range in the Interior relies on old Douglas fir with closed, multi layered canopies, generally over 60% canopy closure, on moderate to steep warm slopes (85%, BC silviculture practices, 2004).
- Douglas fir foliage and lichen falling from those big trees is winter food too: 62 to 89% of the winter diet at Knife Creek near Williams Lake (85%, Waterhouse and others 1994, as cited).
- See also [Food, water and cover](#/s/food-water-cover/0) and [Winter ranges](#/s/winter-range-legal/0).
```

## Weather moves the beds

| Weather | Where to look |
|---|---|
| Cold and clear | Sunny south and east faces, below rock, out of wind (75%) |
| Hot | North and east timber, shady benches, draw heads (75%) |
| Windy | Lee side just below the top, in timber (Tip) |
| Rain | Under big conifers, edges of thick timber (Tip) |
| Snow storm | Thick timber, then sunny faces after (Tip) |

```diagram sa-bed-warm-cold
```

```more the evidence behind the table
- Cold: deer in an Ontario study picked sun exposed day beds in winter (60%, Armstrong and others 1983, white tails, search preview). On cold sunny days deer often bed where the first sun hits (Tip, app tool note).
- Hot: mule deer in a Montana and Idaho study picked cooler northeast and east aspects with overhead cover (60%); summer day beds in Arizona were cooler and shadier than random spots (80%).
- Severe weather: north aspects with dense, high crown closure stands "may be used during severe weather conditions" (85%, BC habitat account).
- Wind and rain rows are hunter observation (Tip). See [Weather fronts and game movement](#/s/fronts-and-movement/0).
```

## Windy days: the lee side

```diagram rl-bd-lee
```

- Wind pours over a ridge and swirls on the lee side (75%).
- Bedded animals may sit just over the top, out of the wind (Tip).

```more lee side details
- The lee side is calmer but the wind eddies there. Your scent can swirl back to a bedded animal from any side (75%). See [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals/0).
- On very windy days, deer may bed in thick cover and move little. A slow still hunt into the wind can work because noise is covered (Tip). See [Moving quietly](#/s/still-hunting/0).
```

|---|---|

| Elk | Dark timber on north faces, high basins (70%) |
| Moose | Thick conifer or willow near water and browse (75%) |
| Black bear | Thick cover near berries and water (Tip) |

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

```photo g-md-buck-bedded The bed you walk into is the buck you never see.
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Glass the beds, hunt the trails. Walk into a bedroom only when you are ready to lose it.

> [!mistake]
> - Walking the ridge top right over the beds.
> - Looking in the open at noon.
> - Forgetting that weather moves the beds every day.

```more the mistakes in full
- Beds sit just below crests and on spur points. Walking the very top puts you above them with your scent and outline (Tip).
- At midday, animals are bedded in cover. Glass shade, timber edges, benches and below rocks (Tip).
- A cold clear morning and a hot afternoon put the same deer on different faces (75%).
```

## Do this in the field

> [!field]
> Pick one face. Mark three likely beds on the map from slope, aspect and cover. Glass them at midday from across the draw.

```checklist rl-bd-field

Pick the face that matches today's weather
Glass beds at midday from across the draw
Find one old bed and note slope, aspect and view
Test security cover: can you see a deer shape at 60 m?
```

## Memory hooks

- **View below, wind behind, cover close.**
- **90 at 60:** security cover hides 90% of a deer at 60 m.
- **10 and 60:** trees over 10 m, crowns over 60% closed catch snow.
- **Cold day, sun bed. Hot day, shade bed.**
- **Two thirds up** is a good first glass on a mountain face (Tip).

```more using the hooks
- Say "view, wind, cover" as you scan each bench or spur point. Mark the ones that have all three (Tip).
```
