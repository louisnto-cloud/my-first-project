> [!why]
> Upland birds live in small, exact places. Learn to see the brushy edge, the aspen draw and the berry slope, and you stop walking empty country.

- This is a photo walk through four kinds of bird cover near our bases (Tip).
- California quail: Okanagan valley bottoms, farms and towns (85%).
- Ruffed grouse: aspen, creek bottoms and mixed forest edges (80%).
- Dusky and spruce grouse: higher conifer forest, ridges and the subalpine (80%).
- Bird ID is in [Grouse](#/s/grouse) and [Quail, chukar, partridge and pheasant](#/s/quail-chukar-partridge-pheasant). This session is the places.

## Quail country: Okanagan valley bottoms

```gallery
hab-quail-vernon | California quail cock near Vernon. Quail perch on a post or stump to watch for danger.
hab-woods-rose-slope | Wild rose along a dry creek. Thorny thickets are quail hiding cover.
hab-blackberry | Blackberry thicket by a river. A covey can vanish into this.
hab-chokecherry | Chokecherry in fruit. Brushy draws with berries hold quail and grouse.
```

- California quail live mainly in the Okanagan and Kettle valleys, mostly at 250 to 500 m and rarely above 750 m (85%).
- They seldom go more than 15 to 30 m from thick cover (75%). Find the brush and you find the birds.
- They favour farmland and suburbs (85%). Most coveys sit on private land: ask first (75%).
- There is no quail season in Region 2 (Lower Mainland) or Region 3 (Thompson) (90%). Quail are a Region 8 (Okanagan) bird.

## Ruffed grouse: aspen and creek bottoms

```gallery
hab-aspen-grove-snow | Aspen grove in winter near Clearwater, BC. Ruffed grouse eat aspen buds all winter.
hab-dogwood-flowers | Red osier dogwood in flower. Its white berries feed grouse in fall.
hab-snowberry | Snowberry. The berries hang on into winter for grouse.
ruffed-grouse-hen | Ruffed grouse hen in the leaf litter. She blends right in.
```

- Ruffed grouse are most common at 750 to 1,250 m in BC (85%).
- They stay in a small area all year, often 2.4 to 16 ha (70%).
- Aspen: smooth white bark, round leaves that shake in the wind (85%). Buds are winter grouse food (80%).
- Walk old roads and trails through aspen and berry brush in October. Grouse come to the edges to pick grit and clover (Tip).

## Dusky and spruce grouse: high forest

```gallery
hab-subalpine-forest | Subalpine forest of spruce and lodgepole pine, Manning Park. Spruce grouse country.
hab-douglas-fir-stand | Open Douglas fir forest. Dusky grouse feed on the needles in winter.
hab-huckleberry-bush | Huckleberry shrubs on a rocky ridge. Late summer food for dusky grouse.
hab-kinnikinnick | Kinnikinnick berries on dry, open ground under pine.
```

- Dusky grouse move uphill in fall into dense conifers and eat needles in winter (70%).
- Many dusky grouse are seen at 1,500 to 2,250 m (85%).
- Spruce grouse are conifer specialists that eat pine and spruce needles (85%).
- Spruce grouse sit tight and trust their camouflage. Look up and look slowly (Tip).

## Grouse sign in snow

```gallery
hab-grouse-tracks-snow | Ruffed grouse tracks winding through soft snow.
hab-grouse-track-line | A grouse track line: 3 forward toes, one print in front of the next.
hab-grouse-snow-roost | Snow roost hole with wing marks. A ruffed grouse burst out of here.
hab-grouse-wing-prints | Wing prints in the snow where a grouse took off or landed.
```

- Grouse tracks show 3 long toes forward and a short one back, about 5 cm long (75%).
- In deep, soft snow ruffed grouse dive in and roost under the snow for warmth (80%).
- A hole with wing marks beside it means the bird flew out from under the snow (75%).
- Fresh tracks in new snow mean the bird is close. Follow slowly and be ready (Tip).

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Hunt the edges, not the middle. Birds live where two kinds of cover meet.

> [!mistake]
> - Walking the open field for quail. They are in the brush beside it.
> - Hunting grouse in dark timber in October when they are out on the berry and aspen edges.
> - Rushing a fresh track line in snow. Slow down; the bird is often within 50 m (Tip).

> [!field]
> Find one aspen stand with berry shrubs on its edge. Walk its border slowly at mid morning. Note every grouse you flush, the plant it came from and the height. Write it in the Journal.
