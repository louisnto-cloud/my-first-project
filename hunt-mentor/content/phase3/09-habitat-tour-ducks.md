> [!why]
> Ducks live by water, food and shelter from wind. Learn to read a pond, a marsh and a wet field, and you know where ducks will land before they do.

- This is a photo walk. Look at the water, the plants on its edge and the fields around it.
- Two kinds of duck country near our bases: Interior ponds, potholes and lake marshes around Kamloops and the Okanagan, and the farm fields and sloughs of the Fraser Valley near Mission (Tip).
- Dabbling ducks, like mallards and pintails, feed in shallow water by tipping up (85%). Diving ducks dive for food in deeper water (85%).
- Interior fall migration mostly ends by late November with freeze up (75%). On the coast, ducks stay all winter (70%).
- Species ID is in [Ducks](#/s/ducks). This session is the places.

## Interior potholes and ranch ponds

```gallery
hab-lac-du-bois-potholes | Grassland potholes north of Kamloops. Small, shallow, with grassy edges.
hab-kamloops-pond-farm | Pond, hay fields and cottonwoods near Kamloops. Water, food and shelter close together.
hab-osoyoos-wetland | Haynes Point wetland on Osoyoos Lake. Reeds and calm water out of the wind.
mallard-hen | Mallard hen. Mallards use almost any shallow pond.
```

- Shallow water with plants on the edge beats deep, bare water for dabblers (80%).
- Look for the calm side. On a windy day ducks sit in the lee of a point or a tall reed bed (Tip).
- Small ponds freeze first. Big lakes and the rivers stay open longer and hold late ducks (75%).
- Tranquille, at the head of Kamloops Lake, is a staging marsh and a No Shooting area on Map C17 (85%).

## Marsh plants

```gallery
hab-cattail-marsh | Cattail marsh. Tall flat leaves; ducks hide and nest in it.
hab-cattail-head | Cattail head: a brown sausage on a stalk. It shouts "marsh" from 500 m.
hab-pondweed | Floating pondweed. Oval leaves on the surface, seed spikes above.
```

- Cattail: tall, flat leaves and a brown sausage shaped head (85%). Cover and nesting, not much food (75%).
- Pondweeds: leaves float on the surface; ducks eat the seeds and the plants (80%). Source: Ducks Unlimited Canada and E Flora BC.
- A gap in the cattails with pondweed in the open water is a landing spot. Set up with the wind at your back facing that gap (Tip).

## Fraser Valley fields and sloughs

```gallery
hab-geese-stubble | Geese feeding in corn stubble in snow. Fraser Valley fields look like this in winter.
pintail-drake | Pintail drake. Pintails and mallards feed in flooded fields.
```

- Fraser Valley fields, sloughs and the delta are a major wintering area. Fields flood after rain and ducks feed on stubble and grass (70%).
- Bert Brink Wildlife Management Area: open fields "partly flooded in the winter" draw wigeon and Canada geese (90%).
- Pitt Addington Marsh, at the south end of Pitt Lake, is wintering, migration and breeding habitat for waterfowl (90%).
- Field hunting needs the farmer's consent (95%). Ask early, in person (Tip).

## Reading a pond edge

```gallery
hab-duck-tracks | Webbed tracks in mud: 3 forward toes joined by skin.
hab-beaver-lodge | Beaver lodge on a pond with ducks. Beaver ponds are duck ponds.
```

- Webbed tracks, droppings and loose feathers on a mud bar mean ducks loaf there (75%).
- Beaver dams make flooded timber and shallow edges that ducks love (75%).
- A pond with fresh sign at noon is a pond to sit at dawn (Tip).
- Never hunt near bait: not within 400 m of a place where bait was put out, unless it has been bait free for at least 7 days (95%).

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Ducks land into the wind. Sit with the wind at your back and let them come to your face.

> [!mistake]
> - Setting up on the windy side of the pond. The ducks are on the calm side.
> - Picking deep, bare water. Dabblers want shallow water with plants.
> - Scouting at noon and hunting the same spot without checking where they actually fly at dawn.

> [!field]
> Visit one pond or slough near home at sunrise. Note the wind direction, where ducks sit, where they land, and what plants grow at that spot. Draw it in the Journal.
