## The coast forest in numbers

| | CWH (coast) | ICH (Interior wet belt) |
|---|---|---|
| Rain and snow a year | 1,000 to 4,400 mm, mean 2,228 (95%) | 500 to 1,200 mm (95%) |
| Share as snow | under 15% in the south (95%) | 25 to 50% (95%) |
| Elevation | sea level to about 900 m (95%) | 400 to 1,500 m (95%) |
| Main trees | western hemlock, redcedar, coast Douglas fir (95%) | western redcedar, western hemlock (95%) |

- The coast is wet but mild. The wet belt is wet and snowy (95%).

```more more from the zone book
- CWH mean yearly temperature is about 8°C; the coldest month averages 0.2°C (95%).
- Amabilis fir and yellow cedar grow in wetter, higher parts. Red alder takes over logged or disturbed sites. Black cottonwood lines big rivers (95%).
- Above the CWH is the MH (Mountain Hemlock) zone, about 900 to 1,800 m in the south, with deep snow for months (95%).
- The ICH is the most productive forest zone in the Interior and has the highest diversity of tree species in BC (95%).
- ICH wet sites grow devil's club and skunk cabbage (95%).
- Source: Meidinger and Pojar 1991, chapters 6, 7 and 11.
```

## Old forest versus dense young forest

```diagram rp-wet-layers
```

- Old forest: gaps, shrubs and logs. Food and cover (85%).
- Crowded young forest: dark floor, little food (90%).
- Deer feed in openings and edges, then hide in the dark (Tip).

```more forest stages in BC terms
- BC's pole and sapling stage: trees over 10 m tall, usually dense, that have overtopped the shrub and herb layers (90%, BC structural stage definitions). Little grows under them.
- Mature and old forests open up again as trees die and gaps form; understories become well developed (90%).
- On the coast, black tailed deer make frequent use of logged habitats in spring and summer (80%, BC Ministry of Environment).
- Full stage by stage story: [Cutblocks and logging](#/s/cutblock-country/0).
```

## Snow and the old crowns

```anim rp-snow-intercept Old crowns catch the snow. Deer can still move underneath.
```

- Old forest crowns catch snow (85%).
- Deer struggle in snow deeper than about 30 cm (85%).
- North and higher on the coast, old growth is winter survival (85%).

```more the black tail winter range story
- Old growth forests provide shelter, intercept snow so it is shallower, and drop forage: broken branches and the lichens on them (85%, BC Ministry of Environment).
- On southeast Vancouver Island and the Gulf Islands, old growth is not critical because snow is usually shallow and brief. Northward and higher, it matters more (85%).
- Coast deer move to low, south facing slopes and floodplains with little snow in winter (80%, from [Habitat tour: deer](#/s/habitat-deer/0)).
- Mission area snowfall is low: about 82 cm a year at the Haney station (85%). Most falls and melts fast (Tip).
```

## What black tails eat

```diagram rp-blacktail-food
```

- Winter: fir, cedar, red huckleberry, salal, ferns, lichens (85%).
- Summer and fall: green herbs, blackberry, fireweed, leaves (85%).

```more food and plants to learn
- Source list from the BC Ministry of Environment deer brochure (85%).
- Salal: thick, leathery, finely toothed evergreen leaves 3 to 9 cm; forms thickets; CWH is its main zone (85%, E Flora BC).
- Red huckleberry: bright green angled stems, small oval leaves, bright red berries; grows on rotting wood in coast forest (85%).
- Salmonberry: 3 leaflets, raspberry like orange to dark red berries, wet ground (85%).
- Himalayan blackberry: introduced; arching thorny canes in thickets on roadsides, ditches and fence rows in the Fraser Valley (85%).
- More plants: [Browse plants](#/s/browse-plants/0) and [Berries, seeds and mast](#/s/berries-mast/0).
```

## Who lives in wet forest

| Zone | Main big game |
|---|---|
| CWH (coast) | black tailed deer, black bear, grizzly, wolf, goat on the mainland |
| ICH (wet belt) | moose, elk, mule and white tailed deer, black bear, grizzly |
| MH (coast subalpine) | black tails, black bear, goat on south rock |

- Black tailed deer: the coast deer (85%).
- Roosevelt elk live in a few coast river valleys (85%).

```more more on coast wildlife
- Black tailed deer, black bear, grizzly and gray wolf are the most common large mammals in the CWH. Grizzly and mountain goat occur only on the mainland (95%).
- The Fraser Lowland part of the CWH has the greatest diversity of birds, amphibians and reptiles in BC (95%).
- Mountain goats use rugged south aspects and come down to forested cliffs near sea level in winter (95%).
- BC black tailed deer numbers have ranged between about 150,000 and 250,000 in recent decades (85%).
- Roosevelt elk live in small scattered herds, each in a major river valley, eating sedges, grasses and ferns plus willow, elderberry, blueberry, cedar and hemlock browse (85%). Check the Rule Book for any elk season before you plan (Tip).
- ICH: moose, elk, mule and white tailed deer, black bear, wolverine; grizzly and caribou listed at risk (95%).
- Sources: zone book Tables 14 and 24; BC Ministry of Environment deer and elk brochures.
```

## Bears in wet forest

```photo hab-skunk-cabbage Skunk cabbage near Aldergrove: early spring bear food.
```

- Coast black bears often den in or under big trees, snags and logs (85%).
- Wet zones hold more bears than dry ones (85%).

```more bear facts for the coast
- Black bear numbers are higher in wet zones, where food is plentiful, and coastal densities are higher because of salmon (85%, BC Ministry of Environment).
- In coastal BC almost all black bear dens are in or under large trees, snags, logs or stumps, sometimes up to 25 m above the ground (85%).
- Bears hibernate about 3 to 5 months on the south coast and probably 5 to 7 months in the Interior (85%).
- Fall: berries and spawning salmon (85%). Bear safety: [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0). Bear hunting: [Black bear](#/s/black-bear/0).
```

## The Interior wet belt (ICH)

```photo hab-aspen-grove-snow Wet belt edge near Clearwater: aspen, cedar country starts.
```

- Cedar and hemlock, devil's club, deep snow (95%).
- Forests regrow fast after fire or logging (95%).
- Burns and cutblocks here are moose feed (90%).

```more the ICH for a hunter
- The ICH lies on the windward slopes of the Columbia Mountains (the wet belt), the Shuswap and Quesnel highlands and the west side of the Rockies (95%).
- Winters are cool, long and snowy; summers warm and dry; valleys narrow and deep (95%).
- Young seral forest, clearcuts and burns hold moose, mule deer, white tails and elk (95%).
- South aspect ICH forests hold moose, deer, elk, mountain goat, black bear and cougar (95%).
- Devil's club: stems 1 to 3 m, armed with yellow spines 5 to 10 mm long, huge maple like leaves. Do not grab it (85%).
```

## Hunt thick cover

```steps Hunt coast cover in 4 steps
rp-wh-1 | Scout old logging roads and edges for fresh tracks.
rp-wh-2 | Sit a timber edge at dusk with the wind to you.
rp-wh-3 | Still hunt slowly in light rain. Wet ground is quiet.
rp-wh-4 | Glass cutblocks, power lines and slides from across.
```

- In thick cover, the edge is the hunt (Tip).

```more the method in detail
1. Old roads and skid trails are travel lanes. Fresh tracks and droppings show who uses them (Tip). See [Tracks](#/s/tracks/0).
2. Black tails feed in openings at dusk and dawn and slip back into cover (Tip). See [Trails and funnels](#/s/funnels/0).
3. Rain softens leaves and covers noise, but you get cold fast. Wool or synthetics, rain shell, spare dry layer (Tip). See [Cold injuries](#/s/cold-injuries/0).
4. Across a valley, one cutblock or slide can be glassed for an hour. See [Glassing](#/s/glassing/0).
- Black tailed deer rut: about November to early December (80%). Rattling and calling: [The rut](#/s/the-rut/0).
- Region 2 has many no shooting areas near towns. Check every spot in the [Region 2 Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-2/0) first. Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.
```

> Grandpa's rule (wisdom, not fact): In the dark woods, hunt the light. Every gap, road and slide is a dinner table.

## Common mistakes

```photo hab-salal
```

> [!mistake]
> - Pushing through salal and blackberry. You sound like a bulldozer and see nothing.
> - Sitting deep in dark young forest where nothing grows.
> - Going out in cotton in the rain.

```more more mistakes
- Shooting into thick brush at a sound or a shape. Identify the whole animal and what is behind it (Tip). See [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety/0).
- Assuming a spot near Mission is open to hunting. Check the Region 2 no shooting areas (Tip).
```
