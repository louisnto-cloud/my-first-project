> [!why]
> From October to April, deer and moose live on twigs. Know the 12 shrubs they eat and you can walk into any slope and say "deer eat here" or "nothing eats here".

```photo hab-moose-willows
```

- [[Browse]] means twigs, buds and leaves of shrubs and trees (90%).
- Fall and winter deer and moose are mostly browsers (85%).
- Learn 12 shrubs. That covers most of your country (Tip).

```more what this session covers
- This session: how to ID a shrub in 5 steps, opposite versus alternate leaves, the evergreen browse, the deciduous browse, the moose list, the coast list, who eats what, and reading browse sign.
- Mule deer winter on shrubs such as sagebrush, bitterbrush, rabbitbrush, snowbrush, saskatoon, rose and serviceberry, plus Douglas fir foliage (85%, BC Ministry of Environment).
- Moose depend primarily on willows for winter food, plus red osier dogwood, cottonwood, paper birch, aspen, high bush cranberry, falsebox and subalpine fir (85%, BC Ministry of Environment).
- Berries and seeds: [Berries, seeds and mast](#/s/berries-mast/0). Seasonal food overview: [Food, water and cover](#/s/food-water-cover/0).
- Photos are illustrations; captions say where each was taken. Plant ID features come from E Flora BC (85%).
```

## ID a shrub in 5 steps

```steps ID a browse shrub in 5 steps
rp-bid-1 | Green in winter, or bare twigs?
rp-bid-2 | Leaves or buds in pairs, or zig zag?
rp-bid-3 | Leaf shape, teeth and tip.
rp-bid-4 | Stem colour, bark and thorns.
rp-bid-5 | Wet or dry ground, and any berries.
```

- 5 questions name most shrubs you will meet (Tip).

```more why these 5
- Evergreen versus deciduous splits the list in half (Tip).
- Leaf arrangement is fixed for a species and works on bare winter twigs too: look at the buds (85%).
- Leaf edge (smooth or toothed) and shape are the main E Flora BC description points (85%).
- Stem colour is the winter giveaway: red osier dogwood has bright red young stems (85%); aspen has greenish white bark (85%).
- Site: bitterbrush is a dry slope plant; willow and dogwood want wet ground (85%).
```

## Opposite or alternate?

```diagram rp-opposite-alt
```

- Opposite: pairs face to face (85%).
- Alternate: one at a time, zig zag (85%).
- Opposite browse: dogwood, maple, snowberry, soopolallie, falsebox, cranberry (85%).

```more which is which
| Opposite leaves | Alternate leaves |
|---|---|
| red osier dogwood (85%) | saskatoon (85%) |
| Douglas maple (85%) | bitterbrush (85%) |
| snowberry (85%) | willows (85%) |
| soopolallie (85%) | trembling aspen, paper birch (85%) |
| falsebox (85%) | roses, chokecherry, hawthorn (85%) |
| high bush cranberry (85%) | snowbrush, redstem ceanothus (85%) |

- Source: E Flora BC leaf descriptions, read 2026-10-11.
```

## The moose menu

```anim rp-moose-browse A moose breaks a tall stem down to reach the tips.
```

- Moose reach twigs to about 2.5 m (85%).
- They break stems down to reach higher tips (85%).
- Winter: willow first (85%).

```more moose feeding facts
- Moose must eat up to about 20 kg of food a day in winter (85%, BC Ministry of Environment).
- They are better adapted to coarse woody browse than other hoofed animals; they chew off large twigs and break branches with their jaws (85%).
- Moose also strip bark from willows and poplars in winter (85%).
- Winter browse stands are along rivers, around wetlands, in burns and cutblocks, and on lower avalanche tracks (85%).
- More: [Wetlands](#/s/wetland-country/0), [Cutblocks](#/s/cutblock-country/0), [Moose](#/s/moose/0).
```

## Who browses what

```diagram rp-browse-who
```

- Deer: dry slope shrubs and fir (85%).
- Moose: willow and wet ground shrubs (85%).
- Elk: grass first, then shrubs (85%).

```more where these lists come from
- Mule deer winter foods: big sagebrush, pasture sage, bitterbrush, rabbitbrush, snowbrush, saskatoon, rose, serviceberry, Douglas fir foliage (85%, BC Ministry of Environment, Mule and Black tailed Deer in BC).
- Black tailed deer winter: Douglas fir, western redcedar, red huckleberry, salal, deer fern, tree lichens (85%).
- Moose winter: willows first; red osier dogwood, cottonwood, paper birch, aspen, high bush cranberry, falsebox, subalpine fir (85%, Moose in BC).
- Interior elk winter: sedges, horsetail and willow in wetlands; bunchgrasses, forbs, serviceberry, rose and other shrubs on uplands (85%, Elk in BC).
- Red osier dogwood for deer: from [Food, water and cover](#/s/food-water-cover/0) (80%).
- Serviceberry and saskatoon are the same genus (Amelanchier); in BC, saskatoon is the common name (85%).
```

## Read the browse sign

```gallery
hab-moose-willows | Moose: twig ends bitten off at chest height.
hab-deer-rub | Not browse: a buck rub on a sapling.
```

- Ragged, torn tip: deer, elk or moose. Clean 45 degree cut: hare (80%).
- Browse line height tells you who (65 to 75%).

```more browse sign details
- Deer have no upper front teeth, so they tear twigs, leaving a ragged end (80%). Hares and rabbits cut twigs clean at about 45 degrees (80%). See [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0).
- Browse line heights: deer to about 1.2 m, elk about 1.5 to 1.8 m, moose about 1.8 to 2.4 m (65%, from Reading sign).
- Fresh browse: pale, moist ends. Old: grey and dry (75%).
- Hedged shrubs, short and bushy from years of browsing, mark winter range (65%).
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom, not fact): Learn the plant, then look for the bite. A bitten saskatoon is a better sign than a track.

## Common mistakes

```diagram sg-browse-cut
```

> [!mistake]
> - Learning plants only from photos. Crush, touch and smell them once in the field.
> - Calling every clipped twig deer browse. Check for the clean hare cut.
> - Looking for moose on dry bitterbrush slopes. Moose want wet willow.

```more more mistakes
- Mixing up snowberry and saskatoon in fall: snowberry leaves are opposite with smooth edges; saskatoon leaves are alternate and toothed on the top half (85%).
- Forgetting fir: in December and January, Douglas fir needles are a big part of the mule deer diet near Kamloops (85%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram rp-opposite-alt
```

- **MAD Cap Horse plus Dogwood:** Maple, Ash, Dogwood, Caprifoliaceae (snowberry, cranberry), Horse chestnut: the main opposite leaved woody plants. A classic botany trick.
- **Saskatoon saws the top:** teeth only on the top half of the leaf.
- **Bitterbrush has 3 bites:** 3 teeth at the tip.
- **Red stems, deer and moose dreams:** red osier dogwood.
- **Willow is moose bread.**
- **2.5:** a moose reaches about 2.5 m.
