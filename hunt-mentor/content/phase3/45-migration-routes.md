> [!why]
> Your October hunts fall right in the fall move. Know who is leaving the high country, when, and by which road, and you hunt where the animals will be next week, not where they were last month.

```diagram rq-mg-types
```

- Four patterns: long distance, up and down, short shifts, stay home.
- October to November is moving month for mule deer and ducks (70 to 85%).

```more what this session covers and where the data lives
- The overview is in [How animals move through the year](#/s/migration/0). This session is the route detail: numbers, timing, elevations and how to find a route yourself.
- Every month and elevation here comes from the app's migration data file (`data/migration.json`, updated 2026-10-03) and its sources. Each species there carries its own certainty.
- Strong data: mule deer (BC collar study), snow geese (Reifel sanctuary counts), moose snow limits (BC account).
- Weak data: elk near your bases (few animals), ducks month by month in the Interior (no local counts), black tailed deer in the Fraser Valley (Vancouver Island data). Those lines say so.
- The Hunt Map has month layers for mule deer, white tails, moose, elk and sheep (general pattern, 60%). Open it at [#/map](#/map).
```

## Mule deer: the numbers

```diagram rq-mg-md-numbers
```

- 74% of collared deer migrated, about 47 km a year (85%).
- Longest: 102 km in a straight line (85%).

```more the Southern Interior Mule Deer Project in full
- BC Wildlife Federation project page: 30,000 km2 study; "74 per cent were migratory, travelling an average of 47.2 km per year" (85%).
- Winter update, 11 March 2020: "78% of our deer migrated. Most deer moved to summer range April-May and returned to winter range in October-Nov. On average the collared deer migrated 55 kms" (85%, quoted with its hyphens).
- Update, 13 November 2019: a doe collared near Apex Mountain, west of Penticton, left on 1 May and arrived west of Aspen Grove on 12 May, 102 km in a straight line (85%).
- Same update: "By mid-October this year, nearly all the collared deer in each study area were back in their winter range." (85%)
- Study areas: West Okanagan, Cache Creek, Boundary. Cache Creek is the closest to Heffley Creek (85%).
- Some deer stay low all year: residents (85%, BC deer account).
```

## Same road every year

```diagram rq-mg-md-route
```

- "Most of the deer choose nearly the same path" both ways (85%).
- Down in October and November, up in April and May (85%).

```more route facts and what they mean for you
- Same path: SIMDeer winter update, 2020 (85%). Strong home range fidelity year to year (85%, deer research notes).
- Trigger for the fall move: snow and cold, not the rut (85%, Monteith and others 2011).
- Wyoming collars: temperature and snowfall timed migration; hunting season barely changed it (85%, Rodgers and others 2021).
- Older BC account: Interior deer "often remain at high elevations until December" (85% as written). It conflicts with the collar data. Use October to December as the window, earlier in deep snow years (75%, research notes reading).
- A route crosses ridges, saddles and draws. Those are the funnels: see [Trails and funnels](#/s/funnels/0).
- My lean: in a mild October, hunt mid slope fir and burn edges; after the first real snow up high, drop to the winter range edges (Tip).
```

## Watch the snow line push them

```anim sg-mig-snowline Snow drops, deer drop.
```

- Snow trouble near 30 cm, deer leave near 50 cm (75%).
- Track the snow line every trip (Tip).

```more snow depth by species
- Deer: "difficulty moving through snow deeper than 30 cm" (85%, BC deer account); leave slopes near 50 cm (80%, UBC (University of British Columbia) thesis).
- Moose: "little or no difficulty" to 40 cm, "only slight difficulty" at 40 to 70 cm, and they "commonly winter in areas with 50 cm or more" (85%, BC moose account).
- Elk "usually keep to regions where the snow remains shallow on winter ranges" (85%, BC elk account).
- So the same snowfall moves deer first, elk next, moose last (75%, my reading of the three accounts).
```

## Who moves when

```diagram rq-mg-calendar
```

- Shaded columns: your hunting months.
- Mule deer and ducks are moving; moose are still high (70 to 85%).

```more the month detail from the data file
- Mule deer: October "Fall migration: collared deer came back in October and November" (70%); November "Most deer on or near winter range. Rut in November and early December." (75%).
- Elk: September rut, most cows bred mid to late September (70%); October "autumn snow pushes herds back toward winter range" (60%).
- Moose: October rut early in the month; "Moose stay high longer than deer, elk or sheep" (70%); November slow drift down (65%).
- Ducks: peak fall passage through the Interior in October; freeze up in late November ends most Interior migration (60 to 65%).
- Snow geese: arrive on the Fraser delta by early October; best numbers mid October to mid December; shift to the Skagit late December (70 to 75%, Reifel).
- Dusky grouse: moving uphill into dense conifers in September and October (60%).
```

## Elevation by month

```diagram rq-mg-elev
```

- Mule deer drop from about 400 to 1,800 m in October to 300 to 1,100 m in December (70 to 75%).
- These bands come from habitat zones, not collar data (65%).

```more the elevation bands and zone names
- Mule deer: October 400 to 1,800 m (Ponderosa Pine, Interior Douglas fir, Montane Spruce zones); December 300 to 1,100 m (Bunchgrass, Ponderosa Pine, Interior Douglas fir) (70 to 75%).
- Near Kamloops the studied winter range is below about 760 m (80%, Willms 1976).
- Moose: October 800 to 1,900 m; December 400 to 1,600 m (65 to 70%).
- Elk: October 600 to 2,000 m; December 0 to 1,200 m across BC including coast herds (55 to 60%).
- White tails: about 300 to 1,000 m in October, 300 to 900 m in December, valley bottoms all year (60 to 65%).
- Zone elevations: Meidinger and Pojar 1991, Ecosystems of BC (90% for the zone ranges; 65% when used as an animal's range).
- Rough heights near your base: valley about 370 to 450 m, Heffley Lake about 950 m, Sun Peaks village about 1,250 m (55%, VERIFY on the Hunt Map elevation readout).
```

## White tails and black tails

```diagram sg-home-range
```

- White tails: short moves along or across valleys (85%).
- Coast black tails: some migrate within a watershed, some stay (80%).

```more the details
- White tails: "Seasonal movements are mostly upstream or downstream along the major valleys, or cross-valley from north-facing slopes in summer to south-facing sites in winter." (85% as written, Kootenay data; 65 to 70% for the Thompson). Quoted with the source's hyphens.
- "Within areas that have a suitable climate, whitetails prefer valley bottoms with farmland." (85%)
- Near Heffley Creek: North Thompson hayfields and cottonwood bottoms (75%, local reading).
- Black tails, coast: migrant annual range about 1,770 ha versus about 140 ha for residents (80%, Vancouver Island data). Winter below about 1,000 m in old forest (80%). Weak for the Fraser Valley (55 to 70%).
```

## Moose and elk

```diagram rq-mg-big
```

- Moose leave the high country last (85%).
- Elk are few near Heffley Creek (85% for the 2000 count).

```more moose and elk in full
- Moose in mountains "usually migrate between winter-spring ranges in valley bottoms and higher-elevation summer ranges, but some Moose remain in the valleys year-round" (85%, quoted with hyphens).
- In autumn they "stay on high-elevation ranges much longer than Elk, deer, or mountain sheep" (85%).
- Plateau moose winter "along river valleys that cut through the plateaus and in burns, logged areas, and wetland complexes" (85%).
- Seasonal home range "seldom exceeds 5 to 10 km2" (85%).
- Bonaparte Plateau, north of Kamloops: provincial moose research area since 2012 (90%). No movement results were found in the report read.
- Elk: Interior Rocky Mountain elk "tend to be more migratory"; "Autumn snowfalls push the Elk back to their traditional winter ranges" (85%). About 1,350 elk in the Thompson Okanagan in 2000 (85% for that date).
- Region 3 moose season for your MUs is in November only (99%, see [Local plan](#/s/local-heffley/0)). Elk: no general season near you (95%).
```

## Ducks and geese: the flyway

```diagram rq-mg-flyway
```

- Plateau ponds freeze, birds move to valley rivers, then the coast (65%).
- Snow geese fly about 5,000 km from Wrangel Island (80%).

```more the birds in full
- BC fall migration "was well under way in late September" (75%, Ducks Unlimited 2025) and "largely concluded by late November, coinciding with freeze-up" (75%, Ducks Unlimited 2024). Quoted with its hyphen.
- Fraser River delta: waterfowl food demand runs "from September to April" (80%, Pacific Birds Habitat Joint Venture 2024).
- Boundary Bay: "a vital link in the Pacific Flyway, supporting over 1.5 million birds" (90%, BC WMA (Wildlife Management Area) page).
- Snow geese: Wrangel Island birds; January 2021 mid winter count of the Fraser and Skagit flocks was 135,000 (80%, Reifel).
- Thompson: Tranquille WMA is spring and fall staging for Canada geese and swans, and a No Shooting area (90%). South Thompson River IBA (Important Bird Area): calm wintering water (80%).
- Swans are not game in BC (90%).
- Daily flights once birds arrive: [Waterfowl daily flights](#/s/waterfowl-daily-flights/0).
```

## Freeze up: the push you can see

```anim rq-mg-freeze Ice locks the food. Ducks go to open water.
```

- When the high ponds ice, valley water gets the birds (Tip, on the freeze up pattern).

```more how to use it (Tip)
- Watch the plateau lakes on your drives. First skim ice up high means the next cold week will move birds down (Tip).
- Legal water near Heffley Creek: Lac du Bois potholes outside the city line, Roche Lake and Bonaparte parks (99%, BC Parks). Tranquille marsh and McQueen Lake are closed to shooting (99%). See [Local plan](#/s/local-heffley/0).
- Snow geese on the coast: a Fraser Valley option after the Interior freezes (Tip). See [Local plan: Mission](#/s/local-mission/0).
```

## Grouse and quail

```diagram sg-grouse-move
```

- Dusky grouse go uphill into conifers in fall (60 to 70%).
- Ruffed, spruce, quail and chukar mostly stay home (75 to 85%).

```more upland birds in full
- Dusky grouse: "Most birds move in autumn from fairly open breeding areas to dense coniferous forest. In most parts of range, this involves moving uphill" (70%, Audubon). Up to about 48 km, usually much less (70%).
- In BC, dusky grouse breed mainly at 1,500 to 2,250 m, so the shift may be smaller here (85% for the atlas range).
- Ruffed grouse: adults stay; young disperse in fall (70%).
- California quail: "Not migratory" (75%). Chukar move lower in heavy snow (75%).
- Daily patterns: [Upland birds' daily pattern](#/s/upland-daily-pattern/0).
```

## Find a route on your map

```steps Find a deer route in 4 steps
rq-mg-step-1 | Find the low, sunny winter range.
rq-mg-step-2 | Find the high, moist summer range.
rq-mg-step-3 | Join them by ridges, draws and saddles.
rq-mg-step-4 | Walk it and look for fresh sign.
```

- Map first, boots second (Tip).

```more the 4 steps in full
1. Winter range: "steep south and west facing sites with broken terrain" in the dry forest zone (85%). Near Kamloops, below about 760 m (80%). Official winter range maps: see [Winter ranges](#/s/winter-range-legal/0).
2. Summer range: moister high country, openings, cutblocks and burns (65%).
3. Join them: routes follow ridges, draws and saddles, and nearly the same path every year (85% for the same path; Tip for the terrain).
4. In October and November, fresh tracks and trails heading downhill on that line confirm it (Tip). See [Tracks](#/s/tracks/0) and [Snow tracking](#/s/snow-tracking/0).
- Put it all on one map: [Build your own hunting map](#/s/build-hunting-map/0).
```

> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Hunt where they are going, not where they have been. Follow the snow down and the green up.

## Common mistakes

```diagram rq-mg-calendar
```

> [!mistake]
> - Hunting August scouting spots in late October.
> - Expecting moose to come down with the deer.
> - Driving past iced plateau ponds to hunt them for ducks.

```more why each one hurts
- Mule deer come back to winter range in October and November (85%). August high benches can go empty (Tip).
- Moose stay high much longer than deer (85%).
- Iced ponds lock up food and the birds leave (75%).
```
