## Puddle duck or diver?

```diagram sb-dabbler-diver
```

```anim hf-duck-takeoff Puddle duck springs up. Diver runs first.
```

- Tips up and jumps straight up: [[Puddle duck]] (80%).
- Dives and runs on the water to fly: [[Diver]] (80%).

```more the full split, and the exceptions
| Clue | Puddle duck | Diver |
|---|---|---|
| Water | Shallow marsh, creek, flooded field (80%) | Bigger, deeper lakes and rivers (80%) |
| Feeding | Tips up, tail in the air (80%) | Dives, swims under water (80%) |
| Take off | Jumps straight up (80%) | Patters along the water first (80%) |
| Wing patch | Bright, often shiny (80%) | White or grey (80%) |
| Wings | Set mid body (80%) | Smaller, set back, faster beat (80%) |
| On land | Walks well, feeds in grain fields (80%) | Clumsy, rarely on land (80%) |

- The bufflehead is the one diver that can jump straight up off the water (80%).
- Mergansers dive for fish. They are legal ducks (95%).
- Old hand's rule (opinion): "If it walks the field, it is a puddler. If it rafts the lake, look again."
```

## Puddle ducks in the air

```diagram mdk-sil-dabblers
```

- Shape first: size, neck, tail, bill (80%).
- Mallard big and slow. Teal tiny and twisting (80%).

```more flight clues for each puddle duck
| Duck | In the air (80%, Ducks Unlimited guides) |
|---|---|
| Mallard | Big, broad wings, slow steady beats, loose flocks, loud quack |
| Northern pintail | Slim cross shape, long neck, needle tail, fast, zig zags down from high |
| American wigeon | White patch on the front of the wing, twisting flight, whistled call |
| Green winged teal | Tiny, low and fast, the whole flock twists as one |
| Gadwall | Small white square on the back of the wing, compact flocks |
| Northern shoveler | Huge bill held low, wings look set back |
| Wood duck | Long square tail, head held up, dark wings, wooded ponds |

- Flight speed: a study found a median of about 82 km/h (51 mph) for mallards, the fastest of the species measured (70%).
- Teal look faster than they are because they are small and twist (Tip).
```

## Divers and mergansers in the air

```diagram mdk-sil-divers
```

```anim mdk-wingbeats Wingbeat speed tells you who.
```

- Divers: wings set back, fast beats (80%).
- Goldeneye wings whistle. Scaup show a white stripe (80%).

```more flight clues for each diver
| Duck | In the air (80%) |
|---|---|
| Goldeneyes | Big white wing patches, wings whistle, often in small groups |
| Bufflehead | Tiny, very fast wingbeat, low over the water, groups of 5 or 6 |
| Scaup | White stripe along the back of the wing; greater runs almost to the tip, lesser only halfway |
| Ring necked duck | Like a scaup but the stripe is grey, and the back is black |
| Canvasback | Long body, long sloped head, fast, flies in lines and V shapes |
| Redhead | Grey wing stripe, round head, flocks often bunch |
| Common merganser | Long and thin, low, straight, follow the leader lines |
| Hooded merganser | Small, fast, choppy wingbeats |

- Lesser scaup is the scaup that breeds in BC (85%).
```

## Wing patch: the speculum

```diagram mdk-speculum
```

- The [[Speculum]] is the coloured patch on the back of the wing (80%).
- It stays the same all season. The head does not (80%).

```more how to use it, and the classic pairs
- Wing feathers are replaced only once a year, so the wing patch does not change during the season (80%).
- Mallard hen or gadwall hen? Mallard: blue patch with white bars. Gadwall: white square (85%).
- Wigeon or gadwall at a distance? Wigeon: white on the front of the wing. Gadwall: white on the back edge (85%).
- Scaup or ring necked duck? Scaup: white stripe and pale back. Ring necked: grey stripe and black back (85%).
- Blue winged teal and shoveler both show a pale blue forewing. The shoveler bill is huge (85%).
- Blue winged teal mostly leave BC by early September, so they are rare on an October hunt (75%).
- See the older chart too: [Ducks](#/s/ducks/3).
```

## Drake, hen and young

```diagram mdk-eclipse
```

- Summer drakes moult into [[Eclipse plumage]] and look like hens (80%).
- By mid November most mallard drakes are back in colour (75%).

```more eclipse, young birds and aging in the hand
- After breeding, drakes moult into a drab eclipse plumage. Some teal and shovelers still look like hens into late fall (80%).
- In a California wintering study, about 90% of mallard drakes had their breeding colour by mid November (75%).
- Young of the year: tail feathers end in a small notch where the down broke off; adult tails are broader and rounded (75%).
- The notch wears off. In mallards it is unreliable from early September; scaup and redheads keep it into January (70%).
- On a ring necked duck wing: rounded tertial coverts mean an adult; narrow, frayed or notched ones mean a young bird (70%).
- Sex in the hand: drakes are bigger and, on most species, show more colour on the wing and head (80%). Hens of most dabblers have orange in the bill (Tip).
```

## Never shoot these

```diagram mdk-noshot-flow
```

- Loons, grebes and swans: no open season (90%).
- Coots are legal with their own limit of 10 (99%).

```more look alikes, legal and not
- Loons and grebes dive like divers and sit on the same lakes. A loon has a dagger bill and sits low; a grebe is small with a thin pointed bill (Tip). Both have no open season (90%).
- Swans: all white, huge, long necks. Trumpeter swans winter in the BC Interior, more than 2,000 of them (70%). No open season (90%).
- Snow geese are white too, but have black wing tips. Tundra swans have no black on the wing (80%).
- American coot: slate grey, white chicken bill. Not a duck, its own limit of 10 a day, same permit and non toxic shot (99%).
- Mergansers are ducks and count inside your 8 (95%).
- Photos of every protected look alike: [Photo ID gallery, do not shoot](#/s/gallery-waterfowl).
- Rule Book: [limits](#/s/rb-migratory-birds/7).
```

## Ducks in BC by the numbers

```diagram mdk-bc-numbers
```

- BC raises about 1.7 million ducks (75%).
- The Interior holds most of them (75%).

```more numbers, trends and sea ducks
- About 1.7 million ducks breed in BC, about 4% of Canada's breeding waterfowl (75%, Ducks Unlimited).
- The Interior (Ducks Unlimited calls it the BC Intermountain) holds about 65% of BC's breeding ducks, about 1.1 million birds (75%).
- 26 duck species breed there. Up to 8 million waterfowl of 38 species migrate through the Interior (70%).
- More than 60% of the world's Barrow's goldeneyes breed in the BC Interior (70%).
- Trend: Ducks Unlimited lists 8 ducks that declined markedly in BC over 30 years: mallard, pintail, blue winged teal, common goldeneye, ruddy duck, harlequin and both mergansers (65%, older estimate, date not given).
- Spring 2026 BC breeding survey: total ducks up from about 417,000 in 2025 to about 477,000, with mallard first and ring necked duck a close second (60%, search preview of a Delta Waterfowl note; the survey area is not stated). VERIFY.
- Up to 80% of wetlands on private valley bottoms have been drained (70%).
- Sea ducks: surf, white winged and black scoters, long tailed duck and harlequin live mostly on salt water (85%). Harlequins also use fast rocky rivers in summer (80%). Full lesson: [Sea ducks and brant](#/s/sea-ducks).
```

## Memory hooks

| Hook | Means |
|---|---|
| "Tip up, jump up" | Puddle duck |
| "Dive down, run off" | Diver |
| "Wing before head" | Use the speculum, not the head |
| 4, 4, 2, 2 | Pintail, canvasback, goldeneye, harlequin limits (99%) |
| "Dagger, white, chicken" | Loon, swan: no. Coot: 10 |

> [!rule]
> Old hand's rule (opinion, widely taught): "Count the ducks you can name, not the ducks you can see."

```more more hooks
- Number trick: a canvasback weighs about 3 teal (1.35 kg against 0.4 kg, 80%).
- "Blue with white bars, that's the mallard's stars." Gadwall is the only puddle duck with a white patch (85%).
- "Whistle on the wing, goldeneye's the thing." Goldeneye wings whistle in flight (85%).
- Old hand's rule (opinion): "Shape, size, beat, patch. Then the head, if you still need it."
```
