> [!why]
> Ducks put safety first, food second, and they learn fast. Read the flock and you know your next 10 seconds.

```diagram mdk-alarm
```

- Necks up and still: they are about to leave (Tip).
- Freeze the moment you see it (Tip).

```more alarm signals in detail
- Relaxed birds feed, tip up, preen and sleep. Alert birds stretch their necks, stop feeding, bunch up and swim away from the threat (Tip, widely reported).
- Hens of most puddle ducks give loud quacks; the whole flock reacts to an alarm (Tip).
- Mallards put safety first, then food, and keep their trips short (80%, Ducks Unlimited research summary).
- Old hand's rule (opinion): "When the necks go up, your feet stop."
```

## How close they let you come

```photo divers-taking-off Divers need a runway, so they leave early.
```

- No good flush distance numbers for BC ducks (VERIFY).
- Pressured birds flush sooner and farther (Tip).

```more what we know, and what we do not
- No published flush distance for hunted BC ducks was found. Do not trust a fixed number (VERIFY).
- Divers must run along the water to take off, so they tend to swim off or leave early (Tip).
- Puddle ducks jump straight up, so they often sit tight in cover until you are close, then burst (Tip).
- In a West Tennessee study, ducks disturbed in their sanctuaries "hunkered down" and moved less rather than spreading out (80%).
- Hunter meaning: on small water, use cover and the bends to get close. On big open water, decoys work better than sneaking (Tip).
```

## They learn the pressure

```diagram mdk-pressure-map
```

```anim mdk-sanctuary-day A pressured mallard day.
```

- GPS (Global Positioning System) mallards avoid heavily hunted areas in shooting hours (80%).
- About 2 flights a day: refuge, food, refuge (80%).

```more the research
- West Tennessee, winters 2019 to 2022: GPS tracked mallards "clearly avoided heavily hunted areas during shooting hours" and made about two flights a day (80%).
- Researchers heard more than 339,000 shotgun volleys over three seasons (80%).
- Mallards moved between refuges only if they were less than about 16 km (10 miles) apart (80%).
- In Arkansas, tagged mallards stayed away from public hunting areas for the whole season (70%).
- In heavily hunted areas, ducks rest in no hunting zones by day and fly out to feed at night (80%).
- Hunter meaning: hunt the flight line between a refuge and food, at first light and last light (Tip).
```

## They remember places

| Week | Your pond |
|---|---|
| 1 | Hunt it once, a morning |
| 2 | Rest it |
| 3 | Hunt it again |

- "Ducks perceive sanctuary in weeks, not days" (80%).
- Rest a spot a week or more (75%).

```more resting a spot
- Ducks Unlimited researchers advise that a property may need a week or more of rest to improve hunting (75%).
- Short closures (half days, some days off) are a weak substitute for real no hunting areas (80%).
- Hunter meaning: you and your brother should have 3 or 4 spots and rotate them (Tip).
- Old hand's rule (opinion, widely taught): "Never hunt the roost." Shooting where they sleep moves them off it.
```

## Safety in numbers

```photo mallards-flushing Pick one bird. A flock is not a target.
```

- Ducks trust other ducks on the water (Tip).
- That is why decoys work (Tip).

```more flock behaviour
- Ducks often land where other ducks already sit; decoys copy that (Tip, the basis of all decoy hunting).
- Mallards in fall flock loosely; teal flocks twist as one; canvasback fly in lines and V shapes (80%).
- A big flock has more eyes. Small groups and singles decoy more easily (Tip).
- Hunter meaning: set decoys in small family sized groups with a clear landing hole (Tip). Spreads: [Ducks, tactics](#/s/ducks/16).
```

## Pairs, hens and broods

```diagram mdk-pairs
```

- Mallards pair up in fall and early winter (75%).
- The drake leaves early in incubation; the hen raises the brood alone (70%).

```more the social calendar
- Mallard courtship peaks in October and November in a California wintering study. Over 80% of hens were paired by early December (75%).
- In New York, courtship starts in September and about 90% of hens are paired by November (65%).
- Green winged teal pair later: 90% of hens paired by early February (75%).
- The pair flies north together; the hen usually picks the nest site (Tip).
- After a few days of incubation the pair breaks and the drake leaves (70%).
- Hunter meaning: in November, drakes and hens often fly as pairs. Name both before you shoot (Tip).
```

## Old birds and young birds

```diagram mdk-young-old
```

- Young of the year are shot more than adults (80%).
- Late season adults are wary: hide better, call less (Tip).

```more the evidence
- Bellrose and others (1961): juveniles are more vulnerable to hunting than adults, and the gap varies by place, time, year and species (80%).
- The age ratio in the bag rises after a late nesting year (75%).
- A popular summary says juvenile mallard hens are 2 to 3 times more vulnerable (60%, not checked against the original study). VERIFY.
- Old hand's rule (opinion): "Opening week teaches you nothing. December teaches you everything."
```

## Predictable mistakes ducks make

```diagram decoy-spread
```

- They land into the wind (80%).
- They return to yesterday's field and flight line (Tip).

```more the habits you can use
- Landing into the wind: you know which way they will come in (80%).
- Same field, same flight line, day after day while the food lasts (Tip, the basis of field hunting).
- They leave to feed earlier on moonlit, windy evenings (75%).
- Fresh birds after a cold front decoy easily (65%, hunter lore).
- They trust a calm, spread out flock of decoys on the water (Tip).
- Pressured birds go straight to refuges: the hunter who knows the refuge knows the flight line (Tip).
```
