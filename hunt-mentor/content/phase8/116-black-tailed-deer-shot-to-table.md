## The shot

```diagram vitals-deer
```

- Broadside: up the back of the front leg, one third up the chest (85%).
- A small deer: the vital zone is about 20 to 25 cm (75%). Short, sure shots (Tip).

```more placement and range
- Broadside: up the back of the front leg, one third up the chest; both lungs and the top of the heart (85%, [Shot placement](#/s/shot-placement)).
- Quartering away: aim so the bullet exits the far shoulder (85%). Quartering to, head, neck, running: not for your first season (Tip).
- Vital zone with margin: about 20 to 25 cm (8 to 10 in) (75%). A black tail's chest is only about 57 cm high (90%, Nyberg and Janz).
- Brush deflects bullets. Find a clear lane to the chest or wait (Tip).
- A .308 Winchester with a 150 to 165 grain expanding bullet is plenty for a black tail (Tip). A centrefire rifle is legal for deer; no .22 rimfire (99%, page 13).
- Your limit: the range where you hit a 15 cm (6 in) plate 9 of 10 from a field rest (Tip).
```

## After the shot, before the rain

```diagram sk-reactions
```

- Mark where you stood and where it stood (Tip).
- Heart or lungs: wait about 30 minutes, sources agree (70%).

```more waiting and tracking in the wet
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Watch it out of sight, reload, safety on. Drop a phone pin where you shot from and where it stood (Tip).
- Heart or lungs: about 30 minutes. Liver and gut: hours; sources differ (70%, [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)).
- Rain or snow coming: go sooner. Rain can erase blood within about an hour (Tip). Rain also lightens blood (65%).
- Mark the exact spot where it stood with flagging before you move (85%). Lost blood: go back to the last sign and grid search (Tip).
- You must make a real effort to recover a wounded animal (95%, [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)).
- Hunting hours still apply while you track. No shooting after legal light (95%).
- A leashed dog may help track (99% leash rule, page 12; 90% my reading for tracking).
```

## Licence and tag

- Kill, then cancel your licence, then touch the deer (99%).
- The shooter cancels their own licence, never a partner's (99%).
- Leave evidence of sex naturally attached (99%).

```diagram hf-deer-licence
```

```more licence detail
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Cancel the species licence immediately after the kill, before you handle the deer, by the steps printed on it (99%, page 11).
- [[Evidence of sex]] for a deer: the unskinned tail, AND for a buck a testicle or part of the penis, naturally attached (99%, page 15, via [After the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill)).
- Region 2 compulsory inspection is for mountain goat and cougar, not deer. No inspection trip for a black tailed buck (99%, page 28).
- Region 2 limit: 2 deer a licence year, at most 1 antlerless; in 2-8, 2-18 and 2-19 the only rows are bucks (99%, pages 27 and 28).
```

## Gut it

```steps Gut a deer in 8 steps
sk-gut-1 | On its back, head uphill, gloves on.
sk-gut-2 | Cut around the anus and tie it off.
sk-gut-3 | Belly cut, blade edge up, two fingers guiding.
sk-gut-4 | Roll the guts out to one side.
sk-gut-5 | Cut the diaphragm, then the windpipe high.
sk-gut-6 | Pull it all out through the back.
sk-gut-7 | Drain, wipe dry, prop it open.
sk-gut-8 | Cool it in shade, off the ground.
```

- Licence first, knife second (99%).
- Wipe the cavity dry with clean towels (80%).

```more gutting in the rain (Tips)
- Gut fast: heat leaves through the open body (Tip).
- Wipe out blood and dry the cavity as completely as you can with clean towels (80%, WDFW, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife).
- Keep rainwater out: work under a tarp or with the cavity tipped down when you can (Tip).
- Gutless method suits a long pack out: [Field dressing](#/s/field-dressing).
```

## Meat: cool, clean, dry

```diagram mbt-wet-meat
```

- Below 4°C (40°F) as fast as you can (80%).
- No plastic or tarps on meat until it is chilled (80%).

```more meat care in wet and cold weather
- Cool wild game to below 40°F (4°C) as quickly as possible; chilling on the day of the kill is ideal (80%, WDFW).
- "Carcasses should not be placed in plastic bags or tarps before being thoroughly chilled" (80%, WDFW). Plastic traps heat.
- "Make sure the carcass is clean and dry before transport" (80%, WDFW).
- Rain: a tarp as a roof above the meat, with an air gap; never wrapped around it (80%, [Meat care](#/s/meat-care)).
- Cannot hang it? Lay it on logs or rocks and prop the cavity open with sticks for air (80%, WDFW).
- Ageing: 34 to 37°F (1 to 3°C), no longer than two weeks (80%, WDFW). No cold room? Cut and freeze within a few days (Tip).
- Above 4°C overnight? Bags of ice in the cavity or a cooler with ice on the bottom and the meat on top, drained daily (Tip, [Meat care](#/s/meat-care)). Typical November night temperatures at Harrison: VERIFY.
```

## Pack it out

```diagram mbt-size-bars
```

- A buck is 48 to 90 kg alive (90%). Plan for two people or quarters (Tip).
- Keep the trip short if you are solo (Tip).

```more pack out detail
- Black tailed bucks: about 48 to 90 kg live weight (90%, BC deer account).
- Quarter it if the pack out is long; bone out to cut weight (Tip, [Packing out](#/s/packing-out)).
- In rain and cold, stop before you are soaked and shaking. Get dry and warm first (Tip, [Cold injuries](#/s/cold-injuries)).
- Evidence of sex stays attached to the meat on the way home (99%, page 15).
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom, not law): "Notch, gut, dry, hang, roof. Then make the coffee."

> [!mistake]
> - Taking a brushy shot in the rain and losing the trail an hour later.
> - Wrapping warm meat in a garbage bag or tarp.
> - Forgetting to cancel the licence before dragging the deer.

> [!field]
> Pack the wet kit before the hunt: game bags, a small tarp and cord, clean towels, flagging tape, a spare dry layer.

```checklist mbt-wet-kit
Game bags
Small tarp and cord for a roof
Clean towels to dry the cavity
Flagging tape
Spare dry layer and a warm hat
Knife, gloves, licence and a pen
```
