## Tracks

```diagram sg-hoof-grid
```

- Heart shape, points show direction (85%).
- One print will not tell white tail from mule deer (60%).

```more track detail
- Two toes, heart shaped, pointed end forward (85%).
- White tail front track about 4 to 10 cm long (70%). Often narrower with tips close together (60%).
- Dewclaws show in mud, snow or when running (75%).
- Big, deep, splayed prints with a wide straddle may be a buck, but a big doe fools people (60%, hunter lore).
- Measure, photograph with a scale, and log it: [Measuring tracks](#/s/rs-track-measuring).
```

## Track patterns

```diagram mwt-track-gaits
```

- Gallop: groups of 4, hind feet ahead (75%).
- Stot: tight groups of 4 means mule deer (85%).

```more pattern detail
- **Walk:** prints in a near line; the hind foot lands in or near the front print (75%).
- **Trot:** same line, longer spacing (70%).
- **Gallop:** groups of 4 with hind prints landing ahead of the front prints; long gaps between groups (75%).
- **Stot** (mule deer): all 4 feet land together in tight groups, groups about 2.4 to 4.6 m apart (60%).
- A gallop trail leading to cover: you spooked it, or something did (Tip).
```

## Droppings

```gallery
hab-deer-pellets-fresh | Fresh: shiny, dark, soft outside.
hab-deer-pellets-old | Old: dull, dry, crumbly.
```

- Oval pellets about 1 to 1.5 cm (70%).
- Shiny and moist = hours old (Tip).

```more droppings detail
- Pellets oval, often with a point at one end, about 1 to 1.5 cm (70%).
- Firm separate pellets in fall and winter; clumped, softer in summer on green feed (70%).
- A deer drops about 13 to 34 pellet groups a day (70%).
- Lots of pellets in one spot: a bedding or feeding area (Tip).
```

## Beds

```diagram mwt-bed-sign
```

- Pressed oval about 1 m, hair, melted frost (75%).
- Pellets beside it: the deer stood and dropped them (Tip).

```more bed detail
- A bed is a flattened oval in grass, leaves or snow, about 1 m long (75%).
- Does and fawns bed in groups; several beds side by side (75%).
- A single big bed, often on a little rise with a view and the wind at its back, may be a buck (Tip, hunter observation).
- Mississippi collared bucks used about 31 bedding areas in a season, and repeat beds had dense screening cover (65%).
- Never walk through bedding cover to check beds in season. Scout it in late winter (Tip).
- More: [Beds and bedding areas](#/s/rs-beds) and [Rubs and scrapes](#/s/rs-rubs-scrapes).
```

## Rubs

```photo hab-deer-rub
```

- Bark shaved off a sapling at knee to waist height (60%).
- White tails rub far more than mule deer (60%).

```more rub detail
- A buck rubs bark off small trees and shrubs with his antlers and forehead (75%).
- The **forehead gland** between the antler bases leaves scent on rubs (65%).
- First rubs in September as velvet comes off; most in October (70%).
- Fresh: pale wet wood, curled bark, sap. Old: grey, dry (Tip).
- A **rub line** is several rubs along a travel route (Tip).
- Big rubs on big trees are often made by bigger bucks, but young bucks rub big trees too (Tip, opinion).
```

## Scrapes

```diagram mwt-scrape-anatomy
```

- Pawed dirt under a low licking branch (75%).
- The branch at about 1.2 to 1.5 m is the key (65%).

```more scrape detail
- A buck paws a patch of bare dirt, often urinates in it, and chews and rubs the branch above with his face (75%).
- The branch carries scent from the **preorbital gland** at the corner of the eye and the forehead gland (65%).
- Ozoga's Michigan study: a branch about 1.2 to 1.5 m (4 to 5 ft) over open ground could get bucks to scrape on that spot (65%, via Mossy Oak).
- Nearly every deer in the area may visit an active scrape (60%, Alsheimer).
- Scrapes appear from mid to late October and get worked mostly at night (60%).
- **Tarsal glands** (inside of the hocks): bucks rub urinate over them; the tufts darken and smell strong in the rut (65%).
- Many scrapes along a field edge: a buck is cruising that edge (Tip).
```

## Trails and browse

```gallery
hab-deer-trail-reeds | Trail: packed soil through tall grass.
hab-deer-trail-snow | Snow trail: count prints, read direction.
```

- Main trails link bedding cover to food (75%).
- Browse: torn, ragged twig ends (75%).

```more trail and browse detail
- Trails are worn paths from bedding brush to field edges, creek crossings and fence gaps (75%).
- The most used trail is not always the best stand: bucks often travel a parallel trail just inside cover (Tip).
- Deer have no upper front teeth, so browsed twigs are torn and ragged (75%). Hares cut clean at 45 degrees (75%).
- Over half of the twigs on a bush nipped off: too many deer for the food (80%, Montana State University Extension).
- More: [Funnels](#/s/funnels) and [Reading sign](#/s/sign).
```

## How fresh is it?

```diagram sg-track-age
```

| Sign | Fresh | Old |
|---|---|---|
| Track | Sharp edges (Tip) | Rounded, debris in it |
| Pellets | Shiny, soft (Tip) | Dull, dry |
| Rub | Pale wood, sap | Grey wood |
| Scrape | Wet dirt, no leaves | Leaves in it |

- Compare with a print you make yourself (Tip).

```more aging sign detail
- Make your own boot print next to the track and watch it age over the day (Tip).
- Rain or frost since the track was made? Then it is older than that (Tip).
- Snow: fresh snow tracks with sharp edges are hours old (Tip). See [Snow tracking](#/s/snow-tracking).
- More: [Aging tracks and sign](#/s/rs-track-aging).
```

## Follow a track or a blood trail

```steps Follow it in 4 steps
mwt-follow-1 | Mark where it stood with flag tape.
mwt-follow-2 | Find the first sign. Mark it.
mwt-follow-3 | Walk beside the trail. Look ahead.
mwt-follow-4 | Lost it? Small loops from the last sign.
```

- Never walk on the sign (Tip).
- Dogs may track deer only on a leash (99%).

```more following detail
- Mark every 10 to 20 m with tape so you can see the line of travel (Tip).
- One person on the sign, one looking ahead (Tip, hunting with your brother).
- Wounded deer often head for thick cover and water, and downhill (Tip).
- A dog may be used to track your deer, but it must be on a leash (99%, synopsis page 12, via [Legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)).
- You must make all reasonable effort to find a wounded animal (95%, via [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)).

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram rubs-scrapes
```

- **Rhyme:** "Rub on the tree, scrape on the ground, lick on the branch where the buck comes round."
- **Number trick:** 1 m bed, 1 to 1.5 cm pellet, 1.2 to 1.5 m branch.
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Old sign tells you where they were. Fresh sign tells you where to sit."

```more more hooks, mistakes and field drill
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, widely taught): "Look at the trail, not the track." One print lies; a trail tells the story.
- **Rhyme:** "Shiny and wet, the deer's close yet."

> [!mistake]
> - Calling the species from one track.
> - Walking right down the trail you plan to hunt and leaving your scent on it.
> - Sitting over an old rub line from last year.

> [!field]
> Walk one field edge in MU (Management Unit) 3-27 on a dry day. Count trails crossing it, fresh rubs and scrapes. Pin each on the Hunt Map.
```
