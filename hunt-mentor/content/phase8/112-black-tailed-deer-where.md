> [!why]
> Coast deer do not spread out evenly. Food, cover and snow pull them to a few places each season. Know the places and you stop walking empty forest.

```diagram mbt-year-places
```

- Spring and summer: logged openings, new green food (90%).
- Winter: old growth and steep south to west slopes (90%).

```more the year in detail
- "Black-tailed deer ... make frequent use of logged habitats during spring and summer" (90%, BC deer account 2000).
- In summer most deer move higher for new growth; some stay low all year (90%). Some coast deer go as high as subalpine and alpine in summer (90%, Nyberg and Janz 1990).
- Winter: old growth is a key part of coast winter range and "critical for their survival": it shelters, holds snow off the ground and drops branches and lichens to eat (90%, BC deer account).
- Farther north and higher up, steep south to west facing sites make the best winter range (90%).
- Snow deeper than 30 cm is hard going for deer (90%).
- November near Harrison: rut, plus the first snow up high. Snow up high is likely to push deer down toward winter range (Tip). The usual first snow date at Harrison elevations: VERIFY.
- Earlier chapter: [Meet the deer](#/s/mc-black-tailed-deer-01).
```

## Stay home or move?

```diagram mbt-resident-migrant
```

- Some deer stay in one area all year; some move between summer and winter ranges (90%).
- The deeper the snow, the more deer migrate (90%).

```more residents, migrants and home ranges
- South coast study data, mostly Vancouver Island (90%, Nyberg and Janz 1990):
  - Seasonal ranges of migrants are often 2 to 5 km apart.
  - Moderate snowpack zone: about 20 to 25% of deer migrate. Deep snowpack zone: 70% or more.
  - Summer home ranges of residents: about 55 ha.
  - Resident deer in the moderate zone: about 140 ha a year. Migrants there: about 1,770 ha a year.
  - Winter ranges: 48 ha in the deep zone, 36 ha in the shallow zone.
- WDFW (Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife): black tails normally live in about 1.3 to 7.8 km2 (half to 3 square miles); in the mountains they move lower for winter (80%).
- What it means for you: a deer you see in October is probably close by in November, unless snow moves it (Tip).
- Which zone is your spot in? Use the elevation bands on the next screen (Tip).
```

## Snow decides winter

```diagram mbt-snow-canopy
```

- Same snowfall: about 50 cm in a clearcut, about 5 cm under 70% conifer cover (90%).
- Snow makes walking costly for deer; cover saves them energy (90%).

```more snow numbers
- All from Nyberg and Janz 1990, south coast data (90%):
  - An adult black tail's chest is about 57 cm high.
  - Once a deer sinks past about 40% of its chest height, energy use climbs fast. Sinking more than 25 cm restricts coast black tails a lot.
  - A doe sinking 30 cm spends about 6 times the energy of one sinking 10 cm. A fawn sinking 30 cm: 8 to 10 times.
  - A late winter 50 cm of snow in a fresh clearcut left about 5 cm under a conifer stand with 70% crown closure. Walking cost: about 500% more in the clearcut, about 10% more in the forest.
  - In cold, snowy spells deer used less than 40% of the area they used in mild winters.
  - Red huckleberry and salal grow tallest under moderately closed canopies. Salal is buried faster by snow than red huckleberry.
- What it means for you: after a heavy snow, look under big timber and on sunny slopes, not in open cuts (Tip).
```

## Harrison's deer winter range

```diagram mbt-uwr-slope
```

- An [[UWR]] (Ungulate Winter Range) order covers deer winter range in the Chilliwack district: U-2-006 (95%).
- Its polygons sit on both sides of Harrison Lake and in the Chehalis area (85%, my reading of the map layer).

```more what the order says
- Order U-2-006, made under the Government Actions Regulation, sets ungulate winter range for mule deer and Columbian black tailed deer in the Fraser TSA (Timber Supply Area), Chilliwack district (95%, read directly).
- Approved 22 September 2009, amended May 2011; about 31,838 ha (95%, BC approved UWR list).
- "Capable winter range": warm aspects 135 to 285 degrees (south east through south to west), slopes 40 to 100%, not shaded by nearby hillsides; gentle snow free slopes in the shallow zone count too (95%).
- Snow zones in the order: shallow under 400 m, moderate 400 to 800 m, deep over 800 m (95%).
- Hunt Mentor read the government shapefile: 115 polygons, all coded for mule deer (black tail and mule deer). Groups sit east of Harrison Lake, west of it toward the Chehalis, north of the lake and up the Fraser Canyon (85%, my reading of polygon positions).
- A UWR limits logging and roads. It does not close hunting by itself (85%, my reading). Hunting rules: [Your Harrison deer hunt](#/s/local-harrison-deer).
- More: [Winter ranges and the law](#/s/winter-range-legal).
```

## Food, by season

```diagram mbt-food
```

- Winter: Douglas fir, redcedar, red huckleberry, salal, deer fern, tree lichens (90%).
- Late spring to fall: grasses, forbs, shrub leaves (90%).

```gallery
hab-salal | Salal: thick oval leaves. Winter food and cover.
```

```more food and where to find it
- Winter and early spring foods: Douglas fir, western redcedar, red huckleberry, salal, deer fern and lichens that grow on trees (90%, BC deer account).
- Late spring to fall: grasses, trailing blackberry, fireweed, pearly everlasting and other forbs; leaves of willow, salmonberry, salal, maple and other shrubs and trees (90%).
- Deer pick food carefully by taste, smell and sight (90%, Nyberg and Janz).
- Wind thrown trees and fresh slash drop lichens and twigs in winter (85%, my reading of "broken branches and the lichens" in the BC account).
- Plant photos: [Browse plants](#/s/browse-plants).
```

## Your November map plan

```diagram hf-cutblock-edge
```

- Mark young cutblocks next to standing timber (Tip).
- Mark steep south to west slopes in big timber below snow (Tip).

```more how to use the Hunt Map for this
- Open the [Hunt Map](#/map). Turn on the cutblock and land layers. Look for young cuts with timber on at least one side (Tip).
- Chapter 5 has why: coast deer use most of a cut within 100 m of cover (90%, Nyberg and Janz).
- Check the slope direction with the map's contour lines: south to west facing, steep, in timber (Tip).
- Check no shooting areas and closed roads: [Your Harrison deer hunt](#/s/local-harrison-deer).
- Every spot is a candidate. Scout it before the hunt (Tip).
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom, not law): "Green food in the open, big trees close by, sun on the slope. Find all three and sit."

> [!mistake]
> - Hunting the middle of a big clearcut after a snow.
> - Walking flat dark timber with no food in it.
> - Taking a polygon on a map as a promise of deer. It is a guide, not a count.

> [!field]
> Scouting day: find one young cutblock edge and one steep sunny timbered slope within 1 km of the truck. Look for tracks and droppings on both.
