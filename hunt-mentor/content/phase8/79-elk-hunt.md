## Scouting

```diagram mc-elk-scout
```

- Map, drive, listen, walk, plan (Tip).
- August and early September scouting pays most (Tip).

```more scouting step by step
1. **Map at home:** burns 3 to 15 years old, cutblocks 5 to 20 years old, benches of 15 to 30% next to timber, water and wet meadows (Tip, chapter 7). [Map scouting](#/s/map-scouting).
2. **Drive the roads:** check gates, closures, private land, ATV (all terrain vehicle) rules. Note where the trucks park; plan to walk past them (Tip). [Land status](#/s/maps-land-status), [ATV rules](#/s/atv-rules).
3. **Listen:** in September, sit a ridge at first and last light and just listen for bugles (Tip).
4. **Walk:** find wallows, rubs, fresh trails and beds; keep it short and on a good wind (Tip). Do not hang around wallows (Tip).
5. **Plan:** one glassing seat and one approach route per wind direction (Tip).
- Trail cameras: a wireless (cell) camera may not be used to hunt from 1 August to 10 December; a camera that only stores photos on its card is not a wireless camera (99% and 95% my reading, page 11, [unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts)).
```

## Glassing

```diagram glassing-grid
```

- Sit high across a basin or burn at first light (Tip).
- Binoculars find elk; the scope counts points (Tip).

```anim hf-glass-point Glass the openings from across the draw.
```

```more glassing in detail
- Pale bodies and dark necks show a long way off, especially in low sun (Tip). See the distance photo in chapter 1.
- Grid the slope: edges first, then openings, then shadows in timber (Tip). [Glassing](#/s/glassing).
- Count tines through the spotting scope, one antler at a time, with the bull standing (Tip). The brow counts (99%).
- Glass the last 30 minutes of light; plan the stalk for the morning if the herd beds near (Tip).
- Glassing beats walking: elk cannot smell or hear you at a kilometre (Tip).
```

## Spot and stalk

```anim stalk-wind Keep the wind in your face.
```

- Stalk the wind, not the elk (Tip).
- In a herd, every head is a lookout (Tip).

```more spot and stalk step by step
1. Find the herd from far away; pick out the bull and count one antler (Tip).
2. Decide if he is legal and if the shot can be clean before you move (Tip).
3. Note landmarks near the herd: a dead tree, a rock. Things look different up close (Tip).
4. Plan a route with the wind in your face or across, out of sight, using dead ground (Tip).
5. Move fast where they cannot see, slow at the end (Tip).
6. Last 200 m: glass every few steps for cows you did not see (Tip).
7. Shot only if broadside, in range, still, with a safe backstop (chapter 10).
- Skills: [spot and stalk](#/s/stalking-footwork), [stay unseen](#/s/stay-unseen).
```

## Calling: cow calls and bugles

```diagram mc-elk-partner
```

- Learn the cow mew first (Tip).
- Bulls often circle downwind to the call (65%).

```video

yt-elk-bugle | Bugle step by step.
```

```anim mc-elk-call-arc Bulls circle downwind to check the call.
```

```more calling step by step
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Legal: mouth calls, hand calls, bugle tubes. Not legal: recorded or electronic calls for elk (99%, page 12, [legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)).
1. Locate: listen first; bugle or cow call softly only if nothing talks (Tip).
2. Set up downwind or crosswind of the bull, in front of cover, with shooting lanes (65%, calling guides).
3. Shooter in front, caller 50 to 100 m behind (65% for the setup; spacing Tip).
4. Start soft: cow mews and chirps. Bugle sparingly; a herd bull may take his cows away (Tip).
5. Bulls often circle downwind to scent check the call. Watch that side (65%, RMEF (Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation) calling champion's "ARC" rule).
6. Shooter draws or shoulders only when the bull's head is behind a tree (65%).
7. Wait 20 to 30 minutes after calling; bulls can come in silent (Tip).
- A cow call can stop a walking bull for the shot (Tip).
- Do not call at a bull you cannot get the wind right on. Back out (Tip).
```

## Sit a wallow or water

```diagram mc-elk-wallow-read
```

- Sit downwind, screened, away from the trail in (70%).
- Afternoons in early September (Tip).

```more wallow hunting step by step
1. Find an active wallow: cloudy water, fresh tracks, rubs on trails leading in (70%, RMEF (Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation)).
2. Check it with as little scent as possible; do not walk through it (Tip).
3. Set up downwind, screened by brush, away from the main trail in (70%). A ground blind or a tree stand works; a tree stand lifts your scent (70%, RMEF).
4. Sit mid day to dark in early September, when bulls wallow before the main rut (70% for timing; hours Tip).
5. Splashing or grunting: close in fast while he is busy (70%).
- Mineral licks and water in dry country work the same way (Tip).
```

## Still hunting timber

```anim hf-step-cadence A few steps, then a long look.
```

- A few steps, then a long look (Tip).
- Late season elk live in the timber (90%).

```more still hunting in detail
- Pressured elk hold in timber by day and feed at night (90%, BC account). Late in the season, that is where they are (Tip).
- Pace: 3 steps, then look for 20 seconds or more (Tip). [Still hunting](#/s/still-hunting).
- Only on a steady wind in your face. Swirling wind: go glass instead (75%).
- Look for parts: a leg, an ear flick, the cream rump through the trees (Tip).
- Near beds, slow further; a bedded herd has eyes on all sides (Tip).
```

## Hunting with your brother

```diagram mc-elk-partner
```

- Caller and shooter; one watches, one moves (Tip).
- Agree hand signals and the shot plan first (Tip).

```more partner work in detail
- Calling team: shooter in front toward the elk and downwind, caller behind (65%).
- Glass and guide: one glasses from a high seat and signals; the other stalks (Tip).
- Agree before the hunt: who shoots, which bull, what range, what happens after the shot (Tip). [Hunting partner](#/s/hunting-partner).
- One elk, two people, many loads. Split packs and plan the meat before you go (Tip, chapter 10).
- Record of Receipt when your brother carries your meat (90%, [after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill)).
```

## Decide on the day

```diagram mc-elk-decision
```

- Five yes answers, or no shot (Tip).
- Count one antler. The brow counts (99%).

```more decision notes
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- 6 point bull: at least 6 tines on one antler; each at least 2.5 cm and longer than wide; the brow tine counts (99%, pages 3 and 4).
- Hunting hours: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (99%, page 11).
- Before the shot, also think: where will he fall, can we get him out before the meat spoils (Tip)?
- Wind went bad? Back out. A spooked herd is a lost week (Tip).
- Rule Book: [Region 8](#/s/rb-region-8), [definitions](#/s/rb-definitions), [legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods).
```

## What a 40 year hunter does differently

```diagram mc-elk-old-hand
```

- Walks past the crowd and hunts the wind (Tip).
- Plans the pack out before the shot (Tip).

```more the old hand's habits
- Walks farther from roads than anyone else. Elk avoid people and vehicles by 500 to 1,500 m (85%, chapter 4).
- Hunts the wind first and the elk second (Tip).
- Listens at first light before moving a step (Tip).
- Calls less and waits longer (Tip).
- Knows the herd from August scouting (Tip).
- Plans the pack out, the cooler and the help before the shot (Tip).
- Counts one antler twice, then the brow, then once more (Tip).
- Takes the close, broadside shot and passes the rest (Tip). [Ethics and fair chase](#/s/ethics-fair-chase).
- All widely taught hunter wisdom: opinion.
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-elk-method-picker
```

- **Rhyme:** "Listen first, then call soft, keep the shooter out in front."
- **Number trick:** 50 to 100: metres between caller and shooter (Tip).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "When you think you have waited long enough, wait 20 minutes more."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Wind in the face, bull in the place."
- **Number trick:** 1 to 9 and 10 to 20: bow days in September; rifle from the 10th to 20 October in Region 8 (99%).
- **Number trick:** 6 on one: tines on one antler, brow counted (99%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Call less than you want to."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "An elk you hear is an elk you can hunt. An elk you see at a kilometre is already gone." (quick lesson)

> [!mistake]
> - Bugling hard at a herd bull with cows. He takes them away.
> - Standing up the moment the calling stops.
> - Stalking with the wind at your back because the path is easier.

> [!field]
> Practise the cow mew with a video until your brother cannot tell yours from the recording. Then practise the shooter and caller setup in a park.
```
