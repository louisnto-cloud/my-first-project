## Buck, doe, fawn


- Ears about two thirds of the head length (90%).
- Buck: hard antlers fall to winter. Doe: none (90%).

```more ID by sex and age, the full list
- **Ears:** large, about two thirds the length of the head, black edged, white inside (90%, BC deer account 2000).
- **Face:** dark brown forehead, whitish face, black muzzle, white throat patch (90%).
- **Rump and tail:** large white rump patch, narrow tail with a black tip (90%).
- **Coat:** tawny brown in summer, dark or grizzled grey brown in winter (90%).
- **Buck** (male): antlers grow April to August in velvet, hard from about September, drop January to March (90%). Chapter 5 has the antler year.
- **Doe** (female): no antlers, slimmer neck, longer looking face (Tip).
- **Fawn** (under 1 year): born late May through June with white spots (90%). Spots fade with the first fall coat (75%). By October about half a doe's size (75%, quick lesson).
- **Yearling buck** (about 1.5 years): most have 2 points a side (90%, BC account).
```

## Age a buck on the hoof

```diagram mmd-buck-ages
```

- Yearling: most have 2 points a side (90%).
- Point count is a poor age guide. Read the body (90%).

```gallery
g-md-forkhorn | Young fork: about 2 points a side.
g-md-young-bucks | Young bucks: thin necks, small racks.
g-md-buck-4pt | Mature: deep chest, thick neck.
```

```more antlers, body and the 4 point count
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- BC account (90%):
  - Most yearling mule deer have 2 points on each side.
  - Two year olds grow large forks or 3 points.
  - Older bucks have 4, 5 or more points plus a small eye guard (brow tine) near the base.
  - "The number of antler points is not a reliable way to determine the age."
- Mule deer antlers fork once about 15 to 25 cm (6 to 10 in) above the base, then each branch forks again. Brow tines are small or absent, even on mature bucks (85%, Mule Deer Working Group fact sheet 27).
- By about age 4, antlers reach about 80 to 90% of their peak size (85%, Mule Deer Working Group fact sheet 24).
- Body clues for an older buck: deep chest, thick neck that blends into the shoulders, belly that sags, legs that look short for the body (Tip, hunter consensus). A young buck looks like "a doe with antlers" (Tip).
- Legal count: a [[4 point buck]] has at least 4 tines on one antler, not counting the [[Brow tine]] (99%, synopsis page 4, see [Region 3 rules](#/s/rb-region-3)). Count one side. Then count again.
```

```more the look alike table and the legal point
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

| Look alike | Tell it by | Legal point |
|---|---|---|
| White tailed deer | Wide brown tail, white flag; antler tines rise off one main beam; gallops, no stot (85%) | Separate species licence and seasons (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-bag-limits)) |
| Black tailed deer (coast) | Tail dark brown or black for most of its length, smaller rump patch, smaller body (90%) | Same species as mule deer; the synopsis says "mule (black-tailed) deer" (99%). Region 2 has its own limits ([Region 2](#/s/rb-region-2)) |
| Mule deer and black tail hybrids | Mixed traits in some valleys where the two meet (90%) | Treated as mule deer in the synopsis wording (Tip, VERIFY with the Rule Book) |
| Elk cow or calf | Much bigger, dark neck mane, yellow rump, no black tail tip (85%) | Not open in MUs (Management Units) 3-27 and 3-28 (99%, page 34) |
| White tail and mule deer hybrid | Rare; mixed tail and antler traits (70%) | When in doubt, do not shoot (Tip) |

- Mule deer bucks are on a "1 mule deer buck for Regions 3 to 8 combined" limit (99%, page 14, see [bag limits](#/s/rb-bag-limits)).
```

## Size and weight

```diagram mmd-size-bars
```

- Buck 68 to 113 kg; doe 50 to 75 kg (90%).
- Shoulder height 90 to 95 cm (90%).

```more size details and what it means for you
- BC numbers (90%, BC deer account 2000):
  - Mule deer stand 90 to 95 cm at the shoulder.
  - Bucks 68 to 113 kg; bucks in peak condition up to 180 kg.
  - Does 50 to 75 kg.
  - Black tailed bucks 48 to 90 kg; does 40 to 65 kg. Sitka black tails are smaller and darker.
  - Fawns weigh 2.7 to 4 kg at birth.
- US park figures for comparison: bucks 57 to 114 kg, does 45 to 60 kg, 1.2 to 2.1 m nose to tail (80%, US National Park Service, Bryce Canyon).
- Weight swings a lot by season: bucks lose weight in the rut (90%). Chapter 2 has the fat year.
- What it means for you: a field dressed buck is still a heavy load for one person. Plan a partner or quarter it (Tip). Meat math is in chapter 10.
```

## Three races in BC

```diagram mmd-bc-subspecies
```

- Interior: mule deer. Coast: black tails. Haida Gwaii: Sitka (90%).
- The Coast Range splits them, mostly in winter (90%).

```more the three races and hybrids
- One species, Odocoileus hemionus, three races (subspecies) in BC (90%, BC deer account 2000):
  - **Mule deer** (O. h. hemionus): most of the interior, east of the Coast Mountains to Alberta.
  - **Columbian black tailed deer** (O. h. columbianus): the coast, Vancouver Island, Fraser Valley, Sechelt Peninsula.
  - **Sitka black tailed deer** (O. h. sitkensis): north coast. Introduced to the Queen Charlotte Islands (Haida Gwaii) in the early 1900s.
- Where ranges meet they interbreed; deer in some intermediate valleys look mixed (90%).
- The BC account calls the black tail "a species in the making" because of its long isolation on the coast (90%).
- Mule deer are most common in the southern interior and the northeast (85%, BC habitat model 2003).
- Your bases: Heffley Creek and the South Okanagan are mule deer country. The Fraser Valley near Mission is black tail country (85%, my reading of the ranges above).
```

## How many, and the trend

```diagram mmd-numbers
```

- About 100,000 to 170,000 in BC (85%).
- Harvest is far below the 1960s peak (90%).

```more numbers, trend and why
- BC account 2000: the interior "has about 165,000 Mule Deer"; 20,000 to 25,000 on northern ranges (90%).
- WAFWA (Western Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies) 2020 status report: BC mule deer 100,000 to 170,000; harvest 9,986, 87% males; 58,230 hunters (tags) (85%).
- Black tailed deer: 150,000 to 250,000 in recent decades (90%, BC account 2000).
- Deer harvest peaked in the late 1960s at about 75,000 (mule and black tailed), then fell to about 20,000 a year by 2000 (90%). The account blames habitat loss, predation and fewer hunters, not over hunting.
- Trend worries: buck to doe ratios are close to or below the provincial goal of 20 bucks per 100 does after the hunt (85%, WAFWA 2020).
- The Southern Interior Mule Deer Project (SIMDeer, BC Wildlife Federation and partners) studies the decline across 30,000 km2 of the southern Interior (85%). Cougars were the top cause of death of collared adult does and older fawns (85%).
- In the Thompson study area, almost entirely burned in 2017, adult doe survival was 82% and overwinter fawn survival 63% over two years (85%, WAFWA 2020).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mmd-tail-compare
```

- **Rhyme:** "Ears like a mule, forks on forks, rope tail, black tip, bounce like a pogo."
- **Number trick:** 2, 3, 4: yearling 2 points a side, 2 year old 3, older 4 plus (90%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Count one side twice, then decide."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "White rump shows, mule deer goes; wide white flag, white tail's tag."
- **Number trick:** 68 to 113: a buck's weight in kilograms; 2 thirds: the ear to head ratio (90%).
- **Number trick:** 20 per 100: BC's goal, 20 bucks per 100 does after the hunt (85%).
- **Number trick:** 22: hunter days per mule deer in MU (Management Unit) 3-27, 2020 to 2024 (90%, computed).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely taught hunter education wisdom, opinion): "If you are not sure, you are not shooting." Be 100% sure of species, sex and points.
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Read the body before the bone." Neck, belly and legs age a buck faster than the rack.

> [!mistake]
> - Forgetting that a Fraser Valley deer is a black tail under the Region 2 rules.
> - Counting both antlers together for a 4 point buck. Count one side.
> - Judging age by points alone.

> [!field]
> Next drive at dusk: for every deer you see, say out loud "buck or doe, age, how many points a side".
```
