## An edge animal

```diagram mmd-three-needs
```

- Needs food, hiding cover and winter thermal cover close together (85%).
- Hunt the edges where they meet (Tip).

```more habitat needs in detail
- Mule deer are an "ecotonal species" that favour high contrast forest edges with lots of forage close to escape and thermal cover (85%, BC habitat model 2003).
- Typical habitats: open conifer forest, climax brush, aspen stands, steep broken terrain and river valleys (85%).
- Security (hiding) habitat: rugged broken terrain, wooded valleys, shrubby draws, steep slopes, dense trees or shrubs (85%).
- Good hiding cover hides about 90% of a deer at 60 m or less, in patches 180 m or more across (80%, Thomas 1979 via the model).
- Fire and logging open the forest and grow shrubs; burns and young cutblocks are good summer food (85%).
- The 2017 and 2018 fires boosted summer forage but cut winter cover in places (85%, WAFWA, Western Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies 2020).
```

- Browse is the bulk of the year's diet (85%).
- Winter near Kamloops: Douglas fir peaks December and January (85%).

```more the plant table
| Season | Main foods (certainty) |
|---|---|
| Spring | New grass, forbs: balsamroot, clover, wild strawberry, fireweed (90%) |
| Summer | Forbs and leaves of many shrubs (90%) |
| Fall | Forbs, shrub leaves and twigs, fresh grass after rain; grass under 10% near Kamloops (85%) |
| Winter | Shrubs and Douglas fir: big sagebrush, bitterbrush, rabbitbrush, snowbrush, saskatoon, rose (90%) |

- Kamloops stomach study (Willms 1976, September to April): grass under 10% until spring; more forbs in fall; shrubs early fall and late winter; Douglas fir peaks December and January (85%).
- Central interior winter ranges: diets 45 to 76% conifers; Douglas fir 24 to 73% (85%, Waterhouse 1994 via the model).
- Winter diet elsewhere: 79% browse, 15% grasses, 6% forbs (85%, Cowan 1947 via the model).
- Other important plants: willow, chokecherry, soopolallie, snowberry, Oregon grape, aspen, rose, alfalfa (85%, model table; quick lesson).
- Learn 3 by sight first: saskatoon, red osier dogwood, Oregon grape (Tip).
```

## Winter range

```diagram mmd-winter-canopy
```

- South and west slopes, low, gentle, old Douglas fir (85%).
- Crowns catch snow; canopy about 65 to 70% (85%).

```more winter range in detail
- Interior winter range: shrublands in the dry forest zone and steep south and west facing broken terrain (90%, BC account).
- Thermal habitat: warm aspects, gentle to moderate slopes, low elevations, Douglas fir dominant (85%, BC habitat model citing Bunnell 1990 and Armleder 1986).
- Good stands: long crowns that intercept snow; canopy about 65 to 70% closure with small openings; layers of younger fir underneath (85%).
- Snow over about 50 cm shuts deer out; 25 to 30 cm hinders (85%).
- Some interior provincial benchmark winter habitats are rated class 1 in the ponderosa pine and very dry interior Douglas fir zones (85%, model table).
- Winter range is fragile. Respect closures and do not push wintering deer (85%, Mule Deer Working Group fact sheet 17).
```

## Elevation by month

```diagram mmd-elev-months
```

- Low in winter, high in summer, moving in spring and fall (85 to 90%).
- Snow sets the timing each year (85%).

```more elevation in detail
- "In summer, most deer migrate to higher elevations ... but some remain at low elevations all year" (90%, BC account).
- In mountains of BC, mule deer use upper elevation and alpine habitats in summer, lower valleys and south slopes in winter (85%, BC habitat model).
- A Kamloops winter range study area sat below about 760 m (80%, Willms 1976).
- Near Tranquille, ponderosa pine and Douglas fir zones run about 610 to 1,160 m (70%, ecological reserve description, search preview).
- Rough local heights (VERIFY on the map): valley about 370 to 450 m, Heffley Lake about 950 m, Sun Peaks village about 1,250 m (55%).
- No snow yet in October: bucks often mid slope, about 900 to 1,500 m, on open fir and burn edges (75%, [Heffley Creek](#/s/local-heffley)).
- Use the Hunt Map elevation profile and the BEC zone layer rather than fixed numbers (Tip).
```

## Heffley Creek, Region 3

```diagram mmd-three-areas
```

- Kamloops and the North Thompson rank among BC's best mule deer areas (85%).
- No mapped official winter range right around Kamloops (90%).

```more Heffley Creek in detail
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- The BC habitat model lists the highest reported mule deer densities (over 1 deer per 0.2 km2) in places including the North Thompson River, the City of Kamloops and Copper Creek in the Thompson Basin (85%).
- Approved ungulate winter range polygons: none in a box around Kamloops and Heffley Creek (90%, BC data check, research notes 2026-10-03). Use the habitat rule instead: south and west slopes, bunchgrass, ponderosa pine and Douglas fir below about 1,000 m (75%).
- Dewdrop Rosseau Creek WMA (Wildlife Management Area), north shore of Kamloops Lake, is winter range for mule deer and bighorn sheep (90%). The synopsis lists it as a motor vehicle closed area (90%, page 33 via research notes; check [Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3)).
- Lac du Bois Grasslands: mule and white tailed deer (95%, BC Parks). Hunting rules there: see the [Heffley Creek](#/s/local-heffley) session and the Rule Book.
- Local reading (75%): grassland and open Douglas fir benches above the valley; up toward Heffley Lake, Paul Lake and Sun Peaks in summer and early fall; lower, sunnier slopes as snow comes.
- MU (Management Unit) 3-27 success 20 to 41% a year, 2020 to 2024 (90%, chapter 1).
- Closest SIMDeer (Southern Interior Mule Deer Project) study area: Cache Creek and Bonaparte. Mule deer were the most abundant mammal on cameras there (85%).
```

## Fraser Valley, Region 2

```diagram mmd-bc-subspecies
```

- Here the deer is the black tail, same species (90%).
- Spring and summer: logged openings. Winter: old growth higher up (90%).

```more black tail country in detail
- Columbian black tailed deer live along the coast, west of the Coast and Cascade summits (90%, BC account).
- They "make frequent use of logged habitats during spring and summer" (90%).
- Old growth forest is key winter range for coastal black tails, more so northward and at higher elevations; steep south to west facing sites are best winter range (90%).
- Winter and early spring foods: Douglas fir, western redcedar, red huckleberry, salal, deer fern, tree lichens. Late spring to fall: grasses, trailing blackberry, fireweed, pearly everlasting, willow, salmonberry and maple leaves (90%).
- Black tailed deer breed in November and early December (80%, BC wildlife report).
- Region 2 resident "mule deer" (black tail) success: 41% in 2024, 2,708 hunters (90%, harvest statistics).
- Skagit Valley Provincial Park lists mule and black tailed deer along the road and campgrounds (90%, BC Parks, research notes).
- Rules differ from Region 3: see [Region 2](#/s/rb-region-2) and [Mission](#/s/local-mission).
```

## South Okanagan, Region 8

```diagram mmd-winter-canopy
```

- Grassland and dry pine slopes are prime mule deer winter range (85 to 95%).
- White Lake and the South Okanagan grasslands are named winter range (95%).

```more South Okanagan in detail
- BC Parks: South Okanagan Grasslands Protected Area is "class 1 mule deer winter range"; White Lake Grasslands is "mule deer winter range" (95%, research notes 2026-10-03).
- Provincial benchmark winter habitat is rated class 1 in the very dry ponderosa pine and Douglas fir zones of the South Okanagan Basin and Okanagan Range (85%, BC habitat model).
- The Okanagan River area is listed among the highest mule deer densities (85%).
- Access to White Lake is largely through private land; ask first (95%, BC Parks). Rules: [Region 8](#/s/rb-region-8) and [South Okanagan](#/s/local-okanagan).
- Region 8 resident mule deer success: 23% in 2024 (90%, harvest statistics). MU 8-1: 38% in 2024 (90%).
- One mule deer buck for Regions 3 to 8 combined: a Heffley Creek buck uses up your Okanagan buck (95%, [bag limits](#/s/rb-bag-limits)).
```

## Find them on the Hunt Map

```steps Find deer country on the Hunt Map
mmd-map-1 | Layers: mule deer by month, winter range, burns.
mmd-map-2 | Slope direction: find south and west faces.
mmd-map-3 | Slope steepness: find benches below steep ground.
mmd-map-4 | What can I see from here: pick a glassing seat.
mmd-map-5 | Sun and shade: where first sun hits.
mmd-map-6 | Wind and scent cone: plan the approach.
```

- Open the [Hunt Map](#/map). Pick the month at the top of the layers panel (Tip).
- Then go and check on foot (Tip).

```more step by step in words
1. **Layers:** turn on "Mule deer" season layer (pick the month), mule deer ungulate winter range, burns, cutblocks and BEC zones. Land status layers on: Crown land, private land, parks, closures (Tip). The season layer is a general pattern from studies, not mapped corridors (app layer note).
2. **Terrain tools, Slope direction (aspect):** south and west faces for October to winter; north faces for hot afternoon beds (85%, chapter 6).
3. **Slope steepness:** benches (flat) under steep ground are classic beds and feeding edges (Tip). Steep red and purple ground is escape terrain (Tip).
4. **What can I see from here (viewshed):** drop a pin on a ridge or knob; the map shades what you can see. Pick seats that see several benches and openings (Tip).
5. **Sun and shade:** set the time to sunrise; slopes that get first sun on a cold morning get early feeding deer (Tip).
6. **Weather and wind, scent cone:** check the forecast wind and draw your scent cone. Approach so it misses the slope (Tip).
7. Add a waypoint for each seat. Check land status and closures for every spot (Tip, see [land status](#/s/maps-land-status)).
8. Then walk it: sign, beds, glassing at first light. The map finds country; your boots find deer (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mmd-three-needs
```

- **Rhyme:** "Food by cover, sun on the slope; that's where mule deer winter in hope."
- **Number trick:** 90 at 60: good cover hides 90% of a deer at 60 m (80%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Hunt the edges, not the middles."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "South and west when the snow falls; north and shade when the heat calls."
- **Number trick:** 65 to 70: percent canopy of good fir winter range (85%).
- **Number trick:** under 10: percent grass in fall and winter diets near Kamloops (85%).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely taught, opinion): "Find the food, find the deer."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Map the country at home, read it on foot."

> [!mistake]
> - Glassing the middle of big open grass at midday instead of the edges.
> - Hunting high basins after a big snow.
> - Trusting a map layer without checking land status.

> [!field]
> This week: on the Hunt Map, mark 3 glassing seats on south or west slopes within 1 hour of Heffley Creek. Check each with "What can I see from here".
```
