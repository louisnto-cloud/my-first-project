## The kit

```diagram tk-clean-kit
```

- A straight one piece rod and a bore guide protect the bore (95%).
- CLP oil: cleans, lubricates, protects (95%).

```more what Tikka asks for
- "An absolutely straight, sturdy and smooth cleaning rod fitted with bronze bore brush soaked in bore solvent"; a rod guide "to prevent the rod from dragging on the inner surfaces of the bore and receiver" (95%, Tikka manual page 18).
- "Use only high quality gun oil that cleans, lubricates and preserves (CLP)" (95%).
- Felt pellets, a small brush for the bolt, a larger nylon brush or chamber pellet for the chamber (95%).
- Tools: "ensure that the screwdriver is of appropriate size and shape. Poor tools will damage the screw heads" (95%, page 18).
- Prices for a kit: [chapter 9](#/s/mc-tikka-09).
```

## The routine clean, step by step

```steps Clean the Tikka in 6 steps
tk-clean-1 | Unloaded. Bolt and magazine out.
tk-clean-2 | Wet? Dry it warm, out of the case.
tk-clean-3 | Guide in. Brush breech to muzzle, 10 to 15 strokes.
tk-clean-4 | Pellets one way until clean, 5 to 10.
tk-clean-5 | Receiver, bolt, chamber.
tk-clean-6 | Thin oil. Wipe the chamber dry before shooting.
```

- About 10 minutes once you know it (Tip).
- Check the receiver screws at the end (95%).

```more Tikka's 11 steps word for word
- "Routine cleaning should always take place right after use of the rifle (even without shooting)... Corrosion can begin within 24 hours if the rifle is not cleaned after use" (95%, Tikka manual page 18).
1. Ensure that the rifle is unloaded and remove the bolt and magazine.
2. Used in wet conditions: dry it with compressed air or in a warm, dry place "without any protective cover i.e. drag bag".
3. Rod with a solvent soaked bronze brush, push pull about 10 to 15 times. Use a rod guide.
4. Clean felt pellets moistened with solvent, one after another, until they come out clean, about 5 to 10. "Clean only in the direction of bullet movement."
5. Clean the inner surfaces of the receiver.
6. Small solvent wet brush on the bolt.
7. Larger nylon brush or chamber pellet for the chamber.
8. A pellet moistened with rust preventive oil through the bore; a thin coat of oil in the chamber, "remember to wipe it dry before shooting!"
9. A very light coat of oil on the touching surfaces of receiver and bolt.
10. Wipe the stock dry. Wooden stocks: stock oil.
11. Check the tightness of the receiver screws.
- Wash your hands after: lead warning (95%, page 6).
```

## Grease 3 spots

```diagram tk-grease-points
```

- Locking lugs, cocking cam, cocking piece head (95%).
- Light grease only (95%).

```more the manual's words
- "Make sure that locking lug surfaces (A), cocking cam (B) and head of the cocking piece (C) are all lightly greased" (95%, Tikka manual page 7, figure 4).
- The cocking piece head is under the shroud. You reach it when you strip the bolt, or with a little grease on a cotton bud under the shroud (Tip).
- More grease is not better: it holds grit and thickens in cold (Tip; chapter 8 has the cold rule).
```

## Strip the magazine

```diagram tk-mag-strip
```

- Follower and spring out. Floorplate stays on (95%).
- Wash, dry, light oil, refit the spring the right way (95%).

```more the manual's steps
1. "Depress the rear part of the follower with a screwdriver or a similar tool so that the follower's front end is released" (95%, Tikka manual page 14, figure 24).
2. "Pull out the follower and spring from the magazine box. Do not detach the magazine floorplate" (95%).
3. Clean box, spring and follower with warm soapy water or cleaning solvent. Lightly oil before reassembly (95%).
4. Reassemble in reverse. Make sure the spring sits as in figure 25 (95%). Photo it before you take it apart (Tip).
- Assumption: your steel CTR style magazine strips the same way as the polymer T3x magazine in the manual. VERIFY: if it does not come apart the same way, do not force it.
```

## Strip the bolt, step by step

```steps Strip the T3x bolt in 6 steps
tk-strip-1 | Bolt out: lift, press the bolt stop, pull.
tk-strip-2 | Shroud clockwise until it stops. Pull it off.
tk-strip-3 | 3 mm screwdriver in the gap. Leave it there.
tk-strip-4 | Turn counterclockwise. Push the pin unit forward.
tk-strip-5 | Handle off. Stiff? Plastic mallet tap.
tk-strip-6 | Firing pin unit out. Stop here.
```

- Only when oil got inside, or once a year (95%).
- Do not take the firing pin unit apart (95%).

```more the manual's steps for the T3x bolt
- "Generally, disassembling the bolt is not necessary. If you have reason to believe that oil may have penetrated the bolt, you should clean it in order to retain its smooth operation. This is particularly important if you use the rifle in cold or harsh weather" (95%, Tikka manual page 13).
1. "Carefully check that the rifle is unloaded and remove the bolt from the rifle by pressing the bolt release lever on the side of the receiver and pulling the bolt backwards."
2. "Remove the bolt shroud by turning it clockwise (Left-handed model: counterclockwise) until it stops and pull it away from the bolt."
3. "Insert the tip of a screwdriver (approx. 3 mm wide) into the gap between the cocking piece and the bolt handle retainer bushing. IMPORTANT! Do not remove the screwdriver tip from this location until you are ready to reassemble the bolt shroud."
4. "Disengage the firing pin assembly by turning the screwdriver counterclockwise (Left-handed model: clockwise). Push the firing pin assembly forward until the locking cam of the handle retainer bushing touches the bolt's rear surface."
5. "Release the bolt handle from the bolt body. If the bolt handle feels stiff and does not immediately pull out, you can tap it with a plastic mallet at the receiver end."
6. "Remove the firing pin assembly from the bolt."
- "The bolt should not require any further disassembly for cleaning purposes" (95%, page 14).
- Clean with a solvent wet brush, light protective oil, grease lugs, cam and cocking piece head (95%, page 19).
- The pictures are simplified drawings based on the manual's figures 21 and 22, not a scale drawing of the inside. Unsure at any step? Stop and take it to a gunsmith (Tip).
```

## Rebuild the bolt, step by step

```steps Rebuild the T3x bolt in 4 steps
tk-build-1 | Firing pin unit in. Bushing lined up.
tk-build-2 | Handle back on the body.
tk-build-3 | Screwdriver clockwise all the way: handle locked.
tk-build-4 | Spring in the shroud. Shroud on, to the notch.
```

- Then a function check, unloaded (95%).
- Any doubt: gunsmith before live fire (Tip).

```more the manual's rebuild and the check
1. "Push the firing pin assembly into place. Make sure that the bolt handle retainer bushing is in the correct position, so that you can ease the bolt handle back into place without obstruction" (95%, Tikka manual page 14).
2. "Reassemble the bolt handle to the bolt body."
3. "Re-engage the firing pin assembly by turning the screwdriver clockwise (Left-handed model: counterclockwise) as far as it will go. Make sure that the retainer bushing locks the handle."
4. "Put the conical spring in place inside the bolt shroud so that end of the spring does not set on the firing pin groove area. The purpose of the spring is to prevent noise coming from bolt shroud."
5. "Attach the bolt shroud together with the spring to the bolt body and turn counterclockwise (Left-handed model: clockwise) until head of the cocking piece stops in the notch before the cocking slope."
- Function check (Tip, from the manual's yearly list "check the functionality of components", 95%): unloaded, bolt in; lift and close: it cocks; safety ON: bolt locked, trigger dead; safety OFF, muzzle safe, trigger: it decocks with a click. Anything odd: stop, gunsmith (95%, page 19).
```

## Action out of the stock (rarely)

```diagram tk-action-out
```

- Two Torx screws, trigger guard down, action up (95%).
- Refit: recoil block in, action pulled back, 7 Nm (95%).

```more the manual's steps
1. Make sure the rifle is not loaded. Remove the bolt and magazine (95%, Tikka manual page 13).
2. Remove the two receiver fastening screws with a Torx driver.
3. Pull the trigger guard downwards.
4. Lift the barrelled receiver up out of the stock.
- Refit: "Make sure to put metallic recoil block in place into stock and pull barreled receiver backwards before tightening the receiver screws in order to remove the slack from the recoil lug." 7 Nm (62 in lb) with metal trigger guards, max 5 Nm (44 in lb) with plastic ones (95%).
- Why rarely: every time the action comes out, confirm zero (Tip). You need it for the trigger screw on some models, a soaked stock, or a gunsmith visit (95% for the trigger access, page 15).
```

## The trigger: factory adjustment only

```diagram tk-trigger
```

- Factory: 13 to 15 N, about 3 lb (95%).
- Never below 11 N (2.5 lb). Nothing else to adjust (95%).

```more the manual's limits
> [!warn]
> "For safety reason do NOT attempt to reduce trigger weight below 11 N (2,5 lbs)" (95%, Tikka manual page 15).

- "The sear engagement, safety and other components of the action are precision mechanisms, which were properly adjusted at the factory. They should not be altered, modified or changed" (95%).
- Both trigger types "are adjusted at the factory and need no adjustment of any kind" (95%).
- If you must: bolt and magazine out; a 2.5 mm Allen key through a hole in the magazine well (some models need the action out); clockwise heavier, counterclockwise lighter; stay between 11 and 18 N (95%). The screw is self locking (95%).
- "Do not make any other adjustments to the trigger! If you notice any changes in operation of the trigger, please turn to an authorized gunsmith" (95%).
- If the trigger, sear or safety catch must be replaced, the trigger mechanism goes back to the maker (95%).

> [!lean]
> My lean: leave it at the factory weight. Learn it with dry fire (chapter 5).
```

## Once a year: the full service

```diagram tk-yearly
```

- At least once a year, and before storage (95%).
- Copper fouling out of the bore (95%).

```more the manual's 11 points
- "Periodic maintenance should be carried out at least once a year or more frequently under heavy use or in severe conditions... recommended if the rifle is to be stored for prolonged period" (95%, Tikka manual page 18).
1. Routine cleaning. 2. Remove copper fouling with a copper solvent, following its maker: "Badly fouled barrels can shoot inaccurately and will corrode much easier". 3. Light oil film on all metal. 4. Lubricate and inspect the sling swivel mounts. 5. Strip the bolt, clean, oil, grease lugs, cam and cocking piece head. 6. Reassemble and check extractor and ejector. 7. Check sights and scope attachment. 8. Clean accessories. 9. Check all screws, stock and receiver. 10. Assemble and check function. 11. Wooden stock with oil finish: stock oil (95%, page 19).
- "If it is necessary to replace any parts, use only original factory spare parts" (95%, page 18).
- Log it: date, rounds fired since the last clean (Tip). The My guns log in the app can hold it.
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram tk-grease-points
```

- **"24 hours to rust."**
- **"Breech to crown, one way down."**
- **"Clockwise off, counterclockwise on."** The shroud on a right handed bolt.

```more more hooks, mistakes and the field task
- **"Two and a half, no less."** Trigger weight floor.
- **"Lugs, cam, cock."** The three grease spots.
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Clean it tonight, and it shoots tomorrow."

> [!mistake]
> - Pulling the screwdriver out of the bolt gap before the rebuild.
> - Leaving oil in the chamber and firing the first shot through it.
> - Turning the trigger screw below 11 N, or any other trigger screw.

> [!field]
> After the next range day: the 6 step routine clean with the manual open, then the bolt strip and rebuild once, slowly, over a towel. Log it.
```
