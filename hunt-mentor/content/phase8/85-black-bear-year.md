## January to March: in the den

```diagram mc-black-bear-cub-timeline
```

```photo bg-bb-den
```

- Cubs born in January or February, about 400 g (90%).
- Interior bears den about 5 to 7 months (90%).

```more the den months in detail
- BC account (90%): hibernation "three to five months on the south coast" and "probably five to seven months in the interior and the north"; pregnant females den longest.
- Cubs are born "hairless, blind" at about 400 g and nurse while the mother hibernates (90%). WildSafeBC: 250 to 500 g, born late January (75%).
- Embryos implant only in October or November (delayed implantation), and only if the sow has enough fat (75 to 90%). A poor berry year can mean no cubs next winter (Tip, my reading).
- Chapter 2 has the den body: heart, metabolism, weight loss.
```

## April and May: den out, green up

```diagram mc-black-bear-den-order
```

- Males out first; sows with cubs last (80%).
- Lean and hungry: first green grass and forbs (90%).

```gallery
g-bb-cinnamon | Spring bear eating dandelions.
hab-skunk-cabbage | Skunk cabbage: early spring food on the coast.
```

```more spring in detail
- Den exit in the northeast Washington Cascades, next to the Okanagan: males 4 April to 7 May, females 4 April to 22 May (80%, Gaines 2003).
- Alaska: females with cubs usually come out later than lone bears (85%).
- Spring food (90%, BC account): "succulent vegetation in meadows, estuaries, riparian habitats, skunk cabbage swamps, avalanche chutes, grassy south facing slopes, and burned areas".
- WildSafeBC: grasses, sedges, horsetails; skunk cabbage (75%).
- Metabolism stays low for 2 to 3 weeks after den exit (80%, Alaska study): early spring bears move little (Tip).
- Spring season opens 1 April in Regions 2, 3 and 8 (99%). Early April bears are often still denned; late April and May are better (Tip).
```

## June and July: mating and young deer

```diagram mc-black-bear-home-range
```

- Mating early June to mid July (90%).
- Bears hunt newborn deer, elk and moose calves (90%).

```more early summer in detail
- BC account (90%): "usually mate from early June to mid July"; in spring they "prey on newborn deer, elk, moose, and caribou".
- Mule deer study in the southern Interior: most newborn fawn deaths were from bears (85%, SIMDeer, via [mule deer chapter 1](#/s/mc-mule-deer-01)).
- Boars travel widely and may be seen in daylight looking for sows (Tip).
- Spring season ends 30 June in Regions 3 and 8 and 15 June in Region 2 (99%).
- Sows with new cubs keep away from boars in the mating season (65%, chapter 4).
```

## July to September: berries

```diagram sg-berry-calendar
```

```photo bg-bb-devils-club
```

- Berries ripen low first, then higher up (70%).
- A fall bear may eat up to 20,000 calories a day (70%).

```diagram rp-bear-calories
```

```more summer and berries in detail
- In summer, bears eat "insects and larvae, fruits, berries, salmon, and carrion" (90%, BC account).
- Soopolallie (soapberry) ripens late July to mid August and is a main bear food (65%, Sierra Club BC). Huckleberry fattens bears in August and September (70%).
- Berry timing follows elevation: valley bottoms first, high slopes later (70%, [berries and mast](#/s/berries-mast)).
- The fall feeding frenzy, [[Hyperphagia]], builds through August (70%).
- Region 8 only: August bear season on private land with the owner's permission (99%, [Region 8](#/s/rb-region-8)).
```

## September to November: fatten, then den

```

```

- Fall season opens 1 September in most of Region 3 (99%).
- Last berries, fruit and spawning fish (90%).

```more fall in detail
- "Come autumn, they often forage on berries and on spawning fish" (90%, BC account).
- Fall foods near Kamloops (Tip, quick lesson): saskatoon, chokecherry, hawthorn, rose hips, kinnikinnick, fallen apples, Oregon grape.
- Mast years and berry failures move bears: a poor berry crop sends them to orchards and towns (Tip).
- Chapter 7 has the foods by place and month.
```

## Den in

```diagram sg-bear-den
```

- Sows in first; big males last (80%).
- Dens: tree cavities, rock, brush piles, dug holes (90%).

```more denning in detail
- Den entry near the Okanagan: females 15 October to 19 November, males 22 October to 19 November (80%, Gaines 2003). WildSafeBC: typically denned December to April, or November to April (75%).
- Some bears, often males, may not den if food lasts (75%, WildSafeBC).
- Interior dens: tree cavities, or "rock cavities, under brush piles, or in holes dug into the ground"; coast dens are almost all in or under big old trees, logs or stumps, up to 25 m off the ground (90%, BC account).
- "When cold weather arrives, bears become increasingly lethargic and enter their dens" (90%).
- Alaska: females with cubs den earlier and come out later than lone bears (85%).
```

## What October and November change

| Week | Bears doing | You do (Tip) |
|---|---|---|
| Early Oct | Feeding hard, much of the day | Glass berries and fruit, all day |
| Late Oct | Sows denning; boars still out | Focus on the best food left |
| Early Nov | Many denned; cold snaps | Fruit, fish, snow tracks |
| Late Nov | Mostly big males, few bears | Low odds; check tracks |

- The fall season runs to 30 November (99%).
- Big boars den last: late fall can be the best boar time (Tip).

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-black-bear-year-wheel
```

- **Rhyme:** "Green in spring, love in June, berries by August, den by the cold moon."
- **Number trick:** 4 1 to 6 30, 9 1 to 11 30: Region 3 spring and fall (99%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, common hunting advice): "Find the food, find the bear."

```more more hooks
- **Number trick:** 400 g at birth, 2 to 5 kg out of the den (75 to 90%).
- **Number trick:** 5 to 7 months in the den in the Interior (90%).
- **Rhyme:** "Sows in first and out last; boars out first and in last."
- **Tip** (my reading of the den order data): a late November bear track in snow is most likely a male.

> [!mistake]
> - Hunting early April like it is May.
> - Glassing old food after the berries are gone.
> - Forgetting Region 2 spring closes 15 June.

> [!field]
> This October: visit one berry patch every week and note when the fruit ends and the bear sign stops.
```
