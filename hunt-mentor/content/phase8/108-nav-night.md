> [!why]
> An unplanned night out is cold, long and miserable, and almost always survivable if you decide early, get off the ground, stay dry and keep the fire small.

## Decide early

```diagram mc-nav-night-clock
```

- One hour before dark: decide you are staying. Then build (Tip).
- Shelter in daylight takes half the effort of shelter in the dark (Tip).

```more why early matters
- Mid October at Heffley Creek: sunset about 18:08; enough light to work in the open ends about 33 minutes later (85%, my calculation, [Night navigation](#/s/night-navigation)).
- Debris shelters take hours and lots of material: start early (65%, [Shelter](#/s/shelter-night-out)).
- A call or a satellite SOS before dark, while you can still see to signal, helps searchers (Tip; call early 75%, North Shore Rescue).
- Three wood piles before dark: tinder, kindling, fuel. You will burn more than you think (Tip).
```

## Pick the spot

```diagram fs-sh-site
```

- Out of the wind, off the valley floor, near wood, close to an opening (65 to 75%).
- Not under dead trees, not in a draw, not by rising water (Tip).

```more the site in full
- Choose the driest spot you can, out of the wind, away from low points where cold air and water collect (65%, shelter guides via [Shelter](#/s/shelter-night-out)).
- Cold air sinks downhill at night and pools in draws and valley floors (75%, thermals).
- "Bench, not bottom" (Tip, from [Shelter](#/s/shelter-night-out)).
- Near an opening, so you can put a signal where searchers or aircraft can see it (65%).
- Look up: dead trees and hanging branches fall in wind (Tip).
- Avoid hazardous spots: swift water, under cornices, slippery ledges (70%, AdventureSmart via Global News).
```

## Get off the ground first

```diagram fs-sh-ground
```

- The cold ground steals more heat than the open sky (75%).
- A thick bed of boughs, a pack and a foam pad beats a fine roof (Tip).

```more insulation in full
- You lose heat four ways: conduction to the ground, convection to wind, radiation to the sky, evaporation from wet clothes (75%, [Cold injuries](#/s/cold-injuries)).
- Insulation and a barrier from the ground and weather are the core of preventing hypothermia (75%, Wilderness Medical Society via American Family Physician).
- A thick bed matters more than a roof on a dry night (75% physics; Tip on the order).
- Foil blankets reflect radiant heat but do little against conduction from the ground (65%).
- Use what you carry: foam sit pad, empty pack, game bags stuffed with dry leaves or boughs (Tip).
```

## Shelter: tarp first, debris second

```steps Tarp shelter in 4 steps
fs-sh-tarp-1 | Tie a tight cord between two trees.
fs-sh-tarp-2 | Drape the tarp over the cord.
fs-sh-tarp-3 | Pin the windward edge low.
fs-sh-tarp-4 | Build the bed under it before dark.
```

- A tarp or bivy is faster and safer than building from the bush (65%).
- No tarp: debris shelter, small and thick (65%).

```more both shelters
- Tarps and bivy bags are among the most effective shelter tools for their weight, and often safer when tired, hurt or short of light (65%, [Shelter](#/s/shelter-night-out)).
- Debris shelters give emergency insulation but need large amounts of dry material and hours of work (65%).
- Keep it small: less air to heat (Tip).
- Two of you? Share one cover and huddle (Tip; the huddle is taught for cold water, 75%, [Water safety](#/s/water-safety)).
- Hidden in thick timber? Put orange gear in the open nearby (65%).
```

```steps Debris shelter in 4 steps
fs-sh-debris-1 | Pole from a stump to the ground.
fs-sh-debris-2 | Lean close ribs along both sides.
fs-sh-debris-3 | Pile leaves and boughs thick.
fs-sh-debris-4 | Fill the inside, crawl in, plug the door.
```

## Fire: small, safe, legal

```steps Wet weather fire in 5 steps
fs-fire-build-1 | Build a floor of dry sticks.
fs-fire-build-2 | Loose tinder in the middle.
fs-fire-build-3 | Lean small sticks in a tipi, door upwind.
fs-fire-build-4 | Light low on the upwind side. Shield it.
fs-fire-build-5 | Feed the next size only when this one roars.
```

- Check bans before any fire, every time (80%).
- A Category 1 campfire is no bigger than 0.5 m by 0.5 m (80%).

```more fire in full
- Fire needs heat, fuel and air (75%).
- Birch bark and pitch wood burn even when wet (65%). Split a wet piece: the core is often dry (65%).
- Category 1 campfire: no larger than 0.5 m high by 0.5 m wide (80%, BC Wildfire Service brochure, search preview, via [Fire](#/s/fire-wet-weather)).
- Keep at least 8 litres of water or a hand tool on hand while it burns (80%; exact wording varies, VERIFY on the current order).
- Fire bans change. Check the BC Wildfire Service before each fire (80%). Kamloops Fire Centre status after 9 October 2026: VERIFY.
- Do not light a fire in strong wind (75%, BC Wildfire Service).
- Smoke by day, flame by night; three fires mean help (75%).
- Out means cool to the touch: drown, stir, feel, repeat (80%).
- Two lighters in two pockets plus a fire starter (Tip, [Lost](#/s/lost-survival-kit)).
```

## Stay warm and dry

```diagram fs-cold-heat-loss
```

- Wet loses heat fast. Vent before you sweat; dry layer on when you stop (70%).
- Eat and drink: your body is the furnace (Tip).

```more warmth in full
- Wet clothing loses much of its insulation, and evaporation pulls heat from you (70%, [Cold injuries](#/s/cold-injuries)).
- In wind and rain, a wrap over wet clothes beats stripping off (75%, Wilderness Medical Society via American Family Physician).
- Toque and neck cover on; dry socks on at night (Tip).
- Small snacks through the night; get up and move when you shiver hard, then lie back down (Tip).
- Kamloops Airport averages: October low 3.5°C, November low minus 1.1°C (75%). Higher ground is colder (70%).
- Leave the flask at camp: hypothermia is common with alcohol (75%).
```

## Water at night

```diagram fs-wat-rank
```

- Boil 1 minute if you can. Otherwise filter, then treat (80%).
- Thirsty is better than sick, but dehydration makes cold worse (Tip).

```more water in full
- Do not drink untreated water from lakes, streams, rivers, ponds or springs (80%, HealthLink BC).
- Boil at least 1 minute; above 2,000 m, at least 2 minutes (80%).
- Giardia symptoms usually start 1 to 3 weeks later (80%). Getting home tonight matters more (Tip).
- Chlorine alone is weak on Giardia; filter first, then disinfect (75 to 80%).
- If you must drink untreated water to survive, do it; see a doctor later (Tip).
- Full detail: [Water](#/s/water-safe-drinking).
```

## Watch for hypothermia, in you and your partner

```diagram gr-hypo-scale
```

- Stumbles, mumbles, fumbles, grumbles: early signs (75%).
- Shivering that stops is an emergency (75%).

```more stages and what to do
- Mild: alert, shivering strongly. Shelter, insulation, heat to the trunk, food if alert (75%, Wilderness Medical Society via American Family Physician).
- Moderate: drowsy, shivering slows or stops. Gentle, horizontal, no food or drink, wrap, warm the upper trunk, evacuate (75%).
- Severe: unconscious, no shivering. Check breathing and pulse up to 60 seconds; CPR (cardiopulmonary resuscitation) if no signs of life (75%).
- Shivering slows around 32°C and stops by about 30°C core (75%).
- [[Hypothermia]] care in full: [Cold injuries](#/s/cold-injuries). First aid: [First aid in the field](#/s/field-first-aid).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-nav-survival-order
```

- **Rhyme:** "Ground before roof, roof before fire, fire before food."
- **Number trick:** 0.5 by 0.5 m fire, 8 litres of water on hand (80%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, short form of North Shore Rescue's advice): "Pack like you will sleep out."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Bench, not bottom" (cold air drains down, 75%).
- **Rhyme:** "Stumbles, mumbles, fumbles, grumbles" (75%).
- **Number trick:** 3 wood piles before dark: tinder, kindling, fuel (Tip).
- **Number trick:** 1 hour before dark: decide (Tip).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety) Grandpa's rule): "Tell someone where you went. Pack like you will sleep out."

> [!mistake]
> - Walking on in the dark instead of stopping in the last light.
> - A fine roof over bare cold ground.
> - A big fire under the tarp, or a fire during a ban.

> [!field]
> One fall evening near home: with your brother, build a tarp lean to and a bough bed in 30 minutes, from the kit you actually carry. Light a legal stove or fire only where allowed. Note what was missing.
```
