> [!why]
> More animals are lost in the first ten minutes after the shot than in any other part of the hunt. Watch, mark, wait, then track slowly.

- Watch it. Mark it. Wait. Hit site. Trail. Grid. Help (Tip, built on the facts below).
- The law: all reasonable effort to find it (95%).

## Watch the reaction

```diagram sk-reactions
```

- Mule kick and hard low run: often heart or lungs (70%).
- Hunched back, slow walk: often a gut hit (70%).

```more reactions in detail
| What you saw | Often means |
|---|---|
| Mule kick, hard low run | Heart or lungs (70%) |
| Hunched back, short run, then walking | Gut (70%) |
| Hard run with a limp | Leg (70%) |
| Drops on the spot | Spine or brain; may get up. Be ready (Tip) |
| No sign at all | Not proof of a miss. Check the hit site (70%) |

- Reactions from the National Deer Association and hunter education (70%, [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)).
- Keep the scope on it. Reload at once and stay ready for a second shot if it is legal and safe (Tip).
- Listen after it leaves the view: a crash, then quiet, is a good sign (Tip).
- Moose seldom drop and may show no sign of a hit (85%, [Shot placement](#/s/shot-placement)).
```

## Mark the spot

```steps Mark it in 4 steps
mc-skills-mark-1 | Note where it stood: a tree, a rock.
mc-skills-mark-2 | Note where you last saw it.
mc-skills-mark-3 | Pin your shooting spot on the phone.
mc-skills-mark-4 | Flag where it stood. Then flag the trail.
```

- Mark it before you move. Everything looks different from over there (Tip).
- Flag where it stood with tape (70 to 85%).

```more marking in detail
- Walk to where the animal stood and flag it (70%, hunter education via [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)).
- Two landmarks: where it stood and where it vanished. Say them out loud or into the phone (Tip).
- Drop a pin at your shooting spot and another at the hit site on the [Hunt Map](#/map) (Tip).
- Take a compass bearing from your seat to the last spot seen (Tip, [Compass mastery](#/s/compass-mastery)).
- Two hunters: one stays at the shooting spot and guides the other to the hit site by hand signals (Tip).
```

## Wait: how long?

```diagram sk-wait-chart
```

- Heart or lungs, out of sight: 30 minutes minimum (70%).
- Liver: hours. Gut: 6 to 8 hours or overnight in cool weather (70%).

```more waiting times, both sources
| What you saw | Hunter education | National Deer Association |
|---|---|---|
| Heart or lungs, out of sight | 30 minutes minimum (70%) | 30 minutes minimum (70%) |
| Liver: dark blood, no bubbles | 4 to 6 hours (70%) | 2 to 4 hours (70%) |
| Gut: green or brown, smells | 6 to 8 hours, or overnight if cool (70%) | 4 to 6 hours (70%) |
| Muscle or leg | 30 to 60 minutes, then track hard (70%) | |
| Not sure where it hit | Several hours (70%) | |

- These are opinions from hunter education and experienced trackers, not law (70%).
- Pennsylvania Game Commission: gut hit, at least 8 hours (70%, via [Blood, hair and tissue](#/s/rs-blood-hair-tissue)).
- Pushed early, a gut or liver hit animal runs and is lost (85%).
- Weather: heat or rain coming can shorten the wait for meat or sign; back off a little and still go slow (Tip).
- A rifle hit in the heart or lungs: the deer is usually dead within about 137 m (150 yd) (70%, National Deer Association).
```

## The hit site

```diagram mc-skills-hit-site
```

- Look for blood, hair, bone, tissue, scuffed ground (70%).
- Read it all together, plus the reaction (65%).

```more what the hit site tells you
| Find | Likely |
|---|---|
| Pink froth, spongy tissue | Lungs (65 to 70%) |
| Dark red, no bubbles | Liver (70%) |
| Green or brown matter, smell | Gut: back out (65 to 70%) |
| Bone chips | Bone was struck; with marrow and light fur, maybe a leg (65%) |
| Hair only, no blood | Back out, give it more time (70%) |

- Norma Academy, lung hit: "light coloured blood, blistered blood, and soft and light coloured lung tissue on the ground" (65%).
- Stomach matter beats blood colour for calling a gut hit (65%).
- Photograph what you find before you move it (Tip).
```

## Read the blood

```diagram mc-skills-blood-read
```

- Bright and frothy: lungs. Dark: liver. Thin with green bits: gut (70%).
- Drop tails point the way it went (75 to 80%).

```more blood in detail
- A drop that hits at an angle grows a small tail on the far side (80%, forensic bloodstain research). From a moving animal the tail points the direction of travel (Tip, built on that physics).
- Blood darkens and turns brown as it dries; frozen blood looks dull; rain lightens it (65%).
- Hydrogen peroxide fizzes on blood you cannot see in leaves (70%, National Deer Association).
- Clotting is the most common reason a trail fades (60%). No blood does not mean no hit (70%).
- Blood on brush at chest height and on both sides of the trail suggests a pass through (Tip).
- A deer must lose about a third of its blood to go into shock or die (60%, Tip level; it varies with speed of loss).
```

```anim rs-bl-drops The tail of each drop points the way it went.
```

## Follow the trail

```anim sk-trail-beside Walk beside the blood, not on it.
```

- Walk beside the trail, never on it. You may need it again (70%).
- Flag every few metres of sign. Look ahead for the animal (Tip).

```more trailing in detail
- One person follows the sign, one looks ahead with the rifle ready, in a safe zone of fire (Tip, [Chapter 10](#/s/mc-skills-10)).
- Wounded animals head downhill, to water and thick cover, often on a game trail (70%, National Deer Association).
- Lost the blood? Go back to the last flag. Never wander ahead making a second set of tracks (70%).
- Look up: blood on brush and saplings at body height (Tip).
- Look at the flags behind you: they show the line it was travelling (Tip).
- Bears come to wounded animals. One watches with spray and rifle, one tracks (85%, Alberta Be BearSmart).
```

## Lost the trail: grid it

```anim sk-grid-search Walk lanes about 10 m apart, in sight of each other.
```

- Circle the last flag: 10 m, then 20 m (Tip).
- Lanes about 10 m apart, each person in sight of the next (70%).

```more the search in detail
1. Back to the last sure sign (70%).
2. Circle it slowly at 10 m, then 20 m (Tip, [Following a track](#/s/rs-track-following)).
3. Check the likely spots: thick cover downhill, creeks, the nearest timber patch (70%).
4. Still nothing? Lanes about 10 m apart, each searcher in sight of the next, along the likely direction (70%, National Deer Association).
5. Look for tracks, scuffs, broken twigs, not just blood (70%).
- Plan the likely route on the map before you start (Tip, [Blood, hair and tissue](#/s/rs-blood-hair-tissue)).
```

## When to get help, and the rules

```diagram mc-skills-help-grid
```

- Thick timber, snow coming, gut hit: get your brother now, not later (Tip).
- All reasonable effort is the law. Ten minutes is not reasonable (95%).

```more the rules while you track
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- "No person shall kill, cripple or wound game without making all reasonable effort to locate, dispatch, retrieve and include it in their bag limit" (95%, synopsis page 12, via [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)).
- "Hunt" includes "following after or on the trail of" wildlife "while in possession of a firearm" (95%). Tracking with your rifle after legal hours, or with a headlamp, is hunting outside hours and with a light. Do not (85%, my reading in [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)).
- Tracking with no firearm only to find a dead animal: the synopsis does not say. Ask a [[Conservation officer]] (VERIFY).
- Dogs on deer in BC must be on a leash (95%). A leashed tracking dog can help (Tip).
- The Trespass Act has no tracking exception. Stop at a posted fence and ask (95%).
- Killed the wrong animal or by accident: cancel the licence, write that you intend to self report, call RAPP (Report All Poachers and Polluters) (95%).
- Full rules: [Rule Book: it is unlawful](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts) and [After the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill).
```

## What a 40 year tracker does differently

| Beginner | Old hand (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Runs to the spot | Watches, marks, waits |
| Walks on the blood | Walks beside it, flags it |
| Stares at the ground | Looks ahead and up |
| Gives up when blood stops | Grids it, calls for help |
| Goes in on a gut hit | Backs out for hours |

- All lines are hunter wisdom, labelled Tip.

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-skills-after-flow
```

- **Rhyme:** "Watch it, mark it, wait it, track it."
- **Number trick:** 30 minutes lungs, 4 to 6 hours liver, 6 to 8 hours gut (70%, hunter education).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, short form of "pushing the animal early is how it gets lost", 85%, [Shot placement](#/s/shot-placement)): "Pushed early, lost forever."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Pink and bubbly, lungs; dark and smooth, liver; green and smelly, back away."
- **Number trick:** 10 m lanes in the grid (70%); 137 m (150 yd) for most lung shot deer (70%).
- **Number trick:** 1 third of the blood lost before collapse (60%, Tip level).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, from BC Outdoors Magazine: you may need the blood line again): "Walk beside the trail. You may need it twice."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, short form of the National Deer Association advice): "Downhill, water, thick cover. Look there first."

> [!mistake]
> - Walking straight to a gut shot deer after 5 minutes.
> - Losing the hit site because nothing was marked.
> - Tracking with the rifle and a headlamp after legal hours.

> [!field]
> Practise with your brother: one hides a trail of red food colouring drops and tape in the bush. The other marks, flags and follows it. Swap.
```
