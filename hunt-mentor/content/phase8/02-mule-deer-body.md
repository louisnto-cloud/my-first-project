## Bones that stop bullets

```diagram mmd-skeleton
```

- Shoulder blade covers the front of the chest (75%).
- Aim behind it, one third up from the brisket (85%).

```more the bones in detail
- The **shoulder blade** (scapula) is a broad flat bone over the front of the rib cage, joined to the upper leg bone (humerus) (75%, hunter education drawings).
- When the near front leg steps forward, the shoulder moves forward and opens the chest behind it (85%, [shot placement](#/s/shot-placement)).
- **Ribs** are thin. A hunting bullet passes them easily; a heavy shoulder joint can stop or deflect it (85%, Texas Parks and Wildlife hunter education).
- **Spine** runs high along the back, well above the lungs. A miss high over the lungs can graze it or miss clean (Tip).
- Aim: up the back of the front leg, one third up the chest (85%).
- Quartering to you: the near shoulder is in the way. Beginners wait for a better angle (85%, Texas hunter education: "wait for a better angle").
```

## How it breathes

```anim mmd-breath Diaphragm pulls back, lungs fill.
```

- The lungs are the biggest vital target (85%).
- A hole in the chest lets the lungs collapse (70%).

```more lungs, diaphragm and what a lung hit looks like
- Lungs fill most of the chest cavity above and around the heart. That makes them the largest vital target (85%, Texas and Massachusetts hunter education).
- The **diaphragm** is the dome of muscle behind the lungs. When it pulls back, air rushes in (75%, general mammal anatomy).
- The diaphragm slants: its top sits farther back than its bottom (75%). A shot too far back slips behind it into the liver or rumen.
- Lung hit blood: bright red, often frothy or bubbly (70%, National Deer Association via the [blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing) session).
- Double lung deer: often run hard, under about 100 m (85%, [shot placement](#/s/shot-placement)).
- A newborn fawn's resting heart rate averages 177 beats a minute and drops to about 60 when a person comes close, while it holds its breath (85%, Mule Deer Working Group fact sheet 26). Adult rates: no reliable source found (VERIFY).
```

## Heart and the big pipes

```diagram mmd-heart-vessels
```

- Heart: low in the chest, just behind the front leg (85%).
- Aorta runs back under the spine (65%).

```more heart, vessels and hit reactions
- The heart sits low, just above and slightly behind the front leg (85%, hunter education).
- Heart hits: deer often bolt hard, sometimes with a jump or kick; rarely over about 50 m (85%, [shot placement](#/s/shot-placement)).
- The **aorta**, the main artery, arches from the heart and runs back just under the spine. A high chest hit can cut it (65%, hunter education guides). Do not aim for it; it is a bonus, not a target (Tip).
- The big veins (vena cava) run back from the heart along the chest floor and up through the liver (70%, general anatomy).
- Aim for the middle of the lungs and you have margin for the heart below and the big vessels above (Tip).
```

## The four chamber stomach

```anim mmd-rumen-flow Food loops up as cud, then moves on.
```

- One stomach, four chambers: rumen, reticulum, omasum, abomasum (75%).
- Fill up fast, then bed and chew cud (85%).

```more how a ruminant eats, and what it means for you
- A **ruminant** (cud chewer): the rumen is a fermentation vat where microbes break down plant cell walls (75%, extension and biologist sources).
- Food is swallowed fast, brought back up as cud, chewed again, then passes on through the reticulum, omasum and abomasum (75%).
- Mule deer are selective feeders. Rumen bacteria are specific to their foods, so a sudden new food can kill: winter fed deer can "die with full stomachs" (85%, Mule Deer Working Group fact sheet 2).
- Browse (twigs and leaves of shrubs and trees) is the bulk of the year's diet (85%, BC habitat model). Chapter 7 has the plants.
- The rumen is big: it fills the middle of the belly. A shot behind the diaphragm usually hits it (Tip).
- Gut hit sign: thin, green brown blood with stomach matter and a sour smell; the deer often hunches and walks off (70 to 85%, [blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)). Wait hours, not minutes.
- Why it beds midday: a full rumen needs rest time to chew cud (85%, quick lesson).
```

## Coat and body heat

```diagram mmd-coat
```

- Tawny summer coat, grey brown winter coat (90%).
- In winter it keeps warm mostly by picking warm slopes (85%).

```more how it handles heat and cold
- Coat: reddish or tawny brown in summer, dark or grizzled brown in winter (90%, BC deer account).
- Winter hairs are hollow with larger air pockets than summer hairs (mule deer: about 26 vs 13 micrometres) (60%, Utah Tech conference poster). Good insulation.
- The main way mule deer manage winter cold is habitat choice: warm south and west aspects, gentle to moderate slopes, low elevations, Douglas fir cover (85%, BC habitat model 2003).
- Mule deer have "little requirement for summer thermoregulation" in the habitat model; summer cover is about hiding (85%).
- Moult timing follows day length, and deer in good condition moult earlier (65%, Massachusetts wildlife agency, search preview).
- For you: on hot October afternoons look in shade, on north facing slopes and in timber; on cold mornings look on the first sunny slopes (80%, bedding studies via the quick lesson).
```

## The fat year

```diagram mmd-fat-year
```

- Fat peaks in fall; lowest in spring (60 to 65%).
- Bucks burn fat in the rut and enter winter thin (90%).

```more fat, winter and survival
- Deer eat less and lose weight in winter even in good conditions (85%, Mule Deer Working Group fact sheet 17).
- Wyoming mule deer does used their fat about 2.5 times faster in November and December than in late winter (65%, study data summary).
- Fat stores tend to build through summer, peak in late autumn and bottom out in spring (60%, white tail study, same pattern expected).
- After the rut "bucks have lost weight and some have wounds or broken antlers, and they tend to hide and rest" (90%, BC deer account).
- Snow costs energy: walking in 25 cm of snow takes about 50% more energy; 41 cm about 150% more (85%, BC habitat model citing Parker 1984).
- Starving deer use up the last fat in the bone marrow: healthy winter marrow is pale and fatty; marrow under 10% fat is red and jelly like (65%, Massachusetts, search preview). You may see this in a winter kill.
- People disturbing wintering deer make them burn reserves they need (85%, fact sheet 17).
- For you: a late season buck is lean. Do not expect fat on the meat (Tip).
```

## Teeth and lifespan

```diagram mmd-jaw-age
```

- No upper front teeth: it tears browse (85%).
- Few live past 8 to 10 years (90%).

```more teeth, ageing and lifespan
- Deer have 6 lower incisors plus incisor like canines, pressed against a hard upper pad. No upper incisors (85%, Mule Deer Working Group fact sheet 35). That is why browse looks torn, not cut (75%, chapter 8).
- Age by the lower jaw (85%, fact sheet 35):
  - 1.5 years: tooth 3 (the last premolar) still has 3 cusps and is worn; the last molar is not fully up.
  - 2.5 years: tooth 3 has 2 cusps; the last molar is fully up.
  - 3 to 5: a brown band of dentine shows on the back cusp of the last molar.
  - 6 to 8: tooth 4 (first molar) worn flat.
  - 8 plus: tooth 4 worn to the gum.
- Tooth wear is good to about 3.5 years; past that a lab count of cementum rings is more accurate (85%).
- Yearlings in October are 15 to 17 months old (85%, Idaho Fish and Game).
- Few mule deer live more than 8 to 10 years (90%, BC account). 45 to 70% of fawns normally die (90%).
- The Region 3 pages ask hunters to submit deer heads for CWD (Chronic Wasting Disease) testing; details in the Rule Book, [Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3) (95%, via [the rut](#/s/the-rut)).
```

## What the body means for your shot and your meat

```diagram vitals-deer
```

- Broadside: behind the leg, one third up, both lungs (85%).
- Wait for the leg to step forward (85%).

```more shot and meat notes
- Broadside: up the back of the front leg, one third up the chest. Both lungs and the top of the heart (85%, [shot placement](#/s/shot-placement)).
- Quartering away: aim so the bullet exits the far shoulder (85%).
- Vital zone with margin: about 20 to 25 cm (8 to 10 in) (75%).
- A shot through the shoulder blade wastes meat and can deflect (85%, Texas hunter education).
- A gut hit taints meat near the wound; gut and cool fast once you recover it (Tip, [field dressing](#/s/field-dressing)).
- Rutting bucks carry thick necks and shoulders: aim behind the shoulder, not in it (75%).
- Full meat chapter: [Chapter 10](#/s/mc-mule-deer-10).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mmd-body-cutaway
```

- **Rhyme:** "Up the leg a third of the way; lungs and heart are where you lay."
- **Number trick:** 4 chambers, 3 cusps at 1.5 years, 2 cusps at 2.5 (85%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Shoot the lungs, eat the shoulders."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Hollow hair and a sunny slope keep a mule deer's winter hope."
- **Number trick:** 25 and 41: centimetres of snow that cost 50% and 150% more energy (85%).
- **Number trick:** 8 to 10: few mule deer live longer (90%).
- **Number trick:** 177: a newborn fawn's resting heart rate; 60 when you walk up (85%).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely taught, opinion): "Aim small, miss small." Pick one hair behind the leg, not the whole deer.
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Too far back, wait it back." A gut hit means hours of waiting.

> [!mistake]
> - Aiming at the middle of the deer. That is the rumen.
> - Aiming at the shoulder of a quartering to deer.
> - Expecting fat on a December buck.

> [!field]
> Next deer you see: picture the lungs and heart inside it. Say "leg, third up" out loud.
```
