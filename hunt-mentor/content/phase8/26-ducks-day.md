> [!why]
> Ducks run on a daily commute: roost, food, rest, food, roost. Be on the route at the right minute.

```diagram sb-duck-day
```

- Fields at dawn and late day. Water at midday and night (80%).
- Legal from 30 minutes before sunrise (99%).

```more the daily pattern
- Mallards feed early morning and late afternoon in harvested fields, then return to marshes and creeks for the night (80%).
- Wigeon raft offshore until late afternoon, then move into marshes and ponds to feed (80%).
- Redheads spend the day rafted on deep water and feed morning and evening in the shallows (80%).
- Legal hours: half an hour before sunrise to half an hour after sunset (99%). [Rule Book](#/s/rb-migratory-birds/17).
```

## The day clock

```diagram mdk-day-clock
```

- Two flights a day: out to feed, back to rest (80%).
- The first 30 minutes of legal light are prime (Tip).

```more how the clock shifts
- GPS (Global Positioning System) tracked mallards under pressure made about two flights a day (80%).
- The clock moves with sunrise. In October sunrise gets later each week, so the dawn flight does too (Tip).
- Where hunting is heavy, the feeding flight shifts to after dark and the birds are back on the refuge before dawn (80%).
- Old hand's rule (opinion, widely taught): "Be set up and quiet before the first bird can see."
```

## Weather: how sure are we?

```diagram mdk-weather-evidence
```

- Ice, snow and pressure: strong evidence (80%).
- Barometer on its own: no good study found (VERIFY).

```more the evidence behind each line
- Freeze up and snow: a Missouri study built a weather severity index from cold, days below freezing and snow cover. When it passed a threshold, mallards were more likely to leave (80%).
- Warmer winters with less snow are expected to delay fall migration, most for mallards and least for pintails (70%).
- Wind: ducks land into the wind (80%).
- Moon: waterfowl leave to feed earlier on moonlit, windy nights than on dark, calm nights (75%).
- Full moon makes mornings slow because ducks fed all night: hunter lore, not shown in a study found here (Tip).
- Cold front with a north wind brings new birds: hunter lore, widely repeated (65%).
- Barometric pressure on its own: no duck study found. Pressure falls before a front, so it may only be a sign of the front (VERIFY).
```

## Cold fronts

```anim mdk-cold-front A cold front pushes new ducks in.
```

- Hunt the morning after a front passes (65%, lore).
- New birds decoy better than the locals (Tip).

```more reading a front
- A [[Cold front]] brings a wind shift to the north or northwest, colder air and often clearer skies (Tip).
- Migrating ducks ride tailwinds. A tracked mallard was clocked at about 165 km/h (103 mph) ground speed on a strong tailwind (70%).
- Before the front: birds may feed hard (Tip). After it: fresh birds on new water (65%).
- Check forecasts 3 days ahead and plan your ponds for the morning after (Tip).
```

## Wind and rain

```anim hf-duck-landing Ducks circle, then land into the wind.
```

- Wind sets the landing direction (80%).
- Windy days push ducks to calm, sheltered water (Tip).

```more wind and rain in practice
- Ducks land into the wind; set the spread so they cross in front of you (80% landing, Tip setup).
- Strong wind: ducks hold on the lee side of ponds and sloughs (65%).
- Rain and low cloud: ducks often fly lower and move later in the morning (Tip).
- Calm, clear "bluebird" days: ducks fly high and decoy poorly (Tip, widely reported).
- Cold rain plus wind is the hardest on you: plan for cold water safety. See [Boat and kayak safety](#/s/first-duck-hunt/10).
```

## The moon

| Moon | What ducks do | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Bright, windy night | Leave to feed earlier | Medium (75%) |
| Full moon | Feed all night, slow morning | Lore (Tip) |
| Dark, calm night | Leave later | Medium (75%) |

- The moon is a small factor. Pressure and weather are bigger (Tip).

```more what the research says
- Ducks Unlimited (Ducks after dark): waterfowl leave to feed earlier on moonlit, windy nights than dark, calm nights (75%).
- Full moon claims for duck hunting are mostly field stories, not studies (70% that no study backs them).
- A deer study found little moon effect on buck movement, a reminder not to over trust the moon (65%).
```

## Hunting pressure changes the day

```diagram mdk-pressure-map
```

- Pressure moves feeding into the dark (80%).
- Weekdays and quiet spots beat Saturday crowds (Tip).

```more pressure facts
- West Tennessee: the study area was hunted almost every day; mallards avoided hunted ground during shooting hours (80%).
- Disturbing their refuges cut the landscape's daily shotgun volleys by about a third: ducks hunkered down and moved less (80%).
- Mallards prioritize safety, then food, and keep commutes short (80%).
- Hunter meaning: find where the refuge birds feed and the line they fly. Hunt that line at first and last light (Tip).
```

## Puddle ducks and divers keep different hours

```photo g-redhead-flight Redheads raft by day and feed at the ends of the day.
```

- Puddle ducks: dawn and dusk to fields and shallows (80%).
- Divers raft by day, feed in the shallows early and late (80%).

```more by species
| Duck | Daily habit |
|---|---|
| Mallard | Fields at dawn and late day; marsh at night (80%) |
| Wigeon | Raft offshore until late afternoon (80%) |
| Redhead | Raft on deep water by day; feed morning and evening (80%) |
| Bufflehead, scaup, ring necked | Feed more by day than at night (75%) |
| Mergansers | Feed almost only in daylight (75%) |
| Gadwall (studied in Louisiana) | Feed and rest more at night (70%) |

- Diving ducks on the Great Lakes leave roosts at dusk and feed overnight near shore (75%).
```
