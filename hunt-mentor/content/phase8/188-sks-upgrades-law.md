> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!why]
> The SKS (Simonov self loading carbine) has a huge market of add ons. A few of them turn a legal rifle into a restricted or prohibited one, and that is a crime.

```diagram sks-upgrade-lights
```

- Green: change freely. Orange: ask a qualified gunsmith first. Red: never (Tip on the sorting; each red item is law below).
- Never alter the rifle in a way that changes its legal class.

```more how to read this chapter
- Class depends on the Criminal Code s. 84(1) definitions and the prohibited list in SOR/98-462 (99%, read directly, both current to 30 September 2026).
- The RCMP Firearms Reference Table (FRT) applies those rules to each model. It says it "is not a legal instrument"; the Act and Regulations are the authority (99%, FRT disclaimer).
- When in doubt: a qualified gunsmith, or the RCMP Canadian Firearms Program, before you buy the part (Tip).
```

## Barrel length decides the class

```diagram sks-barrel-length
```

- Standard 520 mm barrel: non restricted (99%).
- Shortened SKS records in the FRT are restricted, or prohibited when cut (99%).

```more the law and the FRT records
> [!law] Criminal Code s. 84(1), "restricted firearm", paragraph (b)
> "a firearm that (i) is not a prohibited firearm, (ii) has a barrel less than 470 mm in length, and (iii) is capable of discharging centre-fire ammunition in a semi-automatic manner"

> [!law] Criminal Code s. 84(1), "prohibited firearm", paragraph (b)
> "a firearm that is adapted from a rifle or shotgun, whether by sawing, cutting or any other alteration, and that, as so adapted, (i) is less than 660 mm in length, or (ii) is 660 mm or greater in length and has a barrel less than 457 mm in length"

- FRT Type 56 SKS records (99%, read directly, valid 19 October 2026):
  - 520 mm: non restricted (FRN 142679, 141924, 142871, 33820).
  - 478 mm and 483 mm: non restricted (FRN 181122, 2387).
  - 408 mm, 419 mm, 422 mm: restricted under s. 84(1) "restricted firearm" para. (b) (FRN 177622, 2387, 127793).
  - 400 mm, cut down: prohibited under s. 84(1) "prohibited firearm" para. (b)(ii) (FRN 2387).
- A muzzle brake does not count toward barrel length: barrel length excludes "any component, part or accessory designed or intended to suppress the muzzle flash or reduce recoil" (99%, s. 84(2)).
- Never cut, thread or shorten the barrel (99% on the law; the advice is a Tip).
```

## The 5 round magazine law

```diagram sks-mag-law
```

- 5 rounds max in a magazine made for a semi automatic centrefire long gun (99%).
- Never remove or defeat the pin or rivet (99%).

```more the law in full
> [!law] SOR/98-462, Schedule, Part 4, item 3(1)(a)
> A cartridge magazine is prohibited if it is "capable of containing more than five cartridges of the type for which the magazine was originally designed" and made for "a semi-automatic firearm other than a semi-automatic handgun".

- A larger magazine is legal only if altered so the change "cannot be easily removed" and it "cannot be easily further altered" (99%, item 3(4)). Approved methods include a plug, sleeve, rod, pin or flange fixed by "welding, brazing or any other similar method" in a steel casing (99%, item 3(5)).
- RCMP: possession is legal if the magazine "has been permanently altered so that it cannot hold more than the number of cartridges allowed by law" (95%, RCMP page modified 18 October 2024).
- Since Bill C-21 (December 2023): "it is a Criminal Code offence to alter or modify a cartridge magazine to hold more than its lawful capacity", and "individuals require a valid firearms licence to acquire a cartridge magazine" (95%, RCMP).
- "Cartridge magazine means a device or container from which ammunition may be fed into the firing chamber of a firearm" (99%, s. 84(1)). So the fixed SKS magazine counts (90%, my reading).
- Capacity is counted in the cartridge the magazine was designed for (95%, RCMP).
- Aftermarket detachable magazine conversions: the FRT notes the SKS D "can use any magazine intended for use with the Kalashnikov AK design. As such higher capacity magazines will be encountered" (99%). Any magazine you use must be a legal 5 round one (99%). Changing your rifle to take detachable magazines: ask a gunsmith first (Tip).
```

## Stocks: the 660 mm line

```diagram sks-stock-rules
```

- A stock that lets it fire when folded under 660 mm: restricted (99%).
- A bullpup stock: a prohibited device (99%).

```more stocks and the law
> [!law] Criminal Code s. 84(1), "restricted firearm", paragraph (c)
> "a firearm that is designed or adapted to be fired when reduced to a length of less than 660 mm by folding, telescoping or otherwise"

- Bullpup stock: a prohibited device, "a stock that, when combined with a firearm, reduces the overall length of the firearm such that a substantial part of the reloading action or the magazine-well is located behind the trigger of the firearm when it is held in the normal firing position" (99%, SOR/98-462 Part 4 item 2).
- The FRT notes "a large number of aftermarket accessories, including scopes and mounts, thumbhole stocks, peep sights" on non restricted Type 56 SKS records (99%). A fixed replacement stock that keeps the length is common (85%, my reading). Folding, telescoping or bullpup: ask a gunsmith and check the law first (Tip).
- Keep the original stock. It holds value and is always legal on your rifle (Tip).
```

## Never do these

```diagram sks-red-list
```

- Full auto, rapid fire triggers, suppressors: prohibited (99%).
- Each one is a crime, not a gun shop choice (99%).

```more the law behind each red item
- Automatic: an "automatic firearm" is one capable of "discharging projectiles in rapid succession during one pressure of the trigger". It is a prohibited firearm (99%, Criminal Code s. 84(1)).
- Trigger devices: "Any electrical or mechanical device that is designed or adapted to operate the trigger mechanism of a semi-automatic firearm for the purpose of causing the firearm to discharge cartridges in rapid succession" is a prohibited device (99%, SOR/98-462 Part 4 item 1).
- Suppressor: "a device or contrivance designed or intended to muffle or stop the sound or report of a firearm" is a prohibited device (99%, s. 84(1)).
- A slam fire or a rifle that doubles is a fault, not a feature: stop and see a gunsmith (Tip; chapter 1).
- Bayonet: the law texts read for this course do not name bayonets (85%, my reading). Folding it away is the norm; removing it is a gunsmith job and it should not be on the rifle when hunting (Tip). VERIFY with the RCMP if you plan to remove or replace it.
```

## Check the status every season

```diagram sks-check-status
```

- The SKS is under federal review (80%, 21 October 2026).
- Before each season: check the FRT and the news. VERIFY.

```more how to check
- Read the marks on the left side of the receiver: "Type 56" in Chinese script, an arsenal number in a triangle or rectangle, the serial number (99%, FRT notes).
- Find the matching record in the RCMP [Firearms Reference Table](https://rcmp.ca/en/firearms/firearms-reference-table) under Norinco, "Type 56 SKS" (99%). The PDF is very large; a gunsmith or the RCMP can look it up for you (Tip).
- News: the classification review announced 5 December 2025 includes the SKS; on 21 October 2026 the advisory group had not met (80%, The Canadian Press).
- If the law changes, the government publishes an order and the RCMP updates its lists. Do not guess. Ask the RCMP Canadian Firearms Program (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): If a part changes what the rifle is, it is not an upgrade. It is a charge.

> [!mistake]
> - Buying a folding or bullpup stock online because "the shop sold it".
> - Prying the rivet out of the magazine "just to clean it".
> - Cutting the barrel to make a handy carbine.

> [!field]
> Photograph the left side markings of your receiver. Bring the photo to a gunsmith with your questions list.
