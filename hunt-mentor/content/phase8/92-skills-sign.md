## Measure a track

```diagram mc-skills-track-measure
```

- Measure the floor of the print, not the rim (80%).
- Average 3 to 5 prints (80%).

```more how to measure, and hoof sizes
- **Length:** tip of the hoof or toe to the back of the heel, along the foot's own line (80%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife).
- **Width:** widest point, at right angles to the length (80%).
- **Stride:** same foot to same foot, which is 2 steps (75%, the app's convention in [Tracks](#/s/tracks)).
- **Straddle:** outside edge of the left prints to outside edge of the right prints (75%).
- Mud and snow push the rim out, so the top looks bigger. Measure the floor (80%).
- "Measure several footprints and average the size if possible" (80%, Halfpenny and Furman via Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife).
- Front prints are a little bigger in deer, elk, moose and the dog and cat families; a black bear's hind print is longer (75 to 80%).

| Animal | Front hoof length |
|---|---|
| White tailed deer | 3.5 to 10 cm (80%) |
| Mule deer | 5.7 to 10 cm (80%) |
| Elk | 7.6 to 12.7 cm (80%) |
| Moose | 11.4 to 17.8 cm (80%) |

- From North Dakota Game and Fish, converted from inches (80%). Deer and elk overlap at the top end: look at shape too (70%).
```

## Gait tells the mood

```steps Three deer gaits
anim:sg-gait-walk | Walk: calm. Prints in a tidy line.
anim:sg-gait-trot | Trot: going somewhere. Longer gaps.
anim:sg-gait-stot | Bound or stot: spooked. Groups of four.
```

- Faster gait: longer stride, narrower straddle (85%).
- A change in pattern marks an event: stopped, spooked, fed (Tip).

```more reading gait
- Walking tracks out of a bed: it left on its own to feed. Stot or gallop groups out: something spooked it, maybe you (80%, [Beds](#/s/rs-beds)).
- Feeding deer wander: short steps, side trips, nibbled twigs (Tip).
- Travelling deer go straight on a trail (Tip).
- Prints splay wider on slopes, in mud and when running (80%, North Dakota Game and Fish).
- Detail with measured groups: [Measuring tracks](#/s/rs-track-measuring).
```

## Droppings: shape, size, menu

```diagram mc-skills-scat-shapes
```

- Pellets: hoofed animals and hares. Tapered rope: dog family. Pile: bear (75 to 80%).
- Gloves and a stick, every time (85%).

```more droppings in detail
- Pellets: deer, elk, moose, hare, rabbit, porcupine (80%).
- Tapered rope, often with hair: coyote, wolf, fox (75%). Coyote scat up to about 13 cm long (85%, North Dakota Game and Fish).
- Segmented with blunt ends, sometimes scratched over: cougar, bobcat, lynx (75%).
- Big variable pile, never covered: bear (75 to 80%).
- White cap on a small curved dropping: a bird (80%).
- Sizes: deer pellet about 1.3 cm long, pointed one end (85%); moose about 1.9 cm across and 2.5 to 3.8 cm long (80%); elk 1.3 to 2.5 cm, often dimpled one end (65%).
- Green summer feed makes soft clumps; woody fall and winter feed makes hard separate pellets (70%).
- Piled: the animal stood or bedded. Scattered: it was walking (80%).
- Why gloves: a tapeworm of wolves, coyotes and foxes is in parts of BC; risk to people is "very low" but gloves and soap prevent it (85%, Alberta Health; [Droppings](#/s/rs-droppings)).
```

## Beds, rubs, scrapes, trails

```diagram mc-skills-sign-heights
```

- Deer bed 0.9 to 1.2 m. Rubs mostly knee to waist high (60 to 70%).
- Scrape: pawed soil under a licking branch 1.2 to 1.5 m up (65%).

```more the big four in detail
- **Bed:** oval of flattened grass, duff or snow, 0.9 to 1.2 m long, with hair and pellets (70%). Elk beds 1.2 to 1.5 m, in groups (65%). Moose: bigger, often alone, near water (60%).
- Beds sit where temperature and cover suit the animal: shade and canopy in heat, sun in cold (75%, mule deer bed studies via [Beds](#/s/rs-beds)).
- **Rub:** bark scraped by antlers. Most deer rubs sit about 30 to 105 cm up; centred 75 cm or higher suggests a mature buck (60%). Fresh: bright wet wood, curled bark below. Old: grey, dry (65%).
- Rub height says more than tree size (60%). The rubbed side usually faces where the buck came from (60%).
- **Scrape:** a pawed patch of bare soil under an overhanging licking branch about 1.2 to 1.5 m up (65%). About 80% of scraping in the north happens mid October to mid November (65%). Most scrape visits happen at night (65%).
- Mule deer make fewer rubs and rarely scrape; white tails do both (60%).
- **Trail:** packed paths between bed, feed and water. Deep cut, many prints: an old route; faint: a seasonal one (Tip). Funnels: [Trails and funnels](#/s/funnels).
- Elk rub higher, often above your head; moose thrash willow and alder (65%).
```

## Feathers and bird sign

```diagram mc-skills-feather-clues
```

- Plucked whole quills: a bird of prey. Bitten shafts: a mammal (60%).
- Grouse dust bowl about 20 cm; quail bowls 7 to 15 cm (65%).

```more bird sign in detail
- Ground birds walk, one print ahead of the next; many songbirds hop in pairs (65%).
- Ruffed grouse track about 5 cm (85%, Maine Inland Fisheries and Wildlife). Duck 5 to 7.6 cm, goose 7.6 to 10 cm, webbed (85%, North Dakota Game and Fish).
- Upland birds dust "to help minimize body parasites" (85%, North Dakota Game and Fish).
- Piles of curved white capped droppings: a night roost (70 to 85%).
- Wing marks in snow: a take off, or a raptor strike (65%).
- Feathers: a ring of plucked feathers on a stump with a breastbone left suggests a raptor; chewed, wet, clumped feathers suggest a mammal (60%, [Bird sign](#/s/rs-bird-sign)).
- Mark every downed bird and retrieve it: retrieval is the law for migratory birds (99%, [Rule Book: migratory birds](#/s/rb-migratory-birds)).
```

## How fresh? The age ladder

```diagram mc-skills-age-ladder
```

- Sharp edges, moist, shiny: fresh. Rounded, dry, debris inside: old (65 to 70%).
- Give a range, never a minute (Tip).

```more aging in detail
- Honest warning: even expert trackers sort sign into broad bands, "fresh" and "old", more easily than into hours (65%, [Aging tracks and sign](#/s/rs-track-aging)).
- As a track ages "bits of disturbed soil will dry and the track edges will round out" (65%).
- Leaves, needles, insect trails or spider web inside: time has passed, often a night or more (70%).
- Mud ages slowest; sand and dust fastest. Shade and cold keep sign crisp for days (65%).
- Droppings: use moisture, shine and crust, not colour (60%, Tip).
- Your own boot print beside it is the clock: compare the two (Tip).
- Trackers test their own ground: make prints daily and watch them age (65%).
```

## Weather stamps the time

```diagram mc-skills-weather-stamp
```

- Rain pits inside: made before the rain. Clean print on pitted ground: after (70%).
- Frost inside: before the frost. Cut through frost: this morning (65%).

```more weather clues in detail
- Know the last 72 hours of weather before you read sign (Tip).
- Snow inside a track: made before or during the snowfall (60%).
- Dark line through dewy grass: made after the dew formed, often this morning (65%, Nature Mentor via search preview).
- Rain lightens blood and washes it (65%). See [Chapter 8](#/s/mc-skills-08).
- Sun melts snow prints bigger and rounder over days: do not measure an old snow print (65%).
```

## Follow a track to the animal

```steps Follow a track in 4 steps
mc-skills-follow-1 | Flag the last sure print.
mc-skills-follow-2 | Glance down, then look 40 to 100 m ahead.
mc-skills-follow-3 | Track stick: stride mark finds the next print.
mc-skills-follow-4 | Lost it? Circle 10 m, then 20 m.
```

- Following a trail with a firearm is hunting. All hunting rules apply (95%).

```more following in detail
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Track with your eyes ahead, not at your feet (60%, [Following a track](#/s/rs-track-following)).
- Low side light shows faint tracks: get low and look toward the light (65%, Wildwood Tracking via search preview).
- Track stick: a pole about 0.9 to 1.2 m long with rubber bands set at stride length (65%).
- Skip meandering doe and fawn trails if you want a buck (60%, MeatEater via search preview).
- When the trail slows to feeding, slow right down and scan every direction (60%).
- Never wander ahead making a second set of tracks over the trail (70%).
- A buck may loop back and watch his back trail (60%, Realtree via search preview).
- Hunt includes "following after or on the trail of" wildlife "while in possession of a firearm" (95%, [Rule Book: definitions](#/s/rb-definitions)). Legal hours and land status still apply (95%).
```

## What a 40 year tracker does differently

| Beginner | Old hand (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Sees one track | Reads the whole pattern |
| Guesses "fresh" | Compares with his own boot print |
| Follows the first track | Picks the track worth following |
| Stares at his feet | Looks ahead for the animal |
| Forgets the weather | Knows the last 3 days of it |

- All lines are hunter wisdom, labelled Tip.

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-skills-age-ladder
```

- **Rhyme:** "Sharp and wet, close you get. Round and dry, it said goodbye."
- **Number trick:** 3 to 5 prints to average; 1.2 to 1.5 m for a scrape's licking branch (65 to 80%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, short form of "track with your eyes ahead", [Following a track](#/s/rs-track-following)): "Read it, then lift your eyes."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Pellets hoof, rope dog, pile bear, white cap bird."
- **Rhyme:** "Pits inside, rain came after the ride."
- **Number trick:** 0.9 to 1.2 m, a deer bed; 30 to 105 cm, rub height (60 to 70%).
- **Number trick:** 80% of scraping, mid October to mid November (65%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, from [Aging tracks and sign](#/s/rs-track-aging)): "Your boot print is the clock."

> [!mistake]
> - Measuring the rim of a snow print.
> - Calling a track "minutes old" with no weather check.
> - Walking on the trail you are following.

> [!field]
> Make a boot print and a stick scrape near the truck. Check it every visit for a week. Photograph it each time. That is your aging calibration.
```
