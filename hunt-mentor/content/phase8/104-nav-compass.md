## The parts that matter

```diagram compass
```

- Baseplate, travel arrow, turning ring, orienting arrow, needle (95%).
- Red needle in the outline arrow: "red in the shed" (Tip).

```more parts and buying
- Baseplate: the clear plate with a ruler and the direction of travel arrow (95%, [Maps 2](#/s/maps-navigation)).
- Bezel or ring: turns, marked 0 to 360 degrees (95%).
- Orienting arrow: the outline arrow in the ring, "the shed" (Tip name).
- Needle: red end to magnetic north (95%).
- Index line: where you read the bearing (95%).
- Buy one with adjustable declination; a sighting mirror helps (Tip). Prices: VERIFY at a local store.
- A [[Bearing]] is a direction in degrees clockwise from north (95%).
```

## Job 0: set declination once

```steps Set an adjustable compass to 15° east
fn-decl-set-1 | Find the adjuster: a small screw or a key.
fn-decl-set-2 | Turn it until the orienting arrow sits 15° east.
fn-decl-set-3 | Now red in the shed gives a true bearing.
```

- Heffley Creek and Mission: about 15 degrees east. Osoyoos: about 14.6 (95%).
- Check it each spring and fall (Tip).

```more declination traps
- Set east, not west. 15 west makes you 30 degrees out: 577 m off per km (98%, maths).
- No adjustable compass? Map to compass subtract 15; compass to map add 15 (90%).
- The Hunt Map and phone bearings are from true north. A compass set to 15 east also reads true, so the numbers match (90%).
- Declination drifts about 1 degree every 7 or 8 years here (95%, NOAA yearly change).
```

## Job 1: take a bearing off the map

```steps Map bearing in 4 steps
fn-mapbrg-1 | Lay the edge from you to the target.
fn-mapbrg-2 | Turn the ring: north lines with map north.
fn-mapbrg-3 | Read the bearing at the index line.
fn-mapbrg-4 | Lift it, red in the shed, walk.
```

- On the map, ignore the needle (95%).
- Check your answer with the Hunt Map Measure tool (99%).

```more map bearings in full
1. Edge of the compass from where you are to where you want to go, travel arrow toward the target (95%, Little Book of Orienteering Techniques).
2. Turn the ring until its north lines run with the map's north lines, N to the top of the map (95%).
3. Read the number at the index line (95%).
4. Lift the compass, hold it flat at your belly, turn your whole body until red is in the shed, then walk (95%).
- On paper NTS (National Topographic System) grid lines, allow for grid north, about 2.5 degrees east of true near Heffley Creek (85%, [Chapter 3](#/s/mc-nav-03)).
- Hunt Map: Tools, Draw and measure, Measure. It shows the last leg's bearing in degrees from true north, for example "toward NE (48°)" (99%, app).
```

## Job 2: follow the bearing, tree to tree

```anim mc-nav-leapfrog Sight a tree on the line, walk to it, sight the next.
```

- Pick a tree on the line, walk to it, take the bearing again (95%).
- Eyes on the tree, not on the needle (95%).

```more following a bearing in full
- Hold the compass flat, red in the shed, look up along the travel arrow and pick something on that line: a snag, a rock, a split tree (95%, [Maps 2](#/s/maps-navigation)).
- Walk to it without the compass. Repeat (95%).
- Pick targets that stay easy to see when you are close: a dead tree, not "one of the firs" (Tip).
- In thick timber, targets are 20 m apart. In the open, 200 m (Tip).
- Detours around a swamp or blowdown: box around with counted paces, or sight a tree across it (Tip, see [Compass mastery](#/s/compass-mastery)).
```

## Two hunters: the leapfrog

```diagram mc-nav-partner-leapfrog
```

- Your brother walks ahead; you wave him onto the line; you walk to him (Tip).
- Best in fog, timber and flat ground with no landmarks (Tip).

```more the two person method
1. You hold the compass on the bearing. He walks ahead on roughly the line, as far as you can clearly see him (Tip).
2. You wave him left or right until he stands on the line. He stops (Tip).
3. You walk to him. Repeat (Tip).
4. Swap who holds the compass every few legs, so both of you practise (Tip).
- Keep each other in sight at all times. If you lose sight, stop and call out (75%, partner rules in [Hunting with a partner](#/s/hunting-partner)).
- Firearms during navigation: muzzles in a safe direction and never at your partner. Unload when you are moving to the truck after legal light (99% for hours, [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods); unloading on the walk out is Tip).
```

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

## Job 3: the back bearing

```diagram mc-nav-back-bearing
```

- Back bearing: add 180 if under 180, take 180 if over (95%).
- Out at 40, home at 220.

```more back bearings in full
- A back bearing is the reverse direction of the bearing you took (95%).
- Under 180: add 180. Over 180: subtract 180 (95%, [Maps 2](#/s/maps-navigation)).
- Shortcut: many compasses let you walk "white in the shed": put the white (south) end of the needle in the outline arrow and walk the travel arrow (Tip; check yours).
- Write it down when you leave the truck: "Out 40, home 220" (Tip).
- A straight back bearing aimed at a point rarely lands on it. Aim off for the road instead (next screen but one).
```

## Job 4: find yourself

```steps Two bearings, one cross
fn-tri-1 | Take a bearing to a landmark you can name.
fn-tri-2 | Draw its back bearing on the map from the landmark.
fn-tri-3 | Do the same for a second landmark.
fn-tri-4 | Where the lines cross is you.
```

- Landmarks about 90 degrees apart give the sharpest cross (85%).

```more cross bearings in full
- Proper name: resection; with three lines people call it [[Triangulation]] (85%, Wikipedia via [Compass mastery](#/s/compass-mastery)).
- Steps: bearing to a mapped feature, correct for declination if not set, convert to the back bearing, draw from the feature; repeat; the cross is you (85%).
- A third line makes a small triangle; you are most likely inside it (Tip). A big triangle means retake the bearings.
- Peaks, lake ends, a fire lookout or a radio tower make good landmarks (Tip).
```

```diagram mc-nav-line-handrail
```

- Shortcut: on a road, creek or ridge, one bearing is enough. Its back bearing crosses your handrail at you (Tip).

```more the one bearing shortcut
- You already know you are somewhere on the line feature. One more line pins the spot (85%, resection logic).
- Pick a landmark roughly side on to the handrail, not straight ahead along it (Tip; lines that cross near 90 degrees are sharpest, 85%).
- Check it against the shape of the land: does the slope around you match the map? ([Chapter 5](#/s/mc-nav-05)).
```

## Aiming off: miss on purpose

```anim fn-nav-aimoff Aim to one side on purpose. Then you know which way to turn.
```

- Truck on a road? Aim well to one side, hit the road, turn (85%).
- Aim off more than your likely error (Tip).

```more how much to aim off
- "Aiming deliberately to one side of a feature to know which way to turn when you hit it" (85%, Little Book of Orienteering Techniques).
- Error after 2 km at 5 degrees: about 175 m (98%, maths). So aim off 200 to 300 m (Tip).
- At night or in fog, aim off more (Tip; see [Night navigation](#/s/night-navigation)).
- Classic hunter uses: the truck on a road, a boat on a lake shore, a camp on a creek (Tip).
```

## How far off is "a little off"?

```diagram mc-nav-error-km
```

- 1 degree off misses by 17 m per km (98%).
- Forget declination: 268 m per km. Set it west: 577 m (98%).

```more errors and metal
- Miss distance = distance walked × tangent of the error angle (98%, maths).
- A hand held compass is good to about 2 degrees (Tip, [Compass mastery](#/s/compass-mastery)).
- Steel, magnets, phones and vehicles bend the needle (80%, common compass guidance). Step away from the truck, lean the rifle away, phone in a pocket (Tip).
- A tilted compass drags the needle. Hold it level (Tip).
- Check the compass against a known line before trusting it: a straight road on the map (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-nav-compass-jobs
```

- **Rhyme:** "Red in the shed, then go ahead."
- **Number trick:** 1 degree, 17 metres per km. 15 degrees, 268 (98%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, from [Compass mastery](#/s/compass-mastery) Grandpa's rule): "Trust the compass over your gut. Your gut has never been to this draw."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Under 180 add it, over 180 take it."
- **Rhyme:** "Tree to tree, not needle to knee."
- **Rhyme:** "Miss on purpose, find it for sure" (aiming off).
- **Number trick:** 90 degrees apart for a sharp cross (85%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, [Maps 2](#/s/maps-navigation)): "Take a bearing to the truck before you leave the truck."

> [!mistake]
> - Setting declination west instead of east.
> - Taking a bearing beside the truck or with the rifle on your shoulder.
> - Walking with eyes on the needle instead of a target tree.
```
