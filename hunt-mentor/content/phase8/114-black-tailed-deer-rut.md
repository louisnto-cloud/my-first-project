> [!why]
> The coast rut peaks in late November, inside your season. Bucks that hid all fall start moving in daylight. That is your best window.

```diagram mbt-rut-calendar
```

- Coast deer breed in November and early December (90%).
- Most does conceive 15 November to 5 December (90%).

```more timing and the season
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- "Vancouver Island blacktails breed during November and early December, with most does conceiving during their second ovulation, from November 15 to December 5" (90%, Nyberg and Janz 1990, citing Thomas 1970).
- Thomas (1970, University of British Columbia thesis, 444 does from Vancouver Island): 8 or 9 day follicular cycles in the November to early December breeding season (80%, abstract via search preview).
- BC deer account: rutting season "in November and early December" (90%).
- WDFW (Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife): the rut "normally occurs in November and December" (80%).
- MUs (Management Units) 2-8, 2-18, 2-19: bucks 10 September to 15 December; bow only bucks 1 to 9 September (99%, page 28).
- Harrison is not Vancouver Island. Exact local peak dates: VERIFY. Treat late November as the likely peak (Tip).
```

## Before the rut: displays

```diagram mbt-displays
```

- Bucks circle rivals: stiff steps, back arched, head low, tail flicking (90%).
- They thrash bushes with their antlers (90%).

```more displays and fights
- In the weeks before the rut, bucks show more dominance and indirect threats (90%, BC deer account).
- A dominant buck circles a rival with deliberate steps, back arched, head low, tail flicking. The smaller buck often bolts (90%).
- Bucks thrash bushes with their antlers (90%). Look for shredded saplings and stripped bark (Tip).
- Young bucks of unequal size spar a lot. It is bonding, not fighting (90%).
- In the rut, mature bucks of equal size fight head to head (90%).
- Rubs, scrapes and the rut in general: [The rut](#/s/the-rut).
```

## The tending bond

```diagram mbt-tending
```

- A buck follows a doe 2 to 3 days before her heat (90%).
- She is receptive about a day (90%).

```more how breeding works
- A doe is receptive for a short time, about a day. If not bred, she cycles again, usually 22 to 29 days later (90%, Nyberg and Janz, citing Wallmo 1978, Thomas and Cowan 1975).
- A buck may follow a doe 2 or 3 days before her heat, then stay with her 3 or 4 days after mating (90%).
- So one buck is not likely to breed more than 3 or 4 does in a 28 day period (90%).
- [[Tending bond]]: the buck keeps other bucks away until she mates or another buck pushes him off (90%, BC deer account).
- Older, dominant bucks do most of the breeding, though yearlings can breed (90%).
- What it means for you: find the does and you find the bucks in late November (Tip).
```

## Body and antlers through the year

- Heaviest about mid November to early December (90%).
- Antlers usually shed by January; regrow in summer (90%).

```more the body year
- Columbian black tails are heaviest about the time of the rut, mid November to early December; through most winters adults lose weight, often 20 to 25% of their peak fall weight (90%, Nyberg and Janz).
- Antlers are usually shed by January and regrow during summer (90%). BC deer account: dropped January to March in BC, older bucks first; regrown April to August (90%).
- Fawns are born after about 200 days, mostly in the first half of June (90%).
- After the rut bucks are thin, some hurt, and they hide and rest (90%, BC deer account).
```

## Your November weekend

```diagram mbt-day-clock
```

- Dawn and dusk still matter most (80%).
- Rut weeks: stay out through midday if you can (Tip).

```more a rut weekend plan (all Tips)
- Before: find does on cutblock edges at dusk in early November. Note where (Tip).
- Dawn: sit or still hunt the edge where does fed (Tip).
- Midday: still hunt slowly through cover between feeding and bedding areas; bucks cruise for does (Tip).
- Dusk: back to the edge (Tip).
- A doe that keeps looking back: wait. A buck may be behind her (Tip).
- Region 2 deer: bucks only in your MUs. Never shoot the doe (99%, page 28).
- Rattling and calls: no black tail specific study found. VERIFY before relying on them.
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom, not law): "In the rut, the doe is the map. Watch her, then look behind her."

> [!mistake]
> - Hunting only the first weekend of the season and missing the November rut.
> - Shooting the first deer out after a doe without checking for antlers.
> - Walking fast through a doe area and pushing the buck out ahead.

> [!field]
> On your November weekend: at every doe you see, stop, wait 5 minutes and glass behind her.
