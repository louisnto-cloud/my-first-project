> [!why]
> A white tail is a nervous homebody. It knows every bush in its range and learns your habits fast.

- Lives its whole life in a small home range (75%).
- Learns pressure in a day, and hides rather than leaves (70%).

## Alarm signals

```steps 5 stages of alarm
sg-alarm-1 | Alert: head up, staring. Freeze.
sg-alarm-2 | Head bob: fakes feeding, snaps up.
sg-alarm-3 | Foot stamp: it wants you to move.
sg-alarm-4 | Snort: every deer near is alert.
sg-alarm-5 | Escape: white tails flag and gallop.
```

- Stamp and snort are tests: freeze (Tip).
- Relaxed tail flicks side to side: calm deer (Tip).

```more alarm detail
1. **Alert:** head up, ears forward, stare (75%).
2. **Head bob:** drops the head as if to feed, then snaps it up (70%, hunter lore).
3. **Foot stamp:** stamps a front hoof. It may also leave extra scent from the gland between the toes (60%).
4. **Snort or blow:** a loud whoosh, a bit like a whistle. It means danger (80%, Idaho Fish and Game).
5. **Escape:** tail up (the flag), smooth gallop to cover (85%).

- Tail flicking side to side while feeding usually means calm (Tip, hunter observation).
- Tail half up, stiff legged walk: suspicious, about to go (Tip).
```

## The voice of a white tail

| Call | Who | Means |
|---|---|---|
| Snort | All | Danger (80%) |
| Grunt | Bucks, does | Contact, low threat (80%) |
| Grunt snort wheeze | Rut bucks | Big challenge (80%) |
| Tending grunt | Buck with doe | Rut (70%) |
| Bleat | Does, fawns | Contact, distress (70%) |

- Researchers recorded 12 white tail calls (80%).

```more voice detail
- Atkeson, Marchinton and Miller (1988) recorded 12 white tail vocalizations and analysed 10 (80%).
- Agonistic (fighting) calls: the low **grunt**, the **grunt snort** (grunt plus 1 to 4 snorts), and the **grunt snort wheeze**, typical of dominant bucks in the breeding season (80%).
- **Tending grunt:** longer and drawn out, made by a buck with a doe (70%, Marchinton via Realtree).
- **Bleats:** fawns and does call to stay in touch, and fawns bleat in distress (70%).
- How you use this: a few soft grunts in late October and November (chapter 9). Never snort wheeze at a small buck: it can scare him off (Tip).
```

## The flag

```gallery
g-wt-buck-flag | Buck flagging as he leaves.
g-wt-run | Low, smooth gallop to cover.
```

- Why it flags is still debated (70%).
- A flag means it is leaving. Let it go (Tip).

```more why the flag
- Ideas: warn other deer, tell the predator "I see you, do not bother", or a beacon for fawns to follow (65%).
- Caro and others (1995) found little support for the "warn other deer" idea (70%).
- Idaho Fish and Game teaches it as a warning and a beacon for fawns in thick brush (80%, agency education).
- For you: a flagging deer is in full flight. No shot (Tip, matches the quick lesson's rule).
```

## Flight: where it runs

```anim mwt-escape-run Flag up, run for the nearest thick cover.
```

- Runs to the nearest thick cover, not over the hill (75%).
- Often stops just inside to look back (Tip).

```more flight detail
- White tails rely on cover close by. They gallop to it, often along a trail they know (75%).
- They can make an almost 90 degree turn at full speed (60%, Field and Stream).
- Standing jump of about 2.4 m (8 ft) is reported (60%, Tip).
- Swims of over 3 km (2 miles) are documented (60%).
- Many hunters see a spooked white tail stop just inside cover and look back (Tip). That look back is not a reason to rush a shot through brush.
- No flight distance study for white tails was found. VERIFY any number you hear.
```

## How it learns pressure

```diagram mwt-pressure-map
```

- GPS deer reacted the day before opening (70%).
- They hide inside their home range. They do not leave (70%).

```more pressure detail
- Penn State satellite collar study (Diefenbach): deer began reacting to hunters the day before season opened (70%, news and radio reports).
- Deer found cover within their home range during hunting hours. One doe moved to a steep hillside refuge once rifle season began (70%).
- Adult males were the hardest of all to kill and reacted fastest (70%).
- Deer return to the same home range year after year, so pressure is not forever (85%).
- Pressured white tails move more at night (70%).
- More: [Hunting pressure](#/s/pressure-push).
```

## Family life

```diagram mwt-social-groups
```

- Does live in family groups with their daughters (80%).
- About 70% of yearling bucks leave home (80%).

```more social detail
- Doe groups are **matrilineal**: an old doe, her daughters and their fawns (80%).
- Yearling does often rejoin their mother and her new fawns after fawning and stay until the next spring (80%, Montana State University Extension).
- Bucks form **bachelor groups** of 2 to 4 in summer (80%, Montana State University Extension). They break up in September and October (75%).
- About 70% of yearling bucks disperse, mostly in October and November in Pennsylvania (80%, Pennsylvania Game Commission). Missouri: 77% of buck fawns left at about 1 year, average about 14 km (9 miles) (75%).
- Dispersing yearlings go farther where there is less forest (80%).
- Does hide newborn fawns alone and return only to nurse (85%).
```

## Hierarchy and fights

```photo g-wt-buck-front
```

- Bucks spar after velvet comes off, then sort out rank (80%).
- White tail bucks fight more than mule deer bucks (75%).

```more hierarchy detail
- After antlers harden, bucks spar and form a **dominance hierarchy** that decides who breeds in the November rut (80%, Montana State University Extension).
- Bucks mark areas with rubs and scrapes but do not defend a territory. They defend the doe in heat (80%).
- Combat is common among dominant bucks in the same area (80%).
- Montana State University Extension: white tail bucks are more aggressive toward each other than mule deer bucks (75%).
- That is why rattling (fake fight sounds) works on white tails (70%, chapter 9).
```

## Old buck vs young buck

```diagram mwt-old-vs-young
```

- Old bucks: smaller home range, thickest cover, night moves (65%).
- Young bucks: daylight, careless, easy to see (Tip).

```more old and young in detail
- A review of 15 years of collar studies found mature bucks' home ranges get smaller with age, not bigger (65%, Boone and Crockett).
- A South Texas study of 33 bucks: home range shrank from yearling to 3 years, then grew in mature bucks (65%). Results differ by place.
- A Mississippi study of 60 collared bucks: the average buck used 31 bedding areas in a season. The few beds used again and again had dense screening cover (65%, Field and Stream).
- Mature bucks are most at risk in the rut **search** phases, before and after peak breeding (65%).
- Yearlings disperse, wander and move in daylight (80% dispersal; daylight Tip).
```

## Predictable mistakes it makes

```diagram rubs-scrapes
```

- Rut bucks move in daylight chasing does (75%).
- Same trail, same field edge, evening after evening (Tip).

```more mistakes you can count on
- **Rut:** bucks cover far more ground and move midday in late October and November (75%).
- **Habit:** a calm white tail uses the same trail from bed to food night after night (Tip, hunter consensus).
- **Curiosity:** a buck may come to a soft grunt or rattling near the rut (70%).
- **Following a doe:** a buck trailing a hot doe walks into the open (Tip).
- **Funnels:** deer squeeze through the same pinch points: creek crossings, fence gaps, brush necks (Tip, see [Funnels](#/s/funnels)).
- **Return:** a deer spooked once is often back in a few days if you leave it alone (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mwt-social-groups
```

- **Rhyme:** "Stamp and stare, freeze right there."
- **Number trick:** 12 calls, 2 to 4 bachelors, 70% of yearlings leave.
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Pressure him and he hides at home."

```more more hooks, mistakes and field drill
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, widely repeated): "An old buck didn't get old by being careless." Matches collar findings that adult males react fastest.
- **Rhyme:** "Does in the family, bucks in a gang; yearlings wander when the rut bells clang."

> [!mistake]
> - Moving when a doe stamps at you.
> - Hunting the same stand every day until the deer pattern you.
> - Using a loud snort wheeze call on young bucks.

> [!field]
> Watch one family group of does at a field edge for an evening. Note who leads, where they enter, and what makes heads come up.
```
