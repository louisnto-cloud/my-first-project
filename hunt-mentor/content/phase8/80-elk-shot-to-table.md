## Shot placement and range

```diagram vitals-elk
```

- Up the back of the front leg, one third to one half up (85%).
- Stay inside your proven range, about 200 m for most (Tip).

```diagram sa-308-range
```

```more placement and range in detail
- Aim: up the back of the front leg, one third to one half up the chest, middle of the lungs (85%, [shot placement](#/s/shot-placement)).
- Broadside heart and lung area on a mature bull: roughly 46 to 61 cm (18 to 24 in); narrower at a quartering angle (65%, hunting media).
- Quartering away: aim for the far front leg. Never through the paunch (85%).
- Head and neck shots: avoid; more wounded and lost elk (85%).
- Your .308 Winchester: 165 to 180 grain premium bullets, about 200 m (219 yd) or your proven limit, whichever is shorter (65%, hunter consensus).
- Proven limit: 9 of 10 hits on a 15 cm plate from a field rest (Tip, [shooting skills](#/s/shooting-skills)).
- Legal: centrefire rifle or bow class A, C or D for elk (99%, page 13).
```

## Reactions, wait, then track

```diagram sk-reactions
```

- Watch, listen, mark the last spot (Tip).
- Lungs out of sight: wait 30 minutes or more (70%).

```more reactions in detail
- A lung hit elk may run hard and show little at first; bulls hit through both lungs usually go down within about 100 yards (91 m); one lung, hundreds of metres (65%, onX citing guides).
- A hunched walk away: often a gut hit (70%, deer patterns in [blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)).
- A herd may run together; watch your elk, not the herd (Tip).
- Mark where the elk stood and the last place you saw it. Note a landmark and drop a waypoint (Tip).
- Listen: a crash, then silence, is good news (Tip).
```

```diagram sk-wait-compare
```

- Gut: back out for hours (70%).
- Liver and gut: hours. Sources differ: liver 2 to 10 h, gut 4 to 12 h (70%).
- Your call. The app does not pick one. NDA: National Deer Association. All sources: [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing).

```more waiting and tracking in detail
> [!lean]
> Your call. The sources disagree on liver and gut waits. The app shows them all and picks none.

| Hit | Hunter Ed blog 2026 | NDA (National Deer Association) 2012 | NDA 2023 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heart or lungs | 30 minutes | 30 minutes (rifle) | A few minutes (rifle) |
| Liver | 4 to 6 hours | 2 hours minimum, sometimes 3 or 4 | 8 to 10 hours |
| Gut | 6 to 8 hours, or overnight | 4 to 6 hours, overnight if cool | 10 to 12 hours |

- Heart or lungs, down in sight: go when it stops moving (Tip).
- Not sure where it hit: Hunter Ed blog says several hours; NDA 2023 says count down from the gut wait (70%).

- Deer derived times; elk are bigger and tougher, so lean to the longer side (Tip).
- In September heat the meat clock fights the waiting clock: wait the minimum for the hit, then move (Tip).
- Bright, frothy blood: lungs. Dark: liver. Green or sour: gut (70 to 80%).
- A dog may help track only on a leash for elk (99%, page 12).
- Lost the trail: grid search, then call for help; never give up early (Tip). [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing).
```

## Field care

```diagram mc-elk-quarter-map
```

- Cancel the licence first (95%).
- Gutless method: quarter it where it lies (Tip).

```video
yt-quarter-elk | Quarter an elk in the field, one side at a time.
yt-gutless-vs-gutting | Gutless or gutting: take the meat, leave the guts.
```

```more field care step by step
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

1. Make sure it is dead; approach from behind (Tip).
2. Cancel the species licence "immediately after the kill and before handling" (95%, page 11, [tagging](#/s/tagging-legal)).
3. Photos, then knives, game bags, rope, headlamp (Tip).
4. [[Gutless method]]: skin one side, take the front and hind quarter, backstrap and neck meat, then the ribs; roll and repeat; reach in for the tenderloins (Tip).
5. Every edible portion comes out: four quarters, loins, neck and ribs (99%, page 11, [[Edible portions]]).
6. Keep [[Evidence of sex]]: the tail or a hide piece of at least 6 cm² plus a testicle or part of the penis (99%, page 15).
7. 6 point bull seasons: the antlers, attached to part of the skull, go with the licence; do not alter them (99%, pages 11 and 15).
8. Hang bags in shade, off the ground, away from the kill site (Tip).
- Bears come to elk kills: work fast, keep your rifle close, make noise when you return (70%, [packing out](#/s/packing-out)).
```

## Meat care

```diagram sk-cooler
```

- Target: meat below 4°C as fast as you can (80%).
- Elk hide holds heat: skin and quarter fast (80%).

```more meat care in detail
- Target: meat below 4°C (40°F) (80%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife).
- Danger zone 4 to 60°C: bacteria can double every 20 minutes (80%, Penn State Extension).
- Above freezing: skin elk, moose and bear (80%, [meat care](#/s/meat-care)).
- September afternoons in the Okanagan can be warm: ice in the cooler before the hunt, a plan for the nearest cold storage (Tip).
- Age at 1 to 3°C for 7 to 14 days, or not at all if the elk ran hard or the meat warmed (80%).
- Freeze at minus 18°C; game keeps about a year (80%).
```

## Pack out and yield

```diagram mc-elk-meat-math
```

- A bull gives about 75 to 99 kg of boneless meat (85%).
- At 25 kg a load: 4 trips alone, 2 each with your brother (Tip).

```diagram mc-elk-pack-trips
```

```more yield and pack out in detail
- University of Wyoming bull weighed at each stage (85%): whole 258 kg (568 lb); field dressed 181 kg (70% of whole); skinned carcass 151 kg; boneless lean 75 kg (50% of field dressed).
- Six bulls averaged 198 kg field dressed and 99 kg boneless; six cows 154 kg and 77 kg (85%).
- One locker plant averaged 43% boneless; trimming fat lowers yield (85%).
- 20 to 30 kg a trip is sustainable for a fit adult; 40 kg is a hero load (Tip, [packing out](#/s/packing-out)).
- Bone in quarters weigh more: a front quarter can be heavier than you think (Tip). Bone out on site to save weight (Tip).
- Record of Receipt when someone else carries your meat (90%, page 14).
```

## Yield and cooking

```diagram sa-yield-species
```

- Lean meat: cook tender cuts hot and fast (Tip).
- Ground meat to 71°C (95%).

```more cooking notes
- Elk is lean; rut bulls leaner still (85%, chapter 2). Add fat to burger (Tip).
- Backstrap and tenderloin: hot and fast, rest before slicing (Tip).
- Shoulder, neck, shank: braise 2.5 to 3 hours (Tip).
- All wild game: cook to an internal 71°C per federal advice; whole muscle cuts many hunters cook less, at their own risk (95% for the advice, [cooking](#/s/cooking)).
- [[CWD]] (Chronic Wasting Disease): Region 3 and Region 8 ask for elk heads for testing (99%). Wait for a test result before eating if you can (Tip).
- Ivories and antlers: a keepsake; see [trophy care](#/s/trophy-care).
```

## Rules recap

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

```diagram re-r8-moose-elk-bars
```

- Region 8: 6 point bulls, 10 Sept to 20 Oct; bow only 1 to 9 Sept; bag 1 (99%).
- Regions 3 and 2: no general open season for elk (99%).

```more the rules recap, with links (no new rules)
- Region 8 elk, page 68 (99%, [Region 8](#/s/rb-region-8)): MUs (Management Units) 8-1 to 8-15 and 8-21 to 8-26; 6 point bulls 10 September to 20 October; bow only 6 point bulls 1 to 9 September; bag 1. Footnote: antlers must accompany the species licence.
- Region 3, page 34: no elk row (99%, [Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3)). Region 2, page 28: no elk row (99%, [Region 2](#/s/rb-region-2)).
- [[6 point bull]]: at least 6 tines on one antler, brow included; tines at least 2.5 cm and longer than wide (99%, pages 3 and 4, [definitions](#/s/rb-definitions)).
- Licences: resident hunting licence plus an elk [[Species licence]], $25.00 for residents (99%, page 8, [licences and fees](#/s/rb-licences-fees)). No waiting period for elk (99%).
- [[LEH]] (Limited Entry Hunting): an authorization is not a licence; elk wins in Regions 1 or 2 cut your odds 66% for 3 years (99%, [LEH strategy](#/s/leh-strategy)). Region 8 hunt 2218 antlerless elk, 8-04 Princeton (99%). Region 3 elk LEH: none in the 2026 to 2027 LEH synopsis (95%, read 2026-10-17).
- Legal methods: centrefire or bow A, C, D; no shotgun, rimfire, air rifle; no recorded or electronic calls; dogs leashed; hours 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (99%, pages 11 to 13, [legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)).
- No elk parts or urine from outside BC for hunting (99%, page 11).
- After the kill: cancel the licence before handling, evidence of sex, antlers with the licence, edible portions out (95 to 99%, pages 11 and 15, [tagging](#/s/tagging-legal), [after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill)).
- [[Mandatory Hunter Report]]: every elk licence buyer, by 31 March, kill or no kill; new for elk in this edition (99%, page 16).
- Region 3 and 8 ask for elk heads for CWD (Chronic Wasting Disease) testing (99%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-elk-shot-to-table
```

- **Rhyme:** "Mark it, wait it, notch it, quarter, bag it, cool it, carry it, cook it."
- **Number trick:** 70, 50: field dressed is 70% of whole; boneless is 50% of field dressed (85%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "One elk, two men, three trips. Plan the pack before the shot." (quick lesson)

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Six on one, brow is one."
- **Number trick:** 4, 25, 4: meat below 4°C; 25 kg a load; about 4 loads for one person (80% and Tip).
- **Number trick:** 31 March: Mandatory Hunter Report, every elk licence (99%).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely taught, opinion): "The real hunt starts after the shot."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Cold meat, clean meat, good meat."

> [!mistake]
> - Shooting a bull at the bottom of a canyon at 4 pm in September heat.
> - Forgetting to keep the antlers with the licence in a 6 point season.
> - Skipping the Mandatory Hunter Report because no elk was killed.

> [!field]
> Before an elk trip: game bags, a sharp knife and spare blades, 15 m of rope, a pack frame, a headlamp, flagging tape, ice in the cooler, and your licence in one kit.
```
