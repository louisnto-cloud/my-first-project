## Bones that guard the vitals

```diagram mwt-shoulder-bones
```

- Shoulder blade covers the front of the lungs (80%).
- Aim tight behind it, one third up (75%).

```more skeleton and muscles that matter
- The front leg hangs from the shoulder blade by muscle only. Deer have no collarbone, so the shoulder slides as the leg moves (75%).
- A forward leg (walking) pulls the shoulder forward and opens the lungs. A back leg covers more lung (Tip, hunter education).
- The heavy leg bone (humerus) runs from the shoulder joint down to the elbow. Hitting it can stop a bullet short of the lungs (Tip).
- Biggest meat: the hindquarters, then the backstraps along the spine, then the shoulders (75%).
- A shoulder shot wastes the most front meat (Tip).
```

## How it breathes

```anim mwt-breath Diaphragm back, air in. Forward, air out.
```

- The diaphragm pulls the lungs open (85%).
- Hit both lungs: fast, humane collapse (75%).

```more breathing detail
- Like all mammals, a deer breathes with its **diaphragm**, a sheet of muscle between chest and belly (90%).
- The diaphragm moves back, the chest gets bigger, air rushes in. It moves forward, air goes out (90%).
- A bullet hole through the chest wall lets air in around the lungs, and they collapse (80%).
- Both lungs hit: the deer runs on stored oxygen, often 50 to 100 m, then falls (Tip, quick lesson; National Deer Association says usually within 137 m, 150 yd) (70%).
- Breath shows on cold mornings. A steaming breath in the brush can give a bedded deer away (Tip).
```

## Heart and big blood vessels

```diagram mwt-heart-vessels
```

- Heart sits low, between the elbows (80%).
- Main artery runs under the spine (85%).

```more heart and vessels detail
- The heart sits low in the chest, roughly level with the elbow (80%).
- Aim one third up the chest to take both lungs and the top of the heart (75%, standard hunter education).
- The **aorta** (main artery) runs from the top of the heart back under the spine (85%).
- A shot high in the lungs can cut the big vessels under the spine, but also risks a spine hit and a deer that drops and gets up again (Tip).
- Heart shot deer often bolt hard, then crash (Tip). See chapter 10.
- Link: [vitals diagram](#/s/shot-placement).
```

## Digestion: the rumen

```diagram mwt-four-stomachs
```

- 4 chambers. Microbes do the digesting (85%).
- Eats 2 to 4% of body weight a day, dry (80%).

```more rumen detail
- White tails are **ruminants** with a four chambered stomach (85%, Montana State University Extension).
- Chamber 1, the **rumen**, holds bacteria and protozoa that break plants down (85%).
- The microbes specialise. A sudden diet change (like hay in a hard winter) can leave a deer starving on a full stomach until the microbes adjust (80%, Montana State University Extension).
- A deer needs 2 to 4% of its body weight in dry matter a day. Mature bucks peak at about 2 to 3 kg (4.5 to 6.5 lb) a day in spring; does peak at about 1.4 to 2.7 kg (3 to 6 lb) in fall (80%).
- They are **selective browsers**: a narrow muzzle picks buds, leaves and twigs (85%).
- They chew cud lying down, which is why a bedded deer stays put for hours (75%).
- Meat note: a gut shot spills rumen and gut contents. Clean the cavity fast and trim (Tip). See [field dressing](#/s/field-dressing).
```

## Body heat and the coat

```diagram mwt-winter-yard
```

- Red summer coat, grey brown winter coat (80%).
- Deep snow over about 40 cm makes travel hard (85%).

```more heat, coat and winter
- Summer coat: thin and reddish. Winter coat: thick, grey brown, hollow hairs that trap heat (75%).
- Walking in 40 cm (16 in) of snow costs a small deer 7 to 8 times the energy of bare ground (85%, US Forest Service species review).
- In 30 cm (12 in) of snow in New York, about 97% of food was buried (85%, US Forest Service species review).
- In hard winters, white tails **yard up**: they stay in a few hectares of dense conifer cover and pack trails (80%, Montana State University Extension).
- Deer cut their own food intake in winter and lower their energy use (75%, studies on northern deer).
- The metatarsal gland is bigger in northern deer. One idea is that it helps with temperature, but its role is unclear (60%).
```

## The fat cycle

```diagram mwt-fat-cycle
```

- Bucks: fattest in September, burn it in the rut (75%).
- Everyone is leanest in late winter (80%).

```more fat cycle detail
- Bucks fatten through late summer, then nearly stop eating in the rut and lose a lot of weight (75%).
- Does are leanest after nursing fawns in summer and late winter, and fatten in fall (75%).
- A white tail that enters winter fat can lose about 30% of its weight and live (60%, popular press, Tip).
- A rut worn buck enters winter thin. Hard winters kill more old bucks and fawns (70%).
- Meat note: an October deer carries more fat than a December buck (Tip). Deer fat tastes strong. Trim it before you freeze (Tip, see [cooking](#/s/cooking)).
```

## How long they live

```diagram mwt-lifespan
```

- Few pass 10 years (85%).
- A 4 year old buck is already old for a hunted herd (Tip).

```more lifespan detail
- White tails can live past 20, but few pass 10 (85%, US Forest Service species review).
- One review: most die by age 4 or 5 (80%).
- Heavily hunted Pennsylvania: average life expectancy about 2 years for males and 3 for females (85%, US Forest Service species review).
- Idaho Fish and Game: a deer that eats well and avoids predators may live to 9 or 10 (80%).
- Exact age needs tooth wear or a tooth section in a lab (75%).
```

## What the body means for you

```diagram vitals-deer
```

- Aim behind the shoulder, one third up (75%).
- No shoulder bone, no gut: more meat, faster death (Tip).

```more shot and meat summary
| Body fact | What you do |
|---|---|
| Shoulder blade covers front lungs | Aim tight behind it (75%) |
| Heart low | One third up, not higher (75%) |
| Rumen fills mid belly | Never shoot behind the diaphragm (Tip) |
| Hindquarters hold the most meat | Avoid shots angled into the ham (Tip) |
| Winter coat insulates | Skin and cool fast (Tip) |
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mwt-body-cutaway
```

- **Rhyme:** "Lungs behind the leg, heart down low; gut is green, so let it go."
- **Number trick:** 4 chambers, 1 diaphragm, one third up.
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Shoot the shoulder crease, not the shoulder."

```more more hooks, mistakes and field drill
- **Number trick:** 2 to 4% a day. A 90 kg buck eats about 2 to 3.5 kg of dry food a day.
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, widely taught): "Follow the back of the front leg up one third." Matches hunter education aim advice.
- **Rhyme:** "Fat in fall, thin by spring; the rut takes the buck's whole fling."

> [!mistake]
> - Aiming at the middle of the deer. That is the rumen.
> - Aiming too high "for the heart" and hitting above the lungs.
> - Shooting the shoulder of a deer with its near leg back.

> [!field]
> At home, print the cutaway. With a pencil, mark your aim point on 3 angles. Check it against [Shot placement](#/s/shot-placement).
```
