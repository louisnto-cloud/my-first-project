> [!why]
> The elk calendar decides where the herd is, what the bulls are doing and which week you can hunt. Learn the year and you stop guessing.

```diagram mc-elk-year-wheel
```

- Winter low, summer high, rut in September (90%).
- Antlers drop late winter, grow April to August (90%).

```more the year at a glance
| Month | What elk do | % |
|---|---|---|
| January to March | Winter range; shallow snow; antlers drop from late February | 90 |
| April | Green up on winter range; new antlers start | 90 |
| May to June | Move up: bulls first, cows after calving; calves late May, early June | 90 |
| July to August | Subalpine basins and avalanche tracks; antlers in velvet; bull groups | 85 to 90 |
| September | Rut: bugles, wallows, harems; most cows bred mid to late month | 90 |
| October | Late rut, second heat; snow starts pushing herds down | 90 |
| November to December | Back on winter range in most years; bulls thin | 85 to 90 |

- Sources: BC elk account 2000 (90%); app migration data (55 to 70%).
```

## January to March: winter

```diagram mc-elk-snow-depth
```

```photo bg-elk-winter-range
```

- Deep snow is the limit: restricted past about 46 cm (80%).
- Antlers drop late February to early April (90%).

```more winter in detail
- Elk usually keep to regions where snow on winter range stays shallow; they are less suited to deep snow than moose (90%, BC account).
- Winter range: open forest, grassy benchlands and floodplain marshes; south and southwest slopes preferred in winter and spring (80 to 90%).
- Snow thresholds from BC habitat models citing field studies (80%):
  - Crust or 30 cm or more: elk move into shrub and conifer habitats.
  - About 40 cm: elk move to areas with less snow.
  - Over 46 cm: movement restricted.
  - Southeast BC: elk avoided areas over 50 cm.
  - Over 61 cm: browse replaces grazing.
  - Over 70 cm: movement severely limited.
- In very deep snow elk use dense conifer: stands over 12 m tall with over 70% canopy (80%).
- Late winter is the hardest time; severe winters kill adults and calves (90%).
- Antlers drop from late February to early April, oldest bulls first (90%).
- Off season: shed antler hunting is a spring pastime for many hunters; give winter range elk space (Tip). Winter range rules for vehicles: see [winter range](#/s/winter-range-legal).
```

## April to June: green up, the move up, calves

```anim mc-elk-migrate Snow pushes them down in fall; green up pulls them up in spring.
```

```photo bg-elk-cow-calf-jasper
```

- May and June: up to subalpine basins (90%).
- Calves: late May and early June (90%).

```more spring in detail
- April: new antlers begin to grow (90%, BC account).
- May and June: most Rocky Mountain elk migrate to subalpine and alpine basins and avalanche tracks with lush herbs (90%).
- Bulls move up first, following the new growth; cows wait until calves can follow (90%).
- Distances vary a lot between herds; some elk do not migrate (90%).
- Gestation is about eight months (90%). Calves are born late May and early June (90%); US Park Service: May to late June (80%).
- One spotted calf of about 13 kg; twins rare (90%).
- Coat: the spring moult in May and June makes elk look ragged (90%).
- Roosevelt herds on the coast: most go up to subalpine meadows and avalanche tracks in summer; a few stay low all year (90%).
```

## July and August: velvet summer

```diagram mc-elk-antler-growth
```

```photo bg-elk-velvet
```

- Antlers grow up to about 1.7 cm a day (80%).
- Velvet off late August or early September (90%).

```more summer in detail
- A mature bull grows antlers for about 140 days; a yearling about 90 days (80%, US Park Service).
- Mature antlers grow about two thirds of an inch (1.7 cm) a day; about 70% of the growth comes in the second half (80%).
- Full size by late August or early September; the bull rubs off the velvet (90%, BC account).
- Bulls in bachelor groups or alone; cows, calves and yearlings in herds (85%, RMEF (Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation)).
- Summer heat: shade and north slopes for beds (75%, chapter 6).
- Scouting month: glass high basins at dawn and dusk and find the herds (Tip).
```

## September: the rut

```diagram mc-elk-rut-calendar
```

```photo bg-elk-bugle
```

- Most cows bred mid to late September (90%).
- Region 8: bow 1 to 9 Sept, rifle from 10 Sept (99%).

```video
yt-elk-bugle | Hear the bugle: low start, high whistle, grunts.
```

```more the rut in detail
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Rut: early September to mid October (80%, US Park Service). BC account: the "mid September rutting period" (90%).
- Bulls start wallowing about two weeks before cows come into heat; early September in the northern Rockies (70%, RMEF (Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation)).
- Bulls bugle, wallow, spray urine and thrash brush to attract cows and warn rivals (90%).
- Most cows are bred from mid to late September (90%).
- Bulls barely feed and burn their fat (90%, chapter 2).
- Region 8 seasons, page 68 (99%, [Region 8](#/s/rb-region-8)):
  - Bow only, 6 point bulls: 1 to 9 September.
  - 6 point bulls: 10 September to 20 October.
- Region 3 and Region 2: no general open season for elk (99%, pages 34 and 28).
```

## October: what changes

```diagram mc-elk-october
```

- Second heat: some bugling returns (90%).
- Region 8 rifle season closes 20 October (99%).

```more October in detail
- Cows not bred in September come into heat about a month later; some may have a third heat in November (90%, BC account). That gives a second, quieter wave of rut activity in October (Tip).
- Bulls are spent and lean after the main rut (90%).
- Autumn snowfalls push elk back to traditional winter ranges; a few bulls may stay high for a long time (90%).
- In the app migration data: October elk band about 600 to 2,000 m (60%).
- Hunting pressure is high by October; pressured elk hold in timber and feed at night (90%, BC account; chapter 4).
- Your plan: early October, call less and glass more; the last week, look lower and in timber (Tip).
```

## November and December: back to winter range

```diagram sa-elk-year
```

- Back on winter range in most years (85 to 90%).
- Bulls go into winter thin (90%).

```more late fall in detail
- November: a possible third heat for a few cows (90%).
- Snow and cold push herds to low benches, grassland and open forest (90%).
- Bulls that did not feed through the rut start winter in poor condition; more bulls than cows die in hard winters (90%).
- Elsewhere in BC some elk seasons run to 31 October, for example parts of the Peace (99%, page 62, [Region 7B](#/s/rb-region-7b)). Not in your three areas.
- Use winter to find winter range herds from roads and glassing points; note them in your Journal for next year (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-elk-year-wheel
```

- **Rhyme:** "Drop in March, grow in May, scream in September, walk down in the grey."
- **Number trick:** 8 months: elk gestation; September plus 8 = late May (90%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Listen in September, look in October."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Bulls go first up the hill; cows wait for the calves."
- **Number trick:** 140 days of antler growth; 1.7 cm a day at peak (80%).
- **Number trick:** 46 cm of snow: elk start to struggle (80%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "The second heat is the second chance."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Find the herd in August, hunt it in September."

> [!mistake]
> - Hunting high basins after the first big October snow.
> - Expecting full bugling in late October.
> - Forgetting the Region 8 rifle season ends 20 October.

> [!field]
> In August, sit a high ridge at dawn with your brother. Count bulls and note the time they leave the open.
```
