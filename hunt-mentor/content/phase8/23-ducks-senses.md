> [!why]
> Ducks beat you with their eyes. Hide from the eyes and you beat almost every duck.

```diagram mdk-vision-field
```

- A mallard sees almost all the way around (70%).
- It can watch the whole sky without turning its head (65%).

```more field of view, and how sure we are
- Ducks Unlimited describes a mallard's view as a full 360 degree circle (70%). A vision scientist quoted by hunting media calls ducks "an extreme case" of panoramic vision (65%).
- Ed Yong's book on animal senses, citing vision research, says the mallard field has no blind spot above or behind (60%, secondhand).
- Some sources say there may be a small blind area right behind the head. Not settled: treat ducks as seeing everything (Tip).
- Most waterfowl see each side with one eye. Only a thin zone in front is seen by both eyes (75%).
- Hunter meaning: there is no "behind" a duck. A circling flock sees your hide from every side (Tip).
```

## Colour and ultraviolet

```diagram mdk-cones
```

- Four colour cones; you have three (75%).
- Shine and glare are your enemy (Tip).

```more what they see that you do not
- Birds have an extra set of cones that pick up ultraviolet light: "tetrachromatic" vision (75%, Ducks Unlimited).
- They see reds, yellows and blues more vividly than we do (70%).
- Ultraviolet helps them see well at dawn and dusk (70%).
- Feathers reflect ultraviolet. Young pintail drakes reflect it differently from adults (70%).
- Delta Waterfowl scientists say ducks likely see ultraviolet, and much is still unknown (70%).
- Hunter meaning: avoid shiny guns, glasses, phones, new plastic and a bare face. Some decoys use ultraviolet dulling paint; how much it matters is not proven (Tip).
```

## Motion: the number one giveaway

```anim mdk-flare One face lifts: the whole flock flares.
```

```diagram mdk-look-down
```

- Bird eyes are built to catch motion (75%).
- They look DOWN into your hide. Keep your head down (Tip).

```more why motion kills a hunt
- Bird retinas keep blood vessels in one comb shaped structure (the pecten) instead of across the retina. That reduces visual clutter and helps them see motion (70%).
- Florida's wildlife agency: ducks have great eyesight and are very good at seeing movement; full camouflage and sitting still are the answer (75%).
- No camouflage pattern hides a hunter who stares up or fidgets (Tip, widely reported).
- Look with your eyes, not your face. Use a hat brim, a face mask or face paint (Tip).
- Let one person call the shot. Everyone rises together, once (Tip).
```

## Detail and depth

```photo mallard-drake-flight A mallard sees fine detail at 2 to 3 times your distance.
```

- They see fine detail 2.5 to 3 times farther than you (70%).
- They judge depth by moving the head (70%).

```more how they judge distance
- With eyes on the sides, most of what a duck sees is flat. It bobs or turns its head to see an object from two angles, which builds depth (70%).
- Birds control the shape of both the cornea and the lens. Divers can see well under water (70%).
- Hunter meaning: a fake looking decoy spread, or a hide that sticks up, is seen from far out. Match the shape of the cover around you (Tip).
```

## Dawn, dusk and dark

```anim rb-sunrise-hours Legal light for ducks starts 30 minutes before sunrise.
```

- Ducks see well in low light and fly at night (75%).
- Most migration happens at night (80%).

```more low light facts
- Ultraviolet sensitivity lets birds work fully at dawn and dusk (70%).
- Most waterfowl migrate at night. Movement picks up soon after sunset and peaks near midnight (80%).
- Under hunting pressure ducks fly out to feed at night (80%).
- Birds see a flickering light as flashes, where we see a steady light (70%).
- Legal hours for ducks: half an hour before sunrise to half an hour after sunset (99%). See the [Rule Book](#/s/rb-migratory-birds/17).
```

## Hearing

```diagram mdk-hearing
```

- Best hearing: 1 to 3 kHz, where duck calls sit (75%).
- A clank or a slammed truck door carries (Tip).

```more what the studies found
- A mallard hearing test (3 birds) found a range of about 66 Hz to 7.6 kHz, with best hearing near 2 kHz (75%). Mallards do not hear the very low sounds that pigeons do (75%).
- Ten diving bird species, including scaup and ruddy duck, shared their best hearing at 1 to 3 kHz. The ducks had the most sensitive hearing of the group (75%).
- Duck calls (their own voices) mostly sit in that same 1 to 3 kHz band (75%).
- Hunter meaning: a mouth call sits right in their best band. Call softly and rarely when birds are close (Tip).
- Hunter meaning: metal on metal, a dog whistle or loud talk in the hide carries (Tip).
```

## Smell and wind

```anim hf-duck-landing Ducks circle, then land into the wind.
```

- Duck smell is barely studied. Do not count on it either way (Tip).
- Wind matters because ducks land into it (80%).

```more what we know about smell
- The old belief that birds cannot smell is not supported. Reviews list ducks among birds with a working sense of smell (70%).
- No study found here shows ducks avoid hunters by scent (VERIFY). Ducks Unlimited and state agencies advise on sight and motion, not scent (Tip).
- Wind still sets your hide: ducks land into the wind, so set the spread with the wind at your back or side (80% for landing into wind; the setup is a Tip).
- Old hand's rule (opinion): "Play the wind for the landing, not for the nose."
```

## Asleep, but watching

```diagram mdk-one-eye
```

- Edge ducks sleep with one eye open, facing out (80%).
- Half the brain stays awake (80%).

```more the study
- Mallards sleeping in a row of four: the two edge birds slept with one eye open about 32% of the time, the middle birds about 12% (75%).
- The open eye faced away from the group, where a predator would come from. The awake half of the brain reacted fast to threats (80%).
- This is called unihemispheric sleep (80%).
- Hunter meaning: a sleeping raft is never fully asleep. Sneaks on rafted ducks rarely work in the open (Tip).
```

## Beat each sense

| Sense | Strength | Beat it (Tip) |
|---|---|---|
| Eyes | Near 360°, motion, ultraviolet | Still, head down, no shine, roof on hide |
| Ears | Best at 1 to 3 kHz | Quiet gear, soft calls |
| Nose | Not well known | Ignore; play the wind for landing |
| Lookouts | One eye open at the edges | Do not sneak open water rafts |

```more the 40 year hunter's checklist (opinion)
- Face covered or painted, gloves on, nothing shiny.
- Hide matches the cover around it and has a roof or brim.
- Eyes move, head does not. One caller, one "take them" call.
- Gear packed quiet: no loose shells rattling, no metal on metal.
```
