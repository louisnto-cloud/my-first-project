> [!why]
> Your moose season near Heffley Creek is two weeks in November. Knowing what the moose did in the ten months before tells you where it stands on 1 November.

- Calves: late May and June (90%).

## January to March: hold on

```gallery
hab-moose-trail-snow | A moose trail ploughed through deep snow.
hab-moose-willows | Winter browse: willow tips bitten off.
```

- Twigs, deep snow, ticks feeding (85 to 90%).
- Last antlers drop; yearlings keep theirs longest (90%).

```more January to March in detail
- Food: up to 20 kg of twigs a day, mostly willow, plus red osier dogwood, cottonwood, paper birch, aspen, high bush cranberry, false box and subalpine fir; they also strip bark from willow and poplar (90%, BC account).
- Where: dense browse along rivers, around wetlands, in burns and cutblocks, low avalanche tracks and the subalpine spruce willow birch zone (90%).
- On plateaus they gather in winter along river valleys and in burns, logged areas and wetland complexes (90%).
- Antlers: shed from mid November to March, older bulls first; "by March only the yearlings may still have antlers" (90%).
- Winter ticks take their January blood meal; adult females engorge from March (85%).
- Deep crusted snow can starve moose and cut next year's calf survival (85%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game).
```

## April to June: calves

```gallery
g-moose-calf | Spring calf beside its mother.
g-moose-cow-calf | Cow and calf: she defends it hard.
```

- Antlers start: late April or early May (90%).
- Calves: late May and June, 11 to 16 kg (90%).

```more April to June in detail
- Spring: once snow is patchy, many moose move quickly to spring ranges near their winter ranges (90%, BC account).
- Ticks drop off in April and lay eggs (85%). Ghost moose are most visible March and April (85%).
- Calving: after about 8 months of gestation (230 days), in late May and June; pregnant cows seek seclusion and chase off last year's young (85 to 90%).
- Single calves are the rule; 10 to 20% of BC cows have twins (90%). Twinning tracks food quality: under 10% in poor habitat up to 75% in good habitat in Alaska (85%).
- Calves stand on day one; cows often give birth lying down; calves start eating plants at 2 weeks (90%).
- Most cows that die of poor condition in central BC die April to June (90%, BC moose research report).
- Spring food: grazing as well as browsing; sedges, horsetail, pondweeds, grasses (85%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game).
```

## July and August: grow

```gallery
hab-pondweed | Pondweed: moose dive for it in summer.
hab-willow-shrub | Willow leaves: summer salad.
```

- Antlers grow fast in velvet (90%).
- Summer food: water plants and leaves (90%).

```more July and August in detail
- Antlers are "the fastest growing tissue in any mammal": a big Alaska bull can grow about an inch of antler a day (85%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game).
- About 80% of antler growth is done by mid July (75%, Canadian Encyclopedia).
- Summer food: horsetail, burweed and submerged pondweeds, plus new leaves of shrubs and herbs (90%, BC account).
- Plateaus: summer moose spread out and are "strongly attracted to lake shores, swamps, and Beaver ponds" (90%).
- Mountains: moose move to higher summer ranges; some stay in the valleys (90%).
- Calves nurse; a cow gives at least 150 L of milk June to September (90%). Calves grow more than 10 times their birth weight in 5 months (85%).
- Bulls build fat over summer for the rut (85%).
```

## September: velvet off, rut starts

```diagram mc-moose-antler-year
```

- Antlers full size by early September (90%).
- Velvet dries and is rubbed off (90%).

```more September in detail
- BC account (90%): "In British Columbia, Moose antlers reach full size by early September. Then the velvet dries, and the Moose rubs it off against trees or shrubs."
- Bulls rub saplings and brush; the rut pits start (75%, chapter 8).
- Mid September to mid October: winter tick larvae climb on (85%).
- Moose breed from September to November in BC (90%).
- The Region 3 general moose seasons in some MUs (Management Units) open 20 September; not near Heffley Creek (99%, chapter 10).
```

## The rut

```diagram mc-moose-rut-timeline
```

- Over 80% of calves are conceived in 2 weeks (90%).
- Late September to early October (90%).

```video
yt-moose-calling-eastmans | Hear real moose calls and when to use each one.
```

```more the rut in detail
- BC account (90%): "more than 80 percent of calves are conceived during the first estrus period (heat), which occurs during two weeks in late September and early October. Cows that do not conceive during the first estrus have second or third estrus periods at about 25 day intervals."
- Cows give a long quavering moan audible up to 3 km (90%).
- "Several cows often gather around a prime bull, or two fighting bulls, and wallow in the urine soaked rutting pits the bulls have made." Bulls do not form a harem (90%).
- Alaska: rut peak late September and early October; the winner usually mates with several cows (85%).
- The [[Rut]] in other words: elk first, then moose, then deer (80%, [the rut](#/s/the-rut)).
```

## October: the rut winds down

```diagram mc-moose-oct-nov
```

- Bulls tired and lean by late October (85%).
- Moose stay high longer than deer and elk (90%).

```more October in detail
- Second heat: cows that missed in early October cycle again about 25 days later, so some rut activity carries into late October (90%, BC account; my reading of the timing).
- "By late October, adult males have exhausted their summer accumulation of fat and their desire for female company. Once again they begin feeding." (85%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game)
- Moose "are able to stay on high elevation ranges much longer than Elk, deer, or mountain sheep" (90%, BC account).
- Leaves drop; the menu turns to twigs (85%).
- Tick larvae take their first blood meal (85%).
```

## November: your season

```diagram re-r3-moose-sheep-bars
```

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- MUs (Management Units) 3-27 and 3-28: spike fork bulls, 1 to 15 November (99%).
- Mature bulls start shedding antlers from mid November (90%).

```more November in detail
- Seasons: page 34, Region 3; MUs 3-12 to 3-14, 3-18 to 3-20, 3-26 to 3-30 and 3-39, spike fork bulls, 1 to 15 November, bag 1 (99%, [Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3)). Region 8 rows: 1 to 15 November too (99%, [Region 8](#/s/rb-region-8)).
- A third heat about 25 days after the second may still bring a little rut behaviour in early November (75%, my reading of the 25 day cycles).
- Antler shedding: "between mid November and March. The older bulls shed earliest" (90%, BC account). Alaska: mature bulls shed as early as November, mostly December and January (85%). A big bull may already be bald by late November; your legal spike fork usually keeps his antlers (85%, my reading).
- Moose return to valley bottoms gradually; with light snow they may stay at mid elevation until late winter (90%, BC account).
- Deer rut starts mid November: you share the woods with busy deer hunters (80%, [the rut](#/s/the-rut)).
```

## December: settle in

```diagram mc-moose-snow
```

- Snow drives moose to winter range, slowly (90%).
- Bulls feeding to rebuild before deep winter (85%).

```more December in detail
- Winter ranges: dense browse along rivers, wetlands, burns, logged areas (90%, BC account). The Hunt Map has a Moose winter range layer (99%, chapter 7).
- Most of the year's antlers drop December and January in Alaska (85%).
- Moose are hit on roads and rail lines every winter, especially along the Yellowhead corridor (90%, BC account).
- Tick larvae go dormant until January (85%).
```

## The year at a glance

| Month | What happens |
|---|---|
| Jan to Mar | Twigs, deep snow, ticks feed, last antlers drop (85 to 90%) |
| Apr to Jun | Antlers start, ticks drop, calves born (85 to 90%) |
| Jul to Aug | Antlers in velvet, water plants (90%) |
| Sep | Velvet off, rut starts (90%) |
| Late Sep to early Oct | Peak rut (90%) |
| Oct | Rut fades, bulls feed (85%) |
| Nov | Your season; old bulls shed (90 to 99%) |
| Dec | Down to winter range (90%) |

```more why this matters for a November hunter
- Your 1 to 15 November bull has finished the main rut, is lean, feeding hard and likely still high or mid slope (85%, my reading of the sources above).
- Rut calling is a September and October game; in November, think food and cover (Tip, chapter 9).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-moose-year-wheel
```

- **Rhyme:** "Velvet in summer, rubbed in September; rut till October, dropped by December."
- **Number trick:** 2, 80, 25: in 2 weeks, 80% of calves conceived; misses cycle every 25 days (90%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Moose come down last."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Calves in June, ticks in fall, ghosts in spring, antlers fall."
- **Number trick:** 230: days of gestation, about 8 months (85 to 90%).
- **Number trick:** 11 to 16, then 135 to 180: calf kilograms at birth, then by early winter (90%).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely reported, opinion): "The calendar calls the rut; the cold calls the moose down."

> [!mistake]
> - Expecting a rut on 10 November in MU 3-27: the main rut is a month earlier.
> - Following the deer down to the low country in early November.
> - Thinking a bald bull in late November must be a cow: mature bulls shed early.

> [!field]
> Put three dates in your phone: 25 September (rut peak, scout and listen), 1 November (season opens), 31 March (Mandatory Hunter Report).
```
