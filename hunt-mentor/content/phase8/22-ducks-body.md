> [!why]
> Know where the heart, lungs and air sacs sit, and you know why some hit ducks drop and others fly off to die.

```diagram mdk-anatomy
```

- Heart and lungs: high and forward, under the wing roots (75%).
- The back half is air sacs and gut (80%).

```more the parts that matter to a hunter
- Lungs: small, stiff, pressed up against the spine and ribs (80%).
- Air sacs: thin bags in front of and behind the lungs, filling much of the body. They move air but do not take up oxygen (80%).
- Heart: big for the body, low and forward in the chest, behind the keel (75%).
- Breast: the big flight muscle on the keel. This is most of the meat (80%).
- Gizzard: a hard muscle pouch low in the belly, behind the liver (80%).
- Spine and neck: a pellet here drops the bird at once (Tip, from the vital areas in chapter 10).
- Anatomy positions are drawn from general bird anatomy, simplified (75%).
```

## How a duck breathes

```anim mdk-breath Two breaths move one gulp of air.
```

- Air flows ONE way through a bird lung (80%).
- It takes two breaths to move one gulp through (80%).

```more the two breath cycle in plain words
- Breath 1 in: fresh air goes past the lung, mostly into the rear air sacs (80%).
- Breath 1 out: the rear sacs squeeze that fresh air forward through the lung (80%).
- Breath 2 in: the used air moves on into the front air sacs (80%).
- Breath 2 out: it leaves through the windpipe (80%).
- So the lung gets fresh air on the in breath and the out breath. Bird lungs get oxygen better than ours, even high up (80%).
- No diaphragm: the ribs and breastbone pump the air sacs like bellows (75%).
- Hunter meaning: a duck can fly hard for hours. A lightly hit duck can still fly a long way before it falls (Tip).
```

## Nine air sacs

```diagram mdk-air-sacs
```

- 9 air sacs: 4 pairs plus 1 at the base of the neck (80%).
- Air even fills the hollow wing bones (80%).

```more what the sacs do
- The pairs: neck, front chest, rear chest and belly. The single one sits at the base of the neck (the clavicular sac) (80%).
- Side pockets from the sacs run into bones: the upper wing bone (humerus), the shoulder girdle, the hips and some neck bones (80%).
- Air in the bones and sacs makes the body light for flight (75%).
- Divers squeeze air out of the sacs and feathers before a dive (Tip).
- These details come from anatomy studies on pigeons and kingfishers. Ducks follow the same bird plan with small differences (75%).
```

## What the air sacs mean for your shot

```diagram mdk-shot-zones
```

- A pellet in the belly mostly hits air sac and gut, not vitals (Tip).
- Head, neck and the front half kill. Aim at the front (70%).

```more the shot logic, and how sure we are
- To kill, a pellet must reach a vital area: brain, spine, or heart and lungs (75%).
- Published lethality tables count 1 to 2 hits in the vitals with the right pellet size as enough for ducks (70%).
- The lungs and heart sit forward and high. A bird hit only in the rear belly can keep flying and die later, unrecovered (Tip, my reading of the anatomy).
- A wing hit alone breaks the bird down but leaves it alive: it swims and dives (Tip).
- Ducks over decoys: Ducks Unlimited advice is to look at and aim at the bill on the first shot (65%).
- Lost birds: the commonly quoted figure is about 1 in 5 ducks shot but not recovered in the United States (65%).
- Chapter 10 covers pattern, pellet counts and what to do after the shot: [Masterclass 10](#/s/mc-ducks-10).
```

## Heart, blood and diving

```anim sb-duck-feed Watch how it feeds: tip up or dive.
```

- When a diver goes under, its heart slows for a moment (75%).
- Forced under, it slows hard to save oxygen (80%).

```more what happens under water
- Free diving tufted ducks (a European diver) show a short heart slow down at the start of a dive, then the rate comes back near normal within 6 to 8 seconds (75%).
- On long dives, or when a duck is held under, the heart slows a lot to save oxygen. This drop is stronger in diving ducks than in puddle ducks (80%).
- Hunter meaning: a crippled diver can stay under a long time and come up far away. Most cripples head for the shore and the bank cover (65%).
- Old hand's rule (opinion): "A wounded diver is a lost diver unless you shoot it on the water now."
```

## Bones, keel and breast

```photo mallards-flying The breast muscle drives every wingbeat.
```

- The keel bone anchors the big flight muscles (75%).
- That breast is your meat (80%).

```more the flight engine
- The keel is a ridge down the middle of the breastbone. The downstroke muscle (pectoralis) and the upstroke muscle (supracoracoideus) both attach to it (75%).
- Divers have legs set far back for swimming. That is why they patter along the water to take off and walk badly on land (80%).
- Ducks burn about 12 times more energy flying than resting (75%).
- Breast meat on ducks is dark red, built for long flights (Tip).
```

## Digestion: no teeth

```diagram mdk-gut
```

- No teeth. The gizzard and grit grind the food (80%).
- A mallard can stuff about 113 g of grain in its gullet (75%).

```more food in, energy out
- Ducks have no true crop, but the gullet (oesophagus) stretches to hold food (80%).
- Order: bill, gullet, glandular stomach (proventriculus: acid and enzymes), gizzard (muscle plus grit), gut and two caeca (80%).
- The gizzard grows bigger when a duck eats hard food. Divers that eat clams and snails have bigger gizzards for their size (75%).
- Why they gorge fast: eat quickly in the field, then fly to safe water to digest (75%).
- Hunter meaning: a duck with a fat gullet of grain tells you what field it fed in. Check the gullet when you clean birds (Tip).
- Laying wood duck hens may need more than 300 invertebrates an hour for eight hours (65%).
```

## Body heat

```diagram mdk-feet-heat
```

- Warm and cold blood pass side by side in the legs (80%).
- Feet stay cold, the body stays warm (80%).

```more cold, wind and clear nights
- The setup is called countercurrent heat exchange. It works so well ducks can stand on ice for hours (80%).
- Down under the outer feathers holds the warmth. Preened, waterproof outer feathers keep the water out (Tip).
- Clear, cold nights cost a duck the most energy. Ducks pick sheltered roosts out of the wind (75%).
- Hunter meaning: on a cold, windy day ducks feed harder and seek calm, sheltered water. Hunt the lee shore (Tip).
```

## Fat through the year

```diagram mdk-fat-year
```

- Fall: they fatten before migration (75%).
- Hens spend their fat on eggs and nesting (75%).

```more the fat cycle and the meat
- Before fall migration ducks eat more and store fat faster (75%).
- They reach winter waters fairly lean, then regain fat in early winter on grain and seeds (75%).
- From mid to late winter body mass usually drops, likely set by the body itself (75%).
- Hens rely on fat, protein and calcium built up over winter and spring for eggs and incubation (75%).
- Hunter meaning: late October and November birds are often fat, with a yellow layer under the skin. Pluck them to keep it (Tip). See [Cooking](#/s/cooking/4).
```

## Lifespan

```diagram mdk-survival
```

- About 6 in 10 adult mallards live through a year (75%).
- Few wild ducks reach 5 years (75%).

```more survival numbers
- Adult mallard yearly survival in band studies: about 0.6 to 0.66. Young birds survive less (75%).
- Of 100 adults, about 63 are left after a year, 40 after two, 10 after five (number trick using 0.63, Tip).
- Oldest known wild mallard: a drake about 27 years old (70%).
- Clutch 5 to 15 eggs replaces the losses each year (75%).
- Young birds of the year are more likely to be shot than adults (80%). More in [Masterclass 4](#/s/mc-ducks-04).
```
