## Bed, feed, water

```anim sg-bed-feed Bed in cover, feed in the field, back at dawn.
```

- Feed in fields late afternoon into the night (75%).
- Back to willow and cottonwood beds by first light (75%).

```more the daily loop
- Evening: leave bedding cover, stage at the edge, enter the field near dark (75%).
- Night: feed in hay and alfalfa, browse along edges (75%).
- Dawn: drift back to bedding cover at or before first light (75%).
- Midday: bedded, chewing cud, getting up to stretch and nibble now and then (70%).
- Water: deer drink about 3 lb of water per lb of dry food, but only half a pound per lb of green food (80%, Montana State University Extension). In a valley bottom water is never far (Tip).
- Penn State October data: about 120 to 140 m an hour at dawn and dusk, under 50 m an hour midday, about 60 m an hour at night (75%, via [Weather fronts](#/s/fronts-and-movement)).
```

## Season changes the day

| Season | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Summer | Dawn and dusk (85%) |
| Fall | Dawn, dusk, night fields (75%) |
| Rut | Any hour, midday too (70%) |
| Winter | Active most of the day (85%) |

- Winter deer feed in the warm midday sun (Tip).

```more why the pattern shifts
- BC account: "In summer, they are more active at dawn and dusk, but in winter they are active most of the day" (85%).
- Starkey, Oregon (deer and elk): activity peaks about 04:00 to 09:00 and 17:00 to 21:00 (85%, Stewart and others 2002).
- Rut bucks search for does at all hours (70%).
- Winter: short days and cold push feeding into the warmest hours (Tip).
```

## Weather: what the evidence says

```diagram mwt-weather-evidence
```

- Time of day and season beat any weather effect (75%).
- Weather effects are real but small and mixed (70%).

```more the studies
- Auburn University thesis (South Carolina GPS, Global Positioning System, collars): weather, temperature, wind, pressure and moon mattered only in some seasons and hours, and less than the daily rhythm (75%, search preview).
- Mississippi Department of Wildlife, Fisheries and Parks: across many deer and a whole season, weather effects are weaker than hunters think. Very high barometric pressure went with more winter buck movement (70%).
- Penn State: does through 30 winter storms showed no change before storms and moved less during them (80%, via [Weather fronts](#/s/fronts-and-movement)).
- Oklahoma collar study: temperature explained most of the change in movement (65%).
- Rating in the chart is my reading of these studies, not a published score (Tip).
```

## Wind, rain and snow

```anim hf-scent-shift A wind shift moves your scent cone.
```

- Strong wind: deer bed tighter, move less (60%).
- Light rain: deer often still move (Tip).

```more wind, rain, snow
- One South Texas radio collar study: movement dropped sharply at about 24 to 31 km/h (15 to 19 mph) wind, then rebounded above about 32 km/h (60%, secondary report).
- An Oklahoma collar study found little change in distance moved with wind speed (60%, secondary report).
- So wind effects are mixed. Wind matters most because it carries your scent and covers your sound (Tip).
- Rain: Penn State preliminary notes: bucks moved less in rain than does (65%).
- First snow: fresh tracks show exactly where deer are moving. Great scouting (Tip, see [Snow tracking](#/s/snow-tracking)).
- Deep snow over about 40 cm pushes white tails onto winter range and packed trails (85%).
```

## Cold fronts and the barometer

```diagram rq-wx-storm-bars
```

- "Feed before the front" is not proven (75%).
- A hard cold snap after a warm spell: watch for daylight feeding (Tip).

```more front detail
- Penn State storm study: no change before winter storms (80%).
- Mississippi: very high pressure correlated with more winter buck movement (70%).
- Hunter consensus: the day after a cold front clears, with rising pressure and cold calm air, is a great morning to sit (Tip, widely repeated, weak evidence).
- Hot October days: deer bed in shade and move at the edges of legal light (Tip).
- Log temperature, pressure and wind in the Journal each sit. Your own valley data beats any article (Tip).
```

## The moon

```diagram hf-moon-vs-rut
```

- collar studies: dawn and dusk win in every moon phase (75%).
- Moon effects, if any, are small (75%).

```more moon studies
- Texas King Ranch: 43 collared bucks showed no moon phase pattern (65%, via Outdoor Life).
- North Carolina State: over 22,000 collar locations, deer moved most at dawn and dusk regardless of phase, with only small midday upticks near the full moon (65%).
- Auburn and SEAFWA (Southeastern Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies) 2016: 38 collared bucks. Small solunar effects that changed with the moon phase; the authors warn solunar charts "may be misleading" (80%).
- Penn State: no moon phase link in collared does (65%).
- The moon does not set the rut. Day length does (75%).
- My lean (Tip): ignore the moon. Hunt the wind and the time of year.
```

## Hunting pressure

```anim hf-pressure-shift Pressure pushes deer into the thick stuff.
```

- Pressure turns white tails nocturnal and into thick cover (70%).
- Midweek beats the weekend (Tip).

```more pressure detail
- Penn State collar study: deer reacted the day before opening and hid inside their home range (70%).
- Pressured white tails move more at night and use thicker cover (70%).
- Opening day and weekends push deer from roads and field edges (Tip).
- Hunters moving midday can push deer past a partner on a stand (Tip, see chapter 9).
- Walk 500 m past the road, park quietly, close the truck door with your hip (Tip, matches [How a deer thinks](#/s/deer-mind)).
- More: [Hunting pressure](#/s/pressure-push).
```

## Your hunt day

```diagram mwt-day-plan
```

- In your seat 30 minutes before legal light (Tip).
- Last 2 hours of light: your best sit (Tip).

```more the full day
1. Check the wind and pick the stand that fits it (Tip).
2. Walk in on a route that does not cross the deer's path from field to bed (Tip).
3. Sit through the first 2 hours of light (Tip).
4. Midday: scout, still hunt slowly in thick bottoms, or rest (Tip).
5. Back in your evening stand at least 2 hours before dark (Tip).
6. Leave after dark without walking through the field the deer are feeding in (Tip).

- Legal hours: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (99%, synopsis page 11 item 38, via the [quick lesson](#/s/white-tailed-deer)).

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mwt-day-clock
```

- **Rhyme:** "First light, last light, that's the white tail's right."
- **Number trick:** 2 hours before dark, 30 minutes before light.
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Be there before the deer, not with the deer."

```more more hooks, mistakes and field drill
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, widely repeated): "Hunt the wind you have, not the wind you want."
- **Rhyme:** "Moon or no moon, dawn wins soon."
- **Number trick:** 3 to 1: deer drink about 3 kg of water for each kg of dry food.

> [!mistake]
> - Arriving at first light instead of before it.
> - Leaving the evening stand early and spooking deer already in the field.
> - Skipping a hunt because of the moon chart.

> [!field]
> Sit one evening stand from 2 hours before dark to legal end. Note the minute each deer appears. Repeat 3 evenings.
```
