## A wide world: the field of view

```diagram mgs-vision-field
```

- Almost all the way around, with a narrow blind spot behind (90%).
- They see ground and sky at once (90%).

```more what the eye studies found
- Canada geese have narrow blind areas, wide side vision, wide binocular fields and can see their own bill tips (90%, Fernandez Juricic and others 2011).
- Their sharpest retina band runs at a slant (oblique), not flat. It lets a goose scan the ground and the sky at the same time, or line up with the horizon by tilting its head (90%).
- Because of this they move their heads less than many birds while watching in open fields (90%). More head movement in cluttered cover (90%).
- The study summary gives no degree values. Do not trust a number you see quoted without a source. VERIFY.
- What it means: there is almost no "behind" to hide in on open ground. Hide under cover, not just out of the line of sight (Tip).
```

## Colour and detail

```diagram mgs-colour-cones
```

- 4 cone types; the shortest tuned to violet (90%).
- Fine detail 2.5 to 3 times farther than you (80%).

```more the colour science
- Canada geese have four visual pigments with peaks at 409, 458, 509 and 580 nm (90%, Moore and others 2012).
- That is a violet sensitive system. Ducks Unlimited describes waterfowl colour vision as running from near ultraviolet to red (80%).
- The brightest patches to another goose are the cheek, crown, neck and upper tail: the marks they use to keep flocks together (90%). That is why decoy heads and chinstraps matter (Tip).
- Waterfowl see fine detail 2.5 to 3 times farther than people (80%, Ducks Unlimited).
- Side set eyes give weak depth sense. Birds move the head to judge distance (80%).
- What it means: a bare face, a shiny gun, fresh blind fabric and frosted decoys all stand out (Tip, 65%).
```

## Motion and shine

```anim mgs-face-flare One face up, the whole flock flares.
```

- Movement and glare flare more birds than anything (80%).
- Keep your face down until the shot call (Tip).

```more motion rules from goose guides
- Waterfowl flare at movement in the blind and at the glint of a gun barrel (80%, Ducks Unlimited senses article).
- Frosted decoys glare at sunrise. Set decoys as late as you can without missing the first flight (65%, Ducks Unlimited goose guide).
- Hide hands and face: face mask or paint, gloves, hat brim low (Tip).
- Watch birds through the mesh or the brim, never by lifting your head (Tip).
- One hunter calls the shot. Everyone moves at once, then not before (Tip).
```

## Light and dark

- Geese fly by light levels: first birds about 15 minutes before sunrise in clear weather (80%).
- Cloud delays them about 15 minutes (80%).

| Light | What a wintering flock did (80%, Raveling 1972) |
|---|---|
| Clear dawn | First birds 15 min before sunrise, main flight at or just after sunrise |
| Overcast dawn | About 15 min later |
| Clear dusk | Back between sunset and civil twilight |
| Cloudy dusk | Back before sunset |
| Full moon visible | Back later in the evening |

```more night and the moon
- A Saskatchewan flock started its morning flight at about 32 foot candles of light (80%, quoted in Raveling and others 1972).
- Barnacle geese in the Netherlands fed at night only around the full moon (75%, Wildfowl journal). For Canada geese night feeding under the moon is hunter report only (Tip).
- Your legal shooting light starts half an hour before sunrise (99%). The first geese often come about then (Tip). See [Chapter 6](#/s/mc-geese-06).
```

## Ears

```diagram mgs-hearing
```

- Most birds hear best at 1 to 4 kHz (kilohertz), the range of your voice (80%).
- A slammed truck door is a goose alarm (Tip).

```more hearing in full
- Bird hearing in air is mostly below 10 kHz, best at 1 to 4 kHz (80%, sea duck hearing paper).
- Diving ducks tested best at 1 to 3 kHz; lesser scaup best at 2 to 3 kHz (75%). No goose audiogram found. VERIFY.
- Waterfowl ears have no outer flap; soft feathers cover the openings and cut wind noise (80%, Ducks Unlimited).
- Hearing keeps mates and families together in noisy flocks (80%). Geese use about 13 calls (80%, Hinterland Who's Who).
- What it means: your call is heard. So are tailgates, zippers, shell boxes and talking (Tip).
```

## Nose

- Smell is weak in waterfowl; they rely on sight (80%).
- No study found showing geese smell hunters (VERIFY).

```more what is known
- Waterfowl smell is not as keen as a dog's, but they do detect odours (80%, Ducks Unlimited).
- Greylag geese can tell food plants from other plants by smell (80%).
- About 400 taste buds in waterfowl versus about 9,000 in people (80%).
- Hunter lore says scent control does not matter for geese (Tip). Wind still decides everything, because geese land into it. See [Chapter 9](#/s/mc-geese-09).
```

## The watchers

```diagram mgs-sentinels
```

- Edge birds and ganders keep heads up more (75%).
- Many heads up at once: they are nervous (Tip).

```more vigilance studies
- In pre nesting Canada geese, males were more vigilant than females and more so at the flock edge (75%, Wildfowl journal).
- In white fronted geese, birds watched a road less as flock size grew (70%).
- Head up does not always mean alarm; some head up time is social (75%).
- A big feeding flock can feel safe and relaxed. A small group of wary birds is harder to fool (Tip).
- Count heads up when you glass a field. Rising numbers mean leave them alone today (Tip).
```

## Beat every sense

| Sense | How to beat it (Tip unless shown) |
|---|---|
| Eyes, shape | Hide in or beside cover; brush the blind with field stubble |
| Eyes, motion | Face down, one shot caller, move together |
| Eyes, shine | Mud the blind, matte gun, face mask, late decoys on frost |
| Ears | Quiet doors and gear, mouth call only (electronic calls banned, 99%) |
| Nose | Not the problem. Play the wind for landing |

```more why the call rule is in this table
- Electronic calls and recordings are banned for migratory birds in BC (99%, [Rule Book: migratory birds](#/s/rb-migratory-birds)). Mouth calls are fine (95%, my reading in the Rule Book).
```

## Memory hooks

> [!tip] Memory rhymes (made for this app)
> - "Eyes first, ears next, nose last: hide well, sit still, stay fast."
> - "Face up, flock up."
> - "Four cones beat your three: if it shines, they see."

- Number trick: **2.5 to 3**. A goose sees detail 2.5 to 3 times as far as you (80%).

> [!rule] Old hand's rule (tradition, opinion)
> Hide first, decoys second, call last. Widely repeated by goose guides: concealment is the biggest single factor.
