## Bones that stop bullets

```diagram mc-elk-skeleton
```

- A bull's shoulder blade can be about 6 cm thick (65%).
- Aim at the ribs just behind it, over the lungs (65 to 85%).

```more bones in detail
- **Shoulder blade** (scapula): a heavy plate over the front ribs. On a big bull it can be up to about 6 cm (2.5 in) thick (65%, hunting media citing guides). A light bullet or an arrow can stop in it (65%).
- **Leg bones and the elbow joint:** heavy bone low on the front of the chest. A hit there can break a leg without reaching the lungs (Tip).
- **Ribs:** thin. A bullet passes easily into the lungs behind them (85%, hunter education).
- **Spine:** runs along the top. A spine hit drops an elk on the spot, but the target is small and a near miss wounds (Tip). Aim at the lungs, not the spine.
- **Pelvis:** at the rear. Never a target (85%).
- Hunter education aim spot for elk: up the back of the front leg, one third to one half up the chest (85%, [shot placement](#/s/shot-placement)).
```

## How it breathes

```anim mc-elk-breath Diaphragm pulls back, lungs fill.
```

- The diaphragm pulls back, the lungs fill (85%).
- Two lungs hit: no air, fast collapse (85%).

```more breathing in detail
- Like all mammals, an elk breathes with the diaphragm, a sheet of muscle behind the lungs. It pulls back, the chest volume grows and air rushes in (85%, standard anatomy).
- Lungs fill most of the chest behind the shoulder, from the spine down to just above the heart (85%).
- A shot through both lungs collapses them: the elk cannot get oxygen and goes down fast (85%, hunter education). Bulls hit through both lungs usually go down within about 100 yards (91 m) (65%, onX citing guides).
- One lung only: an elk can travel hundreds of metres (65%).
- Bright, frothy, bubbly blood on the trail means lungs (80%, [blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)).
```

## Heart and the big pipes

```diagram mc-elk-heart
```

- The heart sits low, at the elbow line (85%).
- The lungs are the bigger, safer target (85%).

```more heart and vessels in detail
- The heart sits low in the chest between the front legs, at about the level of the elbow (85%, hunter education).
- The aorta (main artery) and the big veins run from the heart up toward the spine (85%, standard anatomy).
- Heart shot: very lethal, but small and low; a few cm too low is a leg or brisket hit (Tip).
- Lung shot: a bigger target with the heart and big vessels near its lower edge (85%).
- So aim at the middle of the lungs, slightly forward of where instinct pulls you (65%, onX citing guides).
```

## The four chamber stomach

```anim mc-elk-rumen Food goes down, comes back as cud, then moves on.
```

- Four chambers. Food comes back up as cud (85%).
- Elk: a bigger rumen for their size than deer (80%).

```more digestion in detail
- Elk have a four chamber stomach: rumen, reticulum, omasum, abomasum (85%, RMEF (Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation)).
- They eat fast, then lie down and chew the cud: food comes back up from the rumen to be chewed again (85%, standard ruminant biology).
- Elk have a bigger rumen for their body size than mule deer and bighorn sheep, so they can do better on coarse, poor feed (80%, BC habitat model citing Collins and Urness).
- Elk are mixed feeders: grazers first (grasses, sedges, forbs), browsers in winter and deep snow (80%, BC habitat models).
- What it means for you: a gut shot rumen is huge and smells sour. Green or brown matter on the arrow or trail means a long wait (70%, [blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)).
- The [[Gutless method]] avoids opening the rumen at all (Tip, chapter 10).
```

## Coat and body heat

```diagram mc-elk-coat
```

- Summer: sleek, reddish. Winter: grey body, dark neck (90%).
- Hot days: beds in shade on north slopes (75%).

```more coat and heat in detail
- Summer coat: sleek, rich reddish brown, little or no undercoat (90%, BC elk account).
- Winter coat: head, neck and legs dark brown; sides and back a lighter grey brown (90%).
- Moult in May and June: the coat looks very ragged (90%).
- Summer heat: elk select shaded beds on north aspects, with over 54% canopy closure in a Black Hills study (75%). Thermal cover helps in hot sun but studies disagree on how much it matters to body condition (75%).
- Winter: elk need thermal cover in deep snow: closed conifer stands over 12 m tall with over 70% canopy (80%, BC habitat model).
- Thick hide plus a heavy body hold heat after death. In September heat, skin and cool the meat fast (80%, [meat care](#/s/meat-care)).
```

## The fat year

```diagram mc-elk-fat-year
```

- Bulls barely eat in the rut and enter winter thin (90%).
- Late winter is the low point (90%).

```more the fat year in detail
- During the rut bulls do not feed but burn a lot of energy, so they start the winter in poor condition (90%, BC elk account).
- That gives bulls higher death rates than cows and a sex ratio that favours cows (90%).
- Malnutrition in severe winters is probably the main reason adult elk die; it also kills many calves in their first winter (90%).
- Rutting reduces the fat in bull carcasses (85%, University of Wyoming).
- Elk are more active in summer and least patterned in winter, when cold and deep snow make feeding hard (80%, Alberta GPS (Global Positioning System) study).
- The curve on the chart is a shape sketch from these sources, not measured fat (Tip).
- What it means for you: an October bull is lean and can be tougher; a cow or a September bull carries more fat (Tip). Cook lean meat with added fat (Tip, chapter 10).
```

## Teeth and lifespan

```diagram mc-elk-teeth
```

- Calf 4 cheek teeth, yearling 5, adult 6 (85%).
- Most die by 10 to 12; some reach 20 (90%).

```more teeth and age in detail
- Idaho Fish and Game (85%):
  - Calves: 4 cheek teeth, no wear; 8 baby incisors.
  - Yearlings: 5 cheek teeth; replacing the middle incisors.
  - 2.5 years and older: 6 cheek teeth; the third premolar now has 2 cusps; adult incisors.
- Ivories: rounded upper canines in both sexes (90%, BC account). Hunters keep them as a keepsake (Tip).
- Lifespan: elk can live 20 years, but most die by 10 or 12 (90%, BC account). Yellowstone: typical 13 years (80%).
- Most cows first breed just over 2 years old; bulls can breed as yearlings but usually do not mate until 4 or 5 (90%).
- [[CWD]] (Chronic Wasting Disease): the region asks for elk heads for testing; keep the head with the jaw (95%, [after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill)).
```

## What the body means for your shot and your meat

```diagram vitals-elk
```

- Broadside: behind the front leg, one third to one half up (85%).
- Quartering away: aim for the far shoulder (85%).

```anim sk-angles Which angles work.
```

```more shot and meat notes
- Broadside: up the back of the front leg, one third to one half up the chest, middle of the lungs (85%, [shot placement](#/s/shot-placement)).
- Quartering away: aim so the bullet exits at the far front leg. Never through the paunch (85%).
- Quartering toward or head on: beginners wait for the turn (Tip). A light bullet can deflect off the shoulder bones of elk (85%).
- Bullet for elk with your .308: 165 to 180 grain premium bonded, partition or copper loads (65%, ammo guide). Keep shots short (chapter 10).
- Meat: a heavy shoulder hit ruins a lot of meat; a lung hit wastes little (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-elk-body-cutaway
```

- **Rhyme:** "Up the leg, a third of the way, two lungs hit, it will not stay."
- **Number trick:** 4, 5, 6 cheek teeth: calf, yearling, adult (85%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Shoot the lungs, not the bone."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Rumen big, heart down low, lungs in the middle, that is where to go."
- **Number trick:** 6 cm: how thick a bull's shoulder blade can be (65%).
- **Number trick:** 10 to 12: the age most elk die by; 20 the record (90%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "A rut bull is all bone and fight; a September cow is the best meat on the mountain."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Elk are tough. Hit them right once, not twice wrong."

> [!mistake]
> - Aiming at the "middle of the elk", which is the rumen.
> - Aiming high on the shoulder blade with a light bullet.
> - Leaving a heavy bull whole in September heat.

> [!field]
> Draw an elk outline from memory. Put in lungs, heart, diaphragm and rumen. Check against the cutaway.
```
