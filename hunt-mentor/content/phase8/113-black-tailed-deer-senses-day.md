> [!why]
> In coast timber you rarely see a deer first. It smells, hears or sees you. Know its senses and its day, and the odds turn.

```diagram mbt-day-clock
```

- Most active near dawn and dusk; can move any time (80%).
- Legal hours: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (99%).

```more the day in detail
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Deer "are most active near dawn and dusk" and "may be active at any time of day" (80%, WDFW, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife).
- About half a deer's time is spent in 5 to 10% of its home range (90%, Nyberg and Janz 1990, Vancouver Island data).
- Favourite bedding sites may be used daily for several weeks, and again the next year (90%).
- No hunting game except migratory birds from 1 hour after sunset to 1 hour before sunrise (99%, page 11, item 38). The app shows sunrise and sunset for your plan (Tip: check the night before).
- In the November rut, bucks also move in daylight (80%, [Your Harrison deer hunt](#/s/local-harrison-deer)). Chapter 4.
```

## Beds and cover

```diagram mbt-cover-60m
```

- Coast deer like cover close by, even when they do not need it (90%).
- Beds: flattened plants about 0.9 to 1.2 m long (80%).

```gallery
g-bt-bedded | Bedded: only the head and ears show.
```

```more cover in detail
- Deer prefer cover close by, even when they do not need it; their use of clearcuts falls with distance from forest cover (90%, Nyberg and Janz 1990).
- Mule deer research: the best security cover hides about 90% of a deer at 60 m or less (85%, cited in Nyberg and Janz).
- Coast security cover: old growth with a patchy young conifer understorey, dense young stands with live branches to the ground, thickets of young cedar or hemlock (90%).
- Deer are sometimes found bedded in recent clearcuts with plants only 1.5 to 2 m tall (90%).
- A bed: flattened plants about 3 to 4 feet long and 2 to 3 feet wide (0.9 to 1.2 m by 0.6 to 0.9 m) (80%, WDFW).
- Thermal cover: in summer deer start to pant at about 27°C; in winter a yearling's comfort zone runs from about minus 6°C to 18°C in still air (90%).
- Full lesson: [Bedding, terrain and thermal cover](#/s/bedding-cover).
```

## Nose, ears, eyes

```diagram mmd-beat-senses
```

- Smell is the strongest sense (85%). Wind in your face.
- Eyes catch motion; ears catch high pitched noise (80 to 85%).

```more the senses in detail
- Black tails and mule deer are one species, so the senses are the same (75%, my reading). Full detail: [Mule deer Masterclass 3](#/s/mc-mule-deer-03).
- Smell: the strongest deer sense (85%). "They also have a good sense of smell" (80%, WDFW).
- Hearing: about 115 Hz to 54 kHz, best about 4 to 8 kHz: zips, Velcro, nylon, metal clicks (85%, D'Angelo and others 2007).
- Sight: about 310 degrees without turning the head, poor depth, superb at motion (80%, Penn State Deer Research). "Remain absolutely still, because deer are alert for any movement" (80%, WDFW).
- Colour: no red cone, so blaze orange looks dull to a deer (75%).
- Glands (90%, BC deer account): metatarsal (outside of the lower leg) gives alarm scent; tarsal (inside of the hock) for recognition; interdigital (between the toes) leaves a scent trail. Black tails have smaller hock glands than mule deer (90%, Nyberg and Janz).
```

## Wind in the timber

```diagram thermals
```

- Morning sun: air drifts uphill. Evening: downhill (80%).
- Coast timber swirls wind. Check it often (Tip).

```more wind and rain in detail
- Sun warms a slope and scent rises; evening cooling sends scent downhill (80%, [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals)).
- In tall timber and creek draws the wind swirls. Carry a puffer or thread and check every few minutes (Tip).
- Rain: wet ground is quiet underfoot (Tip, [Wet forests](#/s/wet-forest-country)).
- Heavy rain and wind make deer harder to see and hear you; many hunters still hunt them (Tip). No study found for black tails and rain: VERIFY before trusting any rule of thumb.
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom, not law): "In the timber you hunt with your nose second and your patience first."

> [!mistake]
> - Walking with the wind at your back into thick cover.
> - Moving when the deer's head is up.
> - Swishing nylon rain gear in a quiet stand.

> [!field]
> Next scouting walk: find 3 beds. For each, stand in it and look out. What cover hides it? Which way does the wind come?
