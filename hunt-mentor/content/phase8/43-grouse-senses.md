> [!why]
> A grouse lives by its eyes and ears. Learn what it sees and hears and you get the close, calm shot instead of a roar of wings behind a tree.

```diagram mgr-vision-field
```

- Eyes on the sides: a huge field of view, little overlap (75%).
- Sees colour and motion very well (75%).

```more the eyes
- Grouse are galliform birds, close cousins of the chicken. Most eye studies are on chickens; treat these as a guide for grouse (75%).
- Field of view about 300 degrees, with only about 30 degrees seen by both eyes (75%, chicken data; Martin's review of bird visual fields gives 20 to 30 degrees for most birds).
- A small blind spot sits straight behind the head (75%).
- Colour: four kinds of single cones (violet, blue, green, red), one more than us. Birds see colours we cannot (75%, PLOS One study on chickens).
- Motion: a double cone is thought to handle motion (75%). A grouse notices movement before shape (Tip, 70%).
- Night: grouse roost at night in trees, on the ground or in snow (75%). How well they see in the dark: no source found (VERIFY).
```

## Beat the eyes

```diagram mgr-find-sitting
```

- Move slow, stop behind trees, never skyline yourself (Tip).
- Look for a shape and a twitch, not a whole bird (Tip).

```more how hunters use this
- Your blaze orange does not hurt you much: grouse key on movement more than colour (Tip, 60%). Wear what the law and safety need.
- Walk, stop, look. The stop is when you see the bird and the bird sees no motion (Tip).
- In trees: scan near the trunk on the sunny side of conifers. A grouse is a dark blob with a tail hanging down (Tip).
- On the ground: look for a head moving through the leaves, then freeze (Tip).
- Old hand's rule (tradition): "See the eye, not the bird."
```

## The ears

```diagram mgr-hearing
```

- Most birds hear best at 1 to 4 kHz (70%).
- Grouse ears are tuned to low sounds (65%).

```more what grouse hear
- Bird hearing is sharpest between 1 and 4 kHz, about where we hear best (70%, bird hearing reviews).
- Gallinaceous birds (grouse, quail, chickens) have an inner ear tuned lower, below 1 kHz (65%).
- Chickens hear very low sounds, under 64 Hz, better than people (70%).
- The ruffed grouse drum is a very low sound, under 100 Hz (70%). Other grouse hear it a long way off.
- What carries to a grouse: boots on rock, a slammed truck door, a dropped tailgate, voices (Tip).
- Tip: close doors gently, talk in a whisper, and do not crunch along a gravel road edge.
```

## The nose and the wind

```anim mgr-dog-wind Your dog finds the scent cone downwind.
```

- No study found that grouse smell people (Tip).
- Wind matters for sound and for your dog's nose (Tip).

```more wind and thermals for a grouse hunter
- Birds of the grouse family are not known for a strong sense of smell. We found no source showing grouse detect hunters by scent (Tip, so treat smell as a minor factor).
- Wind carries your noise toward or away from the bird (Tip).
- Wind and rising or sinking air (thermals) carry the bird's scent to your dog. Work a dog into the wind so it meets the scent cone (Tip, common dog handling advice).
- Thermals: air rises up slopes when the sun warms them and sinks at evening (80%, see [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals)).
- Strong wind seems to make grouse wild. Hunters blame noise that hides predators (hunter lore, 60%). See [The day](#/s/mc-grouse-06).
```

## How it puts it all together

```diagram mgr-threat-ladder
```

- First defence: camouflage and stillness (80%).
- Last: a roaring flush and a short glide to cover (75%).

```more the escape plan, in detail
- Spruce grouse rely on camouflage and stillness and often let people close (80%).
- Ruffed grouse often walk or run away under cover before they fly (Tip, 65%).
- The flush is loud on purpose: the roar startles a predator (Tip, 60%).
- After the flush a ruffed grouse glides, usually under 90 m (100 yd), and often lands in a tree (75%, Pennsylvania Game Commission; Outdoor Life).
- Then it freezes again. That is why a follow up works (Tip). See [The mind](#/s/mc-grouse-04).
```

## Beat the ears and the plan

```anim sb-grouse-flush Walk, stop, wait. The stop flushes the bird.
```

- Walk slow, stop often: the silence makes the bird nervous (Tip).
- Be ready when you stop, not when you walk (Tip).

```more why the stop works (hunter lore)
- Many hunting writers say a grouse holds while you keep walking past, then flushes when you stop, because a stopped predator may have seen it (hunter lore, 60%).
- Stop next to an opening with room to swing, not in the thick of it (Tip).
- Hold the gun at ready during the stop (Tip).
- Circle a lone evergreen: a grouse under it may walk to the far side and flush from there (Tip, Outdoor Canada).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mgr-vision-field
```

- Rhyme: "It sees you move, it hears you step, it never smells the sweat you've kept."
- Number trick: "300 to see, 30 to judge": 300 degree view, 30 degrees with both eyes.
- Old hand's rule (tradition): "Walk like you're late, stop like you're lost."

```more more hooks
- "Stop where you can swing."
- "Doors soft, voices low."
- "Dog into the wind, you behind the dog."
- These are memory lines made for this course and widely repeated hunter habits, not science.
```
