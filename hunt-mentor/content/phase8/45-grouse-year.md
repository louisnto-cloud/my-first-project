## January and February: the hard months

```gallery
hab-aspen-grove-snow | Aspen in winter: buds feed ruffed grouse.
hab-subalpine-forest | Spruce and pine: needles feed spruce grouse.
```

- Ruffed grouse eat buds; spruce and dusky eat needles (80 to 85%).
- January to March is when most birds die (75%).

```more winter detail
- Ruffed grouse: buds and catkins of poplar, aspen, birch and alder (85%, Hinterland Who's Who); aspen flower buds preferred (80%).
- Spruce grouse: up to 99% conifer needles in the snow season (75%).
- Dusky grouse: conifer needles, often high in Douglas fir (70 to 75%).
- Sharp tailed grouse: buds and catkins of deciduous trees and shrubs (85%, BC species account).
- Ptarmigan: willow, alder and birch buds; white tailed ptarmigan also eat conifer needles (85%).
- Survival is lowest January to March (75%, Maine study). Monthly survival 80 to 90% then (75%, Pennsylvania).
- No hunting season in these months for these birds in your regions (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-3/12)).
```

## March to May: the drummer

```anim mgr-drum A ruffed grouse drumming: slow, then a whirr.
```

```video
commons-ruffed-grouse-drum | Hear the drum: a ruffed grouse beating its wings.
```

- Ruffed males drum from a log, mostly March to May (75 to 85%).
- The wings beat the air; they do not hit the log (85%).

```more drumming, every number we found
- The male stands on a log, braces with his tail and snaps his cupped wings forward and back (85%, Hinterland Who's Who).
- The beats start slow and speed into a whirr (75%, Pennsylvania Game Commission). About 50 beats in about 8 to 10 seconds (65%, Nova Scotia and Saltscapes accounts).
- The sound is very low, under 100 Hz (70%), and can be heard about 400 m or more in thick woods (60 to 70%). How far depends on which way the bird faces (70%, Archibald 1974).
- Peak drumming is in spring; in one study the peak was mid April (80%). Pennsylvania gives March and April (75%).
- Males also drum in fall (85%, Hinterland Who's Who) and a little all year (65%).
- Why: to warn off other males and draw hens (85%).
- Field use: in April, listen at dawn from a quiet road and map every drummer. Those territories hold birds in October (Tip).
```

## Spring shows: the other grouse

```diagram mgr-displays
```

```video
commons-dusky-grouse | A male dusky grouse showing off for a hen.
```

- Dusky and sooty hoot; spruce struts and claps; sharp tails dance (65 to 85%).

```more each display
- Dusky grouse: males inflate neck air sacs and give deep owl like hoots, heard up to about 400 m (75%, Idaho Fish and Game). The sound is so low you may feel it before you hear it (60%). Hooting runs late March to mid July (65%).
- Sooty grouse: males hoot from high in a conifer, a run of about six deep hoots that can carry about 800 m (65%).
- Dusky grouse leave the high country in late March or early April for breeding ground lower down (70%, Colorado Parks and Wildlife).
- Spruce grouse: males strut with red combs up and tail fanned, then make a short flight that ends in two loud wing claps (65 to 75%).
- Sharp tailed grouse: males dance on a lek at dawn, stamp their feet, rattle their tails and show pink neck sacs (85%). Lek calls carry up to 1.5 km (85%, BC species account). Leks are used for many years if left undisturbed (85%).
- Ptarmigan males display with red combs: willow gargle, rock snore, white tailed scream (85%, Hinterland Who's Who).
- Respect: disturbance at leks can make hens abandon them (85%). Watch from far away.
```

## May and June: nests and hatch

```diagram mgr-clutch
```

- Ruffed: 7 to 12 eggs, hatch usually early June (85%).
- Chicks leave the nest within a day (85%).

```more nests, eggs and chicks
- Ruffed grouse: 7 to 12 eggs, incubation 23 to 24 days, hatch usually early June (85%, Hinterland Who's Who). Pennsylvania gives 6 to 16 eggs, about 24 days (75%).
- Spruce grouse: clutches average 4.8 in Alberta, the Franklin's form (75%). Median hatch there about 29 June (75%). Chicks fly short hops at about 6 days (75%).
- Dusky grouse: about 7 to 9 eggs on average (65%), chicks hatch late May to early June (65%).
- Sharp tailed grouse: first clutch at 11 months old, average 12.8 eggs, one brood a year, often renest if the nest fails (85%, BC species account). Most nests within 1.6 km of a lek (85%).
- Ptarmigan: 5 to 14 eggs, about 3 weeks (85%). Chicks fly clumsily at about a week (85%).
- Only the hen sits on the eggs in ruffed grouse (75%). Male willow ptarmigan stay and help (85%).
```

## July and August: growing up

```gallery
hab-huckleberry-bush | Huckleberry: summer food for dusky grouse.
hab-saskatoon-berries | Saskatoon berries: summer and fall food.
```

- Chicks eat insects first, then leaves, flowers and berries (85%).
- Adults moult their flight feathers (75 to 85%).

```more summer detail
- Ruffed chicks eat insects first; by August flowers, soft leaves, berries and seeds; clover is a favourite (85%, Hinterland Who's Who).
- Chicks make low, buzzy flights in week 1 and fly well by week 3 (75%).
- Yearling plumage by 16 to 17 weeks (85%).
- Adults moult once a year: Hinterland Who's Who says from June, Pennsylvania July into September (75 to 85%).
- Dusky grouse males leave breeding areas by mid July for high forest with huckleberry (70%, Colorado Parks and Wildlife).
- Dog training on game birds under supervision is allowed from 1 August (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-legal-methods/15)).
```

## September: families split, seasons open

```diagram re-r3-grouse-bars
```

- Broods break up; young birds wander (75%).
- Region 3: youth 1 to 9 Sept, everyone from 10 Sept (99%).

```more September detail and dates
- Families split at about 12 weeks; young birds disperse over 2 to 3 weeks, hens farther than males (75 to 80%).
- Restless young birds turn up in odd places, roads and yards (75%).
- Region 3, MUs (Management Units) 3-12 to 3-20 and 3-26 to 3-44: youth only (under 18) 1 to 9 September; general 10 September to 30 November (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-3/12)).
- Region 3 ptarmigan: 1 September to 30 November (99%).
- Region 2: bow only 1 to 9 September; general 10 September to 15 December (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-2/10)).
- Region 8: youth 1 to 9 September; general 10 September to 30 November; bow only 1 to 10 December (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-8/10)).
- Sharp tailed grouse, Region 3, MUs 3-30 and 3-31: 10 September to 30 November (99%).
```

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

## October and November: the heart of the season

```diagram mgr-oct-nov
```

- October: young birds on edges, clover and berries (75%).
- November: birds tighter in conifers, dusky high, snow tells all (70 to 80%).

```more what changes, week by week
- Early October: leaves still up, short shots. Young birds feed on road edges and cut blocks (75%).
- Fall drumming: you may hear ruffed males drum on calm October mornings (85% that they drum in fall; timing Tip).
- Dusky and sooty grouse move uphill into dense conifers and switch to needles (70 to 75%). Some travel tens of kilometres; most much less (70%).
- Sharp tailed grouse move to deciduous cover, berries, then buds; late flocks get big and wild (60 to 85%).
- Ptarmigan are mostly white by mid October (85%), easy to see on brown rock before snow and hard to see after (Tip).
- First snows: grouse sit in conifers and leave tracks (Tip).
- By late November more of the birds left are adults (75%).
- Region 3 and Region 8 seasons close 30 November; Region 2 runs to 15 December (99%).
- Old hand's rule (tradition): "Leaves down, birds up": after leaf fall you see farther and get more open shots.
```

## December: winter begins

```gallery
hab-grouse-snow-roost | Snow roost hole with wing marks.
hab-grouse-tracks-snow | Tracks in soft snow.
```

- Snow roosts start once snow is soft and deep enough (70 to 75%).
- Region 2 open to 15 Dec; Region 8 bow only 1 to 10 Dec (99%).

```more December detail
- Ruffed grouse dive into snow about 20 to 40 cm deep to roost (70 to 75%).
- Hard crust or thin snow pushes birds into thick conifers (85%).
- Region 2 grouse: open to 15 December (99%). Region 8: bow only 1 to 10 December (99%). Region 3: closed after 30 November (99%).
```

> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

## Memory hooks

```diagram mgr-year-wheel
```

- Rhyme: "April drums, June chicks come, September splits, December's numb."
- Number trick: "7 to 12 eggs, 24 days": ruffed clutch and incubation.
- Old hand's rule (tradition): "Find the drummer in April, find the bird in October."

```more more hooks
- "Dusky goes up when the leaves come down."
- "12 weeks to split, 3 weeks to fly."
- "Season 10 to 30": Region 3 opens 10 September, closes 30 November.
- "Ptarmigan white by mid October."
- The drummer rule is widely repeated grouse hunting advice; rhymes are made for this course.
```
