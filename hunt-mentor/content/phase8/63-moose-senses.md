> [!why]
> A moose trusts its nose and ears far more than its eyes. Beat the nose with wind, the ears with patience, and the eyes with stillness.

```diagram sa-moose-senses
```

- Nose: excellent. Ears: very good. Eyes: poor at detail (75%).
- The wind decides most moose hunts (Tip).

```more what this chapter covers
- The nose and how scent moves with wind and thermals, the ears and how calls carry, the eyes, how a moose combines them, and how to beat each one.
- Previous: [Chapter 2, The body](#/s/mc-moose-02). Next: [Chapter 4, The mind](#/s/mc-moose-04).

```

## The nose

```anim mmd-scent-cone Your scent spreads in a cone downwind.
```

- Smell is acute (75%).
- Your scent travels downwind in a widening cone (80%).

```more the nose in detail
- Minnesota Department of Natural Resources: "Moose have rather poor eyesight, but acute senses of smell and hearing" (75%).
- Guides who call moose: "Moose trust their noses every time" (65%, hunter forum via search; opinion).
- Your scent cone starts narrow at you and widens downwind; a moose anywhere inside it can smell you (80%, [scent and wind](#/s/mc-mule-deer-03)).
- A bull coming to a call often swings downwind to smell the "cow" before he shows (65%, Outdoor Canada; Tip). Chapter 9 shows how to set up for that.
- Strong scent sources: you, your dog, your truck, your fire, your camp cooking (Tip).
```

## Wind and thermals

```anim sg-thermal-day Morning air rises, evening air sinks.
```

- Morning: air warms and drifts uphill (80%).
- Evening: air cools and sinks downhill (80%).

```more thermals in moose country
- [[Thermals]]: sun warms slopes and air rises in the morning; at evening the air cools and drains down into valleys, wetlands and lake basins (80%, [thermals](#/s/wind-thermals)).
- Wetlands and lake basins are low spots: cold evening air pools there and carries your scent down to them (Tip).
- So at evening: sit beside or below the swamp with the drainage running past you, not above it at a ridge top whose air slides straight down on the moose (Tip).
- Morning: approach from below or the side, as warming air lifts your scent up the slope (Tip).
- Swirling wind in cutblocks and around lake edges is common; carry a wind checker and look at it every few minutes (Tip, [wind tools](#/s/wind-thermals)).
```


## The ears

```diagram mc-moose-ears
```

- A cow's rut moan carries up to 3 km (90%).
- Calls carry best on calm, cool days (65%).

```more hearing in detail
- BC account (90%): "At rutting time, cow Moose emit a long, quavering moan that is audible for up to 3 km. This call can attract a bull or bulls in forested terrain where visual contact is limited."
- Hearing is acute (75%, Minnesota Department of Natural Resources). No moose hearing range study was found: VERIFY numbers in Hz.
- Outdoor Canada (65%): cool, clear, calm days or a light breeze are best for calling; heavy wind or rain muddies calls.
- Moose make plenty of noise themselves: snapping branches, splashing, grunts, raking brush. A careful, irregular step with pauses can pass for a moose; a steady human rhythm cannot (Tip).
- Metal on metal, voices and car doors are the giveaways (Tip).
```

## The eyes

```diagram mc-moose-eyes
```

- Poor at still shapes; quick to see motion (75%).
- Freeze when it looks. Move when it feeds (Tip).

```more vision in detail
- Agency summaries call moose eyesight "rather poor" (75%, Minnesota Department of Natural Resources).
- Eyes on the side of the head give a wide view and a small blind spot behind (75%, general deer family anatomy; moose specific degrees: VERIFY).
- Like other deer family members, moose likely see blue and yellow better than red; blaze orange looks dull to them (60%, Tip, based on deer research). The law on blaze orange: see the Rule Book.
- Low light: they are active at dawn and dusk, so their eyes work well in poor light (Tip).
- What it means for you: stand still and a moose may look straight at you and not know what you are; move and it will (Tip).
```

## The stare

```photo g-moose-bull-front
```

- A staring moose has noticed you (Tip).
- It may stand for long seconds before it moves (Tip).

```more what the stare means
- Hunter wisdom: a moose often stands and stares instead of running. Do not read that as "it has not seen me" (60%, hunter lore, [Moose](#/s/moose)).
- Use the stare: if you are already set and the moose is a legal bull, broadside, with a clear backstop, the stare can be your shot (Tip).
- If it is a cow with a calf, or a bull with ears back, the stare can turn into a charge. Chapter 4 has the warning signs.
- Never shoot a moose only because it stands still. Count first (Tip).
```

## How a moose puts it together

```diagram sa-call-setup
```

- Ears hear, nose confirms, eyes check last (Tip).
- A sound with no smell makes it curious; smell makes it leave (Tip).

```more combining the senses
- Typical order (Tip, hunter consensus): a sound turns the ears; the moose then tries to get the wind; it looks last.
- A bull that hears a call and cannot see a moose may "clam up and circle downwind" (60%, hunter forum via search; Tip).
- Over calling when a bull is close lets him pinpoint you; if he cannot see a moose he may hang up or circle (60%, Tip).
- A wrong smell usually ends it at once; a wrong sound or shape may only make it wait and watch (Tip).
```

## Beat each sense

| Sense | Beat it by |
|---|---|
| Nose | Wind in your face; thermals in mind (80%) |
| Ears | Few sounds, moose like rhythm, no metal (Tip) |
| Eyes | Freeze when it looks; move when it feeds (Tip) |

```anim stalk-wind Keep the wind in your face.
```

```more beating the senses in detail
- Nose: check the wind every few minutes; play thermals (Tip, [thermals](#/s/wind-thermals)). Keep the shooter downwind of the caller (Tip, chapter 9).
- Ears: still hunt slowly; stop often; let a snapped stick be followed by silence, a soft grunt or a branch rake rather than a curse (Tip).
- Eyes: move when its head is down or behind cover; use trees and willow as screens; keep your outline off the skyline (Tip).
- Scent products: no moose part or derivative from outside BC may be used for hunting (99%, page 12, [unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts)).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram sa-moose-senses
```

- **Rhyme:** "Nose knows, ears tell, eyes are slow, so freeze as well."
- **Number trick:** 3 km: how far a cow's rut moan carries (90%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "A moose trusts its nose every time."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Morning air climbs, evening air falls; sit low by the swamp when the cow moose calls."
- **Number trick:** 1, 2, 3: one sound turns the ears, two the nose checks, three the eyes look (Tip, order of senses).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely reported, opinion): "If he goes downwind, you go there first."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "A staring moose has already seen you."

> [!mistake]
> - Calling from a ridge top at evening with the cold air sliding down onto the swamp.
> - Moving while a moose stares.
> - Using a moose lure bought outside BC: unlawful.

> [!field]
> At the next wetland you visit, stand at the edge at sunset with a wind checker. Watch which way the puff goes in the last 30 minutes of light.
```
