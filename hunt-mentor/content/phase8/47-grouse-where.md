> [!why]
> Each grouse lives in a band of the mountain with its own trees and foods. Find the band, find the food, find the bird.

```diagram mgr-habitat-section
```

- Grass, aspen, fir, spruce, alpine: one grouse for each (80 to 85%).

```diagram sb-grouse-elev
```

```more the bands with numbers
- Sharp tailed grouse: grassland and open forest, breeding at 275 to 1,190 m in BC (85%, BC species account).
- Ruffed grouse: most abundant at 750 to 1,250 m, aspen and poplar near water (85%, BC Breeding Bird Atlas).
- Dusky grouse: breed mainly in Douglas fir and spruce fir zones, many seen at 1,500 to 2,250 m, but breeding at 500 to 1,250 m is not uncommon (85%).
- Spruce grouse: conifer forest with spruce or lodgepole pine, recorded up to 2,500 m (85%).
- White tailed ptarmigan: alpine, breeding at 1,830 to 2,650 m in the Interior (85%).
```

## Ruffed grouse country

```gallery
hab-aspen-grove-snow | Aspen grove: buds all winter.
hab-dogwood-flowers | Red osier dogwood: white berries in fall.
hab-chokecherry | Chokecherry in fruit.
hab-wells-gray-cutblock | Young regrowth in a cut block.
```

- Young forest under about 20 years, aspen, creek bottoms (75 to 85%).
- Edges where two kinds of cover meet (Tip).

```more food and cover through the year
- Young forest less than 20 years old gives food, hiding cover and shelter (75%, Pennsylvania Game Commission).
- Aspen, poplar, birch and alder near water (85%). Aspen flower buds are the top winter food (80%).
- Fall foods: berries, clover, leaves and seeds (85%). Fruit is over a quarter of the adult diet (65%).
- Winter roosts under conifers when snow is poor (75 to 85%).
- Home range a few hectares; adults rarely move more than a few hundred metres a day (75 to 85%).
```

## Spruce grouse country

```gallery
hab-subalpine-forest | Spruce and lodgepole pine, Manning Park.
hab-kinnikinnick | Kinnikinnick berries under pine.
hab-huckleberry-bush | Huckleberry on a rocky ridge.
```

- Dense spruce, fir and lodgepole pine (80 to 85%).
- On the ground in summer, up in the trees from late fall (80%).

```more food and cover
- Conifer specialist: needles of pine or spruce, especially in winter (85%).
- Summer and fall: green shoots, leaves, berries, flowers and insects (75%, Idaho Fish and Game).
- Mostly on the ground from late spring to early autumn, then up in the trees eating needles (80%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife).
- Some move up to about 9.5 km between summer and winter range (70%).
- East of the Coast Mountains in BC (85%).
```

## Dusky and sooty grouse country

```gallery
hab-douglas-fir-stand | Open Douglas fir: dusky winter needles.
hab-huckleberry-berry | Huckleberry: late summer food.
hab-grass-fir-edge | Grass and fir edge: summer and early fall.
```

- Summer: open edges, even sagebrush. Fall: uphill into dense conifers (70 to 75%).
- Sooty grouse on the coast winter high in conifers (65%).

```more food and cover
- Breeding season: forest edges and openings; insects, green plants and berries (75%, Idaho Fish and Game).
- Summer berries: blueberry, huckleberry, chokecherry, gooseberry; grasshoppers (65%).
- Autumn: most birds move from open breeding areas to dense conifer forest, usually uphill (70%, Audubon). Maximum known travel about 48 km (30 miles) (70%).
- Winter: fir and Douglas fir needles, perched high in the trees (65 to 75%).
- Sooty grouse: winter in dense conifers, often higher than their breeding ground; sources disagree in places, so treat direction as likely, not certain (55 to 65%, VERIFY near Mission).
```

## Sharp tailed grouse country

```gallery
hab-lac-du-bois-grassland | Lac du Bois grassland near Kamloops.
hab-kamloops-sagebrush | Sagebrush and bunchgrass.
hab-woods-rose-hips | Rose hips: a key fall and winter food.
```

- Grassland with shrubs, aspen and water birch draws (85%).
- Region 3: only MUs (Management Units) 3-30 and 3-31 (99%).

```more food, cover and the rule
- Key grassland foods in BC: snowberry, rose and dandelion; browse on water birch, trembling aspen, saskatoon and chokecherry (85%, BC species account).
- Birds in clearcuts use kinnikinnick, juniper, prickly rose and scrub birch (85%).
- Fall: roadsides and landings with clover, dandelion and yarrow are used heavily (85%).
- Winter: riparian draws with buds, catkins and berries (85%). Late fall and winter birds may feed high in aspens and birches (75%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game).
- Rule: "SHARP-TAILED GROUSE | 3-30, 3-31 | Sept 10 - Nov 30 | 5 (10)", and no open season in the part of 3-30 south of the Scottie Creek Forest Service Road, Map C18 (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-3/12)).
- Lac du Bois Grasslands Protected Area: hunting allowed in open season, but parts sit inside City of Kamloops limits and have their own closures (85 to 95%, earlier research). Check the map layers before you go.
```

## Ptarmigan country

```gallery
g-ptarmigan-summer | White tailed ptarmigan on alpine rock.
hab-willow-shrub | Willow: the core winter food.
```

- Above the trees: rock, heather, willow basins (85%).
- Winter: willow buds, sheltered basins at treeline (75 to 85%).

```more food and cover
- White tailed ptarmigan are strictly alpine; they raise young in moist pockets among cliffs and snowfields (85%, Hinterland Who's Who).
- Winter: buds and twigs of low willow, alder and dwarf birch; white tailed also eat conifer needles and buds (85%).
- In winter they move a few hundred metres to a few kilometres to reliable food (85%).
- Region 3 ptarmigan row: MUs 3-12 to 3-20 and 3-26 to 3-44, 1 September to 30 November, 5 (15) (99%). Region 2: listed MUs only. Region 8: no row (99%).
- Sun Peaks, MU 3-27: no hunting or shooting within 400 m of any lift, run, facility or development (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-3/31)).
- Region 3 above 1,700 m: vehicles only on existing roads and trails (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-3/22)).
```

```more detail by area
- Heffley Creek, Heffley Lake and Sun Peaks sit in MU (Management Unit) 3-27 on the 2026 Region 3 map (90%).
- Near Heffley Creek: the Douglas fir and aspen benches above the valley hold ruffed grouse; higher pine and spruce toward Heffley Lake and Sun Peaks hold spruce and dusky grouse (Tip, 65%).

- Mission, MU 2-8: many no shooting areas along roads and near town; check [Rule Book Region 2](#/s/rb-region-2/41) before any trip (99%).
```

## Find them on the Hunt Map

```steps Grouse ground on the Hunt Map in 4 steps
mgr-map-1 | Turn on Management Units and Forest Service roads.
mgr-map-2 | Add Cutblocks by age and Burns by year.
mgr-map-3 | Use Slope direction, then Sun and shade.
mgr-map-4 | Check the Elevation profile for the right band.
```

- Open the [Hunt Map](#/map). Layers first, terrain tools second (Tip).

```more what each tool shows for grouse
- Management Units: confirm the MU and that grouse are open there (99% rule; map layer 95%).
- Forest Service roads: access, and where the road rules apply. Read [How to hunt](#/s/mc-grouse-09/6) first.
- Cutblocks by age: young regrowth roughly 10 to 20 years old is ruffed grouse cover (Tip, based on Pennsylvania's under 20 years, 75%).
- Burns by year: older burns regrow aspen and shrubs (Tip).
- Private land, parks, reserves and Motor vehicle closures: check every one before you go (99% rules).
- Slope direction (aspect): south and east faces warm first; aspen benches often sit there in the Interior (Tip).
- Sun and shade: morning sun on a slope can bring birds out to feed (Tip).
- Viewshed: less useful for grouse; use it to plan where a partner can see you (Tip).
- Elevation profile: ruffed 750 to 1,250 m; dusky and spruce higher; ptarmigan above the trees (85%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mgr-habitat-section
```

- Rhyme: "Grass for the sharp tail, aspen for the ruff, fir for the dusky, spruce for the tough, rock for the ptarmigan high enough."
- Number trick: "750 to 1,250": the ruffed band.
- Old hand's rule (tradition): "Hunt the edges, not the middle."

```more more hooks
- "Under 20 years, grouse appears": young forest.
- "Dusky goes up, ruffed stays home."
- "No sharp tail in 3-27."
- The edge rule is a classic piece of grouse hunting advice; the rest are made for this course.
```
