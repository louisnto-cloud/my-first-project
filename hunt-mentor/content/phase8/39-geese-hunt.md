> [!why]
> Goose hunting is won the evening before and the hour before light: the right field, the right hide, the right wind. The shot is the easy part.

```diagram mgs-methods
```

- 4 ways: field over decoys, water over decoys, pass shooting, sneak (Tip).
- Field over decoys is the best start for you (Tip).

```more what this chapter covers, and the options
| Method | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Field over decoys | Close, slow, feet down birds; dry | Permission, decoys, a hide |
| Water over decoys | Your duck gear, boat and kayak work | Birds land short; retrieval |
| Pass shooting | No decoys, little gear | High birds, long shots, cripples |
| Sneak on a feeding flock | Free, exciting | Hard on open fields; one chance |

> [!lean]
> Field over a dozen or two decoys, on a field you scouted, with written permission. Your brother calls the shot, you both lie still.

- Assumption: you hunt with your brother, a 12 gauge with steel, a 10 ft boat and a kayak, no dog yet.
- Study aid only. The official regulations are the law. Rules recap: [Chapter 10](#/s/mc-geese-10).
```

## Permission first

```steps Ask for a goose field in 6 steps
lc-ask-1 | Knock in person, in daylight, before the season.
lc-ask-2 | Say who you are and where you live.
lc-ask-3 | Ask one question. Then stop talking.
lc-ask-4 | Offer your phone and plate. Never money.
lc-ask-5 | Fill the permission form together.
lc-ask-6 | Thank them. Call before every hunt.
```

- No hunting on cultivated land without consent (99%).
- Farmers often welcome goose hunters on new grass and winter crops (Tip).

```more the law and the goose angle
- "A person is not permitted to hunt on cultivated land or on Crown land which is subject to a grazing lease while the land is occupied by livestock, without the consent of the owner, lessee, or occupant of the land." (99%, synopsis page 9)
- Fenced, signed or naturally bounded land is enclosed under the Trespass Act: no entry without consent (99%, page 9).
- No hunting or shooting within 100 m of an occupied dwelling or farm building (99%, page 10).
- One yes, one season, unless they say otherwise (99%, page 77). Get it in writing (Tip).
- Goose angle: ask before you need it. Tell them where you will park, that you will walk in, that you pick up every shell and take the decoys out the same day (Tip).
- Ask before you dig a blind or drive on a field (65%, Ducks Unlimited goose guide).
- Full lesson and form: [Asking for permission](#/s/landowner-permission). Law: [Where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt).
```

## Scout, then commit

```steps Scout the X in 5 steps
mgs-x-1 | Glass fields from a public road at the last hour of light.
mgs-x-2 | Watch where they land. Pin the exact spot.
mgs-x-3 | Let them leave on their own.
mgs-x-4 | With permission, find the thickest fresh droppings.
mgs-x-5 | Plan the hide on the X or in cover nearby.
```

- Hunt the field they used yesterday afternoon (65%).

```more the evening before, in order
1. Last hour of light: find the flock in a field from the road (Tip).
2. Note field, time, flock size and wind (Tip).
3. Check tomorrow's wind on the Hunt Map Weather and wind tool. If the wind will swing, plan the hide for tomorrow's wind (Tip).
4. Call the landowner to confirm (Tip).
5. Pack the night before: permit with stamp, BC licence, steel shells, plug in, decoys, blinds, mud, stubble straps, flag, call (Tip). See the checklist in [Geese](#/s/geese).
6. In the dark: set the blinds first, then decoys, then brush. Be done 30 minutes before legal light (Tip).
```

## The field set up

```diagram mgs-field-spread
```

- Feeders upwind, a landing hole downwind, blinds beside the hole (65%).
- Geese land into the wind (80%).

```more numbers from goose guides
- About 80% feeders and 20% active (heads up) decoys. Feeders near the centre, upwind of the landing area; actives downwind in bunches of 3 to 7 (65%, Ducks Unlimited goose guide).
- Put blinds about 9 m (10 yd) to the side of the landing hole, or parallel to the wind, so the birds see decoys, not blinds (65%).
- Right handed shooters sit slightly right of the hole: swinging left is easier (65%).
- Do not mix decoy types (full body, shell, silhouette, windsock) except same brand full bodies and shells (65%).
- Scatter hunters in small decoy clusters rather than a row of blinds (65%).
- Quick lesson numbers: 1 to 3 dozen decoys for a start (65%).
- Keep decoys off fence lines, brush and ditches (60%).
```

## Layout blinds

```steps Hide a layout blind in 4 steps
mgs-layout-1 | Set it where they want to land: wind at your back.
mgs-layout-2 | Lower the profile. Dig only with the owner's OK.
mgs-layout-3 | Mud it, then brush with stubble from farther out.
mgs-layout-4 | Walk 40 m out, look back, fix it. Pick up hulls.
```

- Concealment beats decoys and calling (Tip, 60%).

```more blind craft
- Hiding is the single biggest factor in goose hunting success (60%, Grand View Outdoors; widely repeated).
- Dig shallow pits for layout blinds only with permission; pack mud or sand on the edges and add natural vegetation (65%, Ducks Unlimited).
- Use stubble from the field itself and add more during the hunt; pick up spent shells (65%).
- Pull stubble about 14 to 18 m (15 to 20 yd) away so the ground right around the blind keeps its colour (60%, Field and Stream search preview). Dig a shallow trench parallel to the rows (60%).
- Mud the blind with a dirt and water slurry to kill the shine; let it dry first (60%).
- Snow: use a snow cover and scuff dirt downwind among the decoys to look like feeding (65%).
- Layout blinds also work on sand bars, mudflats and pond edges (65%).
- No blind? Lie in a ditch, a fence row or tall stubble 45 to 90 m off the X (65%).
```

## Decoys that finish birds

```anim mgs-landing Into the wind, feet down: that is the moment.
```

- Open the hole wide enough to land in (Tip).
- High birds: a few decoys 45 to 55 m downwind pull them down (65%).

```more spread tricks
- Six decoys 50 to 60 yd (45 to 55 m) downwind, in two groups on either side of the spread, help pull high birds lower. Do not call until birds commit, then call and flag hard to stop them landing short (65%, Ducks Unlimited goose guide).
- Change decoy numbers and shape through the season (65%).
- Late season: smaller, very real looking spreads where nobody else hunts (65%).
- Frosty mornings: set decoys last to cut glare (65%).
- Gear and prices: start with what you can carry with a partner; price checks: VERIFY before you buy.
```

## Calling and flagging

```steps Call a flock in 4 steps
sb-gcall-1 | Far flock: loud honks to turn them.
sb-gcall-2 | They turn: switch to soft clucks and murmurs.
sb-gcall-3 | Close: go quiet and still.
sb-gcall-4 | One hunter calls the shot. Rise together.
```

```video
yt-goose-calling-fields | Read the flock and call to match it.
```

- Calling too much is the top mistake (65%).

```more the four sounds, flagging and the law
| Sound | Means (65%, Ducks Unlimited) |
|---|---|
| Honk | Relaxed contact |
| Cluck | Excited, short honk |
| Murmur | Feeding and loafing |
| Moan | All purpose, in flight or on water |

- Far birds: a wall of honks, clucks and moans until they turn. Once they turn: ground talk, murmurs and soft clucks. If they slide away: back to loud, excited calling (65%).
- Big wind: loud and steady. Calm days and local birds: softer and less (65%).
- Two callers: one sets the tempo; stop if the flock shies (65%).
- Flagging: several hunters flag to catch far birds; once they turn only the leader flags; everyone stops on the final approach (65%).
- Short reed calls are loudest and most versatile but hardest; resonant chamber calls are easiest (65%).
- Electronic calls are banned for geese in BC (99%). Mouth calls and flags are fine (95%, my reading).
```

## Water hunting

```diagram mgs-water-setup
```

- Set on a bar or point geese use; hide on the bank (Tip).
- No shooting from a moving motor boat (99%).

```more water rules and the boat
- Floaters near a bar or point the geese loaf on; hide on the bank (Tip, quick lesson). Pros: your duck gear works. Cons: geese often land out of range, and you need a way to fetch (Tip).
- Federal and BC: no hunting migratory birds from or using a moving boat with a motor or sail; gliding after the motor is cut still counts as moving (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-migratory-birds)).
- Your kayak, paddled with no motor or sail, is not covered by that rule (95%, my reading in the Rule Book).
- You must have adequate means at hand to retrieve any bird you kill or injure (99%, s. 42). No boat, waders or dog? Do not shoot birds over deep water.
- Picking up birds: under paddle, or with the motor off and the gun unloaded, until confirmed (80%, my reading in the Rule Book). VERIFY with a conservation officer.
- Wear your PFD (Personal Flotation Device). Cold water kills fast (Tip). See [Water safety](#/s/water-safety).
- Mission and all of MU (Management Unit) 2-4 need the Fraser Valley Special Area licence and insurance, foreshore included (99%, Map B10).
```

## Pass shooting and the sneak

```diagram mgs-pass-shoot
```

- Pass shoot only birds under 30 m (Tip).
- Sneak with cover and permission, or not at all (Tip).

```more both methods in full
- Pass shooting: scout the lanes in and out of the feeding field, hide in cover under them as close to the field as you can (65%, Ducks Unlimited).
- Cons: high birds, long shots and cripples (Tip). Max for you: 35 m (38 yd), better 30 m (Tip).
- Never shoot the roost itself; it drives the flock away (Tip).
- Sneak (jump shooting): use thickets, fence rows and dips to get close to feeding geese and shoot as they flush (65%, Ducks Unlimited).
- On open BC fields a sneak rarely works; a river bend or a dike with cover is better (Tip).
- Law: never shoot from a vehicle, or within or across the [[Road allowance]] (99%).
```

## Hunting with your brother

```diagram mgs-partner-zones
```

- One caller. Two zones. Rise together (Tip).
- Never swing past the middle line (Tip).

```diagram gr-blind-boat
```

```more partner rules
- Agree before light who calls the shot and which birds each takes (65%, Ducks Unlimited).
- Left hunter takes left birds, right hunter right birds. In a boat sit back to back (Tip, [shotgun skills](#/s/shotgun-skills)).
- Keep blinds close enough to talk in a whisper (65%).
- Fewer blinds are better: with two hunters, two blinds side by side (Tip).
- Count birds out loud as they fall: you each need to know when the limit is reached (99% on the limit; Tip on the method).
- Earplugs: magnum loads damage hearing (65%).
```

## Decisions on the day

```diagram mgs-decision
```

- Birds show you what is wrong. Fix one thing at a time (Tip).

```more more calls to make
- Wind swung 90 degrees: turn the spread so the hole is downwind again, and move the blinds beside it (Tip).
- Flock after flock lands in the next field: next time hunt that field, with permission (Tip).
- Bright bluebird day, no wind: expect birds to circle; call less, consider an afternoon hunt (Tip). On calm days take good overhead shots near 27 m (30 yd) with BB or larger, because birds rarely commit (65%, Ducks Unlimited).
- Bitter cold: plan for a late morning or afternoon flight (80%, Raveling 1972).
- Bag full: unload, pick up, and leave the field as you found it (99% on the limit).
```

## What a 40 year hunter does differently

| Old hand's habit | Why (opinion, from goose guides) |
|---|---|
| Scouts every evening before a hunt | Yesterday's field is tomorrow's spot |
| Spends more time hiding than calling | Concealment wins |
| Lets birds work, shoots feet down | Close birds die clean |
| Changes the spread often | Local birds learn |
| Hunts weekdays and afternoons | Less pressure |
| Keeps one field resting | Birds keep coming back |

> [!rule] Old hand's rule (tradition, opinion)
> Let the first flock land. Widely told by goose hunters: a few live birds on the ground are the best decoys you will ever own.

```more where these come from
- Every habit above is opinion from published hunting writers and guides (Ducks Unlimited goose guide, Outdoor Life, Grand View Outdoors), not a study. Label: Tip.
- "Let the first flock land" means passing a small group so they pull in the next bigger flock. Your call; some hunters disagree (Tip).
```

## Ethics on a goose hunt

```diagram sb-goose-range
```

- Feet down, inside 35 m (38 yd). Pick one bird (Tip).
- Kill and retrieve every wounded bird you can (99%).

```more ethics in full
- A goose looks close at 50 m because it is big. Wait (Tip).
- Pick one bird, then the next. Never flock shoot (Tip).
- Carry steel 2 or 4 shells for finishing cripples at about 14 m (15 yd) (65%).
- You must kill and retrieve a wounded bird as soon as you can (99%, s. 42). Make all reasonable effort to find it, and it counts in your bag (99%, page 12).
- Daily limit reached: stop hunting that species for the day (99%, s. 44).
- Dogs: Ducks Unlimited advises a young retriever gets a year of duck hunting before geese (65%). Without a dog, plan every retrieve before you shoot (Tip).
- Leave no shells, no garbage, no ruts. The next hunter needs the same farmer (Tip).
```
