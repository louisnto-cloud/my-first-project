- Rut peaks in November. Fawns: late May or June (85%).

## January to March: survive

```diagram mwt-winter-yard
```

- Valley bottoms, south slopes, dense conifer in deep snow (85%).
- Bucks drop antlers January to March (75%).

```more winter detail
- Snow depth is the main factor in where white tails can live (85%, BC account).
- Winter range: south to southwest slopes, fans and terraces with shrubs; cottonwood and willow groves early in winter; conifer patches when snow is deep (85%, BC account, East Kootenay data).
- In winter they are active most of the day, not just dawn and dusk (85%, BC account).
- In hard winters they **yard up** in dense cover and pack trails (80%).
- Deer can use snow as water in winter (80%, Montana State University Extension).
- Antlers drop after testosterone falls, about January to March (75%).
- Winter browse near Kamloops: Douglas fir peaks in December and January in deer rumens (85%, Willms 1976, mostly mule deer data).
- Leave wintering deer alone: every run costs fat they cannot replace (Tip).
```

## April to June: green up and fawns

```gallery
g-md-fawn-bedded | A hidden fawn lies flat and still. Leave it.
hab-saskatoon-flowers | Saskatoon blooms: green up time.
```

- Fields and riverbanks green first (60%).
- Fawns born late May or June, hidden about 2 weeks (85%).

```more spring detail
- Antlers start growing in April, in velvet (75%).
- Pregnant does drive off last year's young before giving birth (70%).
- Gestation is about 202 days, roughly 7 months (80%, Montana State University Extension; US Forest Service species review range 187 to 213 days).
- Fawns are born late May and June (85%, BC account; Montana State University Extension). Twins are normal (80%).
- Fawns weigh 2 to 4 kg (5 to 8 lb) (80%). Does choose 0.4 to 2 ha (1 to 5 acre) patches with low shrubs and trees to give birth (80%, Montana State University Extension).
- The doe hides the fawn and comes back only to nurse, about 4 to 6 times a day (75%). Fawns lie still about 95% of the day for the first 10 to 14 days (75%, Virginia DWR, Department of Wildlife Resources).
- Fawns have almost no scent at first, and the doe licks them clean (75%).
- Found a lone fawn? It is not abandoned. Leave it (Tip, agency advice).
- The fawn photo above is a mule deer fawn: white tail fawns look the same at this age (75%).
```

## July and August: grow

```diagram mwt-antler-cycle
```

- Bucks in bachelor groups, antlers in velvet (80%).
- Fawns start following the doe at 3 to 4 weeks (70%).

```more summer detail
- Summer: floodplains, river terraces, hay and alfalfa fields (85%, BC account; data/migration.json).
- Bucks in velvet avoid fights and stay in cool, secure, insect free spots (80%, Montana State University Extension).
- Bachelor groups of 2 to 4 bucks (80%).
- Summer activity peaks at dawn and dusk (85%, BC account).
- Fawns lose spots as their first winter coat comes in, in late summer and fall (70%).
```

## September: velvet off

```photo hab-deer-rub
```

- Velvet comes off in early to mid September (75%).
- Bachelor groups break up. Sparring starts (75%).

```more September detail
- Rising testosterone, driven by shorter days, hardens the antler and the buck rubs the velvet off (75%, University of Georgia).
- First rubs appear on saplings (70%).
- Bachelor groups split up through September and October (75%).
- Buck season opens. Region 3: bucks 10 September to 10 December (99%). Region 8: bucks 10 September to 30 November (99%). Details in chapter 10.

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law. Region 3 page 34, Region 8 page 68, 2026 to 2028 synopsis.
```

## October: what changes

```diagram mwt-oct-nov
```

- Rubs early, scrapes late in the month (65%).
- Bucks start moving in daylight late October (70%).

```more October detail
- Early October: sparring, rubs, bucks still on summer feeding patterns (70%).
- Mid to late October: scrapes appear and get worked at night (60%).
- Late October: the **seeking** phase. Bucks cruise for the first does in heat (60%, hunter guides).
- Yearling bucks disperse, mostly October and November (80%, Pennsylvania).
- Antlerless white tails: Region 3 and Region 8, 10 to 31 October, no draw (99%, pages 34 and 68).
- Food shifts: hay regrowth, last green forbs, then browse (75%).
- Hunter's move: hunt trails between bedding and food, add soft grunts late in the month (Tip, chapter 9).
```

## November: the rut

```diagram hf-rut-calendar
```

- Peak breeding mid November (80%).
- Old bucks move midday. Be out all day (Tip).

```more November detail
- The BC account puts the white tail rut peak in November (85%).
- Penn State: most does conceive about 13 to 17 November (80%). The Thompson peak is not measured: VERIFY local dates.
- Northern white tails breed in a tight window of about a month (85%, US Forest Service species review).
- **Chasing** in early to mid November, then **tending** (lockdown) when bucks stay with does (60%, hunter guides).
- Bucks barely eat and lose weight fast (75%).
- Rattling and grunting work best now (70%).
- Region 3: bucks open to 10 December. Region 8: bucks close 30 November. Youth only either sex 1 to 30 November in both (99%, pages 34 and 68).
```

## One doe's heat

```diagram mwt-estrus-cycle
```

- In heat about 24 hours (80%).
- Not bred? Heat again about 28 days later (80%).

```more estrus detail
- A doe is in heat (estrus) about 24 hours (80%, Montana State University Extension; US Forest Service species review says 24 to 48 hours).
- A buck **tends** her for 1 to 3 days before and 2 or 3 days after (80%).
- Not bred: she comes back into heat at about 28 day intervals (80%; US Forest Service species review gives 21 to 30 days).
- So a small **second rut** shows in mid December (Tip, from the 28 day cycle).
- Doe fawns on good feed can breed at 6 to 7 months, often later than adults (80%, Montana State University Extension).
```

## December: post rut

```diagram sg-deer-rut-phases
```

- Bucks worn out, feeding hard, back in cover (70%).
- Snow pushes deer to winter range (85%).

```more December detail
- Post rut bucks rest, feed and stay tight to cover (70%).
- Early winter: river groves first, then south slopes and conifer patches as snow deepens (65%, data/migration.json).
- Region 3: bucks to 10 December, then bow only either sex 11 to 20 December (99%, page 34).
- Region 8: bow only either sex 1 to 20 December (99%, page 68).
- Late season deer are lean. Food is the key: find the best remaining browse (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mwt-year-wheel
```

- **Rhyme:** "Spring in velvet, fall in bone; November bucks won't leave does alone."
- **Number trick:** 202 days, 24 hours, 28 days.
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "When the leaves are down and the frost is hard, the old bucks walk in daylight."

```more more hooks, mistakes and field drill
- **Number trick:** 13 to 17 November: most does bred (Penn State). Count back 202 days: early June fawns.
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, widely repeated): "Hunt rubs in October, scrapes before peak, does in the rut."
- **Rhyme:** "Antlers drop when days grow long; velvet's off when the rut's song's strong."

> [!mistake]
> - Hunting only weekends in November and missing the midweek peak.
> - Expecting a rut on a fixed date every year.
> - Pushing wintering deer for photos or shed antlers.

> [!field]
> From 15 October, log every rub, scrape and chasing buck you see, with date and place. In 3 years you will know your valley's rut.
```
