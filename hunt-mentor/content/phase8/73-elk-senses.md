## The nose

```anim mc-elk-scent-cone Your scent spreads downwind in a cone.
```

- If an elk smells you, it leaves (75%).
- In a herd, one nose is enough (Tip).

```more the nose in detail
- Smell is the elk's most developed sense; an elk that catches human scent will leave (75%, Colorado Parks and Wildlife course).
- A world champion elk caller's rule: if the elk smells you, the hunt is over; no cover spray beats a wind blowing straight at the bull (65%, RMEF (Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation) article).
- Agitated elk flare their nostrils (85%, RMEF).
- Your scent travels downwind in a widening cone (Tip). Big herds put many noses across that cone (Tip).
- Bulls in the rut also scent check: a bull coming to a call often circles to get downwind of it (65%, calling guides; chapter 9).
- Scent products: elk urine or parts from outside BC may not be used for hunting (99%, page 11, [unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts)).
```

## Thermals: which way your scent goes

```diagram mc-elk-thermal-slope
```

- Cool air sinks. Warm air rises (75%).
- First light and evening: come from below. Sun on the slope: from above (Tip).

```anim hf-thermal-clock Sun on the slope: air rises. Evening: air sinks.
```

```more thermals in detail
- Colorado Parks and Wildlife: breezes flow downhill early in the morning and reverse as the ground warms; hunt up the slope early and down later in the day (75%).
- Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation: cool air sinks at dawn carrying scent, moves upslope as the day warms, and sinks again at nightfall (70%).
- The switch time depends on sun: a slope that gets early sun turns upslope sooner than a shaded draw (Tip).
- When winds swirl, "the elk will probably win" (75%, Colorado Parks and Wildlife). Back out and come another day (Tip).
- Thermal skills in depth: [wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals).
```

## The ears

```diagram mc-elk-ears
```

- A snap makes it look and listen (75%).
- Ears turn to the sound (85%).

```more the ears in detail
- Elk hearing is good enough that a snapped twig draws attention, but elk tend to check a sound with eyes or nose before they run (75%, Colorado Parks and Wildlife).
- Alarmed elk rotate their ears to listen (85%, RMEF (Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation)).
- Elk are noisy themselves: they talk with chirps and mews, and a herd moving through timber makes noise (65%, hunting guides). A steady, natural pace may pass as elk; a clank of metal or a human voice does not (Tip).
- A bull's bugle can be heard a mile (1.6 km) or more away (80%, US National Park Service).
- No measured hearing range for elk was found (VERIFY). Do not assume deer numbers apply.
```

## The eyes: field of view

```diagram mc-elk-eye-field
```

- Eyes on the sides: a very wide view (80%).
- Only a narrow strip behind is blind (80%).

```more field of view in detail
- Deer family animals have eyes set on the sides of the head, horizontal pupils and a visual streak across the retina: built to scan the horizon for movement (80%, Newman and D'Angelo 2024 review).
- Most of the view is seen by one eye only; the zone ahead seen by both eyes is small, so judging distance is weaker (80%).
- Exact degrees for elk: not found (VERIFY).
- Motion: deer family eyes likely have very fast temporal resolution, so quick movement stands out (75%, review based on white tailed deer).
- So: move only when heads are down or behind cover, and freeze when a head comes up (Tip).
```

## The eyes: colour and low light

```diagram mc-elk-colour
```

- Two colour cones: blue clear, reds and oranges dull (80%).
- Outline and motion give you away (Tip).

```more colour and night vision in detail
- Deer family animals are dichromats: two colour cone types (short and middle wavelength) (80%, Newman and D'Angelo 2024).
- Their detail vision is low: red deer, the elk's close relative, measured under 6 cycles per degree (80%).
- A reflective layer behind the retina (tapetum) helps them see in dim light (80%).
- Most deer family eyes likely pass ultraviolet light; some brighteners in washed clothes glow in UV (75% for the eye, Tip for clothing).
- Whether elk can tell blaze orange from other colours: one 1981 report says they may; the study was not found (50%, VERIFY).
- The colour strip is a simulation, not a photo (Tip).
```

## How an elk combines its senses

```diagram mc-elk-sense-check
```

- Sees you, stares, then checks with nose or ears (75%).
- A bark means you are made (65%).

```more combining the senses
- Colorado Parks and Wildlife: elk see shapes but rely on other senses to "validate" what they see (75%).
- A stare is the elk gathering more evidence: hold still, let the wind work for you (Tip).
- An alarm bark from a cow warns the herd; a herd that bunches and trots usually leaves the drainage (65%, hunting guides).
- Every animal in a herd is a sentry. The odds of being caught rise with the number of elk (Tip).
- Next chapter: alarm and flight in detail ([the mind](#/s/mc-elk-04)).
```

## Beat each sense

```diagram mc-elk-beat-senses
```

- Nose: the wind first, always (75%).
- Ears and eyes: slow, quiet, still when heads are up (Tip).

```video

```

```more the full beat the senses list
| Sense | What beats it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Nose | Wind in your face; plan with thermals; back out of a swirling wind | Smell is first (75%) |
| Nose | Approach from below early and late, from above at midday | Thermals (75%) |
| Ears | Few steps, long pauses; soft clothes; no metal clicks | A snap draws a look (75%) |
| Ears | No talking; hand signals with your brother | Voices are not elk (Tip) |
| Eyes | Freeze when a head is up; move when heads are down | Motion detection (75%) |
| Eyes | Stay off skylines; keep cover behind you | Outlines (Tip) |
| All | Glass first, from far away | Elk cannot sense you at 1 km (Tip) |
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-elk-beat-senses
```

- **Rhyme:** "Nose knows, ears ask, eyes guess."
- **Number trick:** 1, 2, 3: nose, ears, eyes, in that order (75%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Hunt the wind first, the elk second."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Cool air falls, warm air climbs; check the wind a hundred times."
- **Number trick:** 1.6 km: how far a bugle carries, or more (80%).
- **Number trick:** 2 cones: an elk sees blue clearly, red and orange dull (80%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, widely taught): "When the wind swirls, go home and come back tomorrow."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "A stare is a question. Do not answer it by moving."

> [!mistake]
> - Walking in with the wind at your back because the trail is easier.
> - Moving while a cow is staring.
> - Trusting a cover scent instead of the wind.

> [!field]
> On your next hike: test the wind with fine dust or a thread every 10 minutes and note when it switches from downhill to uphill.
```
