## Where to wait

```diagram mc-skills-ambush-spots
```

- Funnels, saddles, creek crossings, field corners, trail junctions (Tip).
- Pushed game heads for steep, thick, far from roads (Tip).

```more ambush points in detail
- A funnel squeezes travel: a narrow strip of timber, a saddle, a bench, a gap in a fence (Tip, [Pinch points](#/s/pinch-points)).
- Field deer often enter at the same corner or gap nearest thick cover, with the wind in their favour (60%, hunter lore, [Farmland](#/s/farm-country)).
- In a Pennsylvania GPS (Global Positioning System) study, 73% of hunters stayed within 0.5 km of a road; the average farthest point was 0.84 km (90%, [Hunting pressure](#/s/pressure-push)). Opening morning, sit the trail between the crowd and the nearest steep, thick timber (Tip).
- Sit downwind or crosswind of the trail, 100 to 150 m off with a clear lane, for a rifle (Tip, [Pinch points](#/s/pinch-points)).
- Mark seats and both winds on the [Hunt Map](#/map) before the season (Tip).
```

## Ground blinds

```diagram mc-skills-ground-blind
```

- Dark inside, dark back wall, small windows: you vanish (Tip).
- Brush it in and set it early so animals get used to it (Tip).

```more blinds in detail
- A ground blind or a natural blind of brush hides your outline and hand movement (Tip, [White tailed deer senses](#/s/mc-white-tailed-deer-03)).
- Wear dark clothes inside; open only the windows you shoot through (Tip).
- Set it a week ahead where you can (Tip, [White tailed deer: how to hunt](#/s/mc-white-tailed-deer-09)).
- No fall risk, room for two, and a partner can watch the back (Tip).
- Private land: the landowner's permission covers what you may set up and leave (Tip, [Landowner permission](#/s/landowner-permission)). Leaving a blind on Crown land: the app's Rule Book does not cover it. Ask a [[Conservation officer]] (VERIFY).
- Duck and goose blinds: [Ducks: how to hunt](#/s/mc-ducks-09) and [Geese: how to hunt](#/s/mc-geese-09).
```

## Tree stands: the harness rule

```steps Up and down the tree safely
mc-skills-stand-1 | Inspect the stand, straps and harness.
mc-skills-stand-2 | Full body harness on at the truck.
mc-skills-stand-3 | Clip in before your feet leave the ground.
mc-skills-stand-4 | Haul line up the unloaded firearm.
```

- Most falls happen climbing up or down, or getting in and out of the stand (70%).
- Connected the whole time. Feet on the ground to feet on the ground (70%).

```more tree stand safety in detail
- Wisconsin DNR (Department of Natural Resources): "Always wear a full-body harness also known as a fall-arrest system." "Always have three points of contact while climbing." "Always use a haul line to raise and lower your unloaded firearm or bow" (80%, read 2026-10-11).
- Iowa DNR with the Tree Stand Safety Awareness group teaches the ABC's: Always inspect your equipment, Buckle on a full body harness, Connect to the tree before your feet leave the ground (70%, search preview).
- About 60% of falls happen climbing up or down or moving into the stand; in one 13 state database, 79% of people who fell wore no harness and the rest were not attached to the tree (70%, Iowa DNR search preview).
- University of Wisconsin trauma review, 1999 to 2013: 55% of tree stand injuries involved the spine; 4 of 117 patients reported any harness use (75%, Wisconsin Public Radio, 17 January 2018).
- Suspension trauma, hanging in a harness, "can occur in under 20 minutes and can be fatal" (80%, Wisconsin DNR). Carry a suspension relief strap and a phone on you, not in the pack (Tip).
- A firearm never goes up or down the tree loaded, and never on your back while climbing (80%, haul line rule).
- No BC tree stand rule found in the app's Rule Book (85%, my reading). Ask a conservation officer before putting a stand on Crown land (VERIFY).
- My lean: a ground blind or a natural seat is safer and is plenty for BC rifle hunting. If you climb, wear the harness every time.
```

## Calls and decoys: what is legal

```diagram mc-skills-legal-calls
```

- Mouth and hand calls, grunt tubes, rattling: allowed (95%, my reading).
- Electronic or recorded calls: only wolf, coyote, cougar, lynx, bobcat and Schedule C birds (99%).

```more the Rule Book lines
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Unlawful: "To use recorded or electronic calls to hunt wildlife except wolf, coyote, cougar, lynx, or bobcat or any bird listed in Schedule C" (99%, page 12, item 41).
- Unlawful: "To use live birds as decoys or recorded bird calls to hunt game birds" (99%, item 42).
- Fake decoys: allowed (99%). A decoy is not bait (99%).
- Full table: [Rule Book: legal methods, calls and decoys](#/s/rb-legal-methods).
```

## Your call: coyote wind

```diagram mc-skills-coyote-wind
```

- Coyotes often circle to come in from downwind (65%).
- Wind in your face or a crosswind? Good callers disagree. Your call.

| | Wind in your face | Crosswind |
|---|---|---|
| Idea | Scent never reaches the calling area | Coyote's downwind loop passes in front of you |
| Who says it | Kevin Wilson, Outdoor Canada (65%) | Geoff Nemnich, Grand View Outdoors; Heath Wood, Bass Pro (65%) |
| Risk | Call muffled; coyote circles behind you | Coyote may wind you if you sit too far downwind |

```more both sides in their words
> [!lean]
> Your call. Both setups are taught. Try each over a few dawns this winter and log where every coyote came from.

### Wind in your face
- Kevin Wilson (Outdoor Canada, 3 March 2025): "Whenever possible, I try to call from a downwind position." A crosswind "may be the next best play" (65%, read 2026-10-11).
- He adds that "with either a crosswind or the wind directly in your face, your chances are good" (65%).
- Nemnich lists the headwind pros: it muffles your walk in, and "your scent is not blowing into the area you intend to call" (65%).

### Crosswind
- Geoff Nemnich (Grand View Outdoors, 20 June 2018) calls crosswind "my preferred wind direction while on stand": the call carries farther and "the downwind side of the call is now off to your right or left, not behind you" (65%, read 2026-10-11).
- He lists headwind cons: the call "doesn't travel as far" and it is hard to cover the area directly downwind (65%).
- Heath Wood (Bass Pro, 2019, updated 2026): "I prefer a crosswind"; "Always sit where the downwind side is visible for the shot" (65%, read 2026-10-11).

### What they agree on
- Coyotes usually try to come in from downwind of the sound (65%).
- See the downwind side. Outdoor Canada: at least 50 yd (46 m), ideally 200 yd (183 m) or more (60%, search preview).
- Electronic caller in front of or upwind of you: 20 to 40 yd (Nemnich), within 50 yd (Wood), 40 to 50 yd (Mossberg) (60 to 65%).
- Electronic calls are legal for coyotes (99%, Rule Book item 41). Check the season and your area first ([Rule Book](#/s/rb-legal-methods)).
- The older [Predators](#/s/predators) lesson shows one setup of each kind. Neither is the rule (Tip).
```

## Patience: how long to sit

```diagram mc-skills-sit-clock
```

- Glassing seat: an hour, then again (65%). Coyote stand: 20 to 30 minutes (Tip).
- Stand or blind: be in before first light, stay through mid morning (Tip).

```more patience in detail
- Kirchner glasses "one hour at a time at each spot before moving" (65%, onX).
- Coyote stands: many callers play a sequence for 20 to 30 minutes, then move (Tip, [Predators](#/s/predators)).
- Deer movement peaks around dawn and dusk (70%, [Glassing](#/s/glassing)). Mule deer activity peaks about 04:00 to 09:00 and 17:00 to 21:00 (85%, from the [Mule deer cheat sheet](#/s/mc-mule-deer-10) and its sources).
- Be on the seat before legal light and stay until the end of legal light in the evening (Tip). Legal hours: [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods) (99%).
- Cold kills patience: a foam pad to sit on, a layer more than you think, hand warmers (75%, [Cold injuries](#/s/cold-injuries)).
- Grandpa's rule (wisdom, from [Glassing](#/s/glassing)): "Sit longer than you think. When you want to move, glass it one more time."
```

## What a 40 year hunter does differently

| Beginner | Old hand (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Sits where it looks pretty | Sits where the trail squeezes |
| Arrives at sunrise | Is settled before first light |
| Calls every 5 minutes | Calls little, watches a lot |
| Climbs without a harness | Clipped in, ground to ground |
| Leaves at 8 a.m. | Stays through mid morning |

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-skills-ambush-map
```

- **Rhyme:** "Feet off the ground, harness on and bound."
- **Number trick:** 3 points of contact; 20 minutes to suspension trauma (80%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, short form of [[Stand hunting]]): "Let the animal do the walking."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Mouth call fine, wire call only for the dog line": electronic only for coyote, wolf and the cats (99%, simplified).
- **Number trick:** 60% of falls happen getting up, down, in or out (70%).
- **Number trick:** 4 of 117 injured stand hunters wore a harness (75%).
- **Your call** (opinion): coyote wind in your face, or crosswind. Test both.
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, short form of "Less is more" in the deer grunt call video): "Call less, watch more."

> [!mistake]
> - Climbing a stand without being clipped in.
> - Sitting where the wind takes your scent down the trail.
> - Leaving the seat just before the animal arrives.

> [!field]
> Pick one funnel on the Hunt Map near home. Sit it two mornings with the wind right. Log what passed, when, and from where.
```
