## Bed to food to water

```diagram mc-black-bear-habitat
```

- Feeds, then beds in cover close by (Tip).
- Travels in cover between food sites (90%).

```more the daily loop in detail
- Home ranges are "several feeding areas connected by travel routes... often located in wooded areas that provide cover" (90%, BC account).
- Day beds: shallow scrapes in thick cover or at the base of a big tree near food (Tip, chapter 8).
- Hot days: water and shade in creek bottoms (Tip, quick lesson).
- In hyperphagia a bear may forage up to about 20 hours a day (65%, agency and news reports); feeding stretches into midday in cool fall weather (Tip).
```

## When it moves

```diagram mc-black-bear-day-clock
```

- WildSafeBC: "mainly diurnal" (75%).
- Glass the first and last 2 hours hardest (Tip).

## Weather

```diagram mc-black-bear-weather
```

- Most weather effects are hunter experience, not studies (Tip).
- No good bear data on the moon: VERIFY.

```more weather in detail
- Heat: bears bed in shade and near water on hot afternoons (Tip). Early fall heat pushes feeding to dawn and dusk (Tip).
- Cool, cloudy days: longer daylight feeding (Tip).
- Rain: feeding continues; wet ground lets you move quietly (Tip).
- Wind: hard gusts swirl scent and noise; hunt sheltered slopes (Tip, [wind mastery](#/s/wind-mastery)).
- Cold weather makes bears "increasingly lethargic" before denning (90%, BC account). A first heavy snow as the den trigger is common hunter belief (Tip).
- Barometric pressure and the moon: no bear studies found (VERIFY). For deer, no moon effect was found (70%, [mule deer chapter 6](#/s/mc-mule-deer-06)).
```

## Hunting pressure

```anim mc-black-bear-scent-cone One whiff and a pressured bear goes nocturnal.
```

- Bears near people feed more at night (75%).
- Bumped bears come back after dark, if at all (Tip).

```more pressure in detail
- WildSafeBC: bears are mainly diurnal "but may feed at night in high risk, human active areas" (75%).
- Massachusetts GPS (Global Positioning System) collars on 86 bears: in developed areas bears moved their foraging to night (65%, secondary write up).
- A road hunted cutblock sees the most pressure. Walk past the gate or glass across the valley (Tip).
- Do not walk through the same patch every evening; glass it from far away (Tip).
- Spring bear hunters crowd the first green road edges; the far side of the valley gets less traffic (Tip).
```

## A sample October day near Heffley Creek

| Time | Plan (Tip) |
|---|---|
| 1 hour before sunrise | On a glassing knob before light |
| Sunrise to 9:00 | Glass berry slopes and cutblock edges |
| 9:00 to 15:00 | Scout sign, hang trail cameras, check the wind |
| 15:00 to sunset | Back on glass over the best food |
| Last light | Stay until 1 hour after sunset, then out safely |

- Legal hours are the frame; the bear decides inside it (Tip).
- Plan with the [planner](#/plan) and the [Heffley Creek](#/s/local-heffley) page.

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-black-bear-day-clock
```

- **Rhyme:** "First light, last light, bears are in sight."
- **Number trick:** 1 and 1: hunt 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (99%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, Jim Shockey via Boone and Crockett): "The big bear holds the best food at the best time."

```more more hooks
- **Number trick:** 20: up to about 20 hours a day of fall foraging (65%).
- **Rhyme:** "Hot noon, shady bed; cool day, longer spread."
- **Tip:** glass the far side, not the road side.

> [!mistake]
> - Leaving the glass at 9:00 in October, when bears feed longer in cool weather.
> - Driving the same road every evening.
> - Believing moon rules for bears with no data.

> [!field]
> Pick one cutblock. Glass it at dawn, noon and dusk on one day and log every bear in the Journal.
```
