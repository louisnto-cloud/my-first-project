## Tracks to scale

```diagram mmd-track-scale
```

- Split heart, 2 toes; the point shows the way it went (85%).
- Front track about 5.7 to 10 cm long (60%).

```more track details
- Two toes, heart shaped; the pointed end shows the direction of travel (85%).
- Mule deer front track about 5.7 to 10 cm long (60%, research notes). Front tracks are usually a bit bigger than hind (75%).
- Dewclaws (two dots behind the print) show in soft ground, snow or when running (Tip).
- One track cannot tell mule deer from white tail; too much overlap (60%). The trail pattern can (chapter screen below).
- Measure, never guess: carry a tape or know your knife length (Tip).
- Splayed toes and deep prints: running or a heavy animal on soft ground (Tip).
```

## Trail patterns: walk, trot, stot

```diagram mmd-gait-pattern
```

- Stot: groups of 4 prints, bounds 2.7 to 5.2 m (60 to 80%).
- A stot trail means alarm. A walk means calm (Tip).

```more gaits in detail
- Walking: prints in pairs or one line, short even steps; the hind foot often lands in the front print (Tip, see [Tracks](#/s/tracks)).
- Trot: longer, straighter steps (Tip).
- Stot: all 4 feet push off together; groups of 4 prints 2.4 to 4.6 m apart (60%, research notes); park service gives 2.7 to 5.2 m per bound (80%).
- A white tail bounding gallop leaves groups too, but long and low with the hind feet landing ahead of the front (70%, [Tracks](#/s/tracks)). The bouncy stot is the mule deer mark (85%).
- A stot trail near you: you or something else spooked it. Do not follow at once (Tip).
```

## Droppings

```diagram sg-scat-age
```

- Oval pellets about 1 to 1.5 cm (70%).
- Dark, shiny, moist: hours old (60%).

```more pellets in detail
- Pellets: oval, about 1 to 1.5 cm long, often with a small point; firm and dry in fall and winter (70%).
- Summer on green feed: softer, clumped (Tip).
- A deer drops roughly 13 to 34 pellet groups a day (70%). Lots of piles = lots of deer time there (Tip).
- Hours: wet, shiny, maybe warm. Days: dull with a crust. Weeks: faded, crumbling, mouldy (60%).
- Cold slows ageing: frozen pellets can look fresh for a week (Tip).
- Mixed ages in one spot: a bed or feeding area used again and again (Tip).
```

## Beds

```diagram mmd-bed-reading
```

- Oval about 1 m, pellets and hair nearby (70%).
- Melted frost or a warm bed: minutes to hours (Tip).

```more bed reading in detail
- A deer bed is an oval of flattened grass, needles or snow about 0.9 to 1.2 m long (70%).
- Beds sit where a deer sees below and smells above: benches, under a tree with a view, the lee side of a roll (60 to 65%, hunter lore and Rinella).
- Mule deer often bed facing downhill (65%, Rinella).
- Several beds together: a doe group. A lone big bed away from others: often a buck (Tip).
- In snow, an old bed melts and refreezes into an icy bowl; a dry, crisp floor is recent (65%, [Reading sign](#/s/sign)).
- Frost: a dark green oval in a white frosted slope at 08:00 means it just got up (Tip).
- Warm bed: the deer may be watching you. Freeze and glass ahead (Tip).
```

## Rubs and thrashed brush

```photo hab-deer-rub
```

- Mule deer rub less than white tails, on small conifers and shrubs (60%).
- Thrashed brush in October: a buck is near (90% for the behaviour, Tip for the meaning).

```more rubs in detail
- Bucks rub velvet off on saplings and bushes in late August and September; velvet strips in 24 to 48 hours (85%, Mule Deer Working Group fact sheet 27).
- Bucks show dominance by "violently thrashing the bushes with their antlers" (90%, BC account).
- Mule deer make fewer rubs than white tails, mostly on small conifers and shrubs (60%).
- Rub height says more about buck size than tree size; a rub centred 75 cm or higher suggests a mature buck (60%, [Reading sign](#/s/sign)).
- Scrapes are mostly a white tail sign; mule deer rarely scrape (60%).
- Fresh: wet white wood, curled bark shavings, sap; old: grey, dry wood (Tip).
```

## Browse sign

```diagram sg-browse-cut
```

- Deer tear twigs; hares cut them clean at 45 degrees (75%).
- Green stem ends = fresh (Tip).

```more browse in detail
- Deer have no upper front teeth, so browsed twigs look torn and ragged (75 to 85%, chapter 2).
- Snowshoe hares clip twigs clean at about 45 degrees (75%).
- A browse line: shrubs eaten up to about head height on winter range shows heavy deer use (Tip, [Reading sign](#/s/sign)).
- Fresh browse: green, moist stem ends; old: brown, dry (Tip).
- Check saskatoon, red osier dogwood, bitterbrush and rose (85%, chapter 7).
```

## How fresh: put it together

```diagram sg-fresh-ladder
```

- Read 3 clues, not 1: track edges, pellets, bed (Tip).
- Note weather: rain and frost reset the clock (Tip).

```more freshness checklist
- Track edges sharp, no debris or frost in the print: fresh (Tip).
- Last rain or frost: a print on top of it is newer than the weather (Tip).
- Pellets shiny and soft: hours (60%).
- Bed with no frost while the slope is frosted: very fresh (Tip).
- Browse ends green and wet: today (Tip).
- Decide: fresh sign plus good wind = slow down and glass; old sign = mark it in the Journal and move (Tip).
```

## How to follow a trail

```steps Follow a mule deer trail
mmd-follow-1 | Mark the last sure track. Flag and pin it.
mmd-follow-2 | Walk beside the trail, not on it.
mmd-follow-3 | Look ahead 20 to 40 m, not at your feet.
mmd-follow-4 | Lost it? Circle out from the flag.
```

- Follow slow. The deer is usually ahead, watching back (Tip).
- Snow is the best classroom (Tip).

```more following in detail
- Mark the last sure track with flagging and a phone pin; you will need it if you lose the trail (Tip, [blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)).
- Walk beside the line so you do not wipe out sign for a second look (Tip).
- Glass ahead every few steps; a following hunter usually sees the deer only if he looks up (Tip).
- A trail that turns uphill and slows, with the deer stopping to look back, often leads to a bed on a bench (Tip).
- Mule deer often bed where they can watch their back trail (hunter lore 60%). Follow the trail from the side and above (Tip).
- Lost it: back to the last flag, then circle in small then bigger loops (Tip).
- Wounded animal tracking is its own skill: [blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mmd-gait-pattern
```

- **Rhyme:** "Point shows the way, shine shows the day."
- **Number trick:** 1, 1 to 1.5, 5.7 to 10: a 1 m bed, 1 to 1.5 cm pellets, 5.7 to 10 cm tracks (60 to 70%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Fresh sign beats a good map."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Torn twig, deer; clean cut, hare."
- **Number trick:** 4 prints per group in a stot; 2.7 to 5.2 m between (60 to 80%).
- **Number trick:** 13 to 34 pellet groups a day (70%).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely taught, opinion): "Look up from the track." The deer is ahead, not under your boots.
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "A warm bed means freeze, not sit."

> [!mistake]
> - Calling species from one track.
> - Following with eyes on the ground.
> - Trusting pellets alone for freshness in frost.

> [!field]
> Next scouting walk: find 3 pellet piles and age each one. Photograph them with your knife for scale. Check your guesses against the [sign drill](#/s/sign-drill).
```
