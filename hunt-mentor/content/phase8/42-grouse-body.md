> [!why]
> Know what is inside the bird and you know where to shoot, how to keep the meat, and why grouse act the way they do.

```diagram mgr-body-cutaway
```

- Big white breast for bursts; crop, gizzard and ceca for tough food (75 to 85%).
- Lungs high on the back, heart low and forward (80%).

```more the body in one list
- Crop: a stretchy pouch at the base of the neck that holds a meal (85%).
- Gizzard: a muscular stomach that grinds food with swallowed grit (85%).
- Ceca: two long blind pouches where bacteria break down woody fibre (75%).
- Lungs: small and rigid, tucked against the ribs and backbone (80%).
- Air sacs: thin balloons that move air through the lungs; they also reach into some bones (70 to 80%).
- Heart: four chambers, low and forward in the chest (80%).
- Breast: two big flight muscles on the keel bone, white meat (70%).
```

## Bones and muscles that matter

```diagram mgr-keel-muscle
```

- The keel is a blade on the breastbone that anchors the flight muscles (80%).
- White breast: power for a few seconds. Dark legs: walking all day (70%).

```more why white and dark meat
- Grouse breast meat is white: fast muscle fibres with little myoglobin that run on stored sugar and tire fast (70%, studies on turkeys and chickens, close cousins).
- That is why a grouse explodes up, flaps hard, then glides: the breast cannot keep flapping long (70%). Pennsylvania notes the glide is usually under 90 m (100 yd) (75%).
- Leg and thigh meat is darker: slow muscle that works all day as the bird walks (70%). Grouse walk more than they fly (75%, Pennsylvania Game Commission).
- Bird bones are light; some are hollow and filled with air sac extensions (70%).
- Meat note: the breast is most of the meat on a grouse. The leg is small but tasty (Tip).
```

## How a grouse breathes

```anim mgr-air-sacs Fresh air crosses the lung one way.
```

- Rigid lungs, air sacs as bellows (80%).
- Air flows one way through the lung, in breath and out (80%).

```more the air sac system in plain words
- A bird's lungs do not swell like ours. The air sacs expand and squeeze to push air through them (80%, 2024 review in a peer reviewed journal; biology textbook).
- Most birds have about 9 air sacs, in a front set and a rear set (70%).
- On the in breath much of the fresh air goes past the lung into the rear sacs. On the out breath that air is pushed through the lung. Gas exchange happens only in the lung, not in the sacs (80%).
- No diaphragm: the ribs and keel move to pump the sacs (75%).
- Result: fresh air crosses the lung on both in and out breaths. Birds get more oxygen per breath than we do (80%).
- Hunting meaning: a pellet through the lungs or the big sacs takes the bird's breath; a hit only in the back half often does not stop it fast (Tip, 60%).
```

## Heart, blood and the vitals

```diagram mgr-vitals-bird
```

- Four chambered heart, big for the body (80%).
- Brain, spine, heart and lungs are all in the front half (80%).

```more what it means for the shot
- Bird hearts have two atria and two ventricles, with the two sides fully separate, like ours. The main artery curves right, not left (80%, avian heart review).
- Birds carry relatively large hearts for flight (80%).
- Body temperature runs hot: about 41.5 C in captive ruffed grouse, range 40.4 to 42.6 C (75%).
- .22: the head or upper neck kills cleanly and saves the breast (Tip, 70%). See [How to hunt](#/s/mc-grouse-09/3).
- Shotgun: put the pattern on the head and front half. Pellets in the head, neck and chest stop the bird (Tip, 70%).
- A grouse hit only in the wing or rear may run and hide. Mark it and go fast (Tip). See [Shot to table](#/s/mc-grouse-10).
```

## The food path: crop to ceca

```diagram mgr-gut-path
```

- Crop stores, gizzard grinds, ceca ferment (80%).
- Open the crop of every bird you shoot: it shows the food (Tip).

```more digestion step by step
- A grouse can fill its crop with a day's worth of buds in about 20 minutes, then sit somewhere safe to digest (60%, Northern Woodlands).
- The gizzard uses swallowed grit to grind seeds, buds and needles (85%). That is why grouse pick gravel on road edges and dust bowls (75%).
- The ceca hold bacteria that ferment fibre from woody food like aspen and birch buds (75%).
- Ruffed grouse prefer aspen flower buds and avoid birch and alder catkins when they can choose (80%, Guglielmo and Karasov 1995).
- Field use: slit the crop and look. Clover leaves, kinnikinnick berries, aspen buds or pine needles tell you exactly where to hunt next (Tip, common hunter practice).
```

## The winter gut

```diagram mgr-gut-seasons
```

- In winter the gut grows longer to handle needles and buds (75%).
- Spruce grouse: 99% needles once snow covers the ground (75%).

```more numbers
- Alberta spruce grouse: conifer needles were 79% of the year's diet by dry weight and 99% during the snow season (75%, Pendergast and Boag 1973, via summaries).
- Gut sections grow about 40% longer in winter (75%). One secondary source says the gizzard grows about 75% (60%, VERIFY).
- Ceca enlarge in winter when only needles are eaten (70%).
- Taste: late season spruce grouse living on needles can taste of pine; soak or brine (Tip).
```

## Body heat and the snow roost

```diagram mgr-snow-roost
```

- Grouse run about 41.5 C inside (75%).
- Soft snow 20 cm deep turns into a shelter (75%).

```more winter survival tools
- Snow roost: under 20 cm of soft snow, roost temperatures stayed between -3 and -12 C while the air dropped as low as -35 C (75%, Wilson Bulletin).
- In Maine, grouse plunged into the snow in flight once it was about 40 cm deep (70%). A study found grouse stress hormones fall sharply once snow is deeper than 20 cm (70%).
- Snow roosts can be 11 to 17 C (20 to 30 F) warmer than open air (75%, Idaho Fish and Game).
- Snowshoes: in winter grouse grow comb like scales on the sides of their toes, called pectinations (85%, Hinterland Who's Who).
- Ptarmigan grow feathered feet, unique among chicken like birds (85%).
- No good snow? A crust or bare cold ground forces grouse into thick conifers; they lose weight and some starve or freeze (85%, Hinterland Who's Who).
```

## Lifespan and the fat cycle

```diagram mgr-survival
```

- Most chicks die in the first weeks (75%).
- About half of adults live through each year (75%).

```more survival numbers
- Over three quarters of ruffed grouse chicks die within 35 days of hatching, most in the first week (75%, Pennsylvania Game Commission).
- About half of hens lose their whole brood in the first 1 to 2 weeks (85%, Hinterland Who's Who).
- Adult yearly survival: about 50% in Pennsylvania (75%); 53% in Maryland; 32% for adults and 16% for young birds in Maine (75%). Winter, January to March, is the hardest time (75%).
- Few ruffed grouse live to age 2 (75%). Some are thought to live 11 years (60%).
- Dusky grouse: about half die in their first year (65%).
- Fat: grouse carry little fat compared with ducks. They survive winter by eating every day and roosting well, not by living off fat (Tip, 60%).
- Hunting: in one large study hunting made up 12% of known deaths and looked compensatory, meaning it replaced other deaths (75%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mgr-vitals-bird
```

- Rhyme: "Crop to store, gizzard to grind, ceca to brew what's left behind."
- Number trick: "41 inside, minus 35 outside, snow between."
- Old hand's rule (tradition): "Check the crop, find the crop": what is in it grows nearby.

```more more hooks
- "White to fly, dark to walk."
- "Front half or nothing": brain, spine, heart and lungs are all forward.
- "Two sets of sacs, one way through."
- "3 in 4 chicks gone by 5 weeks."
- The crop check is common hunter practice written up in many hunting guides; the rhymes are made for this course.
```
