> [!why]
> Elk country is big, but elk use small parts of it: the edge where food meets cover, at the height the season puts them. Find that and the mountain shrinks.

```diagram mc-elk-habitat-xsection
```

- Summer high basins; fall timber and openings; winter low benches (90%).
- Always near cover (80%).

```more what this chapter covers
- The edge, key BC foods by season, winter range, elevation by month, your three areas (Heffley Creek, Mission, South Okanagan) and how to find elk country on the Hunt Map.
- Elk are habitat and diet generalists that quickly use new openings like burns and cutblocks (80%, BC habitat model).
- They live in both dense coastal rainforest and grassy interior valleys, but keep to places where winter snow stays shallow (90%, BC account).
```

## An edge animal

```diagram mc-elk-three-needs
```

- Food, cover and water close together (80%).
- Security cover hides 90% of an elk at 61 m (80%).

```gallery
hab-north-thompson-burn | A burn: new grass and shrubs under the snags.
hab-wells-gray-cutblock | An old cutblock with meadow edges.
hab-grass-fir-edge | Grass meets fir: the edge.
hab-elk-wallow | Water and a wallow in a wet meadow.
```

```more the edge in detail
- Elk favour the boundary between forest and open ground: lots of food right next to cover (80%, BC habitat model citing Skovlin 1982).
- Best feeding habitat: early growth stages such as grassland, parkland, avalanche tracks, clearcuts, burns, wetlands, riparian strips and roadsides; recently burned areas are often very productive (80%).
- Security cover: vegetation over 2 m that hides 90% of an elk at 61 m (80%). Gullies, ridges and hummocky ground also hide elk (80%).
- Elk will not feed in the middle of very large clearcuts far from cover, especially near people (80%).
- Logging helps for a while: openings feed elk until the young forest closes the canopy (90%, BC account).
```

## Key foods by season

```gallery
hab-lac-du-bois-grassland | Bunchgrass: fall, winter and spring feed.
hab-saskatoon-berries | Saskatoon (serviceberry): winter browse.
hab-woods-rose-hips | Wild rose: winter browse.

```

- Grasses and sedges first, all year when they can get them (80%).
- Winter and deep snow: shrubs and twigs (80%).

```more the food list
| Season | Interior Rocky Mountain elk | % |
|---|---|---|
| Spring, summer | New grass, sedges, forbs (broad leaved herbs) in subalpine basins and avalanche tracks | 80 to 90 |
| Fall | Grasses on openings and burns; forbs at higher elevation | 80 |
| Winter | Sedges, horsetail and willow in wetlands; bunchgrasses, forbs, saskatoon, rose and other shrubs on uplands | 90 |
| Deep snow | Browse: saskatoon, water birch, aspen, willow, red osier dogwood; conifers except spruce | 80 |
| Farms | Alfalfa and clover hay fields | 90 |

- Coast Roosevelt elk: sedges, grasses and ferns, plus browse of willow, elderberry, blueberry, cedar and hemlock (90%, BC account).
- RMEF (Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation): summer grasses and forbs; spring and fall grasses; winter grasses, shrubs, bark and twigs; mineral licks (85%).
- Learn the plants: [browse plants](#/s/browse-plants), [berries and mast](#/s/berries-mast).
```

## Winter range

```diagram mc-elk-snow-depth
```

- Low, south and southwest slopes, grass and open forest (80 to 90%).
- Dense conifer when snow gets deep (80%).

```photo hab-douglas-fir-stand
```

```more winter range in detail
- Winter ranges: open forest, grassy benchlands and floodplain marshes (90%, BC account).
- South to southwest exposures preferred in winter and spring; snow free slopes and chinook weather give the best access to feed (80%).
- Sites sheltered by terrain or dense trees are sought out against wind, crusting and drifting snow (80%).
- Very deep snow: closed conifer stands over 12 m with over 70% canopy (80%).
- On the Hunt Map the official Elk winter range layer covers map area F (East Kootenay) only. Near your bases, use the "Elk by month" layer, south slopes and habitat zones instead (99% for the layer coverage, app data).
- Winter range access rules: [winter range](#/s/winter-range-legal).
```

## Elevation by month

```diagram mc-elk-elev-months
```

- June to August: about 1,000 to 2,300 m (55%).
- Winter: below about 1,200 m (60%).

```more elevation in detail
- These bands come from the app's migration data: habitat zones and elevations by month built from the BC account and BC ecosystem zone ranges, not from collar data (55 to 70%).
- January to March: 0 to 1,200 m, bunchgrass, ponderosa pine and interior Douglas fir zones, coastal western hemlock on the coast.
- May: 300 to 2,000 m, migrating.
- June to August: 1,000 to 2,300 m, montane spruce, Engelmann spruce subalpine fir and alpine zones.
- September: 800 to 2,200 m, rut (70%).
- October: 600 to 2,000 m (60%).
- November: 0 to 1,500 m (55%).
- Real herds vary a lot; some do not migrate at all (90%, BC account).
```

## Find them on the Hunt Map

```steps Find elk country on the Hunt Map
mc-elk-map-1 | Layers: Elk by month, burns, cutblocks, land status.
mc-elk-map-2 | Slope direction: north for summer beds, south for winter.
mc-elk-map-3 | Slope steepness: benches of 15 to 30%.
mc-elk-map-4 | What can I see from here: pick a glassing seat.
mc-elk-map-5 | Wind and scent cone: plan the approach.
```

- Open the [Hunt Map](#/map). Set the month first (Tip).
- Then walk it: sign, sound, glass (Tip).

```more step by step in words
1. **Layers:** turn on "Elk by month (general pattern)" and pick the month; add "Burns by year", "Cutblocks by age" and "Habitat zones". Turn on land status: Crown land, private land, parks, closures (Tip). The elk month layer is 60% (app layer note: general pattern, not mapped corridors).
2. **Elk winter range** (official layer) only covers map area F, East Kootenay (99%, app data).
3. **Slope direction:** north faces for hot day beds in September; south and southwest faces for late fall and winter feed (75 to 80%, chapters 5 and 6).
4. **Slope steepness:** benches and slopes of 15 to 30% next to timber (80%). Above 40 to 50% elk use drops (80%).
5. **What can I see from here:** drop a pin on a knob across a basin or burn; pick seats that see several openings and timber edges (Tip).
6. **Sun and shade:** set the time to sunrise to see which slopes warm first; thermals switch there first (Tip).
7. **Wind and scent cone:** check the forecast wind; plan a route where the cone misses the openings (Tip).
8. Add waypoints, then go and listen at dawn in September. The map finds country; your ears find elk (Tip). Detail: [Hunt Map pro](#/s/hunt-map-pro), [map scouting](#/s/map-scouting).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-elk-three-needs
```

- **Rhyme:** "High in summer, low in snow, edge of the timber is where they go."
- **Number trick:** 61 and 183: metres for security cover and for most elk to cover (80%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Find the food next to the dark timber, and wait for the evening."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Burn and block, near the black; feed in the open, bed in the back."
- **Number trick:** 3, 2, 8 by herd size: Region 3 has hundreds, Region 2 and Region 8 thousands (90%).
- **Number trick:** 2-8: your home MU (Management Unit) took 67 elk in five years, all by draw (90% count, 85% reading).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Big country, small spots."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "If you can hear a road, the elk can too."

> [!mistake]
> - Glassing a huge clearcut middle instead of its edges.
> - Hunting high basins after the snow came.
> - Applying for an LEH (Limited Entry Hunting) hunt without checking access first.

> [!field]
> On the Hunt Map, mark three Region 8 burns 3 to 15 years old with timber on their north side. Plan a dawn seat for each.
```
