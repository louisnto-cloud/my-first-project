## Orient the map with the compass

```anim mc-nav-orient Turn the map until its north lines run with the needle.
```

- Turn the map, not yourself, until north lines run with the needle (95%).
- Now left on the map is left on the ground.

```more orienting in full
- "Make sure you are holding the map the same way as the ground features." Turn the map so its north lines run with the needle, red end to north (95%, Little Book of Orienteering Techniques, via [Compass mastery](#/s/compass-mastery)).
- You do not need to turn the compass ring to do this (95%).
- With 15 degrees of declination, the needle points 15 degrees right of the map's true north lines. For a rough orientation that is close enough; for a careful one, set the ring to 0 with declination set, then box the needle (90%).
- Keep the map and compass in one hand, thumb on where you are (Tip).
```

## Orient the map by the land

```diagram fn-cmp-orient
```

- No compass? Line up a road, ridge or lake on the map with the real one (95%).
- Two features lined up, and the map is set.

```more orienting by land
- You can orient by the land: line up a ridge or a road on the map with the real one (95%, Little Book of Orienteering Techniques).
- Best features: long and straight, like a road, a power line, a long lake, a valley (Tip).
- Check with a second feature off to the side. If it does not line up, you picked the wrong first one (Tip).
- On the phone, the Hunt Map's compass mode turns the map the way you face (99%, app: tap the locate button twice). It needs compass permission on iPhone (99%).
```

## Thumb the map

```diagram mc-nav-thumb
```

- Thumb on where you are. Move it as you walk (Tip).
- Fold the map small so only your area shows (Tip).

```more thumbing in full
- An orienteering habit: "thumb the map", keeping your thumb next to your position so one glance finds it (Tip).
- Fold the paper map so the leg you are walking faces out, inside a zip bag (Tip).
- Every check on your 15 to 30 minute rhythm, move the thumb ([Chapter 2](#/s/mc-nav-02)).
- On the phone: the blue dot is your thumb. Keep the screen off between checks to save battery (Tip, [Chapter 6](#/s/mc-nav-06)).
```

## Terrain association: 4 steps

```steps Match the land to the map
mc-nav-ta-1 | Name 3 big features you see.
mc-nav-ta-2 | Find the same three on the map.
mc-nav-ta-3 | Check their order, left to right.
mc-nav-ta-4 | Mark where you must be to see them that way.
```

- Big shapes first: ridges, valleys, lakes, saddles (Tip).
- Small things like a single boulder are rarely on the map (Tip).

```more terrain association in full
1. Orient the map first (previous screens).
2. Pick three big features you can see and name: a long ridge, a saddle, a peak, a lake, a valley junction (Tip).
3. Find them on the map. Contours show the shape: a saddle is an hourglass of lines, a ridge has U shapes pointing downhill (85%, [Chapter 3](#/s/mc-nav-03)).
4. Check their order and spacing left to right, and their direction from you (Tip).
5. Ask: where would I have to stand to see them like this? Mark it (Tip).
6. Confirm with one compass bearing or the phone (Tip).
- The trap: "bending the map", making the land fit what you hoped. If one feature does not fit, you are probably wrong (Tip).
- Forest changes the view: timber hides ridges, cutblocks open new views. Use the Hunt Map Satellite basemap at home to learn what timber edges look like (Tip, [Hunt Map like a pro](#/s/hunt-map-pro)).
```

## Where am I? Try in this order

```diagram mc-nav-where-flow
```

- Fastest first: handrail, landforms, cross bearings, phone (Tip).
- None works? STOP and stay ([Chapter 7](#/s/mc-nav-07)).

```more the order explained
1. On a handrail (road, creek, ridge)? One bearing to a landmark finds you on it (Tip, [Chapter 4](#/s/mc-nav-04)).
2. Clear landforms around you? Terrain association (Tip).
3. Two landmarks you can name? Cross bearings, about 90 degrees apart (85%).
4. Phone fix? Use it, then check it on paper: does the land match the dot? (Tip)
5. None of these? STOP: Stop, Think, Observe, Plan (75%, AdventureSmart).
- Your last sure point helps every method: you know you are within an hour's walk of it (Tip; about 4 km off trail, 75%).
```

## Slope and aspect: two more clues

```diagram fn-hm-aspect
```

- Which way does the slope fall away? It must match the map (Tip).
- Hunt Map: Slope direction shows which way each slope faces (99%).

```more using the ground under your feet
- Stand on a slope and take a bearing straight downhill. That is the aspect (Tip).
- On the map, the slope at your spot must fall the same way. If the map says it faces south and your compass says north, you are not there (Tip).
- Hunt Map Slope direction colours: north blue, east green, south yellow, west red, with in between colours (99%, app).
- Vegetation hints, rough only: in the Interior, south and west faces are drier and more open, north faces hold thicker timber and late snow (Tip, [Hunt Map like a pro](#/s/hunt-map-pro); 85% for October feed and warmth on south faces, [Maps 1](#/s/maps-terrain)).
```

## The sun as a rough compass

```diagram mc-nav-sun-arc
```

- Mid October near Heffley Creek: sun rises about 103 degrees, sets about 257 (85%).
- At about 12:46 the sun is due south (85%). Rough check only.

```more sun numbers worked out
- My calculation for Heffley Creek, 50.858 N 120.269 W (85%; true bearings):
  - 15 October 2026: rises 7:25 at 103 degrees, highest at 12:46 daylight time, due south, 30 degrees up; sets 18:08 at 257 degrees.
  - 15 November 2026, BC time (UTC minus 7; BC no longer falls back, 95%, [Weather, daylight and season timing](#/s/daylight-weather-bc)): rises 8:18 at 119 degrees, highest 12:45, 21 degrees up; sets 17:14 at 241 degrees.
- Mission is close: 15 October solar noon about 12:55, rise 102 degrees, set 257 (85%).
- So in October the sun rises south of east and sets south of west, and gets more so into November (85%).
- Use: a sunny afternoon sun is in the southwest; your shadow points northeast (Tip).
- Clouds and timber often hide it. Never use the sun instead of the compass (Tip).
```

## The shadow stick: rough east and west

```steps Shadow stick in 3 steps
mc-nav-shadow-1 | Stick upright. Mark the shadow tip.
mc-nav-shadow-2 | Wait 15 minutes. Mark it again.
mc-nav-shadow-3 | First mark west, second east.
```

- The first mark is west, the second east (60%).
- Rough only: errors from slope, a bent stick, too short a wait (60%).

```anim mc-nav-shadow The tip moves west to east as the sun moves.
```

```more the method and its limits
- Plant a straight stick about 1 m long on level ground, mark the shadow tip, wait 10 to 15 minutes, mark again; the line runs roughly west to east, first mark west (60%, scouting and survival guides, search preview).
- Standing with the first mark on your left, you face roughly north (60%).
- Sources differ on the wait; longer is more accurate (60%).
- Errors: sloped ground, a bent stick, too short a wait (60%).
- A more precise variant marks the morning shadow, draws an arc, and marks where the afternoon shadow touches the arc (60%, scouting source). Too slow for a hunting day (Tip).
- Night: Polaris from the Big Dipper pointers, about 51 degrees up at Heffley Creek (85 to 90%, [Night navigation](#/s/night-navigation)).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-nav-where-flow
```

- **Rhyme:** "Turn the map, not your feet. Big shapes first, then complete."
- **Number trick:** 103 and 257: mid October sunrise and sunset bearings at Heffley Creek (85%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, the orienteer's warning about forcing a match): "Don't bend the map to fit your hope."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Thumb on the map, never a gap."
- **Number trick:** 3 features, 1 order, 1 spot (terrain association, Tip).
- **Rhyme:** "First mark west, second mark east" (shadow stick, 60%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, [Compass mastery](#/s/compass-mastery)): "Every leg, ask: does the ground look like the map?"

> [!mistake]
> - Reading the map upside down because it was not oriented.
> - Forcing the land to fit the map when one feature is wrong.
> - Trusting the sun over a compass.

> [!field]
> On your next Heffley Creek scouting trip: at three stops, orient the map, name three features, mark a pencil dot, then check with the phone. Write the miss. Full drill: [Chapter 10](#/s/mc-nav-10).
```
