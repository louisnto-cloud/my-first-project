## Bed, feed, water

```diagram mmd-day-route
```

- Dawn: feed on open slopes, then drift up to bed (85%).
- Dusk: down to feed, water in dry weather (80%, Tip).

```more the daily loop
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Night: feeds on and off in open grass and brush, rests between bouts (75%).
- Before first light to about 09:00: a strong activity peak (85%, Stewart and others 2002, Oregon telemetry).
- Then it drifts uphill or into cover to bed (85%, quick lesson).
- Mule deer "tend to bed not far from a food source" (65%, Rinella).
- Midday: bedded, chewing cud; gets up to stretch, nibble and re bed (80%).
- Late afternoon: rises, browses near the bed, then moves to open feeding slopes (80%).
- Sunset to about 21:00: second peak (85%).
- Water: in dry fall weather near water at dusk or dawn; with green feed and dew, less need (Tip).
- Deer activity is highest within about 3 hours of sunrise and sunset (85%, Mule Deer Working Group fact sheet 17).
- Legal hunting hours: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (99%, page 11, see [legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)).
```

## Midday: the bedded deer

```diagram sa-bed-warm-cold
```

- Warm day: shade, north slopes, timber (80%).
- Cold day: sun, south and west slopes, out of wind (80%).

```more where it beds and why
- Day beds give hiding cover and shelter (80%, Germaine 2004).
- Warm days: north facing timber, benches, draws, shade (80%).
- Cold days: sunny south and west slopes out of the wind (80%).
- On a slope it often beds facing downhill with the wind at its back, on the leeward side of a roll (65%, Rinella).
- In a desert study, bed sites had the most overhead cover in summer (60%, Arizona 2001). Temperature and canopy decide summer beds (60%).
- Lore: bucks bed in the top third of a slope (60%, not a rule).
- Midday is the time to find beds with binoculars and plan a stalk (Tip, chapter 9).
```

## Wind, rain and snow

```diagram mmd-weather-lean
```

- Strong or shifting wind ruins stalks (65%).
- Most weather effects on deer are weakly tested (50 to 55%).

```more weather, one by one
- **Wind:** strong wind makes deer edgy and hard to approach (Tip, hunter lore 55%). Clouds and incoming weather kill a steady wind; back out and wait (65%, Rinella).
- **Rain:** light rain dampens your noise and scent; deer often feed through it (Tip). Heavy rain: bedded (Tip).
- **Snow:** fresh snow shows every track (Tip). Deep snow pushes deer down and limits movement (85%, chapter 5).
- **Cold:** cold, crisp mornings put rutting animals on their feet in daylight; warm wind hides them (70%, [the rut](#/s/the-rut)).
- **Heat:** warm October afternoons push deer into shade and timber (80%).
- **Cold fronts:** hunters say deer feed hard before a front and move after it (Tip, 50%). Not well tested for mule deer.
- In GPS studies of white tails, weather had an inconsistent effect within a season (70%, Webb and others 2010).
```

## Barometer and moon

```diagram mmd-evidence-meter
```

- Moon phase: GPS studies found no effect (70%).
- Barometer: no solid evidence (40%, Tip).

```more the evidence, honestly
- Moon: 32 GPS collared white tails in Oklahoma over 7 years. Moon phase had no effect on daily, night or day movement (70%, Webb and others 2010). Other GPS work found small or no moon effects (65%).
- Earlier studies that found moon effects used older radio collars (65%, Penn State).
- No mule deer moon study found. Treat moon tables as entertainment (Tip).
- Barometric pressure: one white tail study tied very high pressure to more winter buck movement, with caveats; others found little (50%, search previews). Not a fact for mule deer.
- What does move deer, with good evidence: light (dawn and dusk), snow and cold, the rut, hunting pressure (70 to 85%).
- My lean: hunt every day you can. Log weather and sightings in the Journal and find your own pattern (Tip).
```

## Hunting pressure and the day

```anim hf-pressure-shift Pressure pushes deer into thick cover.
```

- Pressured deer use thicker cover and more night (65 to 70%).
- Weekends and roads push deer away (Tip).

```more pressure in detail
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Pressured deer move more at night and use thicker cover (70%, quick lesson).
- Arizona: hunted deer shifted to better escape cover as the season went on (65%). Montana: more use of timber in hunting season (65%).
- Hunted populations flee people at longer distances (65%, Stankowich 2008).
- Roads and ATVs (all terrain vehicles) raise buck vulnerability (65%, Idaho Fish and Game).
- Off trail people and dogs disturb more than on trail (85%, fact sheet 17).
- For you: be in your seat before first light, stay through midday on busy weekends, and hunt the quiet weekdays if you can (Tip).
- Rule recap (study aid only): no ATV for hunting 1 September to 10 December in MUs (Management Units) 3-28, 3-29 and 3-30 (99%, page 33, [Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3)). Fewer quads, calmer deer (Tip).
```

## How the day shifts through the fall

| Period | Day pattern (certainty) | Your move (Tip) |
|---|---|---|
| September | Dawn and dusk peaks, beds high (80%) | Glass basins at first light |
| October | Same, bucks start roaming late month (70%) | Sit glassing seats dawn and dusk |
| November rut | Bucks move all day near does (65 to 75%) | Glass doe groups all day |
| December | Post rut, bucks hide and rest (90%) | Slow glassing of thick edges |

- Rut days are long days: stay out (65%, Rinella).
- Post rut bucks hide (90%).

```more why
- Peaks at dawn and dusk come from the Starkey study (85%) and agency advice (85%, fact sheet 17).
- Rut: bucks travel and show in daylight (75%, [the rut](#/s/the-rut)); "hunt all day" (65%, Rinella).
- Post rut: "they tend to hide and rest" (90%, BC account).
```

## Evidence ladder

| Claim | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Dawn and dusk peaks | Strong (85%) |
| Snow pushes deer down | Strong (85%) |
| Pressure shifts deer to cover | Good (65 to 70%) |
| Rut brings bucks out by day | Good (75%) |
| Feeding before a front | Lore (50%) |
| Barometer rules movement | Weak (40%) |
| Moon phase rules movement | Not supported (70% for no effect) |

- Plan on light, snow, rut and pressure. Log the rest (Tip).

```more how to read this
- Strong: several studies or agency sources agree.
- Good: one or two studies, or agency advice.
- Lore: widely told by hunters, not tested for mule deer.
- Weak or not supported: tested, little or no effect found, mostly in white tails.
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mmd-day-clock
```

- **Rhyme:** "Four to nine and five to nine; between them deer lie down in line."
- **Number trick:** 3 hours: peak activity within 3 hours of sunrise and sunset (85%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Be on the glass before the light."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Hot day, shady slope; cold day, sunny hope."
- **Number trick:** 04 to 09, 10 to 15, 17 to 21: active, bedded, active (80 to 85%).
- **Number trick:** 32 deer, 7 years, no moon effect (70%).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely taught, opinion): "Hunt the weather you have." Go every chance you get.
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Noon is for finding beds, not for napping."

> [!mistake]
> - Walking in at daylight. The deer were already up and leaving.
> - Planning around the moon.
> - Leaving at 10:00 on a cold rut day.

> [!field]
> Each trip, log: time of each deer seen, temperature, wind, cloud, moon. After 10 trips, sort by hour.
```
