> [!why]
> Geese run on light, food and safety. Learn their clock and you are hidden and quiet before the first bird leaves the roost.

```diagram mgs-day-clock
```

- Roost, feed, rest on water, feed, roost (80%).
- First birds leave about 15 min before sunrise in clear weather (80%).

```more what this chapter covers
- The daily loop and the flight line.
- How cloud, cold, storm, wind, moon and pressure change it.
- How sure we are about each claim.
- Most timing numbers come from one strong study: about 40,000 wintering Canada geese at Crab Orchard, Illinois, over two winters in the 1960s (80%, Raveling and others 1972). BC birds likely follow the same rules of light and cold (Tip, 70%).
```

## The daily loop

```anim sb-goose-day Watch the field at dusk. Be there at dawn.
```

- Feed a few hours morning and late afternoon, rest on water between (80%).
- Back to yesterday's field in the morning (65%).

```more the loop in full
- Winter geese feed in fields a few hours early morning and late afternoon, then rest on lakes or big rivers (80%, Hinterland Who's Who).
- The main morning flight starts at or just after sunrise; evening return between sunset and civil twilight in clear weather (80%, Raveling 1972).
- Individual families vary inside the flock pattern (80%).
- Geese often feed 12 hours a day or more when days are long (80%).
- Legal hours for geese: half an hour before sunrise to half an hour after sunset (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-migratory-birds)). Use the Legal light screen in Field Mode.
```

## The flight line

```anim mgs-flight-line Same roost, same line, same field.
```

- Undisturbed geese use the same roost and line day after day (Tip, 65%).
- The line is where you pass shoot, or where you scout from (Tip).

```more how to use it
- Watch the line two evenings in a row before you plan a hunt (Tip).
- Pass shooting: hide under the line, as close to the feeding field as you can (65%, Ducks Unlimited goose guide). See [Chapter 9](#/s/mc-geese-09).
- Never set up on the roost itself. Shooting a roost pushes the whole flock out (hunter wisdom, Tip).
```

## Weather changes the clock

```diagram mgs-weather-grid
```

- Cloud: later and longer in the fields (80%).
- Very cold: no dawn flight; feed in the afternoon (80%).

```more weather in full
- Overcast skies delayed the light level that starts the flight by about 15 minutes (80%, Raveling 1972).
- On cloudy days geese stayed longer in feeding areas (80%).
- Below 15°F (about minus 9°C) at sunrise there was usually no heavy morning flight; from minus 9 to minus 7°C (16 to 20°F) there was sometimes a partial flight (80%).
- Stormy weather: more flying through the day, and geese sometimes roosted in fields instead of on water (80%).
- Wind: geese land into it (80%). Hunters say they fly lower and decoy better on windy days (Tip).
- Rain and fog: hunters report later flights and lower birds (Tip, 60%).
```

## Cold fronts, barometer and migration

- A cold snap may move new birds in. Evidence is weak (Tip).
- Barometric pressure: no study found. Treat it as lore (VERIFY).

| Claim | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Daylight sets the migration window | Widely stated, general (Tip, 60%) |
| Cold fronts and north winds push migrants | Blog and hunter reports only (Tip) |
| A falling barometer makes geese feed hard | No study found (lore) |
| Frozen lakes push birds to open water | Fits Interior pattern (75%) |

```more what to do with weak evidence
- Ducks Unlimited migration reports show cold snaps shifting some flocks south while others keep moving their own way (60%).
- Log weather, wind and flocks in your [Journal](#/s/hunt-journal-guide). Your own notes for your fields beat any general rule (Tip).
- See the [Weather and moon](#/s/weather-moon) lesson for how to read a forecast.
```

## The moon

```diagram mgs-evidence-bars
```

- Full moon visible: geese come back to the roost later at dusk (80%).
- Night feeding under the moon: proven in barnacle geese, hunter report in Canada geese (Tip).

```more moon in full
- In the Illinois study, evening flights later than civil twilight usually happened when a full or nearly full moon was visible at sunset (80%).
- Barnacle geese in the Netherlands fed at night only around the full moon (75%, Wildfowl journal).
- Hunters report Canada geese feeding at night and showing up late or not at all the next morning after a bright full moon (Tip). Some say heavy cloud cancels it (Tip).
- Lean (opinion): after a bright full moon, plan an afternoon field hunt as well as a morning one.
```

## Hunting pressure

- Hunted geese stay, but move less and flush sooner when close (80%).
- Weekends bring more hunters (Tip).

| Day | What to expect (Tip unless shown) |
|---|---|
| Opening weekend | Many hunters, birds move around a lot |
| Weekdays | Quieter, birds settle |
| After shooting on the roost | Birds may leave the area |
| Hunt days in a study | Less flight and walking (80%) |

```more pressure in full
- Pennsylvania 2025: GPS geese on hunted Wildlife Management Areas flew less and walked less during hunting hours and on hunting days (80%).
- Geese left places where repeated disturbance kept them from feeding (70%, Illinois refuge work).
- MUs (Management Units) 3-27 and 3-28 near Heffley Creek had under 30 estimated goose hunters a year in the BC survey (99%, survey estimate). Light pressure there (Tip). MU 2-4 had 251 in 2024 (99%).
```

## Memory hooks

> [!tip] Memory rhymes (made for this app)
> - "Clear and bright, fly at first light. Grey and low, fifteen minutes slow."
> - "Below minus nine, they sleep in fine."
> - "Big moon at night, late birds in sight."

- Number trick: **15, 15, minus 9**. First birds 15 minutes before sunrise, cloud adds 15 minutes, below minus 9°C no dawn flight (80%).

> [!rule] Old hand's rule (tradition, opinion)
> Scout in the evening, hunt in the morning. The standard rule in goose guides, built on birds returning to yesterday's field.
