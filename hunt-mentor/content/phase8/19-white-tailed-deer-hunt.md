## Methods at a glance

| Method | Best when | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Stand or blind | Field edge, dawn, dusk | Wrong wind |
| Still hunt | Midday, thick bottoms | Bumping deer |
| Glass and stalk | Open slopes, fields | Little cover |
| Grunt and rattle | Late Oct to mid Nov | Overcalling |
| Partner push | Small brush patches | Safety |

- My lean: stand on a trail, add calls in the rut (Tip).

```more pros and cons in full
- **Stand or ground blind:** you are still, deer come to you, easy steady shot. Needs scouting, permission and patience (Tip).
- **Still hunting:** covers ground, works midday in thick cover. White tails in thick brush are hard to sneak up on (Tip).
- **Glass and stalk:** great for open fields and broken hills. Valley white tails are often too close to cover for long stalks (Tip).
- **Grunt and rattle:** can pull a buck close. One Texas study saw about 65% of bucks respond, best on cool, calm, overcast mornings (70%).
- **Partner push:** moves deer out of small cover. Highest safety risk. Never shoot toward your partner (Tip).
- Electronic or recorded calls: not allowed for deer (99%, synopsis, via [Legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)). A fake (non live) decoy is allowed (99%).

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.
```

## Scouting: map, then boots

```video
yt-escout-terrain | E scouting white tail terrain features.
```

- Map: find bedding cover beside food on land you can hunt (Tip).
- Boots: confirm fresh trails, rubs, scrapes (Tip).

```more scouting step by step
1. On the [Hunt Map](#/map): White tailed deer by month, Private land, Habitat zones (chapter 7).
2. Mark pinch points: creek crossings, brush necks, fence corners, the inside corner of a field (Tip, see [Funnels](#/s/funnels)).
3. Ask permission early, before the season (Tip, see [Asking for permission](#/s/landowner-permission)).
4. Walk the edges midday. Do not walk into bedding cover in season (Tip).
5. Note trails, fresh rubs and scrapes. Glass the field from far back at dusk (Tip).
6. Pick 2 stands for 2 different winds (Tip).
```

## Stand or ground blind

```steps Set up a stand in 4 steps
sg-stand-1 | Find the trail between bed and field.
sg-stand-2 | Check the wind for the hour you sit.
sg-stand-3 | Pick a spot with a rest and a backstop.
sg-stand-4 | Walk in from behind. Never cross the trail.
```

- Sit 50 to 150 m off the trail, downwind (Tip).
- Morning: closer to bedding. Evening: closer to food (Tip).

```more stand detail
- Morning stands sit between food and bedding, back from the field, to catch deer heading in (Tip).
- Evening stands sit on the trail inside cover where deer stage before the field (Tip).
- Use the [Hunt Map](#/map) Scent cone to check the stand for the forecast wind (Tip).
- Ground blind: brush it in a week ahead so deer get used to it (Tip).
- A safe backstop of solid earth behind every shooting lane (firearm safety basics, Tip).
- Over 100 m from any dwelling or occupied farm building (99%, synopsis page 10, via the [quick lesson](#/s/white-tailed-deer)).
```

## Still hunting the bottoms

```anim hf-step-cadence Step, step, stop. Look longer than you walk.
```

- Wind in your face, sun at your back (Tip).
- Look for parts: an ear, a leg, a horizontal back (Tip).

```more still hunting detail
- Walk a few steps, stop, look for a minute or more (Tip).
- Look ahead and to the sides through the brush, not at it (Tip).
- Best midday, on damp quiet ground, or in light rain or wind that covers sound (Tip).
- Slip along the edge of bedding cover, not through the middle (Tip).
- Expect close, quick shots. Know your target before the rifle comes up (Tip).
- More: [Still hunting](#/s/still-hunting).
```

## Glassing fields, then the stalk

```anim hf-glass-sweep Sweep the field edge in overlapping passes.
```

- Glass from far back, first and last light (Tip).
- Stalk using cover and wind, never the open field (Tip).

```more glassing and stalking detail
- Sit with the binoculars on a rest. Glass the field edges and corners, not the middle (Tip).
- Use the Hunt Map tool **What can I see from here** to pick a glassing spot (Tip).
- A deer feeding with its head down and tail flicking is relaxed: move when the head is down (Tip).
- Close the distance behind a hedgerow, ditch or rise, wind in your face (Tip).
- Stop at a range you can hit a 15 cm (6 in) plate 9 times out of 10 from your field rest (Tip, quick lesson).
- More: [Glassing](#/s/glassing).
```

## Grunting and rattling

```steps Rattle in a buck in 4 steps
mwt-rattle-1 | Caller in cover, shooter downwind.
mwt-rattle-2 | Start with a few soft grunts. Wait.
mwt-rattle-3 | Rattle 30 to 60 seconds.
mwt-rattle-4 | Wait 20 to 30 minutes. Watch downwind.
```

- Best late October to mid November (70%).
- Cool, calm, overcast mornings (70%).

```more calling detail
- Texas telemetry: about 65% of bucks responded to rattling; best mornings, cool, calm and overcast (70%).
- Bucks often circle downwind to check the fight with their nose. The shooter covers that side (Tip, hunter consensus).
- Soft grunts alone can bring a close buck. Loud, long rattling suits the seeking and chasing phases (Tip).
- The timings above are hunter practice, not tested numbers (Tip).
- Grunt calls are legal hand calls. Electronic or recorded calls are not allowed for deer (99%).
- Hunt with your brother: one rattles, one shoots. Agree where each of you sits and which way you can safely shoot (Tip).
```

## Hunting with your brother

```diagram mwt-partner-push
```

- One still hunts slowly through cover, one sits the exit (Tip).
- Agree on zones of fire. Never shoot toward each other (Tip).

```more partner detail
- Small patches of willow or brush can be worked with a slow walker and a stander (Tip).
- The stander sits where deer leave cover, often a corner or a brushy neck, crosswind so the deer do not wind him (Tip).
- The walker moves slowly. A pushed white tail often sneaks, not runs, and may circle back past the walker (Tip).
- Blaze orange on both of you, and a fixed plan for where each person is (Tip).
- Every shot: know your target and what is beyond it. A [[Zone of fire]] is the arc you may safely shoot (glossary).
- Two people on a farm: both need the landowner's permission (Tip).
```

## Decision on the day

```diagram mwt-day-decision
```

- Wind wrong? Go to plan B. Do not "just try it" (Tip).
- No fresh sign this week? Move (Tip).

```more decision detail
- The wind for the hour you sit matters more than the wind at the truck (Tip).
- If you bump deer walking in, note where and change your route next time (Tip).
- If deer come out of range at the same spot 2 evenings in a row, move your stand there (Tip).
- Midday: scout, rest, or still hunt. Do not wander through bedding cover (Tip).
- Log every sit in the Journal: wind, temperature, deer seen, time (Tip).
- Planner: [Plan a hunt](#/plan).
```

## Ethics and what a 40 year hunter does differently

```photo g-wt-buck-side
```

- Waits for the right shot. Passes the rest (Tip).
- Leaves a spot alone before it is burnt out (Tip).

```more the old hand's habits (opinion)
These are widely repeated hunter habits, labelled opinion, not measured facts.
- Scouts more than he hunts, and does most of it after the season, when sign is fresh and pressure does not matter (Tip).
- Hunts the wind first, the sign second, the spot third (Tip).
- Sits the same good stand only when the wind is right, and rests it for days (Tip).
- Gets in early and stays late (Tip).
- Shoots only broadside or quartering away, inside his proven range (Tip).
- Knows the land, the landowner and the neighbours. Brings meat back as thanks (Tip, from the quick lesson's Grandpa's rule).
- Takes does when the season and the herd call for it, and shares the meat (Tip).
- Writes everything down. After 40 years, the notebook is the real knowledge (Tip).
- More: [Grandpa's 50 rules](#/s/grandpas-rules) and [Hunter etiquette](#/s/etiquette).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mwt-rattle-4
```

- **Rhyme:** "Wind in your face and the trail in sight; sit before dawn and stay till night."
- **Number trick:** 30 to 60 seconds of rattle, 20 to 30 minutes of wait.
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Two stands for two winds, or you have no stand."

```more more hooks, mistakes and field drill
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, widely repeated): "The best stand is the one the deer do not know you use."
- **Rhyme:** "Grunt soft, rattle loud, watch downwind for the buck that's proud."

> [!mistake]
> - Hunting a stand with the wrong wind because it is the best stand.
> - Rattling in mid September and wondering why nothing came.
> - Walking the field edge in the evening to get to your stand.

> [!field]
> Before the season: set up 2 stands for 2 winds on one farm, with your brother. Walk both entry routes once in daylight.
```
