## Shot placement and range

```diagram vitals-deer
```

- Broadside: up the back of the front leg, one third up (85%).
- Your limit: the range where you hit a 15 cm plate 9 of 10 (Tip).

```diagram sa-308-range
```

```more placement and range in detail
- Broadside: up the back of the front leg, one third up the chest; both lungs and the top of the heart (85%).
- Quartering away: aim so the bullet exits the far shoulder (85%).
- Quartering to: not for your first season (Tip). Head, neck, running, skylined: no (Tip).
- Wait for the near front leg to step forward; it opens the chest (85%).
- Vital zone with margin: about 20 to 25 cm (8 to 10 in) (75%).
- Your .308 Winchester with a 150 to 165 grain expanding bullet is plenty for mule deer (Tip, quick lesson).
- Maximum range: where you hit a 15 cm (6 in) plate 9 of 10 from a field rest; for most beginners about 200 m (219 yd) (Tip).
- Mule deer often stop and look back: be on a rest and ready, but only shoot a standing deer you have identified and counted (65%; Tip).
```

## Reactions to the hit

```diagram sk-reactions
```

- Kick and hard low run: often heart or lungs (70%).
- Hunched, slow walk: often a gut hit (70%).

```more reactions in detail
- Keep the rifle on the deer until it is out of sight. Reload, safety on (Tip).
- Mule kick, then a hard low run: often heart or lungs (70%, National Deer Association via [blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)).
- Heart: rarely over about 50 m; both lungs: under about 100 m (85%, [shot placement](#/s/shot-placement)).
- Hunched back, short run, then a walk: often a gut hit (70%).
- Hard run with a limp: often a leg hit (70%).
- Listen for a crash. Fix the last spot you saw it: a tree, a rock (Tip).
- Drop a phone pin where you shot from and where it stood. Note the time (Tip).
```

## Wait, then track

```diagram sk-wait-chart
```

- Heart or lungs: wait 30 minutes (70 to 85%).
- Liver: 4 to 6 hours. Gut: 6 to 8 hours or overnight (70 to 85%).

```more waiting and tracking in detail
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Wait times are opinion from hunter education, not law (70 to 85%, [blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)).
- Mark the exact spot where it stood with flagging before you move (85%).
- Read the hit site: blood colour, bubbles, hair, stomach matter (70 to 85%):
  - Bright red, bubbly: lungs.
  - Dark red, no bubbles: liver.
  - Thin, green brown, smells: gut.
- Track slowly beside the trail; glass ahead (Tip, chapter 8).
- Lost blood: go back to the last sign and grid search (Tip).
- Hunting hours and lights still apply while you track; no shooting a wounded animal after hours (95%, [blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)).
- You must make a real effort to recover it. "Walking away after 10 minutes is not reasonable effort" (95%).
- A leashed dog may help track (99% leash rule, page 12; 90% my reading for tracking).
```

## Field care

```steps Gut a deer in 8 steps
sk-gut-1 | On its back, head uphill, gloves on.
sk-gut-2 | Cut around the anus and tie it off.
sk-gut-3 | Belly cut, blade edge up, two fingers guiding.
sk-gut-4 | Roll the guts out to one side.
sk-gut-5 | Cut the diaphragm, then the windpipe high.
sk-gut-6 | Pull it all out through the back.
sk-gut-7 | Drain, wipe dry, prop it open.
sk-gut-8 | Cool it in shade, off the ground.
```

- Notch your licence first, then the knife (99%).
- Do not cut the paunch; keep evidence of sex attached (Tip; 99%).

```more field care in detail
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Cancel (notch) your species licence immediately after the kill, before you handle the deer, following the steps on the licence (99%, page 11 item 1, via the [quick lesson](#/s/mule-deer)).
- Leave [[Evidence of sex]] naturally attached: the unskinned tail, plus a testicle or part of the penis for a buck (99%, page 15).
- In a 4 point season, the antlers must go with the licence, attached to part of the upper skull on the way home (99%, pages 15 and 34).
- Gutted or [[Gutless method]]: both work; the gutless way suits steep ground and a long pack (Tip, [field dressing](#/s/field-dressing)).
- Remember the body chapter: cut the diaphragm to reach the lungs and heart; the rumen is huge, keep it whole (Tip, chapter 2).
- Look at the liver, lungs and lymph nodes: spots, cysts or bad smells, ask a conservation officer or vet before eating (Tip).
- Region 3 asks hunters to submit deer heads for CWD (Chronic Wasting Disease) testing (95%, [Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3)).
```

## Meat care

```diagram sk-cooler
```

- Target: meat below 4°C as fast as you can (80%).
- Hang at 1 to 3°C for 7 to 14 days if you can (80%).

```more meat care in detail
- One target: meat below 4°C (40°F), fast (80%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife via [meat care](#/s/meat-care)).
- 4 to 60°C is the danger zone; bacteria can double every 20 minutes (80%, Penn State Extension).
- Open the body, prop it open, shade, air (Tip). Above 4°C: bags of ice or snow in the cavity (80%).
- Game bags, not plastic or tarps; tarps trap heat (Tip, [meat care](#/s/meat-care)).
- Ageing: 1 to 3°C for 7 to 14 days lets enzymes tenderise (80%). No cold room? Cut and freeze within a few days (Tip).
- Freezer: minus 18°C; game keeps about a year (80%).
- A warm October afternoon in the Thompson can stay above 10°C: bring ice (Tip).
```

## Yield and cooking

```diagram hf-meat-math
```

- Boneless meat: about a third of live weight (70%).
- Cook gently; 71°C is Canada's safety number for wild game (95%).

```more yield and cooking in detail
- Field dressed weight about 70% of live weight; boneless meat about 48% of field dressed, or about 34% of live weight (70%, University of Wyoming studies via New Mexico Wildlife).
- Average field dressed buck in that study: about 52 kg (114 lb), giving about 25 kg (55 lb) boneless meat (70%).
- Venison is lean; overcooked it turns grey and dry (80%, [cooking](#/s/cooking)).
- Food safety: cook all wild game to 71°C internal (95%, Canadian Food Inspection Agency). Many hunters eat loins pinker; that risk is theirs (Tip).
- Tender cuts (backstrap, tenderloin): hot and fast. Shoulder, neck, shank: braise 2.5 to 3 hours (Tip).
- A rutting buck: more braise and stew, less steak (Tip).
- Late season deer are lean (chapter 2): add fat when you grind (Tip).
```

## Rules recap

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

```diagram hf-deer-season-bars
```

- MUs (Management Units) 3-27 and 3-28: any buck in October; 4 point bucks 10 to 30 September and 1 November to 10 December (99%).
- Region 3: 1 mule deer; antlerless by LEH (Limited Entry Hunting) only (99%).

```regs deerR3
```

```more the rules recap, with links (no new rules)
- Seasons, MUs 3-27 and 3-28 (99%, page 34, 2 October 2026 edition): 10 to 30 September, 4 point bucks; 1 to 31 October, any buck; 1 November to 10 December, 4 point bucks. Full table: [Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3).
- [[4 point buck]]: at least 4 tines on one antler, not counting the brow tine; each tine at least 2.5 cm and longer than wide (99%, pages 3 and 4, [definitions](#/s/rb-definitions)).
- Region 3 deer: 3 in total, only 1 a mule deer; antlerless mule deer by LEH only (99%, page 33). One mule deer buck for Regions 3 to 8 combined (99%, page 14, [bag limits](#/s/rb-bag-limits)).
- Region 2 black tails: 2, one may be antlerless (99%, page 27, [Region 2](#/s/rb-region-2)).
- Region 8: mule deer 1, antlerless by LEH only (99%, page 67, [Region 8](#/s/rb-region-8)).
- Hunting hours: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset; no lights (99%, page 11, [legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)).
- Calls: no electronic or recorded calls for deer (99%, page 12). Dogs on a leash for deer (99%).
- Deer parts from outside BC may not be used for hunting (99%, page 12 item 51).
- After the kill: notch the licence, evidence of sex, antlers with the licence in 4 point seasons, carry the licence with the meat (99%, pages 11 and 15, [after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill)).
- ATV (all terrain vehicle) for hunting closed 1 September to 10 December in MUs 3-28, 3-29, 3-30 (99%, page 33).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mmd-shot-to-table
```

- **Rhyme:** "Watch it, mark it, wait it out; notch it, cool it, no doubt."
- **Number trick:** 30, 4, 71: wait 30 minutes on lungs; meat under 4°C; cook to 71°C (70 to 95%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "The real hunt starts after the shot."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Bubbles, lungs; dark, the liver; green and sour, wait for ever."
- **Number trick:** 70, 48, 34: field dressed 70% of live; boneless 48% of dressed, 34% of live (70%).
- **Number trick:** 1 to 3 and 7 to 14: degrees and days for ageing (80%).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely taught, opinion): "Mark it, then wait it."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Cold meat, clean meat, good meat."

> [!mistake]
> - Walking in 5 minutes after a gut hit.
> - Handling the deer before notching the licence.
> - Wrapping a warm carcass in a tarp.

> [!field]
> Before your hunt: put flagging tape, a headlamp with red light, game bags, a cooler with ice and your licence in one kit.
```
