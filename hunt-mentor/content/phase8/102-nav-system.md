## Box yourself in

```diagram mc-nav-box
```

- Before you go, find four big lines around your hunting area (Tip).
- Inside that box you can always walk out to a known line.

```more the four lines
- **Baseline:** a long line you can always reach by walking one general direction. Usually the road the truck is on (Tip).
- **Handrails:** line features you can follow without the compass: creeks, roads, fences, power lines, timber edges, ridges (85%, Little Book of Orienteering Techniques).
- **Backstop:** a big line you must never cross, which tells you that you went too far: a main ridge, a big river, a highway (Tip; orienteers call a line past the target a "catching feature", 85%).
- The box idea is common land navigation teaching, built on those orienteering terms (Tip).
- Example near Heffley Creek (Tip): the forest road you parked on as the baseline, a creek on one side, a cutblock edge on the other, the main ridge as the backstop. Check each on the [Hunt Map](#/map) before you go.
- Do not trust a line you have not seen on the map and on the ground. A road on the map can be gated, grown in or washed out (85%, BC Forest Safety Council via [Vehicles and roads](#/s/fsr-vehicles-radio)).
```

## Study the map at home: 4 steps

```steps Box in your hunt at the kitchen table
mc-nav-study-1 | Find the baseline and the truck.
mc-nav-study-2 | Name the handrails on each side.
mc-nav-study-3 | Name the backstop you never cross.
mc-nav-study-4 | Write the escape bearing on your hand.
```

- 10 minutes at home saves an hour on the hill (Tip).

```more the home study in full
1. Open the [Hunt Map](#/map), Topo basemap, and find where you will park (99%, app).
2. Turn on Slope steepness (Tools, Terrain) and note red slopes: 30 to 45 degrees, and cliffs over 45 (99%, app). Plan around them.
3. Draw your route with Tools, Draw and measure, Line or route. It shows distance and climb (99%, app).
4. Name the four box lines out loud and write them down (Tip).
5. Escape bearing: the rough bearing that always reaches the baseline. Road to the south: about 180 (Tip). Write it on the back of your hand or the map margin.
6. Save the area in Offline Maps (99%, app; see [Chapter 6](#/s/mc-nav-06)).
7. Put the plan in your trip plan ([Chapter 1](#/s/mc-nav-01)).
```

## Catching features and attack points

```diagram fn-nav-catch
```

- Catching feature: a big line just past your target. Hit it and you know you overshot (85%).
- Attack point: go big and easy, then go small and careful (85%).

```more the orienteer's tools, for hunters
- Catching feature: "features beyond the control to bounce back off" (85%, Little Book of Orienteering Techniques, via [Compass mastery](#/s/compass-mastery)).
- Attack point: a large or obvious feature near the target; the safest is where two handrails cross (85%).
- Collecting feature: something you pass on the way that tells you that you are on track, like a creek you cross (85%, same book, "collecting features").
- Hunter use (Tip): the glassing knob is the target; the saddle beside it is the attack point; the creek you cross on the way is the collecting feature; the main road below is the catching feature for the walk out.
- Planning each leg this way is the orienteer's CARE system: control, attack point, route, exit (75%, [Planning a route](#/s/route-planning)).
```

## Mark the truck before you leave it

```steps Pin the truck in 3 taps
fn-hm-wpt-1 | Press and hold the map. Or Tools, Waypoint.
fn-hm-wpt-2 | Pick an icon: Parking.
fn-hm-wpt-3 | Name it. Done. It saves by itself.
```

- Phone pin plus a paper note of the coordinates (Tip).
- Mark each junction you pass too (Tip).

```more the truck pin in full
- Tools, Waypoint, At my location, or press and hold the map, then Drop waypoint (99%, app).
- Icons include Parking, Camp, Glassing point, Gate, Water (99%, app). Use Parking for the truck.
- Each waypoint stores coordinates with a copy button and its elevation (99%, app).
- Stand still in the open for 30 seconds before you mark it, so the GPS (Global Positioning System) fix settles (Tip, [GPS, phone and battery](#/s/gps-phone-battery)).
- Write the coordinates on paper too, and on the trip plan (Tip).
- Old hands also take a bearing from the truck to the first landmark, and note it (Tip; Grandpa's rule in [Maps 2](#/s/maps-navigation)).
```

## Look back at every turn

```anim mc-nav-lookback Turn around at every fork. That is the view you will see on the way out.
```

- The way out looks different from the way in (Tip).
- Turn around at every fork, creek and change of direction.

```more looking back in full
- Look back at each junction and landmark; the way out looks different (Tip, [Lost and the survival kit](#/s/lost-survival-kit)).
- Name what you see on the return view: "dead snag on the left, rock pile on the right" (Tip).
- In snow, your own fresh tracks are a trail home, until wind or new snow fills them (Tip).
- Flagging tape on a branch at a key junction helps at night. Take it down on the way out (Tip; leave no trace habit).
```

## Check every 15 to 30 minutes

```diagram mc-nav-check-clock
```

- Set a rhythm: every 15 to 30 minutes, find yourself on the map (Tip).
- Shorter in timber, fog, snow and the dark (Tip).

```more why a rhythm works
- Small errors in "straight ahead" add up when there is no sun or landmark (75%, Max Planck Institute, 2009).
- One degree off is 17 m per kilometre; five degrees is 87 m (98%, maths, see [Chapter 4](#/s/mc-nav-04)).
- A rhythm catches the drift while it is still small (Tip).
- Each check (Tip): Where am I on the map? What did I just pass? Look back. How long until my turnaround time?
- "Thumb the map": keep your thumb on where you are and move it as you go (Tip, orienteering habit; see [Chapter 5](#/s/mc-nav-05)).
- The 15 to 30 minute range is my recommendation, not a published standard (Tip).
```

## Time and pace: know how far you went

```diagram mc-nav-speeds
```

- Off trail you cover about 4 km an hour; on a road about 5 (75%).
- Still hunting: a few hundred metres an hour (Tip).

```more time and pace in full
- Aitken: 5 km an hour on paths and roads, 4 km an hour elsewhere (75%, via [Planning a route](#/s/route-planning)).
- Tobler's hiking function, off path: about 3 km an hour on the flat (75%).
- Naismith: add 1 hour per 600 m of climb (75%).
- Note the time at the truck and at each checkpoint. Time times speed is a rough distance (Tip).
- Pace count: count each left foot over a measured 100 m, on flat, rough and uphill ground (75%, Little Book; see [Compass mastery](#/s/compass-mastery)). Ranger beads keep the count (Tip).
- Never pace alone without reading the map too (75%).
```

## The "stop when unsure" rule

```diagram mc-nav-unsure-flow
```

- The moment you are unsure, stop. Do not take one more step (Tip).
- Go back to your last sure point if you can see it or follow your track.

```more stopping early in full
- AdventureSmart's STOP: Stop, Think, Observe, Plan, for when you are lost, disoriented or injured (75%, via [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety)).
- North Shore Rescue: "Stay Calm if You're Lost: panicking makes safe decision making harder" and "wandering usually takes you farther from your last known point" (75%, read directly).
- Early, the fix is small: back 200 m to the creek fork you know (Tip).
- Late, the fix is big: every kilometre you wander grows the search area by the square (95%, geometry; [Lost](#/s/lost-survival-kit)).
- Full STOP routine and the stay or move decision: [Chapter 7](#/s/mc-nav-07).
```

## What a 40 year hunter does differently

| Beginner | Old hand (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Looks at the map at the truck | Studies it the night before |
| Follows his nose | Boxes the area in |
| Checks when worried | Checks on a rhythm |
| Walks on when unsure | Stops on the first doubt |
| Looks ahead only | Looks back at every turn |

- All lines are hunter and orienteering habits, labelled Tip.

```more where these come from
- Study at home: [Hunt Map like a pro](#/s/hunt-map-pro) Grandpa's rule, "play with it at the kitchen table" (opinion).
- Box and handrails: Little Book of Orienteering Techniques (85%).
- Check rhythm and look back: orienteering "thumb the map" habit (Tip).
- Stop on the first doubt: AdventureSmart STOP (75%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-nav-box
```

- **Rhyme:** "Road behind, ridge ahead, creek on the side: never lost, just misled."
- **Number trick:** 15 to 30. Check the map every 15 to 30 minutes (Tip).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, short form of the look back advice in [Lost](#/s/lost-survival-kit)): "Look back, or don't come back."

```more more hooks
- **BHB:** Baseline, Handrails, Backstop. Your box (Tip).
- **Number trick:** 4 and 5. Off trail 4 km an hour, road 5 (75%).
- **Rhyme:** "First doubt, full stop."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, from the Hunt Map lesson Grandpa's rule): "Learn the map at the kitchen table."

> [!mistake]
> - Leaving the truck with no pin and no escape bearing.
> - Never looking back on the way in.
> - Pushing on "a bit further" when unsure.

> [!field]
> Next scouting walk: name the four box lines before you leave the truck, set a 20 minute phone timer for map checks, and look back at every fork.
```
