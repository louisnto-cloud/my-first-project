> [!why]
> Quail find you with their eyes first and their ears second. A covey has many eyes and one lookout. Beat those and you get close.

```diagram mql-sense-ranks
```

- Eyes: the strongest sense. Ears: strong. Nose: weak (70 to 85%).
- A covey adds many eyes and a sentinel (70%).

```more how sure is this chapter
- Bird senses are well studied in general, less in quail. Where a number comes from the chicken or from birds in general, the screen says so (70 to 85%).
- How to beat each sense is hunter craft, labelled Tip.
```

## Eyes: a near full circle

```diagram mql-vision-field
```

- Eyes on the sides of the head see almost all the way around (70%).
- Only a narrow wedge in front is seen by both eyes (70%).

```more vision field in full
- In the chicken only about 26° straight ahead is seen by both eyes (binocular). The rest is seen by one eye at a time (70%, Poultry Hub).
- In most birds the binocular field in front is narrow, about 20° to 30° at most (65%, Martin 2007 review, search preview).
- A study of black grouse, a galliform cousin, found wide side fields and only a small blind area behind the head (65%, search preview).
- So a quail feeding with its head down still watches most of the sky and the ground around it (Tip, 65%).
- Hunter meaning: you cannot sneak up "behind" a quail the way you can behind a deer. Use cover and ground shape, not angles (Tip).
```

## Colour and motion

```photo g-quail-male-vernon Bold face colours: quail read colour and pattern well.
```

- Birds have four colour cone types, including ultraviolet (UV) (85%).
- Movement gives you away more than colour (Tip).

```more colour, ultraviolet and motion in full
- Most birds carry four colour cone types: ultraviolet or violet, short, medium and long wave. Humans have three (85%, Stoddard et al. 2020, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences).
- So a quail likely sees colours we cannot, including ultraviolet patterns (80%).
- Some hunters avoid clothes washed in brighteners that glow under ultraviolet light. No study on quail tests this: Tip only.
- Blaze orange: birds see colour, but safety comes first. Wear it in brush (Tip, from the quick lesson).
- Fowl are day active and need more light to see than some other birds (70%, Poultry Hub). Quail roost at dusk and leave the roost at first light (70%).
- Motion: a moving hunter on a skyline is the classic giveaway. Move when the sentinel is out of view, freeze when it looks (Tip).
```

## Ears

```diagram mql-hearing-band
```

- Birds hear best at about 1 to 4 kilohertz (kHz) (75%).
- Voices, boots on gravel and gun clicks fall right in that band (Tip).

```more hearing in full
- Across many bird species, hearing is most sensitive from about 1 to 4 kHz (kilohertz), with a drop above that; no ultrasound (75%, avian hearing reviews including Dooling 1978).
- Some songbirds are less sensitive than people in that band, so do not assume a quail hears better than you (70%).
- Quail live by sound: assembly calls, contact clucks and alarm calls keep the covey together (70%, Edgewood notes from Calkins et al.).
- Cooper's hawks are known to hunt quail by their calls (70%). Quail fall quiet when a hawk is about (Tip, 60%).
- Hunter meaning: talk in whispers, use hand signals, close truck doors softly, and do not rattle shells in a pocket (Tip).
```

## Nose

```diagram mql-sense-ranks
```

- Smell is not highly developed in fowl (70%).
- Wind still matters: for your sound and for a dog's nose (Tip).

```more smell in full
- In fowl the olfactory organ is not very highly developed, and smell seems secondary to other senses for finding food (70%, Poultry Hub).
- No source says quail detect hunters by scent. Treat scent control as low value for quail (Tip).
- Wind carries your sound. Walk into or across the wind so noise blows away from the cover ahead (Tip).
- A dog finds quail by scent. Hunt so the wind blows from the cover to the dog (Tip).
- Windy days: gray partridge shelter behind tree lines, fence rows, boulders and in lee draws (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife).
```

## The sentinel: eyes on a post

```anim mql-sentinel-alarm The cock on the post sees you before the covey does.
```

- While the covey feeds, one bird, usually a male, watches from a high perch (70%).
- He gives the alarm and the covey runs for cover (70%).

```more the sentinel in full
- "During foraging one individual (usually male) often acts as sentinel, sitting on a high perch and giving Contact and Aerial Alarm Calls when it observes danger." (70%, Edgewood notes from Calkins et al. 1999)
- The male acts as sentry throughout the year (70%, Birder's Handbook).
- In the breeding season the male watches while the female sits on the nest (70%).
- Look for him first: a fence post, a stump, a rock or the top of a shrub (Tip). See [the habitat tour](#/s/habitat-upland).
```

## How they combine the senses

```photo hab-quail-vernon A cock quail on a post: the covey's eyes.
```

- Many eyes, one lookout, and calls to share the news (70%).
- Their answer to danger: run first, fly last (70%).

```more combining in full
- A feeding covey watches in every direction at once. One alarm moves them all (70%).
- They feed close to cover. California records say they seldom go more than 15 to 30 m (50 to 100 ft) from cover to feed (80%).
- With no hawks about they may feed 100 m from cover; under raptor pressure, about 15 m (70%, Edgewood notes).
- First answer to danger: run into cover. They fly only as a last resort (70%).
- Hunter meaning: they rarely let you see the whole covey in the open. Expect to hear them and to see a few birds on the edge (Tip).
```

## How to beat each sense

| Sense | Their edge | Your answer (Tip) |
|---|---|---|
| Eyes | Near 360° view, sentinel | Use cover and ground, freeze when watched |
| Ears | 1 to 4 kHz (75%) | Whisper, soft steps, hand signals |
| Nose | Weak (70%) | Wind for your sound and your dog |
| Covey | Many eyes, alarm call | Close fast at the end of cover |

- Beat the eyes first, then the ears (Tip).

```more the plan in full
- Approach with the sun behind you if you can: the bird looks into the light (Tip).
- Stop often. Stopping makes nervous birds flush (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife). See chapter 9.
- The end of the cover is where they must fly. Be ready there (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mql-vision-field
```

- **Rhyme:** "Eyes all round and ears to match; move slow and low to make the catch." (Tip)
- **Number trick:** 4 cones, 1 to 4 kHz, 15 to 30 m from cover (80 to 85%).

```more more hooks
- **Post first:** "Find the post, find the ghost." Look for the sentinel before you look for the covey (Tip).
- **Old hand's rule (opinion):** "Whisper in quail country." Hunting writers stress quiet: listen for the covey before it hears you (Tip; Missouri Conservationist 1996).
```
