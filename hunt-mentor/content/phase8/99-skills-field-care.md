## The first five minutes

```diagram mc-skills-first-five
```

- Approach from behind, rifle ready. Touch the eye with a stick: no blink (Tip).
- Cancel the licence before you handle it (99%).

```more first minutes in detail
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Approach from above and behind, watch the chest for breathing, touch the open eye with a stick or the muzzle tip held safely: a blink means it is alive (Tip, hunter consensus).
- Whoever kills big game must cancel their own species licence immediately, before handling it (99%, synopsis page 11, item 1, via [Hunting with a partner](#/s/hunting-partner)).
- Photograph the cancelled licence beside the animal (95%, [Field dressing](#/s/field-dressing)).
- Plan which piece keeps the [[Evidence of sex]] before any cut (95%). See the Rule Book screen below.
- Unload the rifle and set it where it will not fall or get blood in the action (70%, BC Outdoors Magazine).
- Bear country: bear spray on your belt, partner on watch (70 to 85%).
- Nitrile gloves: provincial advice for disease safety (95%, synopsis page 18).
```

## Field dressing: guts out

```steps Gut a deer in 8 steps
sk-gut-1 | On its back, head uphill, gloves on.
sk-gut-2 | Cut around the anus and tie it off.
sk-gut-3 | Belly cut, blade edge up, two fingers guiding.
sk-gut-4 | Roll the guts out to one side.
sk-gut-5 | Cut the diaphragm, then the windpipe high.
sk-gut-6 | Pull it all out through the back.
sk-gut-7 | Drain, wipe dry, prop it open.
sk-gut-8 | Cool it in shade, off the ground.
```

- Blade edge up, fingers guiding: never into the paunch (80%).
- Gut contents on meat: wash that spot, dry it at once. Otherwise do not wash (80%).

```more gutting in detail
- Penn State Extension: free the rectum about 10 cm in and tie it off (80%).
- No need to saw the pelvis or breastbone on a deer (80%, North Dakota State University Extension).
- Save the heart and liver in a zip bag on ice if you want them (80%).
- Never leave the guts in overnight: it "will cause spoilage regardless of outside temperature" (80%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game).
- Water removes the dry glaze that slows bacteria, so wash only contaminated spots (80%).
- Full detail and photos: [Field dressing](#/s/field-dressing).
```

## The gutless method

```steps Gutless in 8 steps
sk-gutless-1 | Cut around the knees, legs and belly line.
sk-gutless-2 | Skin the top half back. Hide is your mat.
sk-gutless-3 | Front quarter off through the armpit.
sk-gutless-4 | Hind quarter off at the hip joint.
sk-gutless-5 | Backstrap off along the spine.
sk-gutless-6 | Neck, rib meat and flank.
sk-gutless-7 | Tenderloin from inside, behind the last rib.
sk-gutless-8 | Bag every piece. Flip and repeat.
```

- Meat off, guts stay in. Best far from the truck, and for any moose or elk (Tip).
- Legs come off at the joints. No saw needed (80%).

```more gutless in detail
- Alaska Department of Fish and Game gives the full sequence: hide back, front quarter through the armpit, hind quarter at the hip ball joint, backstrap, ribs and flank, tenderloin, neck (80%).
- The gutless method does not excuse you from the neck, ribs and loins: every edible portion comes out (95%).
- Head for CWD (Chronic Wasting Disease) testing: whole head with about 8 cm of neck (95%, BC CWD page via [Field dressing](#/s/field-dressing)).
- Provincial safe handling: "bone out the meat", "don't saw through bone if you can avoid it", avoid brain and spinal cord (95%, synopsis page 18).
```

```more the edible portions
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- [[Edible portions]] for big game: "the edible portions of the neck, ribs, four quarters and the loins of the animal" (95%, page 3).
- Birds: "the edible portions of both breasts of the bird" (95%, page 3).
- Bone in quarters stay cleaner; boned out meat weighs about half as much. Both are legal if the edible portions all come out (95%, [Field dressing](#/s/field-dressing)).
- Lean in the table: from [Field dressing](#/s/field-dressing) (Tip).
```

## Cool it fast

```diagram mc-skills-temp-ladder
```

- Danger zone 4 to 60°C: bacteria can double every 20 minutes (80%).
- Below 4°C, same day if you can (80%).

```more cooling in detail
- Four enemies: heat, moisture, dirt, gut contents (80%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game).
- The cavity is the hottest part and stays hot: guts out or meat off within the hour (80%).
- Air must move: prop the chest open, hang it or lay it on logs, in shade (80%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife).
- Above freezing: skin elk, moose and bear soon; the hide and fat hold heat (80%).
- Aging, if you age: 1 to 3°C for 7 to 14 days, no longer than two weeks (80%). Do not age a young animal, one shot in heat and not chilled fast, one run hard, or a dirty carcass (80%).
- Freezer minus 18°C; game keeps about a year (80%). Cook wild game to at least 71°C (95%, Canadian Food Inspection Agency); bear always, because freezing does not kill wild Trichinella (95%).
```

## Warm weather: September rules

```steps Warm day in 5 steps
mc-skills-warm-1 | Guts out or meat off at once.
mc-skills-warm-2 | Skin it. Hide off lets heat out.
mc-skills-warm-3 | Bags of ice or snow in the cavity.
mc-skills-warm-4 | Shade and moving air. Never plastic.
mc-skills-warm-5 | Cooler or cold room the same day.
```

- Above 4°C: bagged ice in the cavity, tied shut (80%).
- No plastic, no tarp on warm meat: "Plastic will hold heat in" (80%).

```more warm weather in detail
- Penn State: bags of ice in the chest cavity if the air is above about 4 to 5°C (80%).
- Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife: to a cooler on the day of the kill (80%).
- Never a tarp or a closed truck canopy on a warm carcass; open air on the drive (80%).
- Flies: game bags, plus black pepper or food grade citric acid on the surface (80%). A dry crust "deters egg laying insects" (80%, Alaska).
- Rain: a tarp as a roof above, with an air gap. Never wrapped (80%).
- A cheap thermometer inside the meat: under 4°C or add ice (80%).
- September bow and early rifle seasons in the Interior can be warm. Plan the cooler and ice before the hunt (Tip).
```

## Game bags and the pack out

```diagram mc-skills-pack-loads
```

- Cloth game bags, one per quarter. Never plastic (80%).
- Many trips, light loads. Every edible portion comes out (95%).

```more packing out in detail
- Game bags: about 4 for a deer, 8 for a moose (80%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game).
- Hang bags apart, never stacked, never inside garbage bags (80%).
- Deer meat yield: about 48% of field dressed weight is boneless meat (70 to 80%, Ohio DNR (Department of Natural Resources) and National Deer Association).
- "A load that blocks your view or pulls you off balance is too heavy" (70%, BC Outdoors Magazine). A starting point many use is 20 to 30 kg trips (Tip).
- Heavy meat high and close to your back (Tip, [Packing out](#/s/packing-out)).
- Coming back to a meat cache: wind in your face and make noise. Bears claim meat (70%).
- ATV (All Terrain Vehicle): no live round in the chamber or attached magazine, and check motor vehicle closures for your MU (Management Unit) (95 to 99%, [Packing out](#/s/packing-out)).
- The licence travels with the meat. Your brother carrying your deer needs your Record of Receipt (95%).
```

## Evidence of sex and the rules

```diagram sc-evidence-grid
```

- Leave the evidence of sex naturally attached, as the Rule Book table says (99%).
- No new rules here: read the Rule Book screens (99%).

```more where to read each rule
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Evidence of sex, every species group: [Rule Book: after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill) (99%, page 15).
- Deer: the unskinned tail, and a testicle or part of the penis, or part of the udder and teats, stays naturally attached (99%, page 15). Cut around them, never through them (95%).
- Grouse and quail: one feathered wing. Ducks and geese: one feathered wing or the feathered head (99%, page 15).
- Licence cancelling, transport and the Record of Receipt: [Rule Book: bag limits and tags](#/s/rb-bag-limits) and [Tagging](#/s/tagging-legal).
- Gut piles: not within town boundaries or near trails or campsites; a carcass is an attractant (95%, pages 9 and 15). Bear: gall bladder rule on page 12 (95%).
- CWD (Chronic Wasting Disease) carcass rules: [Rule Book: after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill) and [CWD](#/s/cwd).
```

## What a 40 year hunter does differently

| Beginner | Old hand (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Admires it for an hour | Licence, photo, knife in 10 minutes |
| Plastic bags | Cloth bags, hung apart |
| Washes the whole carcass | Wipes dry, washes only dirty spots |
| One giant load | Many light trips |
| Leaves the cooler at home | Ice and cooler in the truck |

- All lines are hunter wisdom, labelled Tip.

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-skills-temp-ladder
```

- **Rhyme:** "Clean, cool, dry, and it won't go bad on the fly."
- **Number trick:** 4, 60, 71: below 4°C to keep, danger to 60°C, cook to 71°C (80 to 95%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, Alaska Department of Fish and Game's three words): "Clean, cool and dry."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Plastic holds heat, cloth lets it breathe" (80%).
- **Number trick:** 1 hour: guts out or meat off. 1 day: in a cooler (80%).
- **Number trick:** 4 bags a deer, 8 a moose (80%).
- **Number trick:** 48%: boneless meat from a field dressed deer (70 to 80%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, from [Field dressing](#/s/field-dressing)): "Cut around the evidence, never through it."

> [!mistake]
> - Warm quarters wrapped in a tarp.
> - Cutting off the tail or the evidence of sex.
> - Leaving the guts in overnight to come back.

> [!field]
> Before the season, pack the field care kit: knife and sharpener, nitrile gloves, 4 game bags, zip ties, flagging, paper towel, a small saw, and a cooler with ice in the truck.
```
