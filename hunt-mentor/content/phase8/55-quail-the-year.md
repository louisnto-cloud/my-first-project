> [!why]
> Quail do not migrate. The same covey lives in the same draw all year, but its size, its calls and its habits change by the month. Know the month, know the bird.

```diagram mql-year-wheel
```

- Coveys in fall and winter, pairs in spring, broods in summer (70 to 80%).
- Season: 1 October to 30 November in Region 8 (99%).

```more how sure is this chapter
- Most timing data come from California studies (80%). BC birds live farther north and dates may run later: VERIFY against BC records.
- The BC atlas and the app's migration data agree: no migration, the same low country all year (60 to 85%).
```

## January and February: winter coveys

```diagram mql-covey-size
```

- Big coveys near farms, orchards and thick brush (75%).
- Hard winters are the main killer (60%).

```more winter in full
- Winter coveys, sometimes very large, near farms, orchards and suburbs with thick brush; same small range all winter (75%, app migration data).
- In winter they form even bigger coveys, sometimes hundreds of birds (60%, Okanagan naturalist in iNFOnews).
- California winter home range of four coveys averaged 10.5 ha (26 acres), from 6.9 to 18 ha (80%).
- Winter movements cover about twice the area used in summer (80%).
- Snow: Tip, 60%, from the quick lesson: snow after a cold night pushes coveys to sunny slopes and bare ground, closer to houses.
- No season now. Scout only: drive back roads, listen, note coveys in the Journal (Tip).
```

## March to May: break up and pair

```diagram mql-nest-timeline
```

- Coveys break up in March. Pairing takes about 2 months (70%).
- Unpaired cocks give the single "cow" call from a high perch (70%).

```more spring in full
- "Approximately 2 months elapse between covey breakup in March and complete segregation of birds into pair bonds." (70%, Edgewood notes)
- Pairs form from March to early May (80%, California habitat records).
- Spring dispersal: a covey may spread up to 8 km (5 mi) (80%). App data: some birds disperse up to 8 km in April (65%).
- Males fight with the "squill" call while they sort out rank (70%).
- Courtship: the male droops his wings, spreads his tail, bobs his head and may rush at the female; courtship feeding by both sexes (70 to 75%).
```

## May to July: nest and hatch

```diagram mql-nest-timeline
```

- About 14 eggs. The hen sits 22 to 23 days (80%).
- Chicks run at once and fly short hops at about 10 days (75%).

```more nesting in full
- Nests April to August, peak May and June; most hatch in June (80%, California habitat records).
- Clutch averages 14 (range 6 to 16, California records) (80%). Audubon: 1 to 21, usually 13 to 14 (75%). Two hens sometimes lay in one nest (75%).
- Laying: about 3 eggs every 4 days; incubation starts when the clutch is complete (70%, Edgewood notes).
- Nest: a shallow scrape lined with grass and leaves, under a shrub, brush pile or log (75%).
- The male stands sentinel while the female incubates (70%).
- Young leave the nest within a day, follow the parents and feed themselves (75%). The hen broods them at night for the first two weeks; they cannot hold their own heat yet (70%).
- One brood a year, two in good years (75%). In BC, one or possibly two clutches is more likely (65%, BC Outdoors).
- Rain drives the hatch: high numbers follow wet winters with good green growth (80%).
```

## August and September: families join

```photo g-quail-female A hen with a chick. By late summer, broods join into coveys.
```

- Family groups join into coveys (60 to 70%).
- Coveys of 20 or more by September (65%).

```more late summer in full
- August: family groups join into coveys (60%, app migration data).
- September: coveys of 20 or more in brushy draws and farm edges (65%).
- Broods use only a few acres in the first 2 weeks and 4 to 8 ha by one month (80%, California habitat records).
- Moult: young birds replace most feathers through late summer but keep buff tipped coverts into fall (70%, 1963 Condor aging study, search preview). Exact moult months for BC birds: VERIFY.
- Watch broods in August from the road to guess the fall crop (Tip). Count chicks per hen and write it down.
```

## October and November: the season

```diagram mql-octnov
```

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Region 8 quail: 1 October to 30 November, 10 a day, 30 in possession (99%).
- Late October to mid November: leaves down, coveys formed (Tip).

```more October and November in full
- Rule Book, Region 8, page 68: "QUAIL", MUs (Management Units) 8-1 to 8-15 and 8-21 to 8-26, "Oct 1 - Nov 30", "10 (30)" (99%). See the [Region 8 Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-8).
- October: fall coveys in brushy draws, orchard and vineyard edges, riparian thickets (70%, app migration data).
- November: coveys on winter range: brush, farm edges, riparian thickets (70%).
- "Best window: late October to mid November. Leaves are down, coveys are formed, and the season runs to 30 November." (Tip, from the [Okanagan trip plan](#/s/local-okanagan))
- Early October: thick leaves, warm days, fresh birds that hold. Late November: thin cover, cold, birds wiser after flushes (Tip).
- Orchards and vineyards: harvest crews may be working in October. Ask where people are today (Tip, from the quick lesson).
```

## December: back to winter

```diagram mql-year-wheel
```

- Season closed after 30 November (99%).
- Coveys settle on winter range near cover and food (70%).

```more December in full
- The Region 8 quail season ends 30 November (99%).
- December: winter coveys in thick cover near feed (75%, app migration data).
- Use December to clean the Journal: where each covey was, how many birds, what cover (Tip).
```

## Chukar and Hun year, compared

| Month | Chukar (FEIS 80%) | Gray partridge (Audubon 75%) |
|---|---|---|
| Feb to Apr | Pairs form | Coveys break into pairs |
| Apr to Jun | Eggs, about 15, 24 days | Eggs 12 to 18, about 25 days |
| Summer | Broods near water | Chicks eat insects |
| Fall | Coveys of about 20 | Young stay with parents |
| Winter | Move down in deep snow | Burrow in snow for seeds |

- Both: 1 October to 30 November in Regions 3 and 8 (99%).

```more chukar and Hun year in full
- Chukar pair from February to March or April. Egg laying averages about 20 April in Washington. Clutch 10 to 20, average 15; incubation typically 24 days; flights by about 2 weeks (80%, FEIS).
- Chukar coveys form from one or more broods; males often rejoin the brood in early fall (80%). Coveys about 20, sometimes 40 or more (80%).
- Chukar move from higher ground to lower terrain in heavy snow, and onto south facing slopes in bad weather (80%).
- Drought cuts chukar clutch size, and in extreme drought there may be no breeding (80%).
- Gray partridge: 12 to 18 eggs, incubation 21 to 26 days, usually 25; young fly short hops under 2 weeks and stay with the parents through the first winter (75%, Audubon).
- Rules: chukar "5 (15)", gray partridge "3 (9)", both 1 October to 30 November in Regions 3 and 8 (99%, Rule Book).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mql-nest-timeline
```

- **Rhyme:** "March they part, May they lay, June they hatch, fall they stay." (Tip, 70 to 80% for the months)
- **Number trick:** "14, 23, 10": about 14 eggs, 23 days on the nest, chicks fly at 10 days (75 to 80%).

```more more hooks
- **Season hook:** "October first to November's end: ten a day, thirty to tend." (99% for the rule)
- **Old hand's rule (opinion):** "Wet spring, full covey." California data back it: rain and green growth drive the hatch (80%).
```
