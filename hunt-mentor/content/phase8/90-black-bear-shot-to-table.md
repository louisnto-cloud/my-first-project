## Shot placement and range

```diagram vitals-bear
```

- Halfway up, tight behind the shoulder, near leg forward (85%).
- No head, shoulder or quartering to shots (85%; Tip).

```diagram sa-308-range
```

```more placement and range in detail
- Everyone agrees: halfway up the body, tight behind the shoulder once the near leg steps forward, through both lungs; vital zone about a 20 cm (8 in) circle (85%, [shot placement](#/s/shot-placement)).
- Hair adds 8 to 10 cm and fat 2.5 to 5 cm: the bear looks bigger and the chest lower than it is (70%).
- Head shots: the skull is dense and sloped, bullets glance off (85%). Shoulder shots: heavy bone, a hit ahead of the shoulder misses the vitals (85%).
- Your .308 Winchester with a 150 to 180 grain expanding bullet is plenty; keep shots inside 150 m (164 yd) until you have done it once (Tip, quick lesson).
- Legal methods for black bear: centrefire; shotgun 20 gauge or larger with No. 1 Buck or larger; air rifle .35 or larger; bow class A, C or D; no rimfire (99%, page 13, [legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)).
```

## Reactions, waiting and the approach

```diagram sk-wait-chart
```

- Bears leave thin blood trails, even on good hits (85%).
- Double lung bear: usually dies within sight or sound (70%).

```diagram sk-reactions
```

```more after the shot in detail
- Mark exactly where the bear stood and where it went out of sight; listen (85%, [shot placement](#/s/shot-placement)).
- Wait times from hunter education (70 to 85%): heart or lungs 30 minutes; liver 4 to 6 hours; gut 6 to 8 hours or overnight. The reaction drawing shows a deer; bears react in similar ways (Tip).
- Bright red, frothy blood: lungs. Dark red: liver. Thin, green brown: gut (70 to 85%).
- Approach from above and behind with the rifle ready; a bear with open, still eyes is dead (Tip, hunter education).
- Hunting hours still apply while you follow up; no lights to hunt (99%, page 11).
- Wounded black bear: a guide outfitter, a youth's supervisor or a permit to accompany holder may track and dispatch it for the hunter they supervise; that hunter still cancels the licence (99%, page 11, [unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts)).
- Track a wounded bear with a partner, side by side, spray in reach. Not alone into thick brush at dark (Tip, [blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)).
```

## At the bear: the rules in order

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

```diagram mc-black-bear-kill-rules
```

- Cancel the licence before you handle the bear (99%).
- You must take the meat home (99%).

```more the rules at the bear, with links (no new rules)
- Item 1, page 11: cancel the species licence "immediately after the kill and before handling" (99%, [bag limits](#/s/rb-bag-limits)).
- Item 44: the edible portions rule applies to black bear: neck, ribs, four quarters and loins go home or to a meat cutter or cold storage (99%, [unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts)). Bear meat is not optional.
- Item 48: no possessing or trafficking bear galls or bile, or male genitalia separated from the hide and carcass; you may remove the gall and leave it at the kill site, or dispose of the gut pile with the gall within 48 hours if you moved the bear whole (99%).
- Item 49: no trafficking bear paws separated from the carcass or hide; your own for personal use is fine (99%).
- Evidence of sex: page 15 lists no rule for black bear (99%, [after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill)).
- Compulsory inspection for black bear: only MUs (Management Units) 6-12 and 6-13 (99%).
- Out of Canada: a CITES (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species) export permit for any bear or part (99%, page 16).
- Gut piles and carcasses: never where people are or are likely to be, where bears or cougars can reach them (99%, page 15).
- Ear tag or collar: legal to hunt unless the region says no; if you kill one, call the local Ministry office with the number before eating the meat, and return the tag or collar (99%, page 7, [unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts)).
```

## Skin it and save the hide

```steps Bear hide in 4 steps
mc-black-bear-skin-1 | Skin now. Rug cuts down legs and belly.
mc-black-bear-skin-2 | Skin to the last toe joint. Claws stay on.
mc-black-bear-skin-3 | Turn ears, split lips and eyelids.
mc-black-bear-skin-4 | Flesh, salt, fold. Never plastic.
```

- Skin right away: heat ruins hide and meat (85%).
- Not sure? Leave head and paws in, get it to a taxidermist fast (85%).

```more hide care in detail
- Alaska Department of Fish and Game, Bear hides: skinning and field care (85%):
  - "Skin bear immediately to dissipate heat from hide and meat."
  - Keep the hide cool, clean and dry; a tarp under the bear while skinning.
  - "NEVER STORE A HIDE IN PLASTIC!!" Use game bags or burlap.
  - Ears turned inside out; lips and eyelids split; nose cartilage trimmed to about a 2.5 cm (1 in) stump; salt worked into face and paws.
  - Flesh off all fat and meat; fat stops salt reaching the skin.
  - An average black bear hide takes about 4.5 to 7 kg (10 to 15 lb) of non iodized salt; hides can slip within one day in the wrong conditions; do not freeze a salted hide.
  - Wet hide: hang it nose up, tail down. Warm days, cool nights: spread it at night, roll it skin to skin in the morning.
  - Freezing an unsalted hide: spread it flat first; a rolled hide stays warm inside for days.
- Maryland Department of Natural Resources: 7 to 9 kg (15 to 20 lb) of salt; never store or transport a bear hide in plastic (75%).
- Many taxidermists prefer to skin the head and paws themselves: call yours before the hunt (75%).
- Full lesson: [trophy care](#/s/trophy-care).
```

## The skull

```diagram mc-black-bear-skull
```

- Score = length + width of the cleaned skull (75%).
- Dry 60 days before official scoring (75%).

```more skull care in detail
- Boone and Crockett: the skull score is length plus width, lower jaw off, in inches and sixteenths (75%, search preview).
- 60 day drying period before an entry score; for a frozen or boiled skull, it starts when the skull comes out (75%, Pope and Young and Pennsylvania guidance, search preview).
- Minimums for black bear: about 20 inches for Boone and Crockett awards and 21 for the all time book (70%, search preview).
- "No guide or hunter can accurately estimate" skull size in the field (65%, Boone and Crockett, Jim Shockey).
- Keep the skull with the hide if a taxidermist will do a mount; cool it like meat (Tip).
- CITES (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species) applies to skulls taken out of Canada (99%, page 16).
```

## Meat care

```diagram sk-cooler
```

- Skin, quarter, cool: fat and hide trap heat (75%).
- Game bags, not plastic. Ice in all weather (75%).

```more meat care in detail
- Maryland Department of Natural Resources (75%): remove the hide as soon as possible because fat and hide insulate the meat; quarter the bear if there is a delay; pack ice in the body or around the quarters, "advisable in all weather and essential above freezing"; protect meat with cloth, not plastic; keep it dry; trim fat before cooking or wrapping.
- Target: meat below 4°C (40°F) as fast as you can (80%, [meat care](#/s/meat-care)).
- A bear shot on a warm October afternoon and left whole can spoil by morning (Tip, quick lesson).
- Page 18 safe meat habits: no sick looking animals, gloves, bone out, wash up after (99%, [after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill)).
- Fat: render fall fat into lard if the bear ate berries; fish fed bear fat tastes fishy (Tip, hunting media).
```

## Trichinella and cooking

```diagram mc-black-bear-trich
```

- Cook every cut to 71°C (160°F) inside; use a thermometer (85%).
- Freezing does not make bear meat safe (85%).

```diagram sa-trich-temp
```

```more Trichinella, cooking and yield in detail
- CDC (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention): cook wild game to 71°C (160°F); bear meat is dark, so colour is not a reliable doneness sign (85%).
- Trichinella nativa, the northern species in bears, survives freezing for months or years (85%, CDC MMWR (Morbidity and Mortality Weekly Report)).
- Most Canadian trichinellosis outbreaks are T. nativa (75%, CMAJ (Canadian Medical Association Journal) 2007).
- Victoria, BC, 2005: black bear meat frozen at least 3 days, then barbecued, fried or stewed; 26 probable cases among 42 people who ate it; the bear was shot near Port Renfrew (75%, CMAJ 2007, search preview).
- Canadian human cases since 1980: nearly all from raw or undercooked black bear or walrus meat (75%, CMAJ 2007 summary).
- 2022: bear meat from northern Saskatchewan, frozen 45 days, made six people sick in three US states; larvae still alive after more than 15 weeks frozen (75%, Food Safety News citing CDC).
- Maryland: cook to 160°F and hold at least 3 minutes; no pink meat or juice, especially near bones (75%).
- Bear meat can also carry Toxoplasma (75%, Maryland).
- No rare steaks, no undercooked sausage, no tasting raw grind (Tip, quick lesson).
- Yield: a 90 kg bear gives roughly 30 to 40 kg of boneless meat once the fat is trimmed (Tip, assumption, quick lesson).
- Flavour: spring bears are lean and mild; fall berry bears are fat and good; fish eating bears can taste fishy (Tip, hunting media).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-black-bear-trich
```

- **Rhyme:** "Notch it, skin it, cool it fast; cook it through, the worms won't last."
- **Number trick:** 30, 4, 71: wait 30 minutes on lungs; meat under 4°C; cook to 71°C (70 to 85%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, the quick lesson): "Bear meat is good meat, cooked well done."

```more more hooks
- **Number trick:** 10 to 15 lb (4.5 to 7 kg) of salt for a black bear hide (85%).
- **Number trick:** 48 hours: dispose of a moved gut pile with the gall (99%).
- **Number trick:** 60 days: skull drying before scoring (75%).
- **Rhyme:** "Plastic slips the hair; burlap lets it breathe."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, echoing Alaska agency advice): "A slipped hide never grows hair back."

> [!mistake]
> - Leaving a fall bear whole overnight.
> - Cooking bear by colour, or trusting the freezer.
> - Taking the hide and leaving meat: an offence (99%).

> [!field]
> Before your bear hunt: pack a meat thermometer, game bags, a tarp, 7 kg of non iodized salt in the truck, and your taxidermist's number.
```
