## Range it: three ways

```steps Laser rangefinder in 4 steps
ff-jd-lrf-1 | Brace it like a rifle.
ff-jd-lrf-2 | Box on the shoulder, not the edge.
ff-jd-lrf-3 | Three readings. Ignore the one that jumps.
ff-jd-lrf-4 | Read the angle or flat distance.
```

- Laser first, reticle second, eye third (70%).
- Deer range is about half the number on the box (65%).

```more the three tools
| Tool | Good to | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Laser rangefinder | Hundreds of metres on deer (65%) | Fast, exact | Battery, brush, rain, fog |
| Scope reticle | About 400 m with practice (Tip) | Always with you | Needs a known size, steady hold |
| Eye methods | To about 400 m (80%) | Free, silent | Easy to fool |

- Vortex: reticle ranging "provides a good back up should your laser rangefinder fail" (70%, via [Judging distance](#/s/judging-distance)).
- The big number on a rangefinder box is usually the reflective range on a bright flat target; deer range is about half (65%).
- Brush in the beam gives a short reading; a missed beam hits the hill behind (Tip).
```

## Reticle ranging

```diagram mc-skills-mil-range
```

- Metres = size in metres × 1,000 ÷ mils (85%).
- Second focal plane marks are true at one power only (70%).

```more reticle maths
- Vortex: "Known Width or Height of Target (Meters) × 1000 ÷ Target Width or Height (Measured on Reticle in MRADS) = Range (Meters)" (85%).
- MOA (minute of angle) version: size in inches × 95.5 ÷ MOA = yards (85%).
- Deer chest back to brisket is often taken as 46 cm (18 in): hunter lore, 55%, so Tip only. With it: 3 mils = 153 m, 2 mils = 230 m, 1.5 mils = 307 m (95% arithmetic, 55% for the size).
- Brace on a rest when you measure (85%, Vortex).
- Check your scope manual for the power the marks are true at (70%).
```

## Your eye: what fools it

```diagram mc-skills-eye-errors
```

- Bright light, snow, water, clear air, a hidden dip between you: looks nearer (80%).
- Poor light, fog, sun in your eyes, only part showing: looks farther (80%).

```more eye methods in detail
- Nearer (80%, Canadian Army fieldcraft): bright light or sun behind you; sharp colour contrast; over water, snow or a uniform surface; clear mountain air; dead ground (a hidden dip) between; bigger than things around it.
- Farther (80%): over a depression you can fully see; poor light, fog, sun in your eyes; only a small part shows; looking down a tree lined road.
- Unit of measure: picture 100 m and count how many fit; "not accurate beyond 400 metres" (80%, Canadian Army fieldcraft, via [Judging distance](#/s/judging-distance)).
- Appearance: learn what a deer looks like at 100, 200, 300 m (80%).
- Halving: judge to halfway, then double (80%). Bracketing: "more than X, less than Y", call the middle (80%).
- Two guesses averaged beat one: you and your brother both call it (80%, the Army's unit average).
- Practice game: on every scouting walk, guess out loud, then range it, and log the error (Tip).
```

## Your .308: the drop

```diagram mc-skills-308-drop
```

- 100 m zero, 150 gr: 13 cm low at 200 m, 28 cm at 250 m, 48 cm at 300 m (75%).
- A deer's vital zone is about 20 to 25 cm (70%).

```more drop tables
- Hunt Mentor model from Federal data, 150 gr Power Shok, 100 m zero (75%, [Ballistics deep dive](#/s/ballistics-deep)): 150 m 4 cm, 200 m 13 cm, 250 m 28 cm, 300 m 48 cm, 350 m 76 cm, 400 m 112 cm low.
- 165 gr Fusion drops almost the same (75%).
- Federal direct, 100 yd zero: 3.9 in (9.9 cm) low at 200 yd and 14.7 in (37.3 cm) at 300 yd (85%).
- 200 m zero option: about 6 cm high at 100 m, 11 cm low at 250 m, 29 cm low at 300 m (75%, [.308 ballistics](#/s/ballistics-308)).
- Your 20 inch barrel is about 3% slower than the box: about 1 cm more drop at 200 m and 3 cm at 300 m (65 to 70%).
- Guess 250 m, deer at 300 m: about 20 cm low, the full height of the vitals (75%). Inside 150 m a 25 m error costs only 2 to 4 cm (75%).
- Build your own dope card from real groups at 100, 150, 200 and 250 m (Tip).
```

## Your .308: the wind

```diagram mc-skills-308-wind
```

- 20 km/h full crosswind: 17 cm at 200 m, 27 cm at 250 m, 40 cm at 300 m (75%).
- Wind from 3 or 9 o'clock: full value. At 45 degrees: about 70% (85%).

```more wind in detail
| Distance | 10 km/h | 20 km/h |
|---|---|---|
| 100 m | 2 cm | 4 cm |
| 200 m | 8 cm | 17 cm |
| 250 m | 13 cm | 27 cm |
| 300 m | 20 cm | 40 cm |

- 150 gr Power Shok, scaled from Federal's 10 mph drift (75%). The 165 gr Fusion drifts about 30% less (75%).
- Double the distance, about 4 times the drift (75%).
- 10 km/h: felt on the face, leaves rustle. 20 km/h: small branches move, dust lifts (65%).
- Wind you ignore at 250 m in 20 km/h puts the bullet out of the vitals (75%). Get closer or pass (Tip).
```

## Steep shots: hold for the flat distance

```anim ff-bl-angle Range it. Note the angle. Hold for the flat distance.
```

- Up or downhill, hold for the flat distance or you hit high (75%).
- Under 10 degrees, ignore it (75%).

```more angle maths
- Flat distance = slope distance × cosine of the angle (75%, NSSF (National Shooting Sports Foundation), Ryan Cleckner).
- 30 degrees: 250 m slope is 217 m flat (95% arithmetic). Hold for 250 and you hit about 10 to 11 cm high (70%).
- Natural slopes rarely exceed about 30 degrees, except near cliffs (70%).
- Many rangefinders show the flat distance for you (65%).
- Detail: [Ballistics deep dive](#/s/ballistics-deep).
```

## Get steady: the ladder

```diagram mc-skills-steadiness
```

- Prone, sitting, kneeling, standing: steadiest first (85%).
- Lower and more supported is steadier (85%).

```steps Four positions
sk-pos-1 | Prone: lie flat, both elbows down.
sk-pos-2 | Sitting: elbows inside the knees.
sk-pos-3 | Kneeling: sit on your heel, elbow on the knee.
sk-pos-4 | Sticks: two legs to you, one to the deer.
```

```more positions and rests in detail
- Hunter education ranks prone steadiest, then sitting, then kneeling, then standing (85%).
- Sticks make standing stable and stretch your range in tall grass and sage (70%, Richard Mann, Field and Stream). Top about armpit height, feet nearly square (70%).
- Pack under the forend, never the bare barrel on rock: "Don't rest the barrel directly on a hard surface, or the rifle will fire higher than normal" (85%, hunter education).
- Hard rests can shift the shot high, soft rests low. Learn yours on paper (70%).
- The Tikka's front sling swivel stud takes a bipod (95%, Tikka manual).
- Say the position out loud before every shot: "Can I go lower?" (Tip).
```

## Natural point of aim, breath, trigger

```anim sk-breath Breathe, half out, hold, squeeze.
```

- Let your body point the rifle, not your muscles (80%).
- Half a breath out, hold, slow press, follow through (85%).

```more the shot process
- NPOA (natural point of aim): "the point where the barrel naturally orients when the shooter's muscles are relaxed and support is achieved" (80%, US Army training via American Rifleman). Move your body around a pivot, not the rifle (80%).
- Test: relax, shimmy, look again. If the crosshair settles back on target, "you're good to go" (80%, American Rifleman, 27 January 2026).
- Breath: deep breath, let about half out, hold, press. Held too long, the pulse rises: breathe and start over (85%, hunter education).
- Trigger on the pad of the finger, slow steady pressure until it fires (85%).
- Follow through: keep the press and the sight picture until the bullet is gone. Call the shot: where was the crosshair when it broke? (85%, Tip for calling)
- Safety off only when the crosshair is settled on the animal (95%, Tikka manual: keep the safety on until ready to fire).
- Memory aid B.R.A.S.S.: Breathe, Relax, Aim, Squeeze, Squeeze more (85%).
```

```steps Natural point of aim
ff-ps-npoa-1 | Settle in with the crosshair on target.
ff-ps-npoa-2 | Breathe, let every muscle go.
ff-ps-npoa-3 | Look: where did the crosshair settle?
ff-ps-npoa-4 | Move your body around a pivot, not the rifle.
```

## Practise with the .22

```diagram mc-skills-22-drill
```

- Same positions, same trigger, a fraction of the cost (65%).
- Dry fire with a snap cap (95%).

```more the .22 drill plan
- The CZ 457 comes with open sights zeroed at 50 m from the factory (95%, CZ manual). A 50 m scope zero keeps you within 1.5 cm from 15 to 55 m (75%).
- 10 km/h wind moves a 40 gr .22 bullet about 3 cm at 50 m and 11 cm at 100 m (75%): the .22 teaches wind cheaply.
- Drill (Tip): 5 shots prone, 5 sitting, 5 from sticks at 50 m on a 5 cm dot; then 1 cold shot from a walk in, 20 seconds to get down.
- Cleaned bore: 10 to 20 warm up rounds before groups settle (85%, Tikka manual rimfire note).
- CZ: dry fire only with snap caps, and not frequently (95%, CZ manual). Tikka: use a snap cap (95%).
- A .22 can carry well over 1 km: earth backstop every shot (65%).
- Real range sessions with the .308: [Season practice plan](#/s/season-practice).
```

## What a 40 year shooter does differently

| Beginner | Old hand (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Shoots from the bench only | Practises sitting and sticks |
| Trusts the box table | Shoots his own dope card |
| Ignores a light crosswind | Gets closer or passes |
| Shoots offhand in a hurry | Asks "can I go lower?" |
| Practises the day before | Dry fires all summer |

- All lines are hunter wisdom, labelled Tip.

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-skills-308-drop
```

- **Number trick:** 4, 13, 28, 48: cm of drop at 150, 200, 250, 300 m with a 100 m zero (75%).
- **Rhyme:** "Lower is steadier, rest is readier."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, short form of the cold plate drill in [Field positions](#/s/field-positions)): "The cold first shot is the one that counts."

```more more hooks
- **Number trick:** 17, 27, 40: cm of drift in 20 km/h at 200, 250, 300 m (75%).
- **Number trick:** 3 and 9 o'clock full; 45 degrees, 70% (85%).
- **Number trick:** metres = metres × 1,000 ÷ mils (85%).
- **Rhyme:** "Flat distance on a hill, or high you will."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, built on "close shots forgive bad guesses", 75%): "If you can get closer, get closer."

> [!mistake]
> - Holding for slope distance on a steep hill.
> - Ignoring a 20 km/h crosswind at 250 m.
> - Offhand when sitting was a second away.

> [!field]
> Before the season: one cold shot each range day from sticks at your longest planned distance. Miss the 20 cm plate? That distance is too far this year.
```
