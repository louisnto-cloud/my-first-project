> [!why]
> For ducks and geese the shot is set by federal law. For grouse it is your choice. Get it wrong and you can be fined with a duck in your hand.

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

```diagram sg2-shot-law
```

- Ducks, geese, coots, snipe: [[Non toxic shot]] only (99%).
- Not even a lead shell in your pocket in the hunting area (99%).

```more the law, word for word
> [!law] Migratory Birds Regulations, 2022, s. 38(1)
> "A person must not hunt a migratory game bird (a) while having in their possession in the hunting area shot other than non-toxic shot; or (b) using shot other than non-toxic shot."

> [!law] BC synopsis, page 13
> "Federal regulations currently identify steel shot, bismuth shot, tungsten-iron shot, tungsten-bronze-iron shot, tin shot, tungsten - matrix shot, and tungsten-polymer shot as the only non-toxic shot permitted for use on waterfowl, coot, and snipe."

- Lead is still allowed for band tailed pigeon and doves (99%, s. 38(2), (3)).
- Upland birds (grouse, ptarmigan, quail, partridge, pheasant, turkey): the lead ban does not apply, unless a local restriction near an important wetland (99%, page 13).
- Lead is banned in National Wildlife Areas (95%, [Ammo for your guns](#/s/ammo-guide)).
- Near you: no lead shot for any purpose on or seaward of any Delta dyke, or the Surrey dykes facing Mud Bay (99%, page 27, [Region 2](#/s/rb-region-2)).
- Full Rule Book screens: [federal methods](#/s/rb-migratory-birds/32) and [BC shot ban notice](#/s/rb-legal-methods/12).
```

## The four shot types

```diagram sg2-density
```

- Steel: cheapest, lightest, hard on tight chokes (70%).
- Bismuth: softer, closer to lead, safer in old guns (70%).
- Tungsten (TSS, tungsten super shot): densest, priciest (65%).

```more each one in full
| Shot | Density | Good | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steel | about 7.8 g/cm3 | Cheap, everywhere | Lighter, loses energy, needs a steel rated choke |
| Bismuth | about 9.6 to 9.8 | Soft like lead, kinder to older guns | Costs more |
| Lead | about 11.2 to 11.3 | Dense, cheap | Illegal for ducks and geese |
| Tungsten (TSS) | about 18 | Densest: small pellets hit hard | Very expensive. Ask your maker first |

- Densities: NWTF (National Wild Turkey Federation) and Outdoor Life articles, search previews (65%).
- Steel is about 100 times harder than lead and two thirds as dense; it "may therefore damage chokes on older firearms" (70%, Wikipedia, shot).
- Bismuth "is generally safe to use in older shotguns with barrels and chokes not rated for use with steel shot" (70%, Wikipedia, shot). Ask a gunsmith about an old gun anyway (Tip).
- Tungsten super shot in a gun or choke not rated for it: VERIFY with the gun and choke maker before you buy a box.
```

## Shot sizes

```diagram sg2-sizes
```

- Bigger number, smaller pellet (70%).
- Same size, steel is lighter than lead, so go about 2 sizes bigger in steel (Tip, 60%).

```more sizes in full
- Diameters: No. 7.5 2.41 mm, No. 6 2.79 mm, No. 4 3.30 mm, No. 2 3.81 mm, BB 4.57 mm (70%, [Shotgun mastery](#/s/shotgun-mastery/6), Wikipedia).
- Steel No. 2: about 125 pellets per ounce. Lead No. 6: about 225 (70%).
- Deer: buckshot No. 1 or bigger, 20 gauge or bigger, where shotguns are legal for deer. Bird shot never (95 to 99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-legal-methods/8)). This course is about birds.
```

## Read the box

```diagram sg2-box
```

- Gauge, length, material, size, weight, speed (95%).
- Gauge and length must match your barrel ([Chapter 5](#/s/mc-shotgun-05)).

```more the lines on a box
- Weight is the shot load in ounces or grams. Speed is in feet per second (fps) (70%).
- Beretta's A300 manual warns against European "High Performance" (HP) steel loads with pellets over 3.5 mm in that gun (95%, 09/2011 manual). Whether North American steel BB counts: VERIFY with Beretta Canada before you buy goose loads for an A300.
- Use only factory loaded shells: Beretta and Stoeger void the warranty for reloads (95%).
```

## Starting shells by bird

```diagram sg2-picks
```

- Ducks: steel No. 2 or 3. Geese: steel BB. Grouse: lead No. 7.5 or 6 (Tip, 65 to 70%).
- Pattern your pick before the hunt ([Chapter 6](#/s/mc-shotgun-06)).

```more the picks in full
- Ducks and geese: [Ammo for your guns](#/s/ammo-guide) picks steel No. 2 or 3 for ducks, No. 4 for teal, BB for geese (70%).
- Grouse: 28 to 32 g (1 to 1 1/8 oz) of lead No. 7.5 or 6 (60%, [Grouse Masterclass](#/s/mc-grouse-09/4)).
- Duck and grouse on the same day: carry only steel. Steel No. 6 works on grouse (Tip). A lead grouse shell in your vest on a duck hunt breaks s. 38(1) (99%).
- Ranges: ducks inside 35 m (38 yd), grouse inside about 25 m (Tip, [Shotgun skills](#/s/shotgun-skills/5)).
```

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Empty every pocket before the marsh. The officer counts what is in your coat, not what is in your gun.

> [!mistake]
> - A forgotten lead grouse shell in a duck coat.
> - Full choke and big steel in a gun not rated for it.
> - Buying the most expensive shell instead of patterning a cheap one.

> [!field]
> Two shell bags: orange for upland lead, green for steel. Only the green one goes in the boat.
