## Find the spot

```steps Find the spot in 3 steps
sk-aim-1 | Find the near front leg.
sk-aim-2 | Slide straight up its back edge.
sk-aim-3 | Stop one third up the chest.
```

- Aim at a spot, never at the whole animal (85%).
- Lungs are the biggest vital target: room for error (85%).

```more vitals in detail
- Heart and lungs sit in the chest just behind the front shoulder; aim about one third up from the bottom of the chest (85%, hunter education).
- Lungs fill the chest above and behind the heart; on a deer the pair is about volleyball size (75%).
- Heart sits low, just above and behind the front leg (85%). Liver behind the lungs is a slow kill; paunch behind that is a slow death and a hard track (85%).
- A deer's vital zone with margin is about 20 to 25 cm (75%).
- Wait for the near front leg to step forward: it opens the chest (85%).
- Head and neck: small targets, small margins, lost animals. Not for you (85%).
```

## Angles: which shots work

```anim sk-angles Broadside and quartering away work. Wait out the rest.
```

| Angle | Aim | Call |
|---|---|---|
| Broadside | Behind the shoulder, 1/3 up | Best (85%) |
| Quartering away | At the far front leg | Good (85%) |
| Quartering toward | Front of the near shoulder | Wait (Tip) |
| Facing away | None | Never (85%) |

```more angles in detail
- Quartering away: aim on the near side at the spot that sends the bullet out the far shoulder (85%, hunter education).
- Quartering toward: a light bullet can deflect off the shoulder bones of elk, deer or a big bear (85%). Beginners wait for the turn (Tip).
- Broadside test: can you see the roundness of the far hip or far shoulder? Then it is quartering (70%).
- Head on: small target between the shoulders, rarely a clean kill (85%). Walking straight away: never (85%).
- Picture the bullet's path through the body, not the entry hole (Tip).
```

## Big game is built differently

```diagram mc-skills-vitals-compare
```

- Same rule: behind the front leg. The height changes (85%).
- Moose: aim lower than the hump says. Bear: halfway up, tight behind the shoulder (65 to 85%).

```more species differences
- **Deer:** one third up the chest, behind the shoulder (85%).
- **Elk:** one third to one half up; elk are about three times a deer, thicker skin, heavier bone; the shoulder blade sits farther back (65 to 85%). Both lungs hit: bulls usually go down within 100 m (65%).
- **Moose:** one third up, behind the front leg; the hump tricks you into aiming high; moose seldom drop, so wait a few minutes and follow up quietly (65 to 85%).
- **Black bear:** halfway up, tight behind the shoulder once the near leg steps forward; hair and fat make it look bigger; thin blood trails (70 to 85%). No shoulder or head shots on bears (85%).
- Diagrams: [Deer](#/s/shot-placement), elk, moose and bear vitals in the same lesson.
- More detail: chapter 10 of the [Elk](#/s/mc-elk-10), [Moose](#/s/mc-moose-10) and [Black bear](#/s/mc-black-bear-10) Masterclasses.
```

## Shotgun: range and pattern

```diagram mc-skills-pattern-range
```

- Pattern density runs out before pellet energy (70%).
- With steel shot on ducks, 35 m (38 yd) is a long shot (70%).

```more shotgun limits in detail
- Big ducks need about 100 pellets inside a 76 cm (30 in) circle at your longest range (70%, Ducks Unlimited).
- Pattern at the distance you will shoot, not only 40 yd. A 75% pattern at your real range is the goal (70%). How: [Sighting in and patterning](#/s/sighting-in).
- The 7 of 10 test: if you cannot break a clay 7 times in 10 at that angle and range, it is too far for you in the marsh (70%).
- Swing through: start behind, pass the head, fire, keep swinging (85%). A crossing duck at 30 m needs roughly 1 to 2 m of lead (65%).
- No dog? Your range is shorter: a sailed bird over big water is lost (70%).
- Rimfire among game birds: grouse, ptarmigan or turkey only; no .22 for quail (99%, [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)).
- Bird species detail: [Ducks](#/s/mc-ducks-10), [Geese](#/s/mc-geese-10), [Grouse](#/s/mc-grouse-10), [Quail](#/s/mc-quail-10).
```

## The no shoot list

```diagram mc-skills-noshot-icons
```

- No backstop. Not 100% sure. Moving. Too far. Through brush. Skylined (85% to Tip).
- Any one of these: no shot. No exceptions (Tip).

```more why each one
- **No [[Backstop]]:** a skylined animal has nothing behind it to stop the bullet. Hunter education: do not shoot at a skylined animal (85%). A .22 can carry well over 1 km (65%).
- **Not sure of ID:** species, sex, legal points, and what is behind it. Count one side twice (Tip). Wrong animal: cancel the licence and self report (95%, [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)).
- **Moving:** hard to hit a vital on a moving animal; wait for it to stop (85%, hunter education). A whistle or bleat often stops a walking deer (Tip).
- **Too far:** past the distance where you hit a 20 cm plate cold from that position (Tip, [Chapter 6](#/s/mc-skills-06)).
- **Through brush:** there is no "brush busting" bullet; even a small twig can throw the shot (70%, American Hunter). Clear lane or no shot.
- **Lying down or sitting:** vitals hidden or twisted (70%). Wait for it to stand (Tip).
- **Outside your zone of fire** or toward a partner, road or building: never (75 to 99%, [Chapter 10](#/s/mc-skills-10) and [Rule Book: where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt)).
```

## Shoot or no shoot: the flow

```diagram mc-skills-shoot-flow
```

- Legal, sure, safe, in range, steady, right angle: then and only then (Tip).
- Any "no" is a pass. Passing is a skill (Tip).

```more the questions in words
1. Is it legal? Season, licence, species, sex, points, hours, place (99%, [Is it legal to shoot?](#/s/legal-to-shoot)).
2. Am I 100% sure of what it is? (Tip)
3. Is there a safe backstop and nothing behind it? (85%)
4. Is it inside my tested range? (Tip)
5. Am I steady, from the lowest position I can get? (85%)
6. Is it broadside or quartering away, standing still, in a clear lane? (85%)
7. Yes to all: safety off, press. Any no: wait, or let it go (Tip).
```

## Buck fever: the shakes

```diagram mc-skills-buck-fever
```

- Decide before it comes: where you will shoot, from what, at what range (60%).
- Stop looking at the antlers. Look at the spot (60%).

```more beating buck fever
- Make decisions in advance and rehearse shot angles before the animal arrives (60%, Bowhunting.com, search preview).
- Range landmarks around your seat before anything shows up (60%, Deer and Deer Hunting checklist, search preview).
- Slow, full breaths out help settle you (55%: Tip, magazine advice only).
- Break the shot into steps: position, rest, spot, breath, press, follow through (60%, Deer and Deer Hunting).
- An outfitter's rule: once you know it is a shooter, stop looking at the rack and focus on the body (60%, Guidefitter, search preview).
- Shaking too hard to hold the crosshair still? That is a no shoot answer. Let it walk; there will be another (Tip).
- No hunter education source in this app measures buck fever; all lines here are Tip level (60% max).
```

## What a 40 year hunter does differently

| Beginner | Old hand (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Shoots at the deer | Shoots at a spot |
| Takes the first angle | Waits for the near leg forward |
| "Almost broadside" | Waits for broadside |
| Shoots through a gap in brush | Waits for a clear lane |
| Counts a pass as a failure | Counts a pass as a win |

- All lines are hunter wisdom, labelled Tip.

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-skills-noshot-icons
```

- **Rhyme:** "Leg, up, a third. Lungs, not the heart."
- **Number trick:** 20 to 25 cm vital zone; 1/3 up the chest (75 to 85%).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely taught hunter education wisdom, opinion): "If you are not sure, you are not shooting."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Sky behind, put it out of mind." No backstop, no shot (85%).
- **Number trick:** 100 pellets in a 76 cm circle at your longest duck range (70%).
- **Number trick:** 7 of 10 on clays, or it is too far (70%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, short form of "when in doubt, do not shoot"): "The shot you pass is never the one you regret."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, from Guidefitter): "Once it is a shooter, stop looking at the antlers."

> [!mistake]
> - Shooting at the whole animal.
> - Taking a quartering toward shot as "almost broadside".
> - Shooting through a small gap in brush.

> [!field]
> Print a life size deer target. From sticks, have your partner call an angle (broadside, quartering away, quartering toward). Say out loud where you aim, or "no shot". Then dry fire.
```
