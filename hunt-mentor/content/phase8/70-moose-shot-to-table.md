## Shot placement and range

```diagram vitals-moose
```

- Broadside: up the back of the front leg, one third up (75 to 85%).
- The hump is not the top of the chest: aim low (75%).

```anim sk-angles Which angles work.
```

```more placement and range in detail
- Broadside: follow the back edge of the front leg up, one third of the way up the chest; both lungs, heart just below (75%, standard hunter education advice, [Moose](#/s/moose); 85% for deer, [shot placement](#/s/shot-placement)).
- Quartering away: aim back through the ribs toward the far shoulder (75%).
- Quartering to: heavy shoulder bone; beginner, wait (Tip). Head, neck, running: no (Tip).
- Your .308 Winchester: 165 to 180 grain premium bullet, inside about 200 m (219 yd) (65%, hunter consensus).
- Maximum range: where you hit a 15 cm (6 in) plate 9 of 10 from a field rest (Tip).
- Look behind the moose: water or a cliff means a brutal recovery (Tip, [vitals drawing](#/s/moose)).
```

## Reactions, wait, track

```diagram sk-reactions
```

- Moose often show little reaction, walk a few steps and stand (Tip).
- Stay on it; a second shot at a standing, hit moose is often wise (Tip).

```more reactions in detail
- Hunter education: some animals show little or no reaction to a well placed shot; note the reaction and the spot (70%, state hunter education via search).
- A moose often does not drop; it walks or trots a short way. Stay on it and be ready for a second shot (Tip, [Moose](#/s/moose)).
- Hard low run or a kick: often heart or lungs. Hunched, slow walk: often gut (70%, [blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)).
- Do not shoot a moose that walks into deep water: unlawful to hunt big game while it swims "unless it has been previously wounded" (99%, page 11). A wounded moose may be finished in water, but the recovery is dangerous; prevent it by waiting for land (Tip).
- Drop a phone pin where you shot from and where it stood; note the time (Tip).
```

```diagram sk-wait-chart
```

- Then wait: lungs 30 minutes; gut 6 to 8 hours or overnight (70 to 85%).

```more waiting and tracking in detail
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Wait times are hunter education opinion, not law (70 to 85%, [blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)). Pushing a hit animal early can drive it a long way (70%).
- Mark where it stood with flagging before you move (85%).
- Two people: one on the sign, one ahead with the rifle ready (70%, hunter education via search; [partner](#/s/hunting-partner)).
- Hunting hours still apply while you track (95%).
- You must make all reasonable effort to locate, dispatch, retrieve and include it in your bag limit (99%, page 12, [unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts)).
- A dog may help track only on a leash (99% leash rule, page 12; 90% my reading for tracking).
```

## Field care: quarter it

```steps Quarter a moose, gutless, in 8 steps
sk-gutless-1 | Cut around the knees, legs and belly line.
sk-gutless-2 | Skin the top half back. Hide is your mat.
sk-gutless-3 | Front quarter off through the armpit.
sk-gutless-4 | Hind quarter off at the hip joint.
sk-gutless-5 | Backstrap off along the spine.
sk-gutless-6 | Neck, rib meat and flank.
sk-gutless-7 | Tenderloin from inside, behind the last rib.
sk-gutless-8 | Bag every piece. Flip and repeat.
```

- Notch first, keep evidence of sex attached (99%).
- Gutless quartering suits a moose (Tip).

```video
yt-gutless-vs-gutting | Gutless method: take the meat, leave the guts inside.
```

```more field care in detail
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Cancel the species licence "immediately after the kill and before handling" (99%, page 11, [after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill)).
- Evidence of sex for moose: the tail or another hide piece of at least 6 cm², naturally attached, AND for a bull a testicle or part of the penis (99%, page 15).
- Antlers in a spike fork season: keep them naturally attached to a portion of the upper skull, with the species licence, available for inspection while returning from the hunt; do not alter or tamper with them (99%, pages 11 and 15).
- Edible portions: neck, ribs, four quarters and loins must go to your home, a meat cutter or cold storage (99%, pages 3 and 12).
- [[Gutless method]]: no need to open the belly; you can still take the heart and liver through the ribs (Tip, [field dressing](#/s/field-dressing)).
- Look at the liver, lungs and lymph nodes: spots, cysts or flukes, ask a conservation officer or vet before eating the organ (Tip). Winter ticks pose no health risk to people (85%, BC government).
- Region 3 asks hunters to submit deer, elk and moose heads for CWD (Chronic Wasting Disease) testing (99%, page 32, [Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3)).
```

## Cool it fast

```diagram mc-moose-cooling
```

- Below 4°C, fast: the one target (80%).
- Skin it, quarter it, bag it, hang it in shade (Tip).

```diagram sk-cooler
```

```more cooling in detail
- One target: meat below 4°C (40°F), as fast as you can (80%, [meat care](#/s/meat-care)).
- 4 to 60°C is the danger zone; bacteria can double every 20 minutes (80%, Penn State Extension via [meat care](#/s/meat-care)).
- A moose's body is so big the heat sits deep at the bone; skin and quarter even on a cool day (Tip, [Moose](#/s/moose)). Moose are built to hold heat (chapter 2).
- Game bags, not plastic or tarps; tarps trap heat (Tip).
- Hang quarters in shade with air all round, or lay them on poles or logs, never on warm ground in a pile (Tip).
- Ageing: 1 to 3°C for 7 to 14 days if you have a cold room; otherwise cut and freeze within a few days (80%, [meat care](#/s/meat-care)).
```

## How much moose, and cooking

```diagram mc-moose-meat-math
```

- Spike fork bull: about 162 kg of carcass (75%).
- Adult bull: about 249 kg; cow 199 kg; calf 82 kg (75%).

```more yield in detail
- Aitken and others 2012, *Alces*: carcass weights from meat cutter records in the Omineca, central BC, 1995 to 2007. Carcass means the four quarters, no head, hide, lower legs or internal organs (75%, abstract via search).
  - Calves about 82 kg; spike fork bulls about 162 kg; adult cows about 199 kg; adult bulls about 249 kg.
  - Adult bulls were heavier before the rut than after (75%).
- Rule of thumb from hunter sources: boneless meat about 35 to 45% of live weight (60%, Tip, [Moose](#/s/moose)).
- BC account: moose provide "more meat than all other ungulates combined" in BC (90%).
- Your freezer: plan space for well over 100 kg of boned meat from a spike fork bull (Tip, my reading of the carcass weight).
```


- Cook all wild game to 71°C for safety (95%).

```more cooking in detail
- Same primal map as deer: neck, shoulder, shank, backstrap, tenderloin, ribs and flank, hind quarter (Tip, [butchering](#/s/butchering)).
- Moose is lean: overcooked it turns grey and dry (80%, [cooking](#/s/cooking)).
- Canada's food safety number for wild game: 71°C internal (95%, Canadian Food Inspection Agency via [cooking](#/s/cooking)). Many hunters eat loins pinker; that risk is theirs (Tip).
- Backstrap and tenderloin: hot and fast. Shoulder, neck and shank: braise 2.5 to 3 hours (Tip).
- A November bull is lean after the rut: add fat when you grind (Tip, chapter 2).
```

```more packing in detail
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- The trips drawing is a Tip, not a measured figure: quarters, bags of loins, neck and trim, antlers and gear (Tip).
- Mark the kill site on the Hunt Map; flag a route to the road or trail (Tip).
- Vehicle rules: ATV (all terrain vehicle) closed for hunting, hauling hunters, gear or game 1 September to 10 December in MUs (Management Units) 3-28, 3-29, 3-30 (99%, page 33). No motor vehicles above 1,700 m in Region 3 except on existing roads and trails (99%).
- Boat retrieval: lawful if nobody aboard has a loaded firearm, or the boat is not propelled by a motor (99%, page 12).
- An unlicensed companion may help pack meat out if not carrying a firearm (99%, regs safety notes). Your brother carrying meat you killed needs a Record of Receipt (90%, page 14).
```

## Rules recap

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

```diagram sa-moose-seasons
```

- MUs (Management Units) 3-27 and 3-28: spike fork bulls, 1 to 15 November, bag 1 (99%).
- Mandatory Hunter Report by 31 March, hunt or no hunt (99%).

```regs deerR3
```

```more the rules recap, with links (no new rules)
- Seasons, page 34, Region 3 (99%, [Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3)): spike fork bulls; MUs 3-34 to 3-38, 3-40 to 3-44, 3-46: 20 September to 31 October; MUs 3-15 to 3-17, 3-31 to 3-33: 15 October to 15 November; MUs 3-12 to 3-14, 3-18 to 3-20, 3-26 to 3-30, 3-39: 1 to 15 November. Bag 1.
- Region 8, page 68: MUs 8-1 to 8-15, 8-21 to 8-26, spike fork bulls, 1 to 15 November, bag 1 (99%, [Region 8](#/s/rb-region-8)).
- Region 2: no moose row; not open near Mission (99%, page 28, [Region 2](#/s/rb-region-2)).
- [[Spike fork bull]]: "a bull moose having no more than two tines (points) on one antler. (Includes tines on main antler and brow palms.) Does not include a calf." (99%, page 3, [definitions](#/s/rb-definitions)). Only one side needs to qualify (90%, my reading in the Rule Book).
- [[Tine]]: at least 2.5 cm long and longer than it is wide; rounded bumps on the palm may not count (99%, [definitions](#/s/rb-definitions)).
- [[Tripalm bull]] and [[10 point bull]]: other regions only, not Regions 3 or 8 general seasons (99%).
- Cows and calves: closed without an LEH (Limited Entry Hunting) authorization (99%, page 5).
- Methods: centrefire rifle or bow; class A, C or D crossbow; no electronic calls; dogs leashed (99%, pages 12 and 13, [legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)).
- Hours: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (99%, page 11).
- Swimming big game: no, unless previously wounded; no hunting from a boat the motor is pushing; no hunting from a motor vehicle (99%, page 11).
- After the kill: notch the licence, evidence of sex attached, antlers on part of the skull with the licence, all edible portions taken (99%, pages 11, 12, 15, [after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill)).
- Mandatory Hunter Report: every moose licence holder by 31 March; miss it and you cannot buy a licence or LEH application next season until it is filed (99%, page 16).
- No moose part or derivative from outside BC for hunting (99%, page 12).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-moose-shot-to-table
```

- **Rhyme:** "Count it, drop it, notch it, cool it; pack it, hang it, report it."
- **Number trick:** 1, 15, 31: season 1 to 15 November; report by 31 March (99%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "The real work starts after the shot."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Hump is high, the heart is low; aim one third up, and let it go."
- **Number trick:** 82, 162, 199, 249: calf, spike fork, cow, bull carcass kilograms (75%).
- **Number trick:** 30 and 4: wait 30 minutes on lungs; meat below 4°C (70 to 80%).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely taught, opinion): "Cold meat, clean meat, good meat."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Never shoot a moose you cannot get a truck or a boat within a kilometre of."

> [!mistake]
> - Cutting the antlers off the skull plate before you are home.
> - Leaving a moose whole overnight in mild weather.
> - Forgetting the 31 March report because you got nothing.

> [!field]
> Before 1 November: pack 6 game bags, a bone saw, 2 knives and a sharpener, rope, flagging tape, headlamps, the licence in a zip bag, and agree the pack plan with your brother.
```
