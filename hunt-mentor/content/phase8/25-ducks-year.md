> [!why]
> The duck you hunt in October is a different bird from the one in December. Know the calendar and you know who is on the water.

```diagram mdk-year-chart
```

- October: peak fall passage through the Interior (60%).
- Late November: freeze up ends most Interior migration (65%).

```more the year in one paragraph per season
- Winter: open valley rivers, lakes and Fraser Valley fields and sloughs (65%).
- Spring: north again from March; plateau lakes open as the ice goes (55 to 60%).
- Summer: nesting on Interior wetlands and potholes, broods, then the wing moult (60%).
- Fall: early migrants in August, well under way by late September, peak in October, mostly over by late November (60 to 75%).
- These months are a general pattern from the app migration data. There are no month by month counts for the Thompson or Okanagan (55 to 65%).
```

## Month by month

| Month | Ducks are doing (certainty) |
|---|---|
| Jan, Feb | Wintering on open water; pairs (65%) |
| Mar | Move north; valley wetlands open first (55%) |
| Apr | Plateau lakes open; arrive to breed (55%) |
| May, Jun | Nesting; broods hatch (60%) |
| Jul | Broods grow; drakes moult, flightless (55%) |
| Aug | Early migrants move (55%) |
| Sep | Fall migration under way (65%) |
| Oct | Peak passage; plateau lakes start to freeze (60%) |
| Nov | Freeze up; birds pack onto valley water (65%) |
| Dec | Wintering waters (65%) |

```more where they winter near you
- Kamloops: ice free stretches of the South Thompson River (Important Bird Area) (65%).
- Okanagan: Vaseux Lake (a sanctuary, no hunting) and the north arm of Okanagan Lake (65%).
- Fraser Valley: the delta and valley fields and sloughs; Pitt Addington Marsh, Harrison Chehalis and Boundary Bay areas (65 to 90%).
- Fraser delta waterfowl need food from September to April (80%).
- See the Hunt Map layer "Duck staging and winter waters" in [Masterclass 7](#/s/mc-ducks-07).
```

## Nesting and broods

```diagram mdk-nest-timeline
```

- About 9 eggs, 28 days of sitting (75%).
- Ducklings walk to water within a day (70%).

```more the breeding season in detail
- Clutch: 5 to 15 eggs, about 9 on average (70 to 75%).
- Incubation: 26 to 30 days, about 28 (75%).
- Ducklings stay in the nest about 10 hours, then follow the hen to water (65%).
- They fly at about 50 to 60 days (70%).
- Breeding in BC: Interior wetlands and potholes, mostly below 500 m for mallards; lesser scaup, canvasback and redhead on plateau lakes at 500 to 1,500 m (85%, BC Breeding Bird Atlas).
- At Tranquille near Kamloops mallards, goldeneyes and wood ducks nest (60%). Tranquille is a no shooting area (75%).
```

## The wing moult

```diagram mdk-eclipse
```

- Ducks drop all flight feathers at once (85%).
- Flightless for about 3 to 4 weeks (80%).

```more moult facts
- Unlike most birds, ducks, geese and swans lose all flight feathers together after breeding (85%).
- Mallards: about 24 to 26 days flightless in captive birds, about 23 to 24 days in Dutch field work (75%).
- Black ducks in Labrador: 27 to 30 days, peak in mid July (75%).
- Some drakes fly up to 1,200 km to safe moulting lakes before they moult (70%).
- Heavier sea ducks have longer flightless periods (70%).
- Drakes then wear the drab eclipse plumage into fall; the colour comes back over October and November (75 to 80%).
```

## Fall migration, species by species

```diagram sb-duck-migration
```

- Teal, shoveler, pintail leave first (75%).
- Goldeneyes, bufflehead and mergansers go with the ice (75%).

```more who goes when
- Blue winged teal, shoveler and pintail leave breeding areas in August or early September (75%).
- Green winged teal: September to October; most are south by November (75%).
- Mallard, wigeon and gadwall: September or October, and stay as long as water is open (75%).
- Ring necked duck in October; canvasback and redhead October to November (75%).
- Goldeneyes and bufflehead: with the ice, mid to late October, reaching wintering water in November and December (75%).
- Common merganser: one of the last, late October to November (75%).
- Most migration happens at night (80%). With a strong tailwind a mallard can cover about 1,300 km (800 miles) in 8 hours, then needs 3 to 7 days to refuel (60%).
```

## October and November: what changes

```diagram mdk-oct-nov
```

- Colour back, pairs forming, the big push (60 to 75%).
- Late season: fewer, wiser, fatter birds (Tip).

```more how to hunt the change (opinion)
- Early October near Kamloops: local mallards, wigeon and green winged teal, plus the first northern birds (Tip, 60%).
- After a cold snap: new birds. Check your ponds again (65%, lore).
- When plateau lakes freeze: hunt valley water, rivers and the bigger lakes that stay open (65%).
- Late November: goldeneyes and bufflehead on big lakes; remember the goldeneye limit of 2 (99%).
- In District No. 2 (Mission) the season opens 10 October 2026 and runs into January, so you can hunt the wintering birds (99%).
```

## Winter survival

```photo hab-osoyoos-wetland Calm, open water out of the wind holds winter ducks.
```

- Winter ducks need open water, food and calm (75%).
- Clear cold nights cost the most energy (75%).

```more winter in detail
- Body mass usually drops from mid to late winter (75%).
- Ducks roost where it is sheltered; dense cover out of the wind gives a better microclimate (75%).
- Snow and ice cover push ducks south once a cold index passes a threshold (80%, Missouri study of mallards).
- Hunter meaning: in a cold snap, find the one open, sheltered patch of water, then ask for permission (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

| Hook | Means |
|---|---|
| "Teal first, goldeneye last" | Migration order (75%) |
| 9 eggs, 28 days, 50 to 60 to fly | Nesting (70 to 75%) |
| 3 to 4 weeks | Flightless moult (80%) |
| "Ice is the clock" | Freeze up moves ducks (65%) |
| "October peak, November push" | Fall passage (60 to 65%) |

> [!rule]
> Old hand's rule (opinion, widely taught): "The north wind brings the ducks; the ice sends them on."

```more more hooks
- "Moult in July, no birds fly." Drakes are flightless in midsummer (75%).
- Number trick: 9 eggs plus 28 days plus about 55 days to fly is about 3 months (70%).
```
