## The nose you are up against

```diagram mc-skills-nose-facts
```

- A deer's nose is far better than yours. Nobody knows exactly how much (65%).
- The famous "297 million receptors" is an old estimate, not a measured fact (60%).

```more the nose in numbers
- Kip Adams, National Deer Association biologist: deer have about 100,000 times more olfactory receptor cells than people, which "probably" means smell at least on that order better, though not proven (65%, Outdoor Life, 29 September 2026).
- The "nearly 300 million receptors" figure is an "oft cited stat from the early 90s"; experts now think it is low, and no one is sure (65%, same article).
- People: "somewhere between 5 and 50 million" receptors depending on the study (65%).
- The deer's nasal passage "runs from the tip all the way up through the head", lined with folded receptor tissue (65%, Adams).
- No source found gives a measured distance a deer can smell you (60%). Plan as if any animal downwind can (Tip).
- Your scent comes from breath, skin, clothes, boots, gear and the truck (Tip).
- Elk, moose, bears and coyotes all lean hard on scent too (Tip). See each species Masterclass.
```

## The scent cone

```diagram mc-skills-cone-shapes
```

- Steady wind: a long narrow cone. Light wind: wide and short. Swirl: everywhere (Tip).
- Steady wind is your friend. Light, shifting wind is the danger (Tip).

```more the cone in detail
- Scent drifts with the air like smoke from a campfire (Tip, [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals)).
- The cone widens with distance (Tip). The Hunt Map draws it: **Scent cone from my location** in the wind tools (99%, app).
- The app's cone uses forecast wind 10 m above ground and reminds you the real cone is wider in timber and around ridges (99%, app text).
- Deer often travel with the wind at their back or side so they can smell behind and look ahead (60%, hunter observation).
- Strong forecast wind usually beats thermals; on calm days thermals rule (Tip).
```

## Thermals by the clock

```anim mc-skills-thermal-flip Morning: air climbs. Evening: air slides down.
```

- Sun on a slope: air rises soon after (75%).
- Shade arrives: calm, then air sinks (75%).

```more thermals by the hour
- "Upslope winds begin as a gentle upflow soon after the sun strikes the slope." East faces flip first (75%, USDA (United States Department of Agriculture) Fire Weather handbook via [Wind mastery](#/s/wind-mastery)).
- South slopes peak soon after midday; west slopes by mid afternoon; north faces stay weakest (75%).
- Valley floor wind lags: up valley flow starts mid to late morning (75%).
- Slopes going into shade: upslope dies, a brief calm, then gentle downslope flow (75%).
- Switch times are swirl times. Be set up before them (Tip).

| Wind | Speed |
|---|---|
| Upslope | about 5 to 13 km/h (75%) |
| Downslope at night | about 3 to 8 km/h (75%) |
| Up valley, big valleys | about 16 to 24 km/h (75%) |

- Thermal speeds: NWCG (National Wildfire Coordinating Group) and the Fire Weather handbook, via [Wind mastery](#/s/wind-mastery) (75%).
```

## Terrain bends the wind

```diagram mc-skills-terrain-wind
```

- Behind a ridge crest: air tumbles back uphill (70%).
- Saddles squeeze wind. Junctions and bends swirl (75%).

```more terrain in detail
- On the lee side of a ridge "a turbulent reversal or eddies are possible" (70%, NWCG (National Wildfire Coordinating Group) via [Wind mastery](#/s/wind-mastery)). Do not sit just behind the crest (Tip).
- Air over a ridge is "channeled" through low gaps (75%, Fire Weather handbook). Sit a saddle from the downwind side, not in the gap (Tip).
- Eddies "may form at canyon bends and at tributary junctions" (75%). A junction is a swirl trap (Tip).
- Timber: "upslope flow may rise above the treetops while a shallow downslope flow persists at the surface under the canopy shade" (75%). Your scent can creep downhill in shaded timber at 10 a.m. (Tip).
- Draws act like gutters for cool evening air (75%).
- Find aspect and sun with the Hunt Map's Slope direction and Sun and shade tools (99%, app). See [The Hunt Map like a pro](#/s/hunt-map-pro).
```

## Check the wind, every time

```steps Four ways to read the air
hf-wind-puffer | Puffer bottle: one puff shows drift.
hf-wind-milkweed | Milkweed: floats far, shows thermals and swirls.
hf-wind-lighter | Lighter: quick check at your hand only.
hf-wind-thread | Thread on the sling: always on.
```

- Check every few minutes and at every new terrain feature (Tip).
- Puff at your feet as well as chest height in timber (Tip).

```more checking the wind in detail
- Puffer bottle of unscented powder: one puff shows drift and direction (70%, [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals)).
- Milkweed fluff: floats far and shows thermals and swirls over terrain (70%, Buckmasters via search preview).
- Lighter flame: only the air at your hand. Mind fire risk in dry grass (Tip).
- Thread or feather on the sling or bow: always on, no hands (Tip).
- Beaufort cues on land (95%, Royal Meteorological Society): 1 to 5 km/h smoke drifts; 6 to 11 felt on the face, leaves rustle; 12 to 19 leaves and small twigs in constant motion; 20 to 28 dust and small branches move.
```

## Forecast wind is not your wind

```diagram mc-skills-wind-height
```

- Forecasts are for 10 m up. At head height wind may be 50 to 70% of it (90%).
- In draws and timber it can blow a different way (99%, app).

```more why the forecast lies a little
- Beaufort speeds are measured at 10 m and "do not reflect the wind speeds that you would feel on the ground"; at 2 m it may be only 50 to 70% (90%, Royal Meteorological Society).
- The forecast is a model for a grid square. App text: "Wind in draws and timber can differ" (99%).
- Use the forecast to plan the night before. Use the puffer to decide on the day (Tip).
- Grandpa's rule (wisdom, [Wind mastery](#/s/wind-mastery)): "The wind you checked five minutes ago is gossip. The puff in your hand is news."
```

## Plan the approach

```diagram mc-skills-approach-plan
```

- Plan an A seat and a B seat for each likely wind (Tip).
- Walk in with the wind in your face or across you (Tip).

```more approach planning in detail
1. Night before: forecast direction, speed, gusts on the [Hunt Map](#/map) (99%, app).
2. Mark beds, feed and water on the map. Name the terrain at each seat: ridge, saddle, draw, timber, bowl (Tip).
3. Morning: come in from above or the side, after the sun hits that slope (75% thermals, Tip tactic).
4. Evening: sit below or beside; sinking air carries scent down and away (75% thermals, Tip tactic).
5. Wrong wind on the day? Go to B. Do not force A (Tip).
6. Swirling? Back out and come back with steadier air (Tip).
- Before the morning switch, slope air still sinks. A seat right above deer at first light works only once upslope starts (Tip, [Wind mastery](#/s/wind-mastery)).
```

## Clean first, then play the wind

```diagram mc-skills-scent-steps
```

- Scent and movement matter far more than clothing colour (75%).
- No spray beats a bad wind (Tip).

```more scent habits
- National Deer Association: scent and movement matter far more than clothing colour or UV (ultraviolet) brighteners (75%, via [Stay unseen](#/s/stay-unseen)).
- Habits many hunters use (Tip): unscented wash, clothes stored away from the truck and campfire, boots on at the trailhead, rubber boots on wet trails.
- No source here proves a product stops a deer smelling you (60%: not a fact). Treat products as extras, never as a reason to ignore wind (Tip).
- Deer, elk, moose or caribou parts or urine from outside BC cannot be used for hunting, and urine scents can carry CWD (Chronic Wasting Disease) (99%, synopsis page 12 item 51, via [Mule deer senses](#/s/mc-mule-deer-03)).
```

## What a 40 year hunter does differently

| Beginner | Old hand (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Checks wind at the truck | Checks at every feature |
| Trusts the app arrow | Trusts the puff |
| Forces the plan | Has a B seat |
| Moves in at the switch | Sits through the switch |
| Sits behind a crest | Sits on the windward side |

- All lines are hunter wisdom, labelled Tip.

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-skills-cone-shapes
```

- **Rhyme:** "Sun on, air up. Shade on, air down."
- **Number trick:** 10 m forecast, 50 to 70% at your head (90%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, the Grandpa's rule in [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals)): "Hunt the wind you have, not the wind you wanted."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "East first, south strongest, west last" (75%).
- **Rhyme:** "Over the crest, scent is a mess."
- **Number trick:** 3 to 8 mph up, 2 to 5 down (75%): thermals are breezes, not gales.
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, short form of "in a swirl, no setup is safe", [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals)): "A swirl is a no. Back out, come back."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, short form of "check at every new terrain feature"): "Every new ridge is a new wind."

> [!mistake]
> - Walking in during the morning switch.
> - Sitting right behind a ridge crest.
> - Trusting a forecast arrow in a draw.

> [!field]
> On a clear scouting day, puff the wind on an east face and a west face every hour from 7 to 11 a.m. Log when each turns uphill. That is your local thermal clock.
```
