## The mount, step by step

```steps Mount a shotgun in 4 steps
sg2-mount-1 | Ready: feet set, low gun, safety on.
sg2-mount-2 | Eyes lock on the bird's head.
sg2-mount-3 | Push the muzzle toward the bird.
sg2-mount-4 | Stock to the cheek, then the shoulder.
```

- Bring the gun to your face, not your face to the gun (85%).
- Safety off only as the gun comes up on a bird you will shoot (Tip).

```more mount in full
- Feet about shoulder width, front foot toward the target, knees soft so the hips can turn (85%, hunter education, [Shotgun skills](#/s/shotgun-skills/2)).
- Cheek first, then the shoulder; the butt lands on the same spot every time (85%).
- Dry practice at home: gun proven empty, no shells in the room, 20 mounts a night (Tip).
```

## Pull away

```diagram sg2-lead-pullaway
```

- Start on the bird, match it, pull ahead, fire, keep moving (70%).
- Easy to learn on clays (70%).

## Maintained lead

```diagram sg2-lead-maintained
```

- Start ahead, hold the gap, fire, keep swinging (85%).
- Long crossing ducks. Needs practice to judge the gap (85%).

## Swing through

```diagram sg2-lead-swing
```

- Start behind, swing faster, fire as you pass, keep swinging (85%).
- Hunter education teaches it first. Good on fast grouse (85%).

```more which one to learn
- Pull away is the skeet shooter's favourite; maintained lead (also called sustained lead) suits long crossers; swing through suits fast flushes (70 to 85%, [Shotgun skills](#/s/shotgun-skills/3)).
- In every method the gun keeps moving after the shot. Stopping it is the classic miss behind (85%).
- My lean: learn swing through for grouse and pull away on clays for ducks. Pick one and stay with it a season (Tip).
- A crossing duck at 30 m needs roughly 1 to 2 m of lead (65%). Do not measure it in the blind: learn the picture on clays (70%).
```

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Look at the beak, never the barrel. The gun goes where the eyes go.

> [!mistake]
> - Lifting your head to watch the bird fall: the shot goes high.
> - Stopping the gun at the bang: the shot goes behind.
> - Aiming one eyed like a rifle.

> [!field]
> Three clay trips before the opener: one each for pull away, maintained lead and swing through. Keep the one that broke the most.
