> [!why]
> You will hear quail and read their sign far more often than you see them. Tracks, dust bowls, droppings and calls tell you a covey lives here and roughly when it was here.

```diagram mql-dust-bowl
```

- Look for tracks, droppings and dusting holes near cover (75%).
- Then walk the road edge in short bursts (75%).

```more the sign method in full
- Washington Fish and Wildlife: "drive or hike back roads, looking for tracks, droppings and dusting holes that indicate there may be birds nearby." (75%)
- "When you find evidence of birds, walk the edge of the road in short bursts, stopping often near patches of roadside cover." (75%)
- Do it legally: no shooting across or within the [[Road allowance]], and stay off private land without permission (99%, Rule Book).
```

## Tracks

```diagram mql-track-grid
```

- Three toes forward, a short hind toe; prints in one line (60 to 75%).
- Quail track about 3.5 cm long (Tip, VERIFY).

```more tracks in full
- Quail forage on the ground, so tracks usually fall in a single line, one foot in front of the other (60%, Bear Tracker).
- Sandy river bars and dusty lanes show quail tracks best (60%).
- Size: a related quail (Gambel's) track measured about 3.8 by 3.8 cm (1.5 in) (60%, Wildwood Tracking quiz). For California quail, a working figure of about 3.5 cm: Tip, VERIFY with a ruler on a fresh print.
- Grouse tracks are about 5 cm (75%, the app's habitat tour). Chukar and pheasant tracks are bigger than quail: measure and write it down (Tip).
- The hind toe often does not show; in fine dust it can (60%).
- Tracking basics: [Tracks](#/s/tracks) and [Reading sign](#/s/sign).
```

## Droppings and roost piles

```diagram mql-droppings
```

- Bird droppings: a dark coil with a white cap (70%).
- A pile under a roost tree marks a covey's bedroom (Tip).

```more droppings in full
- Birds pass nitrogen waste as uric acid, the white part of the dropping (70%, Poultry Hub).
- California quail roost in dense trees or shrubs, not on the ground (70%), so look for droppings under thick conifers, junipers or shrub clumps near feeding cover (Tip).
- "Watch for roost piles... Fresh roost piles often indicate good places to find birds early or late in the day." (60%, Missouri Conservationist 1996, written about bobwhites, which roost on the ground)
- Chukar roost on the ground under sage or juniper and among rocks, in coveys, sometimes tail to tail (80%, FEIS): piles collect under ledges.
- Do not handle droppings with bare hands (Tip).
```

## Dusting bowls

```diagram mql-dust-bowl
```

- Quail dust bathe as a group, often at midday (70 to 75%).
- Round bowls in loose soil with small tracks around them (Tip).

```more dusting in full
- Dust bathing is a daily communal habit of quail (60%, Wikipedia summary). Washington Fish and Wildlife names dusting as a favourite midday pastime, often at road edges (75%).
- One summary says the bowls are about 7 to 15 cm across: Tip, the figure is unsourced (VERIFY).
- Tip: fresh bowls have loose, fluffy dust and a feather or two. Old bowls crust over and catch leaves.
- Chukar use dusting sites at midday too (75%).
```

## Feathers

```photo g-quail-pair Body feathers are grey and brown; plume feathers are black.
```

- A small pile of feathers can mean a hawk kill (Tip).
- Single grey body feathers at a dust bowl: birds were here (Tip).

```more feathers in full
- Predators of quail include Cooper's and sharp shinned hawks and great horned owls (80%). A plucked pile on open ground often marks a hawk kill (Tip).
- A pile of feathers is sign of a covey's range, not of where the covey sits now (Tip).
- Shot birds: find every feather patch where a bird fell and search from there (Tip). See chapter 10.
```

## Calls as sign

```diagram mql-call-chart
```

- Dawn and dusk calling tells you where a covey roosts (75%).
- Point to the sound, note the cover, hunt it next morning (Tip).

```more calls in full
- Assembly calls start the covey's move off the roost at first light (70%).
- A chorus of calls at dusk as birds gather to roost (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife).
- Hunters can start the chorus with a quail call and use the answers to find spots for the next morning (75%). A mouth call is legal; recorded or electronic calls are not for game birds (99% for the electronic ban, 95% for mouth calls, Rule Book).
- Soft clucks while feeding: you hear them only when you are quiet and close (75%).
```

## How fresh is it?

```diagram mql-fresh-scale
```

- Moist and dark: today. Dry and pale: days old (Tip).
- Note the last rain or wind; it dates every sign (Tip).

```more freshness in full
- No study gives aging times for quail sign. All of this is Tip.
- Tracks after rain: crisp prints on a rain pocked surface were made after the rain stopped (Tip).
- Morning dew: a dark line where birds walked through dewy grass is from this morning (Tip).
- Write sign in the Journal with the date and the weather. After a season, you know each covey's routine (Tip).
```

## Following a covey

```steps Follow a covey, step by step
mql-follow-1 | Dawn or dusk: stand still and listen for calls.
mql-follow-2 | Walk toward them reading tracks and dust bowls.
mql-follow-3 | Walk the cover edge: stop 15 to 20 seconds often.
mql-follow-4 | After the flush, mark every single.
```

- Listen, read, stop and go, mark (Tip and 75%).
- Two hunters: one on each side of the cover (Tip).

```more following in full
- Stop at each patch of cover and wait at least 15 or 20 seconds before moving on; hiding quail may get nervous and fly (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife).
- Move quickly from patch to patch, but stop at each one (75%).
- Mark the singles and go to the nearest first. Singles often hold tight (75%).
- Move slowly and quietly so you can hear a distant covey rise or see birds flying in (60%, Missouri Conservationist).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mql-follow-4
```

- **Rhyme:** "Tracks in a line, dust in a bowl, white capped drops: the covey's on patrol." (Tip)
- **Number trick:** "15 to 20": stop 15 to 20 seconds at each patch (75%).

```more more hooks
- **Sign order:** ears, lanes, bowls, brush. Listen, check the lane, find the bowls, walk the brush (Tip).
- **Old hand's rule (opinion):** "A fresh dust bowl at noon means birds within a stone's throw." Widely repeated hunter lore (Tip).
```
