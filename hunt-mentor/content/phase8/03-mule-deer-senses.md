## The nose

```anim mmd-scent-cone Your scent spreads downwind in a cone.
```

- Smell is a deer's strongest sense (85%).
- Downwind of you = smelled. No exceptions (Tip).

```more how the nose works
- Smell is the strongest deer sense (85%, research notes). The often quoted "297 million receptors" figure is folklore (45%, not a fact).
- Deer also have a second scent organ, the vomeronasal organ in the roof of the mouth. A rutting buck curls his lip (the flehmen face) to pull a doe's urine scent into it (65%, University of Georgia researcher via fishgame.com; Outdoor Life).
- Deer scent glands send messages to other deer (90%, BC deer account):
  - Metatarsal (outside of the lower hind leg): alarm scent. Large on mule deer (10 to 15 cm) (85%).
  - Tarsal (inside of the hock): recognition; bucks rub urinate on it in the rut (90%).
  - Interdigital (between the toes): leaves a scent trail as it walks (90%).
- Your scent comes from breath, skin, clothes, boots and the truck (Tip).
- Scent spreads downwind and widens with distance, like a cone (Tip). The Hunt Map draws it: **Scent cone** in the wind tools.
- Deer, elk, moose or caribou parts from outside BC cannot be used for hunting, and urine scents can carry CWD (Chronic Wasting Disease) (99%, synopsis page 12 item 51 and inside back cover, see [deer mind](#/s/deer-mind)).
```

## Thermals by the clock

```anim hf-thermal-clock Morning air rises, evening air sinks.
```

- Sun on the slope: air and scent drift uphill (80%).
- Evening cooling: scent slides downhill (80%).

```more thermals in detail
- Sun warms a slope in the morning; warm air rises, so scent drifts uphill (80%).
- In the evening slopes cool; cool air sinks, so scent drifts downhill (80%).
- At dawn before the sun hits a slope, air is often still sinking. It flips after the sun warms that slope (75%).
- A shaded north slope can still be draining while a sunny south slope is rising (Tip).
- A strong main wind usually beats thermals (Tip).
- Valley air near Heffley Creek sinks at night and in the evening and rises in late morning (80%, [Heffley Creek](#/s/local-heffley)).
- Full lesson with drills: [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals).

> [!lean]
> Morning: approach from above or from the side once the sun is on the slope. Evening: hunt from below or across, and leave before cold air drains your scent onto bedded deer.
```

## Where the wind lies

```diagram mmd-draw-swirl
```

- Draws, saddles and ridge backs swirl the air (Tip).
- Check the wind every few minutes in broken ground (Tip).

```more swirls, eddies and how to read them
- Wind pouring over a ridge can curl back down the lee side in an eddy (Tip, basic air flow).
- Saddles funnel wind and swirl it; deer like to cross there, and so does your scent (Tip).
- Mule deer often bed on the leeward (downwind) side of a roll in the ground, where they can smell the slope behind them (65%, Steven Rinella, MeatEater).
- Incoming weather and clouds quickly kill a steady wind direction; plan stalks for stable wind (65%, Rinella).
- Tools: a puffer of unscented powder, a light thread on the rifle, or milkweed fluff (Tip). The Hunt Map **Weather and wind** tool shows the forecast wind (Tip).
```

## The ears

```diagram mmd-sound-band
```

- Hears 115 Hz to 54 kHz, best 4 to 8 kHz (85%).
- That band is zips, Velcro, nylon and metal clicks (85%).

```more what the ears catch
- Deer hear about 115 Hz (hertz) to 54 kHz (kilohertz), best at about 4 to 8 kHz (85%, D'Angelo and others 2007).
- People hear about 20 Hz to 20 kHz, so deer hear much higher pitches than you do (85%).
- That best band holds zippers, Velcro, nylon swish, metal on metal and snapping twigs (85%).
- Big ears swivel to point at a sound (75%). A mule deer's ears are about two thirds the length of its head (90%).
- Natural sounds (wind, a squirrel, another deer walking) blend in. Steady two legged crunching, voices and metal do not (Tip).
- Truck doors, tailgates and loud voices at the pullout can clear a slope (Tip).
```

## The eyes: field of view

```diagram mmd-eye-field
```

- About 310 degrees without turning the head (80%).
- Poor depth and detail; superb at motion (80%).

```more field of view, depth and motion
- Eyes set on the sides of the head see about 310 degrees without moving the head (80%, Penn State Deer Research).
- Depth perception is poor; the zone seen by both eyes is narrow and straight ahead (80%).
- Horizontal pupils, typical of prey animals, gather a wide strip of the horizon (80%).
- Many more rods than cones. Rods are better motion sensors (80%). So: motion and outline give you away, not colour (85%).
- Straight behind is a small blind spot (80%). Do not count on it; deer turn their heads all day (Tip).
- Nervous mule deer walk in a "stiff and jerky manner" (80%, US National Park Service). That walk means it has noticed something.
```

## The eyes: colour and low light

```diagram mmd-colour-strip
```

- Two colour types: blue and yellow green. No red cone (80 to 85%).
- Blaze orange looks dull; blue and UV stand out (75%).

```more colour, ultraviolet and night
- Deer have 2 cone types, peaking near 455 nm (blue) and 537 nm (green). No red cone (85%, Jacobs 1994 via research notes).
- They can tell blue from red but not green from red (80%, Penn State).
- So blaze orange looks dull, close to yellowish grey (75%). The colour chart is a rough sketch, not exact (Tip).
- Deer lack a UV (ultraviolet) filter in the eye, so they may pick up UV glow (80%, Penn State). UV brighteners in some detergents may glow to a deer (65%, Tip). Wash in unscented, no brightener soap.
- Low light: rods are more than 1,000 times as sensitive as cones (80%, Penn State). A reflective layer behind the retina (tapetum) sends light back through the receptors; that is the eyeshine in your headlights (80%).
- Lights: a moving headlamp or phone screen in the dark is pure motion. Red light, pointed at the ground (Tip). Hunting with a light is unlawful (99%, page 11, see [legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)).
```

## How it combines the senses

```diagram mmd-sense-check
```

- Hears it, looks for it, tries to smell it, then goes (Tip).
- Freeze while it is only ears and eyes (Tip).

```more the sequence and the alarm stages
- A sound or motion gets its attention; it stares, bobs its head, stamps, and often tries to circle downwind to smell you. Once it smells you, it leaves (Tip, built on the alarm stages in [How a deer thinks](#/s/deer-mind)).
- The 5 alarm stages: alert stare, head bob, foot stamp, snort, escape (70%, hunter lore and research notes).
- The metatarsal gland releases alarm scent that warns other deer (85%, BC account; biologists via Buckmasters).
- Other deer watch each other: one alarmed deer alerts the group (Tip).
- A deer that cannot see or smell what it heard may come closer to look (Tip). Curiosity is a mule deer weakness (see chapter 4).
```

```steps 5 stages of alarm
sg-alarm-1 | Alert: head up, staring. Freeze.
sg-alarm-2 | Head bob: fakes feeding, snaps up.
sg-alarm-3 | Foot stamp: it wants you to move.
sg-alarm-4 | Snort: every deer near is alert.
sg-alarm-5 | Escape: the mule deer stots away.
```

## Beat each sense

```diagram mmd-beat-senses
```

- Nose: wind. Ears: rhythm and soft fabric. Eyes: stillness (Tip).
- Use cover and shadow, never the skyline (Tip).

```more the full kit and drills
- **Nose:** keep the wind in your face or quartering; plan by thermals; check the wind every 10 minutes; leave if it turns (Tip).
- **Ears:** walk, stop, walk, like a deer; soft wool or fleece; tape sling swivels; no talking, use signals (Tip, chapter 9).
- **Eyes:** move only when its head is down or behind cover; stay below ridgelines; sit in shadow with something behind you; glass, do not walk (Tip).
- The BC synopsis has no hunter orange requirement (90%, via [What to wear](#/s/what-to-wear)). Wear it anyway for safety (Tip). It looks dull to deer (75%).
- Drill: sit 20 minutes on a slope at dusk with the wind checker out. Note every time it changes (Tip).
```

```checklist mmd-senses-kit
Wind checker (puffer, thread or milkweed)
Unscented soap, no UV brightener
Soft outer layer, no nylon swish
Tape on sling swivels and pack buckles
Red light headlamp
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mmd-beat-senses
```

- **Rhyme:** "Nose knows, ears hear, eyes see the move. Wind in your face, slow is smooth."
- **Number trick:** 310 degrees of view; 4 to 8 kHz best hearing; 2 colour cones (80 to 85%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "You can fool the eyes, you can fool the ears, you will never fool the nose."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Sun on the hill, scent climbs still; sun goes down, scent slides down."
- **Number trick:** 115 Hz to 54 kHz: the deer's whole hearing range (85%).
- **Number trick:** 1,000: rods are over 1,000 times more light sensitive than cones (80%).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely taught, opinion): "Wind first, then the deer. If the wind is wrong, go somewhere else."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "When it stares, you are a stump."

> [!mistake]
> - Checking the wind once at the truck.
> - Hunting a draw in the evening from above.
> - Moving while a deer is staring at you.

> [!field]
> This week: sit one evening on a slope with a puffer bottle. Write the time the air started to drain downhill.
```
