## Skeleton and muscle

```diagram mql-muscle-burst
```

- Breast muscle is mostly white fibre: fast, short, little staying power (70%).
- So: a whirring burst, a glide, then they land and run (75%).

```more bones and muscle in full
- Bird bones are light. Some are hollow and connect to the air sacs ("pneumatic bones") (70%, Poultry Hub).
- The breastbone has a deep keel. The big flight muscles anchor to it (70%).
- In fowl the breast muscles are mostly white fibres: they lack myoglobin, contract fast and tire fast. Fowl fly short distances with very rapid wingbeats (70%, Poultry Hub muscular system).
- Quail run rather than fly, and fly as a last resort (70%, Birder's Handbook via Stanford). When pushed they burst into fast, low flight on whirring wings (75%, Audubon).
- Leg muscles are darker and built for walking and running all day (Tip, 65%: red fibres carry myoglobin for steady work, Poultry Hub).
- Hunter meaning: a quail flight is short. Watch it down. Singles often land a few dozen yards away (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife).
```

## How a bird breathes

```anim mql-airsac-breath Two breaths move one gulp of air through the lung.
```

- No diaphragm. Ribs and breastbone pump nine air sacs (70%).
- Fresh air crosses the lung on the breath in and the breath out (70%).

```more the air sac system in full
- Fowl have nine air sacs: one clavicular, two cervical, two cranial thoracic, two caudal thoracic and two abdominal (70%, Poultry Hub).
- The air sacs are thin balloons. They hold and move air but do not exchange gas. Gas exchange happens only in the lungs (70%).
- The lungs are small, stiff and pressed up against the ribs on the back (70%).
- Breathing: muscles swing the ribs and breastbone forward and back. That changes the body volume and pumps the sacs (70%).
- One gulp of air takes two breaths to pass through: rear sacs, lung, front sacs, out (70%, Poultry Hub; a vet interview in dvm360 describes the same).
- Hunter meaning: the lungs sit high in the back between the ribs, right beside the heart. Pellets that reach the front half of the body hit the engine room (Tip).
- Air sacs fill much of the body cavity. A hit there alone can let a bird fly on (Tip, 60%).
```

## Heart and big blood vessels

```diagram mql-body-cutaway
```

- Four chambered heart, low in the chest, behind the crop (70%).
- Heart and lungs together: about a thumbnail on a quail (Tip).

```more the heart in full
- The bird heart has two atria and two ventricles, like ours (70%, Poultry Hub circulatory system).
- It sits low in the chest with its point to the rear (70%).
- Big vessels leave the heart forward to the neck and wings and back to the body (70%).
- The head and neck carry the brain, spinal cord and big neck vessels. That is why the head and front of the bird is the target in chapter 10 (Tip).
```

## Crop and gizzard

```diagram mql-gut-path
```

- The crop stores a meal. The gizzard grinds it with grit (70%).
- Open a crop after the hunt: it tells you today's food (Tip).

```more digestion in full
- No teeth. Food goes from the beak down the gullet to the crop, a stretchy pouch at the base of the neck that holds a meal (70%, Poultry Hub).
- Then the glandular stomach adds acid and enzymes, and the gizzard, a thick muscle with a horny lining, grinds the food with grit (70%).
- Two blind pouches, the caeca, ferment tough plant food (70%).
- Young quail pick up the gut microbes they need by eating adult droppings (65%, BC Outdoors).
- California quail eat mostly seeds and green leaves; legume seeds lead the list (80%, California wildlife habitat records). Insects are under 5% of the adult diet (70%, Birder's Handbook) but chicks eat many (80%).
- Grit: Washington Fish and Wildlife says quail pick grit at roadside edges before the morning and afternoon feeds (75%). Gravel lanes next to cover are worth a look.
- In cool weather, quail can get water from green plants, insects and dew (80%). In hot weather they need free water every day (80%).
```

## Body heat

```diagram mql-heat
```

- Body heat about 41 to 41.7 °C in fowl (70%).
- Hot day: they pant and need water daily (80%).

```more heat in full
- Fowl body temperature: 41 to 41.7 °C; death near 46 °C (70%, Poultry Hub, chicken figures).
- Feathers and a fat layer under the skin hold heat in the cold (70%).
- In heat, birds pant and flutter the throat to lose heat by evaporation (70%).
- California quail roost in dense trees or shrubs, not on the ground (70%, Birder's Handbook). BC winters are hard on them: one Okanagan naturalist says a bad winter is the main thing that knocks them back (60%, iNFOnews).
- Gray partridge burrow into snow to reach seeds (75%, Audubon). Chukar move down out of heavy snow and onto south slopes (80%, FEIS).
```

## Lifespan and survival

```diagram mql-survival
```

- Few quail live past their first birthday (65%).
- Most of a fall covey is young of the year (60%).

```more lifespan in full
- California quail can live about six years, but few live much past one year (65%, BC Outdoors).
- An early California banding study: of 101 birds, 52 were known alive at 6 months, 15 at one year, 4 at two years and 1 at three years (60%, search preview).
- Predators: Cooper's and sharp shinned hawks, great horned owls, snakes, ground squirrels, house cats, coyotes, skunks, bobcats and jays (80%, California wildlife habitat records).
- Numbers rise after wet years with good green growth; rain drives the hatch (80%).
- Chukar can live up to 10 years in BC, per one writer (60%, BC Outdoors).
- Hunter meaning: a covey is a renewable crop of young birds. Good spring rain, good fall covey (Tip).
```

## What the body means for shot and meat

```diagram mql-shot-window
```

- Small bird, small shot: lead 7.5 or 8, steel 6 or 7 (75%).
- Aim for the head and front half: brain, neck, heart, lungs (Tip).

```more shot and meat in full
- One small pellet is often enough to bring down a quail (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife).
- A pattern that puts several pellets in the head and front body kills cleanly. A shot to the rear catches gut and air sacs: the bird may fly on (Tip).
- Breast meat is white and lean, so it dries out fast in the pan (Tip). Legs are darker and tougher (Tip, 65%).
- Gut contents and the crop sour fast in warm weather. Cool birds fast (Tip). Chapter 10 has the steps.
- Both breasts are [[Edible portions]] and must go home (99%, Rule Book).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mql-body-cutaway
```

- **Rhyme:** "Front half, fast fall; rear end, no kill at all." (Tip)
- **Number trick:** 9 air sacs, 4 heart chambers, 2 breaths per gulp (70%).

```more more hooks
- **Crop check:** "What's in the crop is what's on the slope." Open one crop after every hunt (Tip).
- **Muscle hook:** white meat flies, dark meat walks (Tip, 65%).
- **Old hand's rule (opinion):** "Watch it down. A quail never flies as far as you think." (Tip, backed by short flights, 75%)
```
