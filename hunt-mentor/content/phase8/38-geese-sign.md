> [!why]
> Goose hunters do not track a bird; they track a habit. Fresh droppings on the right patch of a field tell you where to lie down tomorrow.

## Tracks

```diagram mgs-track-scale
```

- Goose track: 10 to 12 cm, three webbed toes (65%).
- Duck track: half the size, about 5 to 7.5 cm (65%).

```photo hab-duck-tracks Webbed tracks in mud: three forward toes joined by skin.
```

```more tracks in full
- Canada goose print about 10 to 12 cm (4 to 4.75 in) long; one measured about 11 cm by 5.5 cm (65%, naturalist sources).
- Three forward toes joined by webbing, a small back toe that rarely prints, middle toe longest (65%).
- Prints turn in a little and alternate left and right, about 15 cm (6 in) apart (65%).
- Gull tracks top out near 9.5 cm (3.75 in) and are slimmer (60%).
- Where to look: soft mud at the water's edge, field puddles, sand bars (Tip).
- Many tracks pointing the same way along a shore: a loafing spot (Tip).
```

## Droppings

```diagram mgs-dropping-fresh
```

- Tubes 5 to 10 cm long, green on grass (65%).
- Wet and shiny means today (Tip).

```more droppings in full
- Tubular, 5 to 10 cm (2 to 4 in) long, a bit thinner than a finger, green when geese graze grass (65%, naturalist and wildlife control sources).
- They often lie in piles where geese feed and rest (65%).
- Lots of them: grass passes in about 1.5 hours and grazing geese digest only a third of it (70%). See [Chapter 2](#/s/mc-geese-02).
- Freshness ladder (Tip): wet, bright and shiny today; dull with a skin after a day or two; dry, pale and crumbly when old. Rain softens old ones and frost hardens new ones, so compare several spots.
- Many fresh droppings in one patch of a big field marks the X (65%, Ducks Unlimited goose guide).
```

## Feathers, down and grazed ground

```gallery
hab-geese-stubble | Feeding geese in stubble leave droppings, feathers and clipped stems.
hab-kamloops-pond-farm | Hay field edges by water: check for clipped grass and droppings.
```

- Body feathers and down on the X (Tip).
- Grass clipped short, grain picked clean (Tip).

```more what to look for
- Fresh down caught on stubble or floating at the water's edge: birds were there recently (Tip).
- Long flight feathers on summer shorelines: a moult site (70%). Geese drop all flight feathers at once in June to August (70%).
- Grazed grass looks mowed in patches, with droppings among the stems (Tip).
- Puddles in a field with tracks around the edge: a drinking spot (Tip).
```

## Calls as sign

```video
commons-canada-geese-flight | Canada geese in flight: listen to the honks.
```

- At dawn and dusk, listen from the road: honks show the roost and the line (Tip).
- High yelps in the flock: cacklers mixed in (75%).

```more how to listen
- Geese are very vocal in flight and in big flocks (75%, Ducks Unlimited).
- Cackling geese call higher than Canada geese (75%).
- Snow geese give a high "kow luk" (75%). White fronted geese a laughing "kow kow kow" (80%).
- Stand outside the truck with the engine off for 10 minutes before first light (Tip).
```

## Scouting the X

```steps Scout the X in 5 steps
mgs-x-1 | Glass fields from a public road at the last hour of light.
mgs-x-2 | Watch where they land. Pin the exact spot.
mgs-x-3 | Let them leave on their own. Never bump them.
mgs-x-4 | With permission, find the thickest fresh droppings.
mgs-x-5 | Plan the hide on the X or in cover nearby.
```

- They usually return to yesterday afternoon's field (65%).

```more the full scouting method
- Geese usually return to the previous afternoon's feeding spot in the morning (65%, Ducks Unlimited goose guide, Wade Bourne).
- Wait until the flock flies to roost, walk out (with permission), check for fresh droppings, and save the spot on your GPS (Global Positioning System) or as a Spot on the Hunt Map (65%).
- Set up in cover 45 to 90 m (50 to 100 yd) off the X if the X itself is bare; hunting from cover often beats lying on the exact spot (65%, Casey Self, Ducks Unlimited guide).
- Scout where you can see without trespassing: use the "What can I see from here" tool. See [Chapter 7](#/s/mc-geese-07).
- Law: no loaded gun in or on a vehicle while you scout; no shooting from a vehicle or in the [[Road allowance]] (99%, quick lesson rules).
```

## Following a flock without chasing it

```anim mgs-flight-line Follow the line, not the birds.
```

- Drive the roads ahead of the flock, stop, glass (Tip).
- Note time, field, wind and count in your Journal (Tip).

```more how to do it
- Goose flocks fly kilometres between roost and field. Use binoculars and public roads; never follow a flock across private land (99% on trespass).
- Note the direction they leave the roost and the time. Next evening wait farther along that line (Tip).
- A flock that circles and lands in the same field two days running is your best bet (Tip, 65%).
- Keep a record: date, time, field, wind, flock size. After a season you will know your fields better than any book (Tip). See [Journal guide](#/s/hunt-journal-guide).
```

## Memory hooks

> [!tip] Memory rhymes (made for this app)
> - "Green and wet, the best bet. Dry and white, not tonight."
> - "Glass at dusk, find the X; set at dawn, take the next."
> - "Three toes webbed, ten cm long: a goose walked here and moved along."

- Number trick: **10, 10, 1.5**. Track about 10 cm, droppings up to 10 cm, about 1.5 hours from grass to dropping (65 to 70%).

> [!rule] Old hand's rule (tradition, opinion)
> Never bump the birds when you scout. Watch them leave, then walk the field. Standard advice in goose hunting guides.
