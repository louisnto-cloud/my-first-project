## Habitat

```gallery

hab-north-thompson-burn | Burn: new green food under the snags.
hab-bear-berry-patch | Black bear in a fall berry patch.
g-bb-tofino | Coastal bear turning rocks at low tide.
```

- Forest land across BC; absent from alpine, grassland and cities (90%).
- Wet zones hold more bears than dry ones (90%).

```more habitat in detail
- BC account (90%): black bears live in "forestland across the province"; "generally absent from alpine, grassland, and heavily settled landscapes" but often near town edges.
- Habitats: "forests, wetlands, subalpine meadows, avalanche chutes, riparian habitats, and beaches" (90%).
- Clearcuts can give short term berry crops, but "dense second growth stands produce little bear food" (90%). So: young cutblocks good, closed young forest poor.
- Bear numbers are higher in wet zones and on the salmon coast (90%).
- Near Heffley Creek, the dry grassland and sagebrush bottoms have few bears; the timbered slopes, creek draws and cutblocks above hold them (Tip, my reading of the account).
```

## Foods by month

```diagram mc-black-bear-food-calendar
```

- Plants about 80% of the diet (75%).
- Rich, easy food beats everything (90%).

```photo ts-clearcut-holmes Spring food: the bright green avalanche chutes on the far slope green up early.
```

```more foods in detail
- Spring (90%, BC account): meadows, estuaries, riparian strips, skunk cabbage swamps, avalanche chutes, grassy south facing slopes, burns; newborn deer, elk, moose and caribou.
- Summer (90%): insects and larvae, fruits, berries, salmon, carrion.
- Fall (90%): berries and spawning fish. Small mammals all year. Rarely adult moose or elk.
- WildSafeBC (75%): grasses, sedges, horsetails, skunk cabbage in spring; high calorie berries summer and fall; rodents, fish, insects, carrion.
- Bears also strip bark for the cambium in spring, which can kill trees (90%, BC account).
```

- Ripe berries move upslope through the summer (70%).
- A dense patch beats scattered bushes (90%).

```diagram rp-bear-calories
```

```more berries in detail
- Black bears "seek out concentrated food sources such as spawning fish and dense berry patches" (90%, BC account).
- Soopolallie ripens late July to mid August (65%, Sierra Club BC). Huckleberry crops are dropping in some years with heat and fire (65%, Washington State University).
- Calories per kg of fruit: huckleberry about 366, blackberry about 430, chokecherry about 549 (65%, [berries and mast](#/s/berries-mast)).
- Find the ripe patch this week; last week's patch may be done (Tip).
- Seed filled scat on a trail tells you which berry is on (Tip, chapter 8).
```

## Salmon and the coast

```video
commons-black-bear-salmon | Black bears fishing a salmon run.
```

- Coast bears are denser because of salmon (90%).
- Fish fed bears can taste fishy (Tip).

```more salmon in detail
- "Coastal bear densities are higher because of access to spawning salmon" (90%, BC account).
- Reimchen, Haida Gwaii: bears carried about 3,700 salmon (about 10,000 kg) into the forest along 800 m of one stream in a season, eating about half of each fish (75%, University of Victoria).
- Salmon collapses have reduced bears' fall food in places (90%, BC account).
- Fraser Valley: bears use salmon streams where fish run; check local runs (VERIFY timing for each stream).
- Interior: few salmon near Heffley Creek; berries and fruit are the fall engine (Tip, my reading).
- Meat: hunters report that bears feeding on fish, especially dead salmon, taste fishy (Tip, hunting media). Chapter 10.
```

## Elevation by month

```diagram mc-black-bear-elevation
```

- Spring: low south slopes, valley bottoms (85%).
- Summer: chutes, burns, high berries. Fall: back down (70%).

```more elevation in detail
- Alaska (85%): spring at low south facing slopes, riparian forest and wetlands; summer mid elevation herb meadows and high openings; fall berries in subalpine and salmon rivers.
- BC account: spring food on grassy south facing slopes and avalanche chutes (90%).
- Berries ripen later with elevation (70%, [berries and mast](#/s/berries-mast)).
- Dens can be anywhere from valley bottom to high slopes (85%, Alaska).
- The line on the chart is a shape, not data (Tip).
```

## Your three areas

```diagram mc-black-bear-three-areas
```

- Heffley Creek (Region 3), Fraser Valley (Region 2), South Okanagan (Region 8) all have bear seasons (99%).
- Check your exact MU (Management Unit) in the Rule Book (99%).

```more each area in detail (no new rules)
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- **Heffley Creek, Region 3:** fall near Heffley Creek (Tip, quick lesson): berry slopes, old orchards, cutblock edges, creek bottoms with hawthorn and rose hips, grassy road margins. Spring: south facing grassy slopes, road edges and cutblocks that green up first (65%). Seasons: [Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3), local page: [Heffley Creek](#/s/local-heffley).
- **Fraser Valley, Region 2:** bears near farms and houses; no hunting or shooting within 100 m (109 yd) of a dwelling, farm building, school or regional district park (99%, [where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt)). Fraser Valley Special Area rules: see [Region 2](#/s/rb-region-2) and [Mission](#/s/local-mission).
- **South Okanagan, Region 8:** August bear hunting only on private land with the owner's permission (99%); orchards draw bears in fall (Tip). Seasons: [Region 8](#/s/rb-region-8), local page: [Okanagan](#/s/local-okanagan).
- Harvest numbers by MU: chapter 1.
```

## Find bears on the Hunt Map

```steps Find a bear slope on the Hunt Map
fn-hm-slope | Slope steepness: find benches and gentle faces.
fn-hm-aspect | Slope direction: south and west for spring green.
fn-hm-viewshed | What can I see: pick a glassing seat.
fn-hm-sun | Sun and shade: who gets first and last light.
```

- Turn on Cutblocks by age and Burns by year (99%).
- Then glass the edges (Tip).

```more Hunt Map steps in detail
- Habitat layers: Cutblocks by age (5 to 20 years old feed bears, app tip) and Burns by year (3 to 15 years old grow food, app tip) (99%, [Hunt Map](#/s/hunt-map-pro)).
- Spring: Slope direction south and southwest, under 25 degrees, low elevation, near cover (Tip).
- Fall: cutblocks and burns with berries, creek draws, orchards on private land with permission (Tip).
- Run What can I see from a knob over the slope; save it as a Glassing point waypoint (99% app feature; Tip use).
- Turn on Private land and MU (Management Unit) lines before you go (99%, app).
- Open the [Hunt Map](#/map).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-black-bear-food-calendar
```

- **Rhyme:** "Spring is green, summer is sweet, fall is fat and fish to eat."
- **Number trick:** 80: about 80% of a bear's diet is plants (75%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, common hunting advice): "Find the food, find the bear."

```more more hooks
- **Number trick:** 5 to 20: cutblock age in years that feeds bears (app tip).
- **Number trick:** 3,700 salmon: one Haida Gwaii stream's bears in a season (75%).
- **Tip:** last week's berries feed no bear.

> [!mistake]
> - Glassing grassland and sagebrush bottoms for fall bears.
> - Hunting a patch after the fruit is gone.
> - Forgetting the 100 m dwelling rule near Fraser Valley farms.

> [!field]
> On the Hunt Map: find one cutblock 5 to 20 years old near Heffley Creek with a south face and a glassing knob. Save both as waypoints.
```
