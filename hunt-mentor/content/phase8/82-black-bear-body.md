## Frame, feet and claws

```diagram mc-black-bear-claws
```

- Flat footed (plantigrade), five toes, short curved claws (90%).
- Built to climb trees, not to dig (90%).

```more the frame in detail
- BC account (90%): "a chunky body, small black eyes, a broad head, rounded ears, a short tail, and a fine, long pelage"; feet "flat soled (plantigrade), with naked pads and five toes with relatively short curved claws".
- Grizzlies have "much longer claws that are adapted for digging, whereas the shorter, curved claws of black bears are well suited for tree climbing" (90%).
- A black bear can run equally fast uphill or downhill and is an excellent climber (75%, WildSafeBC). Lean bears can top 48 km/h (30 mph) (70%, North American Bear Center). You cannot outrun one (Tip).
- Long fur hides the true outline of the body and legs. The chest is lower than it looks (Tip).
```

## How it breathes

```diagram mc-black-bear-anatomy
```

- Lungs fill the chest behind the shoulder (85%).
- A muscle wall, the diaphragm, pumps them (90%).

```more breathing in detail
- Like all mammals, a bear breathes with its diaphragm: the dome shaped muscle between the chest and belly pulls air in as it flattens (90%, basic mammal anatomy).
- The lungs are the biggest target in the chest. A shot through both lungs collapses them; the bear runs on held oxygen for a short distance (75%, hunter education).
- In the den, oxygen intake drops by half (90%, BC account).
- Bright, frothy blood on a trail means lungs (70%, chapter 10).
```

## Heart and big vessels

```diagram vitals-bear
```

- Halfway up, tight behind the shoulder, near leg forward (85%).
- Lungs reach past the middle of the body (70%).

```more heart and vessels in detail
- The heart sits low in the chest, behind the shoulder; the lungs reach past the middle of the body; the chest is compressed front to back and the shoulder sits farther forward than a deer's (70 to 85%, [shot placement](#/s/shot-placement), from the IBEP (International Bowhunter Education Program) bear guide).
- Fat is 2.5 to 5 cm thick and the hair adds 8 to 10 cm: the bear looks bigger than it is (70%).
- Everyone agrees: halfway up the body, tight behind the shoulder once the near leg steps forward, through both lungs. Vital zone about a 20 cm (8 in) circle (85%).
- The big vessels run from the top of the heart along the underside of the spine. A shot high in the lungs can still cut them (Tip).
- The leg and shoulder bones shield the heart and lungs when the near front leg is back in its stride; wait for the leg to step forward (70%, Virginia hunting tips adapted from the National Bowhunter Education Foundation).
- Thick fur and fat soak up blood: bears often leave a thin blood trail (Tip, quick lesson).
```

## One stomach, short gut

```diagram mc-black-bear-gut
```

- A carnivore's gut that eats mostly plants (90%).
- So it hunts rich, easy food (90%).

```more digestion in detail
- Bears are "classified as carnivores (meat eaters), but they are omnivorous" and "seek out low fibre, easily digestible foods" (90%, BC account).
- Plants are most of the diet: about 80% (75%, WildSafeBC). Plants dominate in spring and summer (90%).
- Because they digest fibre poorly, bears go for concentrated foods: spawning fish, dense berry patches, insects, newborn deer, elk and moose (90%, BC account).
- What this means for you: find the richest food in season and you find the bear (Tip, chapter 7).
- Gut care: one stomach and a short gut. Keep the gut whole when you field dress (Tip, chapter 10).
```

## Fat through the year

```diagram mc-black-bear-fat-year
```

- Lean in spring, fattest just before the den (85%).
- Fall bears may eat up to 20,000 calories a day (70%).

```more the fat year in detail
- About 20% heavier in fall than after the den (85%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game). Some agencies report 20 to 50% gains in fall (65%).
- "Only about six months to build up fat reserves for hibernation" (90%, BC account).
- Fall feeding frenzy: [[Hyperphagia]], up to 20,000 calories a day (70%, WildSafeBC). Chapter 5 has the timing.
- A fall bear carries a thick fat layer under the hide; a spring bear little (75%, hunting media).
- Fat and a thick hide hold heat: skin a bear fast or the meat spoils (75%, Maryland Department of Natural Resources). Chapter 10.
- Rendered fall bear fat makes good lard, if the bear ate berries, not fish (Tip, hunting media).
```

## The den body

```anim mc-black-bear-den-beat Heart slows from about 55 to 14 a minute.
```

- Heart about 50 to 55 drops to 10 to 14 a minute (80 to 90%).
- No eating, drinking, peeing or pooping for months (90%).

```diagram mc-black-bear-den-body
```

```more den physiology in detail
- BC account (90%): heart rate "drops from about 50 beats per minute to around 10"; oxygen intake halves; body temperature drops by about 3°C; no eating, drinking, urinating or defecating; up to a quarter of body weight lost.
- University of Alaska study (Tøien and others, Science 2011, via ScienceDaily) (80%): metabolism fell to about 25% of normal; body temperature only 5 to 6°C lower, cycling between 30 and 36°C; heart from about 55 to about 14 beats a minute; metabolism stayed low for 2 to 3 weeks after den exit.
- WildSafeBC: about 30% of body weight lost over winter (75%).
- What it means: a spring bear is lean, slow to recover, and hungry. That is why spring hunting focuses on the first green food (Tip, chapter 7).
```

## Lifespan and breeding age

```diagram mc-black-bear-cub-timeline
```

- Sows usually first breed at about 4, then every 2 to 3 years (90%).
- Few live past 15 to 20 in the wild (70%).

```more life history numbers
- BC account (90%): females usually mature at about 4 years and breed every 2 to 3 years; where food is scarce, first litter at 6 or 7. Most males mature at 5 or 6. Captive bears live 25 to 30 years; wild bears usually much less.
- WildSafeBC: over 30 years possible, 15 to 20 more typical; cub mortality around 50% (75%).
- Low birth rate means a population recovers slowly. That is why the law protects sows with young (Tip, my reading).
```

| Thin blood trail (Tip) | Wait, then track slowly |

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-black-bear-den-body
```

- **Rhyme:** "Low heart, big lungs, tight to the leg."
- **Number trick:** 55 to 14: summer to den heartbeats a minute (80%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, echoing Alaska and Maryland agency advice): "A bear's hide is a winter coat. Get it off the meat."

```more more hooks
- **Number trick:** 25: metabolism in the den is about 25% of normal; a quarter of the weight is lost (80 to 90%).
- **Number trick:** 4 and 2 to 3: a sow first breeds near 4, then every 2 to 3 years (90%).
- **Number trick:** 20,000: fall calories a day (70%).
- **Rhyme:** "Curved claws climb, straight claws dig."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, from hunter education vitals advice): "Wait for the near leg to step forward."

> [!mistake]
> - Aiming at the middle of a fluffy bear.
> - Leaving a fall bear whole overnight in its hide.
> - Thinking a spring bear and a fall bear handle the same.

> [!field]
> Before the hunt: study the [vitals drawing](#/s/black-bear) and point to the heart on three bear photos.
```
