## The nose: your scent cone

```anim mwt-scent-cone Your scent spreads in a cone downwind.
```

- Smell is its strongest sense (85%).
- Your scent fans out downwind, wider with distance (80%).

```more nose detail
- A deer's nose is its best sense (85%). The often quoted "297 million receptors" is folklore (45%, not a fact).
- Your scent comes from breath, skin, clothes, boots and your truck (Tip).
- It spreads downwind as a **cone**: narrow at you, wide far away (80%, physics of drifting air).
- Light wind swirls the cone. Steady wind keeps it tight and predictable (Tip).
- White tails often bed and travel so the wind brings them news from where they cannot see (Tip, hunter observation).
- The Hunt Map has a **Scent cone** tool: set the wind and see where your scent goes ([Hunt Map](#/map)).
```

## Thermals in the valley bottom

```anim mwt-valley-drain Evening: cool air slides down to the river.
```

- Evening: cool air drains downhill to the bottoms (80%).
- Morning sun: air rises up the slopes (80%).

```more thermals for white tail country
- Sun warms slopes in the morning, warm air rises, scent drifts **uphill** (80%).
- Slopes cool in the evening, cool air sinks, scent drifts **downhill** (80%).
- In a valley, that cool air pools along the river at dusk and dawn, right where white tails bed in willow and cottonwood (Tip, from thermal physics).
- So an evening stand on the slope above a river bottom can leak your scent down to the deer (Tip).
- Better: sit on the field edge with the bottom upwind, or on the bottom's downwind side (Tip).
- At dawn before sun hits, air still sinks. It flips once the slope warms (75%).
```

## The ears

```diagram sg-deer-hearing
```

- Hears about 115 Hz (hertz) to 54 kHz (kilohertz) (85%).
- Best at 4 to 8 kHz: zippers, Velcro, metal clicks (85%).

```more ears detail
- Deer hear from about 115 Hz (hertz) to 54 kHz (kilohertz), best around 4 to 8 kHz (85%, D'Angelo and others 2007).
- That best band is exactly zippers, Velcro, nylon swish, metal on metal and snapping twigs (85%).
- Each ear swivels on its own to point at a sound (75%).
- Natural rhythms (a squirrel, another deer: step, stop, step) pass. Steady two legged crunching does not (Tip).
- Idaho Fish and Game: white tails have very good hearing for danger and give a loud snort when alarmed (80%).
```

## The eyes: wide view

```diagram mwt-field-of-view
```

- Sees about 310 degrees without turning its head (80%).
- Poor depth and detail; great at motion (80%).

```more field of view detail
- Eyes on the sides of the head give about a 310 degree view (80%, Penn State Deer Research; some sources say 300).
- The blind spot is a narrow wedge straight behind (80%).
- Only a narrow zone in front is seen by both eyes, so depth judgement is poor (80%).
- **Horizontal pupil**: a wide slit that scans the horizon, typical of prey animals (80%).
- Rods give strong motion detection and good low light vision (80%).
- A reflective layer behind the retina (tapetum lucidum) brightens dim light. That is the eye shine in headlights (75%).
```

## The eyes: colour and motion

```diagram sg-deer-vision
```

- No red cone: blaze orange looks dull (75%).
- Motion and outline give you away (85%).

```more colour detail
- Deer have 2 colour cones, peaking near 455 nm (blue) and 537 nm (green). No red cone (85%, Jacobs and others 1994).
- So blaze orange looks dull, close to grey or brown (75%). Blue stands out more (65%, Tip: skip blue jeans).
- In low light, deer are most sensitive to blue and blue green (80%, Penn State).
- Deer see more ultraviolet (UV) than we do (75%). Unscented soap without UV brighteners (Tip, 65%).
- A moving phone screen or headlamp in the dark is pure motion (Tip).
```

## Out of the eye line

```diagram mwt-eye-level
```

- Height takes you out of the main view (Tip).
- Deer do look up. Stillness still matters most (Tip).

```more stands and eye level
- A white tail scans the horizon at its own eye level (80%, horizontal pupil).
- A tree stand or a ground blind with a dark back wall hides your outline (Tip, hunter consensus).
- Ground hunters: sit in shadow with something behind you, not on the skyline (Tip).
- Deer that have been hunted from stands learn to look up (Tip).
- Tree stand safety: wear a full body harness and stay clipped in from the ground up (Tip, widely taught tree stand safety). Ground blinds avoid the fall risk (Tip).
```

## How it puts the senses together

```diagram mwt-sense-flow
```

- Ears and eyes raise doubt. The nose decides (Tip).
- Unsure white tails often circle downwind (Tip).

```more how they combine
- A white tail often hears you first, then looks for motion (Tip, hunter observation).
- If it sees something odd but cannot smell it, it may stamp, head bob or stare to make you move (70%).
- Many hunters report unsure white tails circling to get downwind (Tip, widely reported, not measured).
- Once the nose confirms: snort, flag, gone (80%).
- The full alarm ladder (alert, head bob, stamp, snort, run) is in [How a deer thinks](#/s/deer-mind) and chapter 4.
```

## How to beat each sense

| Sense | Beat it with | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Nose | Wind and thermals (80%) | Hunt a bad wind (Tip) |
| Ears | Wool, slow feet (Tip) | Zippers, Velcro (85%) |
| Eyes | Stillness, shade (85%) | Skyline, quick moves (Tip) |

- Check the wind every 10 minutes (Tip).
- Move only when its head is down or behind cover (Tip).

```more the full plan
- **Nose:** pick stands by the wind for the hour you sit. Have a plan B stand for a different wind (Tip).
- **Nose:** walk in on a route your scent will not cross the trail (Tip).
- **Ears:** quiet outer layer, tape metal on sling and pack (Tip).
- **Ears:** walk like a deer: step, step, stop (Tip).
- **Eyes:** shade, a background, slow hands (Tip).
- **All three:** arrive early and let the woods settle (Tip).
- Deer urine lures from outside BC are banned for hunting (95%, synopsis page 12 item 51, via [How a deer thinks](#/s/deer-mind)). Wind beats lures anyway (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mwt-field-of-view
```

- **Rhyme:** "Nose knows, ears hear, eyes see what moves near."
- **Number trick:** 310 degrees, 4 to 8 kHz, 2 cones.
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Hunt the wind, or hunt somewhere else."

```more more hooks, mistakes and field drill
- **Rhyme:** "Sun up, scent up. Sun down, scent down."
- **Old hand's rule** (widely taught, opinion): "You can fool the eyes and the ears. You can never fool the nose."
- **Number trick:** 360 minus 310 = a 50 degree blind spot behind.

> [!mistake]
> - Sitting an evening stand above a river bottom: your scent drains down to them.
> - Unzipping a pack when a deer is in sight.
> - Turning your head quickly when you hear a step.

> [!field]
> Next sit: hang a thread on your rifle. Note the wind direction every 10 minutes in the Journal, plus when it flips.
```
