## Find, judge, aim

```diagram mc-skills-optic-jobs
```

- Binoculars find. Spotting scope judges. Rifle scope aims (Tip).
- Never glass with the rifle scope: the muzzle follows your eye (95%).

```more the three tools in numbers
- "10x42": 10 is the magnification, 42 the front lens in mm (85%, Vortex optics guide).
- Exit pupil = front lens ÷ power. 8x42 = 5.25 mm, 10x42 = 4.2 mm (85%).
- Your pupil: about 2 to 3 mm in bright light, 4 to 5 mm at dawn and dusk (85%, Vortex). So any good binocular is bright at noon; the first and last half hour is where size and glass quality show (70%).
- "7x or 8x magnification is considered adequate for woodland settings, while 10x is preferred for viewing at greater distances" (85%, Vortex).
- Spotting scopes run about 20 to 60x and "must be mounted on a tripod" (85%, Vortex).
- First rule of firearm safety: never point a firearm at anything you do not intend to shoot (95%, [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety)). Glassing with the rifle scope sweeps the muzzle over everything you look at (95%, follows from that rule).
- Full set up (diopter, eyecups, prices): [Optics mastery](#/s/optics-mastery).
```

## The tripod: the quiet upgrade

```diagram hf-shake
```

- A steady view shows the ear flick a shaky view hides (65%).
- Sit, brace, then glass. Standing glass is a quick look only (Tip).

```more why steady beats bigger
- "A successful mule deer glasser almost always uses tripods. The movement you are looking for is often a tiny flick or twitch" (65%, goHUNT mule deer glassing tips, search preview).
- DuWane Adams, a long time guide, uses at least 15x binoculars on a tripod for long glassing (65%, NSSF (National Shooting Sports Foundation) Let's Go Hunting, read 2026-10-11). A guide in Field and Stream calls 15x binoculars "too heavy to hold steady offhand, but easy to use on a tripod" (60%, search preview).
- Josh Kirchner (onX, 2023) spends "one hour at a time at each spot before moving" and warns against thinking you saw everything in 10 minutes (65%, read 2026-10-11).
- A light tripod plus a binocular adapter is enough to start (Tip, [Glassing](#/s/glassing)).
- No tripod: back on a tree, elbows on knees, or binoculars on the top of a pack (Tip).
```

## Your call: near first or far first?

```diagram mc-skills-glass-order
```

- Two schools. Both are used by good hunters. Pick one and stick to it (Tip).
- The app does not choose for you. Your call.

| | Near first | Far first |
|---|---|---|
| Idea | Catch the animal that already sees you | Catch distant animals before they bed or heat haze rises |
| Who says it | Josh Kirchner, onX (65%) | Remi Warren, Western Hunter (65%) |
| Risk | Far animals slip into timber | You spook a close one |

```more both sides in their words
> [!lean]
> Your call. Both orders are taught. Try each for a week of scouting and keep the one that finds you more animals.

### Near first
- Kirchner (onX, 14 July 2023): "The first thing I do when I arrive at a glassing location is not glass, but actually look around with my naked eye." Then "I'll look close and in obvious areas first" (65%, read 2026-10-11).
- A mule deer guide prefers glassing closer areas first, partly to spot bucks that may already have him pegged before they leave; distant deer "have no idea" he is there (60%, search preview).
- Petersen's guide: a quick scan of your nearest surroundings, then a grid of the wider area (60%, search preview).

### Far first
- Remi Warren (Western Hunter): "start by quickly glassing all the obvious openings, meadows, or feeding areas", and "Pick the furthest points to look first before the heat waves distort your vision" (65%, read 2026-10-11).
- An elk hunter on a forum looks at the far or high openings first so elk do not slip away at first light, then the smaller openings, then grids it all (55%: Tip, forum).

### What both agree on
- Naked eye glance first, a quick pass of the obvious open ground, then a slow grid (65%).
- Glass the same ground again later: animals stand up, move and turn as light changes (Tip).
- Note: the older [Glassing](#/s/glassing) grid picture shows near first. That is one of the two options, not a rule (Tip).
```

## The grid: slow strips

```anim hf-glass-sweep Slow strips, a third overlap, stop and look at each view.
```

- Split the slope into strips. Glass each one slowly, with about a third overlap (60%).
- Fast pass for movement, slow pass for parts (65%).

```more the grid in full
1. Pick a seat with a wide view and the wind right ([Chapter 3](#/s/mc-skills-03)).
2. Naked eye look, then a quick binocular pass of the obvious openings (65%, Kirchner and Warren).
3. Split the view into strips in your head (65%).
4. Glass one strip slowly, stop on each view for a few seconds, then move about two thirds of a view (60%).
5. Drop one strip and come back the other way (65%).
6. Alternate "fast scans to locate movement and slow, detailed scans to examine brush, shadow" (60%, glassing guide via search preview).
7. Repeat. Old hands glass the same slope three, four, five times (Tip).
- DuWane Adams: methodical, top to bottom and side to side, until the whole area is covered (65%).
- With a fluid tripod head you can grid by panning; for thick cover, lock the head and grid with your eyes inside the view (65%, Kirchner).
```

## Light and the clock

```diagram mc-skills-light-angles
```

- Glass with the sun behind you or to the side. Never into it (Tip).
- Early: openings and feeding slopes. Later: shade and bedding cover (65%).

```more light, sun and heat haze
- Sun behind you lights the animal and leaves you in shade (Tip, [Glassing](#/s/glassing)).
- One guide: at sunrise glass west facing slopes; the rising sun lights them while you sit in relative shadow (60%, Tract Optics, search preview).
- Warren starts looking east when he can and saves the west for later, because animals stand out more once the sun hits them (65%). Those two tips differ: both use the light, not the compass (Tip).
- DuWane Adams: southeast faces early; northeast faces from about 9 a.m. when animals move to bed (65%).
- Heat waves (mirage) blur far slopes as the day warms. Glass far ground in the cool first hour (65%, Warren).
- Midday: glass shade, timber edges, under trees, benches (Tip). Bedded animals show as a head, an ear, an antler tip (65%).
- Legal light for deer: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (99%, [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)). Be in the seat before it.
```

## What to look for: parts

```diagram mc-skills-parts-grid
```

- Look for parts, not whole animals (65%).
- The flat back line, the ear V, the leg pole, the flicker (65%).

```more the search list
- A **horizontal line** in a world of vertical trees: a back or belly (65%, goHUNT; Adams via NSSF (National Shooting Sports Foundation): "horizontal lines like backlines against vertical lines like trees").
- **Vertical legs** under a shape that does not belong (65%).
- **Ears:** a V or a cup shape; a mule deer's big ears turn first (Tip).
- **Flicker:** an ear flick, tail twitch or head turn. Movement gives most animals away (65%).
- **Colour wrong for the place:** grey brown body on green, white rump, black nose, a shining antler tip (Tip).
- **Shapes that are too regular:** a rock that grows legs, a stump with an ear (Tip).
- Anything that looks out of place: put the scope on it (65%, Adams).
```

## Found one: now what?

```diagram mc-skills-found-flow
```

- Do not move. Keep eyes on it. Note landmarks (Tip).
- Judge with the scope. Then plan wind and cover (Tip).

```more after the find
1. Stay put and stay still. It has not seen you; keep it that way (Tip).
2. Pick two landmarks next to it: a dead tree, a rock, a gap (Tip, [Stalking footwork](#/s/stalking-footwork)).
3. Judge species, sex and legal points with the spotting scope before you move a step (Tip). Legal points: [Rule Book: definitions](#/s/rb-definitions).
4. Watch where it beds. DuWane Adams: when you cannot shoot right away, watch where the animal beds and plan the approach (65%).
5. Buddy up: one partner keeps eyes on it while the other moves in (65%, Adams). Agree hand signals first ([Chapter 4](#/s/mc-skills-04)).
6. Mark it on the [Hunt Map](#/map) and plan the route with wind, cover and dead ground ([Chapter 3](#/s/mc-skills-03) and [Chapter 4](#/s/mc-skills-04)).
```

## Glassing from the truck: ask first

```diagram mc-skills-truck-glass
```

- Rifle in the truck while you glass may count as hunting from a vehicle.
- Ask a conservation officer. Read the Rule Book lines first.

```more the two Rule Book lines
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Hunting includes "searching for" wildlife "while in possession of a firearm or other weapon" (99%, page 3, [Rule Book: definitions](#/s/rb-definitions)).
- It is unlawful "To hunt wildlife from a motor vehicle" (99%, page 11, item 22, [Rule Book: it is unlawful](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts)).
- The same definition is in the [Wildlife Act](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96488_01), s. 1 (99%, BC Laws, checked 2026-10-17). My reading: glassing from a parked truck with a rifle inside, even cased, is searching for wildlife while in possession of a firearm, from a motor vehicle. Treat it as unlawful (85%). Ask a [[Conservation officer]] if unsure (Tip).
- Clear and simple: scout from the truck with no firearm, or step away from the truck before you glass with your rifle (Tip). Loaded firearm rules for vehicles: item 28 in the same Rule Book screen (99%).
```

## What a 40 year glasser does differently

| Beginner | Old hand (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Walks to see more | Sits to see more |
| Glasses 10 minutes | Glasses an hour, then again |
| Looks for a deer | Looks for an ear |
| Glasses into the sun | Picks the seat for the light |
| Moves on a maybe | Puts the scope on every maybe |

- All lines are hunter wisdom, labelled Tip.

```more where these come from
- Sit and glass longer: Kirchner's one hour rule (65%); "Sit longer than you think" ([Glassing](#/s/glassing), Grandpa's rule, opinion).
- Parts, not animals: goHUNT, and Adams via the National Shooting Sports Foundation (65%).
- Light: Warren, Adams, Tract Optics (60 to 65%).
- Every maybe gets the scope: Adams, "inspect anything that looks out of place" (65%).
- Stay on a good spot even if nothing shows right away (65%, Adams: "Hunt the right spot").
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-skills-parts-grid
```

- **Rhyme:** "Back is flat, ear is a V, flicker is the thing you see."
- **Number trick:** 42 ÷ 8 = 5.25. Exit pupil = front lens ÷ power (85%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, short form of Kirchner's one hour rule, onX): "Sit an hour, then glass it again."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Find, judge, aim: three tools, three jobs."
- **Rhyme:** "Sun at your back, the deer goes black." It means lit animal, shaded you (Tip).
- **Number trick:** 1 third overlap, 1 hour per seat, 15x on a tripod for far ground (60 to 65%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, short form of "look for parts", goHUNT and Adams): "The deer you find is part of a deer."
- **Your call** (opinion): near first or far first. Pick one, write it in your journal, test it.

> [!mistake]
> - Glassing with the rifle scope.
> - Sweeping fast like a camera pan.
> - Leaving the seat after 10 minutes.

> [!field]
> Next scouting dawn: 1 hour on one slope with a tripod. Week 1 near first, week 2 far first. Log every animal and which order found it.
```
