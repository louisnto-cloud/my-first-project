> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The law is the law. If a change could alter your rifle's legal class, ask a qualified gunsmith and the RCMP (Royal Canadian Mounted Police) Canadian Firearms Program first.

```diagram cz-barrel-system
```

- Two screws hold the barrel in the receiver (80%).
- CZ: barrel and calibre change "at the highest user level" (95%).

```more how the system works
- "Modular system allowing barrel replacement and calibre change at the highest user level" (95%, CZ manual page 4).
- "The receiver, in which only two front screws retain the barrel, allows for quick and easy barrel changing ... simply remove the receiver from the stock and you'll see the two screws that secure the barrel" (80%, all4shooters test, 2020).
- The parts list names them "Barrel Screw (2x)", part 2 (95%, CZ manual page 12).
- Kits: "CZ's simple barrel change system enables caliber conversion to .17 HMR and .22 Winchester Magnum" (80%, all4shooters). HMR means Hornady Magnum Rimfire; WMR means Winchester Magnum Rimfire.
- Each calibre needs its own magazine; a bolt or extractor change may also be needed: VERIFY with CZ or your dealer before buying a kit.
- Barrels and magazines also fit the CZ 455 (95%, page 4).
```

## Who should swap it

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| You, at home | Cheap, quick (Tip) | CZ's stripping page says go no further than the bolt (95%) |
| Gunsmith | Headspace checked, screws torqued (Tip) | Costs a bench fee (VERIFY) |

> [!lean]
> My lean: first swap at a gunsmith, and ask them to check headspace. Watch, then decide.

```more why a gunsmith first
- The manual's caution: "Do not strip your firearm any further ... entrust this work to a competent gunsmith" (95%, page 9). Taking the stock off and loosening barrel screws goes past bolt removal (90%, my reading).
- Owners report stuck barrels and lost small parts when the receiver came apart (55%, forum reports: Tip only).
- Headspace is the fit between the bolt face and the chamber; aftermarket barrels may need shims and gauges (60%, barrel shim maker listing). Factory CZ barrels are meant to drop in (80%, all4shooters).
- Stock screws back to 3.5 Nm after (95%, CZ page 9).
```

## The law and the barrel

```diagram cz-law-lines
```

- The receiver is the firearm. A barrel is a "firearm part" (99%).
- Cut a rifle under 660 mm, or its barrel under 457 mm: prohibited (99%).

```more the legal text and what it means for you
> [!law] Criminal Code s. 84(1), "prohibited firearm" (b)
> "a firearm that is adapted from a rifle or shotgun, whether by sawing, cutting or any other alteration, and that, as so adapted, (i) is less than 660 mm in length, or (ii) is 660 mm or greater in length and has a barrel less than 457 mm in length"

- Plain English: never shorten your CZ or its barrel below those numbers (99%).
- "Restricted firearm" includes one with a barrel under 470 mm that fires centre fire ammunition semi automatically (99%, s. 84(1)). Your bolt action rimfire is not that (95%, my reading).
- "Firearm part means a barrel for a firearm" (99%, s. 84(1)). "Firearm" includes "any frame or receiver" (99%, s. 2).
- A factory barrel shorter than 457 mm swapped onto a rifle that came longer: could that be "any other alteration"? Not answered here. VERIFY with the RCMP Canadian Firearms Program before you buy one (Tip: keep barrels at 457 mm or longer).
- BC hunting: no firearm with a barrel under 305 mm (99%, synopsis page 12).
- Text current to 30 September 2026, read 2026-10-23.
```

## The trigger: screws A, B and C

```diagram cz-trigger-screws
```

- A: pull weight. B: travel after the shot. Both with the stock off (95%).
- C, in the trigger: travel before the shot (95%).

```more the manual's words
- "The adjustment of the trigger pull and trigger travel distance after firing (Fig. 6) is performed with the shoulder stock removed, using set screws A (pull) and B (travel after firing). The trigger travel distance before firing can be set using screw C positioned in the trigger (Fig. 7)" (95%, CZ manual page 7).
- Parts list: 53 trigger pull setting screw (A), 51 trigger dead travel screw (B), 49 trigger setting screw (C) (95%, page 12).
- Stock off means the stock screws come out: 3.5 Nm on reassembly (95%, page 9).
- My lean: let a gunsmith set it the first time (Tip). CZ's caution on further stripping applies (95%, page 9).
```

## How light is safe

```diagram cz-trigger-range
```

- Standard: 8 to 15 N, factory 11.1 to 14 N (95%).
- Comfort: 5 to 7 N, factory 6 N (95%).

```more limits, the safety check and the law
- "The standard trigger mechanism enables setting the trigger pull in the range of 8-15 N (the trigger pull is set by the manufacturer in the safe range of 11.1-14 N)" (95%, CZ manual page 7).
- "The Comfort trigger mechanism enables setting the trigger pull in the range of 5-7 N (... 6 ± 0.5 N)" (95%).
- In pounds: 8 to 15 N is about 1.8 to 3.4 lb, matching CZ USA's "1.8 to 3.4 lbs" (95%, conversion; 85% CZ USA page).
- "Do not set the trigger pull too low and the trigger travel too short because the rifle could unintentionally discharge a cartridge when being handled improperly" (95%).
- Safety check on the unloaded rifle: open and close the bolt repeatedly, move the safety on and off; "the rifle must not discharge" (95%).
- Law: a trigger that fires more than once per pull makes an "automatic firearm", which is a prohibited firearm (99%, Criminal Code s. 84(1)). Bump fire or trigger devices for semi automatics are prohibited devices too (99%, SOR/98-462 Part 4 s. 1).
- For a hunting rifle, light is not better: stay in the factory range (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram cz-law-lines
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): If a change makes you ask "is this legal?", stop and ask someone who knows, in writing.

> [!mistake]
> - Shortening a barrel because it "looks handier". That can make a prohibited firearm.
> - Setting the trigger below the range and skipping the safety check.
> - Buying a .22 WMR barrel and using .22 LR magazines.

> [!field]
> Write down your trigger setting and barrel calibre on a card in the case. Ask your gunsmith to sign the date.
