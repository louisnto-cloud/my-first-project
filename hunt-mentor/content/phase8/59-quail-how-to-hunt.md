> [!why]
> Quail hunting is permission, listening, slow walking and one safe shot at a time. The skill is in the feet and the ears more than the gun.

```diagram mql-method-flow
```

- Legal spot and permission first. Then match the method to the cover (Tip).
- Methods from Washington Fish and Wildlife (75%).

```more every legal method, at a glance
- Shotgun, air gun or bows B, C and D. No .22 or centrefire rifle for quail, chukar, partridge or pheasant (95 to 99%, [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)).
- Walking up (stop and go), pushing and blocking, hunting singles, glass and stalk for chukar, and a dog are all used (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife).
- Calls: a mouth call is fine (95%, my reading); "recorded bird calls to hunt game birds" are unlawful (99%).
- Live bird decoys: unlawful (99%). Bait rules: see the Rule Book.
- Retrieval is the law: "No person shall kill, cripple or wound game without making all reasonable effort to locate, dispatch, retrieve and include it in their bag limit." (99%)
```

## Permission first

```steps Ask a farmer in 5 steps
ld-ask-1 | Go in person on day 1, no guns showing.
ld-ask-2 | Say who you are and exactly what you ask.
ld-ask-3 | Ask about workers, dogs, irrigation and parking.
hf-permission-note | Write the OK down: name, date, area, phone.
ld-ask-5 | Offer birds or help, then text a thank you.
```

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Cultivated land needs consent, posted or not (99%). An orchard is cultivated land (95%).
- 100 m from any occupied house, barn or school (99%).

```more the law and the script, as the app states them
> [!law]
> "A person is not permitted to hunt on cultivated land or on Crown land which is subject to a grazing lease while the land is occupied by livestock, without the consent of the owner, lessee, or occupant of the land." (99%)

> [!law]
> "It is unlawful to hunt or discharge a firearm within 100 m of a church, school building, school yard, playground, regional district park, dwelling house, or farm or ranch building that is occupied by persons or domestic animals." (99%)

- An orchard is [[Cultivated land]] (95%). Orchards and vineyards are cultivated land (90%, trip plan).
- Go in person on day 1, daylight, clean clothes, no guns showing (Tip).
- Script: "My brother and I hunt quail with shotguns. Could we walk your brushy edges one weekday morning? We stay 100 m from every building, we never shoot toward the house or the road, and we close every gate." (Tip)
- Ask: where are the workers today, where are the dogs, where is the irrigation, where do you want us to park (Tip).
- Write the OK down with the name, date, area and phone. Keep it in the vest (60%, secondary legal advice).
- Offer cleaned birds or a hand with a chore. Send a thank you text that night (Tip).
- Indian Reserves are private land. Permission from the band office is needed to hunt on or across them (99%).
- Full detail: [Okanagan trip plan](#/s/local-okanagan) and [Rule Book: where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt).
```

## Stop and go

```anim mql-stop-go The stop, not the step, makes hiding birds flush.
```

- Walk a few steps, stop 15 to 20 seconds, repeat (75%).
- Stop near each patch of cover. Be ready on every stop (Tip).

```more stop and go in full
- "Each time you stop, wait at least 15 or 20 seconds before moving on; if there are quail hiding nearby, they're likely to get nervous and take to the air." (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife)
- Works for a lone hunter in open country with scattered patches of low or moderate cover (75%).
- For chukar, Washington suggests moving quickly, then stopping for 30 seconds near cover (75%).
- Missouri tip: moving slowly and stopping often can flush a covey or single that would let you walk right by (60%, bobwhite).
```

## Two hunters, or a dog

```steps Walk a brushy draw with two hunters
hf-draw-1 | Walk both edges, level, 15 to 25 m apart.
hf-draw-2 | Quail run ahead. Keep level and slow.
hf-draw-3 | Cover runs out: they burst. Pick one bird.
hf-draw-4 | Mark where singles land. Follow up.
```

- Each hunter owns one side. Never swing through your partner (Tip).
- A blocker at the end of a narrow strip stops runners (75%).

```more partners, blockers and dogs in full
- Groups of three or more can "play dog": some push heavy cover, others wait on the flanks or block the far end. Best in small areas and narrow strips; in big cover the birds just run or refuse to fly (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife).
- Blocker safety: agree a no shot arc before you start; the blocker is in the line of fire (Tip, from the quick lesson). Diagram: [Quail, chukar, partridge, pheasant](#/s/upland-birds).
- Blaze orange hat and vest on both of you in brush (Tip).
- Dogs: "Unleashed dogs may be used to hunt small game" and quail are fine with an unleashed dog (99%, [Rule Book: dogs](#/s/rb-legal-methods)). Training on game birds under supervision: 1 August to 30 April (99%).
- A dog that retrieves finds birds a person would never find (75%). Pointers suit low, open cover; a tough flusher suits thick brush (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife).
- No dog: shoot only over short cover and walk straight to the fall (Tip).
```

## The covey rise

```anim hf-covey-flush Pick one bird, high, in your own wedge.
```

- Pick one bird. Never shoot "the covey" (Tip).
- Straightaways: most hunters shoot high. Hold a hair under (75%).

```more the flush in full
- "Many a quail hunter, suddenly overwhelmed by a cloud of little gray rockets going off in every direction, has stood there stunned" (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife).
- Birds may climb steeply into the open or streak low through weeds and limbs (75%).
- "The tendency of most hunters is to shoot too high, especially at birds that are going straight away. Shooting just under those birds will pay much better dividends." (75%)
- Small birds look faster than they are (75%). Smooth swing, eyes on the bird's head: [Shotgun skills](#/s/shotgun-skills).
- Only birds above head height with open sky or ground beyond. A low bird is a free bird (Tip, from the quick lesson).
- Mark where the first bird falls before you swing on a second (60%, Missouri Conservationist).
```

## Hunting the singles

```anim mql-singles-call Scattered singles call. Walk to the nearest one.
```

- Right after the flush, singles hold tight: your best shots (75%).
- Listen for "chi CA go" and walk to the nearest caller (70 to 75%).

```more singles, calls and manners
- Singles may land only a few dozen yards away and hold very tight (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife).
- Mouth calls: hunters can get a covey talking with a quail call and use the answers to find birds (75%). Electronic or recorded calls: unlawful for game birds (99%).
- One Rokslide hunter: let it settle a few minutes, listen for regroup calls, then imitate them (Tip, one person's experience).
- Manners rule (opinion): "if three or more birds are harvested out of the rise, then you let the covey go." Quoted by Bill Palmer of Tall Timbers (60%, bobwhite).
- Same source: the evidence suggests hunting singles from a healthy covey is fine (60%).
- Stop a bit before dark to give them time to gather (Tip).
```

## Chukar and Huns

```diagram mql-chukar-contour
```

- Chukar: get above them, then follow the same contour (75%).
- Huns: cover a lot of ground; make the first chance count (75%).

```more chukar and Huns in full
- "Whenever possible, approach chukars from above. While they tend to fly downhill, they usually run uphill." (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife) Animation: [the chukar run](#/s/upland-birds).
- "Most birds will be at the same approximate elevation, so if you flush a covey at one point along a hillside, move uphill a little and continue along that line." (75%)
- Glass distant slopes for feeding or roosting birds, then stalk into position (75%). Listen for the "chuck" calls (75%).
- Chukar country is steep loose rock: boots with ankle support, a partner and a plan (Tip). It can be dangerous in snow and ice (75%).
- Huns: "cover a lot of ground"; they feed at grain field edges and weedy patches and hide in hay, tall grass, brush and fence lines (75%).
- Huns often flush wild and farther each time. Singles hold better than coveys (75%).
- Shot: chukar and Huns are bigger. Lead 6 or 7.5 for Huns at 30 to 45 yards (75%, Washington). Loads in chapter 10.
- Rules: chukar 5 (15), gray partridge 3 (9), 1 October to 30 November, Regions 3 and 8 (99%).
```

## What a 40 year hunter does differently

```diagram mql-oldhand
```

- These are opinion: hunter wisdom from educators and articles (Tip).
- Each line traces to a source in this Masterclass.

```more where each rule comes from
1. Listen first, walk second: dawn and dusk calling (70 to 75%, Edgewood notes, Washington Fish and Wildlife).
2. Stop often: Washington stop and go (75%).
3. Get above chukar: Washington chukar page (75%).
4. Mark the bird, not the covey: Missouri Conservationist (60%) and the quick lesson.
5. Three birds from a covey, then move on: traditional manners rule quoted by Tall Timbers (60%).
6. A low bird is a free bird: the app's Grandpa's rule (Tip).
- Also: rest a covey a week between hunts; hunt weekdays; send the landowner a thank you (Tip).
- And: they keep a Journal of every covey, every year. That is how the 40 years add up (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mql-method-flow
```

- **Rhyme:** "Knock, then listen; stop, then go; one bird high, and never low." (Tip)
- **Number trick:** "15 to 25, 15 to 20": walk 15 to 25 m apart (Tip), stop 15 to 20 seconds (75%).

```more more hooks
- **Chukar hook:** "Run up, fly down: you get above, they come around." (75%)
- **Old hand's rule (opinion):** "Permission is the first bird of the day." The app's Grandpa's rule from the quick lesson (Tip).
- **Old hand's rule (opinion):** "Three from a rise, then let it go." (Tip, 60%)
```
