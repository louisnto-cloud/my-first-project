## Canada goose: the 5 marks

```diagram mgs-canada-marks
```

- Black neck, white chinstrap, white under the tail (85%).
- Gander and goose look the same (85%).

```photo canada-goose
```

```more every mark and the sizes
- Black head and neck, a white chinstrap that runs up behind each eye, grey brown body with pale feather edges, black rump and tail, white upper and under tail coverts, black bill, legs and feet (80%, Ducks Unlimited ID).
- Sexes look alike (85%).
- Length about 76 to 117 cm (2 ft 6 in to 3 ft 10 in) (80%, Audubon).
- Wingspan about 90 cm to 2 m, by race (80%, Hinterland Who's Who).
- Weight by race: Hinterland Who's Who gives 1.1 kg for the smallest form to 8 kg for the giant form; Ducks Unlimited gives 2.4 to 9 kg (5.3 to 19.8 lb) for the Canada goose races (75%). The quick lesson's 1.35 to 5.45 kg covers most birds you will see (80%).
- Voice: honks, "ka lunk". About 13 different calls have been described (80%, Hinterland Who's Who).
```

## Gander, goose and young

```diagram mgs-sex-age
```

- Same feathers. The gander is a bit bigger (75%).
- Watch behaviour: the gander guards and leads (80%).

```more how to tell them apart in the field
- Ganders (males) are slightly larger than geese (females); otherwise they look alike (75%, Internet Center for Wildlife Damage Management).
- At the nest the gander stands guard, sometimes several hundred metres away (80%, Hinterland Who's Who).
- Before a family flies, the gander's head toss gets them up fastest (80%, Raveling 1969). See [Chapter 4](#/s/mc-geese-04).
- Young of the year look like adults by fall (80%). Goslings stay with their parents a full year (80%).
- In the hand, hunters say young birds have softer, bendier bills and smaller feet (Tip, 60%).
- The law does not care: no sex or age rules for geese in BC (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-migratory-birds)).
```

## Canada or cackling?

```diagram mgs-cackler-vs-canada
```

- Short neck, stubby bill, high yelp: cackling goose (75%).
- Same bag as Canada geese: 10 a day together (99%).

```gallery
cackling-and-canada-geese | Cackling geese with a Canada goose (right). Compare neck and bill.
cackling-goose | Cackling goose: mallard sized, stubby bill.
```

```more the numbers
- A [[Cackling goose]] is its own species, split from the Canada goose; it is not a young Canada goose (85%).
- Bill: cackling 2.8 to 3.8 cm (1.1 to 1.5 in); Canada 4.1 to 5.8 cm (1.6 to 2.3 in) (75%, Ducks Unlimited).
- Average weight: cackling males about 1.5 kg (1,542 g), females about 0.95 kg (953 g) (75%).
- In flight a cackler looks mallard sized with a short neck (75%). Its call is a higher "hrooonk" (75%).
- In the air the two can be hard or impossible to separate (85%). It does not matter for your bag: the law counts "Canada Geese and Cackling Geese, combined" (99%).
```

## The races you may meet in BC

| Group | Where it lives (75%, Ducks Unlimited) |
|---|---|
| Western Canada goose | Resident from BC to Manitoba |
| Vancouver Canada goose | Coastal Alaska to southwest BC |
| Dusky and lesser Canada geese | Alaska and boreal nesters, winter south of BC |
| Minima and Taverner's cacklers | Winter in the Willamette and lower Columbia valleys |

- Most BC "home" geese are mixed race introductions (85%).

```more races, introductions and why it matters
- Hinterland Who's Who counts 11 races of Canada goose; four former races now make up the cackling goose (80%).
- Ducks Unlimited lists the western race (moffitti) as mostly resident from British Columbia to Manitoba, west and south of the Rockies (75%).
- The Vancouver race (fulva) breeds from coastal Glacier Bay, Alaska, south to southwest BC (75%).
- Dusky geese breed in southern Alaska and winter in the lower Columbia and Willamette valleys; they have declined long term (75%).
- Lesser geese (parvipes) breed from boreal Alaska to Nunavut and winter from western Washington to Mexico (75%).
- Cackling geese: minima and Taverner's winter in the Willamette and lower Columbia valleys of Oregon and Washington (75%). To get there they pass BC (Tip, 65%).
- Okanagan nesting geese are hybrids of several races brought in during the 1960s and 70s; they never learned to migrate (85%, Regional District of Okanagan Similkameen).
- Resident geese in BC towns and valleys are mixed race introductions, often big (85%, quick lesson).
- Why it matters: a September goose is almost always a local bird. Migrants and cacklers mix in from October (Tip, 65%). See [Chapter 5](#/s/mc-geese-05).
```

## Snow goose and Ross's goose

```diagram mgs-white-geese-heads
```

- Snow and Ross's count together in one bag (99%).
- Grin patch on the bill: snow. Stubby, no grin: Ross's (80%).

```gallery
snow-goose | Snow goose: pink bill with a dark grin patch.
ross-goose | Ross's goose: stubby bill, no grin.
snow-and-ross-geese | Snow (left) and Ross's (right).
g-snow-geese-flock | Dark birds in a snow goose flock are the blue form.
```

```more marks, weights and limits
- Snow goose: white with black wing tips, rose pink bill and feet, a dark "grin patch" on the bill (80%, Ducks Unlimited).
- Blue morph: white head and neck, grey brown body. Young white birds are greyish with grey pink bills (80%).
- Lesser snow goose weights: males 1.9 to 2.8 kg (4.2 to 6.2 lb), females 1.7 to 2.9 kg (3.8 to 6.4 lb) (75%).
- Ross's goose: smaller, short stubby bill, no grin patch, bluish warty base to the bill on older birds (70%). Males 1.1 to 2 kg (2.5 to 4.5 lb) (70%).
- BC snow geese are the Fraser and Skagit part of the Wrangel Island, Russia, population (85%, Demarchi 2006).
- Limits: District 3 and District 8, 5 a day (15 in possession) (99%).
- District 2 (Mission): the federal table says 10 a day, not more than 5 Ross's, possession 30; MUs (Management Units) 2-4 and 2-5 add 5 more snow geese a day (99%, [Rule Book: migratory birds](#/s/rb-migratory-birds)).
- The synopsis page 12 prints 5 a day outside 2-4 and 2-5. The [Region 2 Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-2) marks this VERIFY and says take 5 a day in 2-8 to stay safe (Tip).
```

## White fronted goose

```photo white-fronted-goose White fronted goose: white band at the bill, black bars on the belly.
```

- Grey brown, white face band, barred belly: "specklebelly" (70%).
- 5 a day in Districts 2, 3 and 8 (99%).

```more marks, voice and range
- White patch at the base of a pink orange bill, orange legs, black bars on the belly (75%, quick lesson; Ducks Unlimited).
- Weight about 1.95 to 3.3 kg (70%, search preview of the Cornell Lab guide).
- Voice: a high laughing "kow kow kow" from V flocks (80%, quick lesson).
- Two populations, Pacific and mid continent; they travel the Pacific and Central flyways (70%, Ducks Unlimited).
- In the Interior expect passing flocks, not winter birds (Tip, 60%).
- Season limits: 5 a day, 15 in possession, in Districts 2, 3 and 8 (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-migratory-birds)).
```

## Legal or not?

```diagram mgs-legal-or-not
```

- Swans: never. No open season (90%).
- Brant: only MU (Management Unit) 2-4, 1 to 10 March (99%).

```gallery
g-trumpeter-swan | Trumpeter swan: huge, all white, black bill. Protected.
g-tundra-swans-flight | Tundra swans: white wings, no black tips.
brant | Brant: small dark sea goose, white necklace.
```

```more the look alikes in full
- A white bird bigger than a goose, with no black wing tips, is a swan. Trumpeter and tundra swans have no open season (90%).
- Brant: no open season in Districts 3 and 8 (99%). In District 2 only in MU 2-4, 1 to 10 March, 3 a day (99%). Not your bird at Mission or Heffley Creek. Full story: [Sea ducks and brant](#/s/sea-ducks).
- Farm and domestic geese are not wild game. Leave them alone (Tip).
- A dark goose on the coast with no white cheek is a brant, not a Canada goose (85%).
```

## How many, and the trend

```diagram mgs-harvest-bars
```

- BC hunters took 15,539 geese in 2016, 6,053 in 2024 (99%, survey).
- Continent wide, Canada geese boomed since 1950 (80%).

```more the numbers behind it
- BC Hunter Sample Survey, all geese together: 2016, 2,304 hunters, 15,539 geese. 2024, 1,345 hunters, 6,053 geese (99% as published; these are survey estimates).
- Region 2 geese, 2020 to 2024: 4,249, 4,037, 6,207, 2,629, 2,073 (99%). MU 2-4 in 2024: 251 hunters, 1,028 geese. MU 2-8 (Mission): 109 hunters, 201 geese (99%).
- Region 3, 2020 to 2024: 614, 630, 419, 419, 196 (99%). MUs 3-27 and 3-28 near Heffley Creek: under 30 estimated goose hunters a year, 0 to 92 geese (99%; small samples, wide error).
- Region 8, 2020 to 2024: 222, 211, 566, 925, 185 (99%).
- North America: perhaps 1 million Canada geese in 1950, more than 8 million before the fall hunt now (80%, Hinterland Who's Who). Ducks Unlimited's world estimate: 5.1 to 6.2 million (75%).
- Cackling geese: about 3 million, slight recent decline (75%).
- Snow geese: about 1 million in 1970, 19 million in 2013, about 14 million in 2019 (75%).
- Fraser River delta: about 80,000 snow geese wintered in 2004 to 2005; the brackish marsh alone can feed about 17,500 (85%, Demarchi 2006). A peak of about 108,000 was counted in 2016 (70%, Vancouver Sun 2017).
- BC breeding Canada geese more than doubled since 1970 (85%, BC Breeding Bird Atlas, via the quick lesson).
- My reading: fewer hunters, not fewer geese, explains most of the harvest drop (Tip, 60%).
```

## Memory hooks

> [!tip] Memory rhymes (made for this app)
> - "Chinstrap white, neck like night: that is a Canada in sight."
> - "Stubby bill, voice up shrill: a cackler, same bag still."
> - "Grin on the bill, it is a snow. No grin, small: Ross's, so."
> - "All white, no black tips in flight: a swan. Hold tight."

- Number trick: **10, 5, 5, 3**. Canada 10, white fronted 5, snow 5 (more in District 2), brant 3 but only MU 2-4 in March (99%).

> [!rule] Old hand's rule (tradition, opinion)
> If you cannot name it, it is not a goose yet. Let it pass and wait for the next one.
