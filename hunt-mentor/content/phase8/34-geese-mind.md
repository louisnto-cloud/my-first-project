> [!why]
> A goose flock is a family business with a long memory. Read its moods and you know when to sit still, when to shoot and when to go home.

- Flocks are families: pairs plus this year's young (80%).
- Families decide together; the gander leads (80%).

```more what this chapter covers
- The alarm ladder and the preflight head toss.
```

## The alarm ladder

```diagram mgs-alarm-ladder
```

- Heads up, then tall necks, then head tosses, then gone (80%).
- At step 2, freeze. At step 4 they are leaving (Tip).

```more the signals in full
- Head up is the main watch posture. It is not always alarm; some of it is social (75%, Wildfowl journal).
- Tall necks and walking away with honks: rising alarm (Tip, 65%).
- Head tossing is the preflight signal: side to side or up and down shakes with the neck held straight, before the bird flies or walks to a new spot (80%, Raveling 1969; Black and Barrow).
- Ganders recruit their family into the air fastest after starting to head toss (80%, Raveling 1969, Smithsonian summary).
- Ground threat: a gander lowers its head close to the ground and hisses (80%, Audubon).
- In flight, a flock that starts to climb and swing wide has seen something (Tip).
```

## The head toss

```anim mgs-head-toss The gander tosses, the family lifts.
```

- See head tosses? The family is about to fly (80%).
- Shoot only if in range now; never chase them (Tip).

```more how to use it
- Raveling's study followed 77 marked geese from 10 families, 2 pairs and 35 yearlings, and tied preflight movements to family status (80%).
- When you watch a field from the road, head tossing means the flock is about to go. Note where they fly: that is tomorrow's flight line (Tip).
- In a blind, tossing heads on the ground in front of you means your hide or movement has been seen (Tip).
```

## Flight distance

```diagram mgs-flight-zone
```

- How close you get depends on the place (70%).
- On hunt days, tracked geese moved less and flew sooner when a threat came close (80%).

```more the studies
- Belgium: Canada goose flight initiation distance changed with flock size and other birds, and differed by site; most wary in the least visited reserve, least wary in a busy park (70%, Belgian Journal of Zoology, search preview).
- Pennsylvania, 2025: 134 GPS tagged female geese stayed on two hunted Wildlife Management Areas through the season, at least 165 times more likely to be on them than off (80%, Beatty and others, Movement Ecology; Penn State news).
- On hunting days and during hunting hours they took flight less and walked less, but took flight more as a threat got close (80%).
- Older work in Illinois: geese went alert at shots even inside a refuge and left places where disturbance kept them from feeding (70%).
- What it means: hunted geese do not leave the area, they get quieter and harder to approach (Tip, 65%).
```

## How geese learn pressure

| Pressure sign | What to change (Tip, 65%) |
|---|---|
| Birds land 200 m off your spread | Move to where they landed next time |
| Flocks flare at 100 m | Fix the hide; fewer blinds |
| Birds stop using the field | Rest it a week |
| Same flock every hunt | Change the spread size and shape |

- They avoid fields where they are disturbed again and again (70%).
- Same spread, same spot, every day: they learn it (65%).

```more the guide advice
- Hunting the same spot with the same spread teaches local birds to avoid it. Change decoy count and layout through the season (65%, Ducks Unlimited goose guide).
- Smaller, more realistic spreads in places other hunters do not use (65%).
- Fewer blinds: with six hunters, three blinds and rotate shooters (65%).
- "Rest the field" is widely repeated hunter advice. No study measured how long (Tip).
```

## Family life and rank

```diagram mgs-family-flock
```

- Young stay a full year; pairs usually last for life (80%).
- Most first nest at 2 or 3 years old (80%).

```more families in full
- A goose finds a mate in its second year and usually stays paired for life; a bird that loses its mate will pair again (80%, Hinterland Who's Who; Ducks Unlimited).
- Fall flocks are families travelling together, from a few families to thousands of birds (85%).
- Families with young fly as a unit; you will often see 4 to 8 birds in a tight group inside a big flock (Tip).
- The gander guards the nest and the brood; both parents tend the young (80%, Audubon).
- About half of hatched goslings survive to fly (80%).
- Shoot the lead bird of a family and the rest often swing back (hunter lore, Tip). Ethics first: take only clean shots.
```

## Old birds and young birds

```photo hab-geese-stubble A winter flock in stubble: old birds and young birds together.
```

- Young of the year decoy more readily (Tip, widely reported).
- Old birds come in high, circle wide and land short (Tip).

> [!rule] Old hand's rule (tradition, opinion)
> Early season and family groups come easy. Late season singles and pairs are the professors. Hunters widely repeat this; no study tested it.

```more what is fact and what is lore
- Fact: most geese that survive year one live 10 or more years (80%). So many birds in a flock have survived several seasons (80%).
- Fact: hunted geese change behaviour on hunt days (80%, Pennsylvania study).
- Lore: old ganders lead the flock away from danger and young birds break off to decoys (Tip).
- Lore: a lone goose that has lost its family is easier to call (Tip).
- Use the lore to choose tactics, not to decide what is legal or ethical.
```

## Habits that give them away

```diagram mgs-mistakes
```

- Every habit is a place to wait for them (Tip).
- Best one: back to yesterday's field (65%).

```more each habit and its source
- In the morning geese usually return to the field they fed in the afternoon before (65%, Ducks Unlimited goose guide; quick lesson).
- They land into the wind, so the wind sets your hide and landing hole (80%, quick lesson; Ducks Unlimited).
- Cold or cloudy mornings: they fly later (80%, Raveling 1972).
- They avoid decoys near fence lines, brush piles and ditches that could hide a predator (60%, Outdoor Life search preview).
- They trust their own kind: decoys work (Tip).
- Same roost and flight line day after day while undisturbed (Tip, 65%). See [Chapter 6](#/s/mc-geese-06).
```

## Memory hooks

> [!tip] Memory rhymes (made for this app)
> - "Heads up, sit tight. Necks tall, no fight. Heads toss, take flight."
> - "Yesterday's field is tomorrow's yield."
> - "Families come easy; the old ones come wary."

- Number trick: **2, 2 or 3, 10**. Mate in year 2, first nest at 2 or 3, many live 10 years or more (80%).

> [!rule] Old hand's rule (tradition, opinion)
> Never bump a feeding flock off the field. Let them leave on their own, then hunt them tomorrow. A standard rule in goose scouting guides.
