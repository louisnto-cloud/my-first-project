> [!why]
> Coast deer live in thick cover and hunting barely dents them. You win by going slow, watching edges and staying out in the rain.

```diagram mbt-method-picker
```

- Three methods: still hunt, watch an edge, partner walk to a stander (Tip).
- Pushes are on foot only (99%).

```more the methods, pros and cons
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

| Method | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| [[Still hunting]] the timber | You cover ground; close, quiet shots | Hard to do well; easy to bump deer (Tip) |
| Watch a [[Cutblock]] edge | You see deer before they see you; a rest for the shot | Cold, slow; only dawn and dusk (Tip) |
| Partner walk to a stander | Moves deer to a waiting hunter | Needs zones of fire and a plan (Tip) |

- "Hunting has little effect on black-tailed deer herds" because coast forests hold so much security cover (90%, Nyberg and Janz 1990). Translation: deer have many places to hide (Tip).
- Unlawful: "to use a power boat, aircraft, or motor vehicle or other mechanical device to herd or harass wildlife". Pushes are on foot only (99%, page 11, item 25).
- Long glassing works less in timber than in open interior country (Tip, [Rule Book: Region 2](#/s/rb-region-2) strategy).
```

## Cutblocks: hunt the 100 m band

```diagram mbt-cutblock-100m
```

- Deer used clearcuts most within 100 m of cover (90%).
- The middle of cuts over 400 m wide got very little use (90%).

```more the cutblock numbers
- Radio collared deer on Vancouver Island concentrated their use of clearcuts within 100 m of cover and used areas beyond 100 m less than expected (90%, Nyberg and Janz 1990, summarizing Kremsater 1989).
- Openings under 200 m wide (under 100 m to cover from any point) give the most deer use, whatever their area (90%).
- Openings up to 400 m wide get less, but still real, use in the middle; centres of openings over 400 m wide get very little unless the ground gives some cover (90%).
- Use of cover next to clearcuts was highest within 200 m of the edge (90%).
- What it means for you (Tip): sit where you can see the edge band, and expect most deer inside 100 m of the trees. Your shot will usually be under 150 m (Tip).
- Cutblock ages and what grows: [Cutblock country](#/s/cutblock-country).
```

## Still hunting the timber

```diagram mbt-still-steps
```

- Look longer than you walk (Tip).
- Look for parts, not a whole deer (Tip).

```more still hunting detail (Tips)
- Go into the wind or across it. Check it every few minutes with a puffer or thread (Tip).
- A few steps, then stop and look. Move when cover hides you (Tip).
- Look low and through the trees for a leg, an ear, the white of a throat, a tail flick (Tip). Good cover hides 90% of a deer at 60 m (85%, chapter 3).
- Shots in timber are short and fast. Know your rifle off sticks and kneeling (Tip).
- Full lesson: [Still hunting](#/s/still-hunting).
```

## Rain days

```diagram mbt-rain-plan
```

- Rain is normal here. The coast forest zone is the rainiest in BC (95%).
- Wet ground is quiet underfoot (Tip).

```more rain detail
- The CWH (Coastal Western Hemlock) zone is the rainiest in BC: 1,000 to 4,400 mm a year, mean 2,228 mm (95%, [Wet forests](#/s/wet-forest-country)).
- Rain softens leaves and covers noise, but you chill fast. Wool or synthetics, a rain shell, a spare dry layer (Tip).
- Rain can erase a blood trail within about an hour (Tip, [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing)). Take only a shot you are sure of; if rain is coming, track sooner (Tip). Chapter 6.
- Shivering, clumsy hands, slurred words: early hypothermia. Stop, get dry and warm, eat, head for the truck (Tip; see [Cold injuries](#/s/cold-injuries)).
- Deer behaviour in rain: no black tail study found. VERIFY before trusting any "they move in the rain" rule.
```

## With your brother or solo

```diagram zones-of-fire
```

- Pair: one sits at the edge, the other walks slowly toward him, wind at the walker's back (Tip).
- Agree zones of fire, a meeting point and a clock time (Tip).

```more pair and solo detail
- Pair: the stander watches a cutblock edge or a trail crossing; the walker still hunts toward the stander so scent pushes deer ahead (Tip, [Your Harrison deer hunt](#/s/local-harrison-deer)).
- Orange: BC has no blaze orange law. Wear it anyway with a partner (95% for the law, Tip for the advice). Deer see orange as dull (75%).
- Solo: keep the pack out short, about 1 km or less and 100 m climb or less (Tip).
- Leave a trip plan with someone at home: truck, road, time out (75%, AdventureSmart).
- Full lesson: [Hunting with a partner](#/s/hunting-partner).
```

## Roads and where not to shoot

```diagram mbt-road-rules
```

- Never shoot on or across a road allowance (99%).
- Some FSR (Forest Service Road) bands near Harrison ban shooting unless you are lawfully hunting (99%).

```more the Harrison road rules
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- No shooting on or across a road allowance (99%, page 10).
- Unless lawfully hunting or trapping, no discharge within 400 m of the Francis Lake, Weaver Lake and Sts'ailes (West) FSRs and some FVRD (Fraser Valley Regional District) roads (99%, page 27). Hunting is allowed; target shooting is not (90%, my reading).
- No hunting or shooting within 100 m of a house, farm building, school or regional district park (99%, pages 9 and 10).
- Harrison Mills and the Nicomen lowland: no rifles (99%, Closed Areas Regulation; 85% my reading of the boundary). Hunt the hills.
- All of it, with links: [Your Harrison deer hunt](#/s/local-harrison-deer). The [Hunt Map](#/map) shows the no shooting layer.
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom, not law): "Slow is fast in the timber. If you think you are going slow, halve it."

> [!mistake]
> - Walking logging roads all day and never stepping into the timber edge.
> - Sitting in the middle of a big cut instead of where you can see the edge.
> - Going home when it rains.

> [!field]
> Pick one cutblock edge for dawn. Sit 100 m back from the far edge with the wind in your face. Stay 2 hours.
