> [!why]
> The wrong shell in the right gun can burst a barrel. Two numbers prevent it: gauge and chamber length.

```diagram sg2-gauge
```

- Gauge: smaller number, bigger bore (85%).
- 12 gauge has the widest choice of steel duck loads (70%).

```more gauge in full
- Gauge is an old measure: the number of lead balls of bore size that make one pound. So 12 gauge is bigger than 20 (70%, Wikipedia, gauge).
- Nominal bores: 12 gauge 18.5 mm (.729 in), 16 gauge 16.8 mm, 20 gauge 15.6 mm (.615 in), 28 gauge 14.0 mm (70%, Wikipedia, gauge). The .410 bore is 0.410 in, about 10.4 mm (arithmetic).
- .410 is a bore size in inches, not a gauge (70%).
- 20 gauge kicks less but its steel duck loads are weaker (65%, [Choosing your shotgun](#/s/shotgun-guide/3)).
- Migratory birds: no shotgun bigger than 10 gauge (99%, s. 37(1)(c), [Rule Book](#/s/rb-migratory-birds/32)).
```

## Chamber length

```diagram sg2-chamber
```

- A 3 in chamber fires 2 3/4 in and 3 in shells (95%).
- Never a shell longer than the barrel stamp (95%).

```more chamber in full
- The length on the box is the fired length; the crimp unfolds into the cone ahead of the chamber (70%).
- Beretta: the A300 Outlander shoots 70 mm (2 3/4 in) to 76 mm (3 in) shells without adjustment (95%, A300 manual).
- Remington: "NEVER USE AMMUNITION WHICH EXCEEDS THE CHAMBER LENGTH SHOWN ON THE BARREL" (95%, 870 manual).
- 3 1/2 in chambers add recoil and cost for little gain on ducks (65%, [Choosing your shotgun](#/s/shotgun-guide/3)).
- My lean: a 3 in chamber. It covers every duck, goose and grouse load you need (Tip).
```

## Read the stamp, match the box

```diagram sg2-stamp
```

- 1: the barrel stamp. 2: the box. Same gauge, shell no longer (95%).
- Check every box, every time, even "the usual" (Tip).

```more where the marks are
- Beretta: the gauge and chamber markings are on the side of the barrel (95%, A300 manual).
- Remington: "BEFORE LOADING, ALWAYS CHECK THE BARREL MARKING FOR THE CORRECT GAUGE AND CHAMBER LENGTH" (95%, 870 manual).
- The shell head is stamped with its gauge too (95%, 870 manual, Picture B).
```

## The 12 20 burst

```diagram sg2-1220
```

- A 20 gauge shell can drop into a 12 gauge barrel and stick (95%).
- A 12 loaded behind it can burst the barrel (95%).

```more the manual's warning
- Remington calls this "a 12/20 burst, and it can kill you" (95%, 870 manual).
- 20 gauge shells are often yellow, but colour is not a rule (Tip, 60%). Read the head stamp.
- "If the noise or recoil of your firearm seems weak, stop everything, unload your firearm and be sure nothing is lodged in the barrel" (95%, 870 manual).
- Keep one gauge per bag, per pocket, per boat (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): One gun, one gauge, one bag. Mixing is how barrels split.

> [!mistake]
> - Grabbing a friend's 20 gauge shells "just for today".
> - Buying 3 1/2 in shells for a 3 in gun because they were on sale.
> - Reading the price on the box and not the length.

> [!field]
> When you buy the gun, read the stamp out loud with the clerk, and write it on a label inside your shell bag.
