> [!why]
> A bear lives by its nose. Beat the nose and you can get close; lose the wind and the bear is gone before you see it.

## The nose

```anim mc-black-bear-scent-cone Your scent rides the wind to the bear.
```

- Nose lining about 100 times ours (70%).
- Finds food over 1 km away (70%).

```more the nose in detail
- North American Bear Center: smell is "extremely good", limits untested; nasal lining (mucosa) about 100 times the area of a person's (70%).
- WildSafeBC: a bear can smell food over 1 km away (70%).
- BearWise: the best nose in the bush, far better than a dog's (70%, via the quick lesson).
- Bigger claims you will read (2,100 times a person, miles away) are not tested science (Tip: ignore them).
- What this means: a bear that winds you leaves, usually without you ever knowing it was there (Tip).
```

## How scent moves: wind and thermals

```anim hf-thermal-clock Morning air climbs; evening air sinks.
```

- Morning sun: air drifts uphill. Evening: downhill (80%).
- Swirls at the switch hours (Tip).

```more thermals for a bear hunter
- Thermals: air slides up a slope as it warms in the morning and down as it cools in the evening (80%, [wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals)).
- Evening berry slope (Tip): set up below or beside the patch early, then expect your scent to sink toward the bottom as the light fades. Sit where the sinking air carries it away from the bear's likely path.
- A creek bottom at dusk is a scent drain: cold air pours down it (Tip, [wind mastery](#/s/wind-mastery)).
- Wind beats thermals when it blows hard (Tip).
- Carry a puffer bottle or milkweed and check every few minutes (Tip).
```

## The ears

```diagram sa-bear-senses
```

- Over twice as sensitive as ours, higher range too (70%).
- Hearing works in all directions, even in brush (70%).

```more the ears in detail
- North American Bear Center (70%): hearing "over twice the sensitivity of human hearing" and beyond human frequency ranges; "the first line of defense" because it works in all directions; in brushy forest a bear can hear farther than it can see.
- Metal clicks, a zipper, a boot on dry sticks, voices: all carry (Tip).
- Steady natural sounds (wind, creek, rain) cover you; a single sharp sound does not (Tip).
- A bear crunching berries or ripping a log is noisy: that is your moment to move (Tip, chapter 9).
```

## The eyes

```diagram mc-black-bear-beat-senses
```

- Sees colour, sharp up close (70%).
- Distance vision is untested (70%).

```more the eyes in detail
- North American Bear Center: bears "see in color and have sharp vision close up"; distance vision over about 180 m (200 yd) "has not been tested" (70%).
- A 2021 retina study predicts two colour channels (dichromatic) with high sharpness, like many mammals; an older behaviour study pointed the same way (70%, Heyward and others, University of Wisconsin summary).
- Eyesight about as good as a person's, maybe better (70%, WildSafeBC).
- Motion gives you away more than shape (Tip, hunter consensus). Move when it looks away.
- Orange: most hunters wear it for safety. Bears likely see it less vividly than we do, but motion still shows (Tip).
- A bear standing on its hind legs is usually trying to see and smell better, not about to charge (Tip, bear safety guidance).
```

## How a bear puts it together

| Distance | First warning | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Far | Nose, on the wind (70%) | Leaves quietly (Tip) |
| In brush | Ears (70%) | Stops, listens, stands (Tip) |
| Close, open | Eyes, motion (70%) | Stares, then leaves or huffs (Tip) |

- Nose confirms. A bear that only hears you may wait to smell you (Tip).
- Stay downwind and it can only hear and see you (Tip).

## Beat each sense

```diagram mc-black-bear-beat-senses
```

- Wind first, then sound, then movement (Tip).
- Approach from downwind or crosswind only (Tip).

```more the old hand's checklist
- Before the stalk: wind check, then pick a route with the wind in your face or across it (Tip).
- Scent: clean clothes, no food smells, no smoking, no cologne (Tip). Scent control helps, wind control decides (Tip).
- Sound: soft soles, slow steps, sling off the metal, pockets empty (Tip).
- Sight: stay below the skyline, move when the head is down, freeze when it looks (Tip).
- If the wind swings to the bear: back out, circle, try again another day (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

```anim mc-black-bear-scent-cone Your scent rides the wind to the bear.
```

- **Rhyme:** "Nose knows, ears warn, eyes confirm."
- **Number trick:** 100, 2, 1: nose lining 100x ours, hearing 2x, smells food over 1 km (70%).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely taught hunting advice, opinion): "Hunt the wind, not the bear."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Up in the morning, down at night: thermals tell your scent its flight."
- **Number trick:** 200 yards: beyond about 180 m (200 yd) nobody has tested bear eyesight (70%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, common hunting advice): "Head down, you move. Head up, you are a stump."

> [!mistake]
> - Trusting scent spray instead of the wind.
> - Moving while the bear is looking.
> - Ignoring the evening downslope swing on a berry slope.

> [!field]
> Next scouting trip: carry a puffer bottle and check the wind at every stop. Note when the thermal switches.
```
