```more the six checks in words
1. Antlers? No antlers: no shot, unless you hold an LEH (Limited Entry Hunting) authorization for that class (99%).
2. One antler, 2 tines or fewer, brow palm tines included, each tine at least 2.5 cm and longer than wide? Not sure: no shot (99% rule; Tip decision).
3. Swimming? Never. In or at the edge of water: wait (99%; Tip).
4. Clear backstop? Earth behind, no road, house, person or other animal (Tip, [firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety)).
5. Can the two of you get it out today and keep it cool? (Tip)
6. Broadside or quartering away, still, inside your range, on a rest? Then shoot (Tip).
- Time: is there light left to recover and quarter it? (Tip)
```

## Legal methods in brief

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

```diagram sa-308-range
```

- Centrefire rifle or bow only for moose (99%).
- Mouth and hand calls only: no electronic calls (99%).

```more methods in detail
- Moose: centrefire rifle or bow; no shotgun, rimfire or air rifle (99%, page 13, [legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)). Crossbow: class A, C or D (99%).
- "To use recorded or electronic calls to hunt wildlife except wolf, coyote, cougar, lynx, or bobcat" is unlawful (99%, page 12). Mouth calls, hand calls and raking brush are not recorded or electronic (95%, my reading).
- Dogs only on a leash for moose (99%, page 12).
- No hunting from a motor vehicle; no loaded firearm in or on a vehicle (99%, page 11, [unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts)).
- Your .308 Winchester: 165 to 180 grain premium controlled expansion bullet, shots inside about 200 m (219 yd) (65%, hunter consensus, [Moose](#/s/moose)). Zero with the load you hunt with (Tip).
- Limit: the range where you hit a 15 cm (6 in) plate 9 times in 10 from a field rest (Tip, [season practice](#/s/season-practice)).
```

## Scout and glass

```anim hf-glass-sweep Sweep slowly, overlap each look.
```

- Late September: listen at dawn for grunts and cow calls (Tip).
- Glass from a knob above willow and water at first and last light (Tip).
- Look for a dark horizontal line, a pale leg, a flash of antler (Tip).

```more glassing in detail
- Sit, brace the binoculars, sweep in overlapping slices, near to far (Tip, [glassing](#/s/glassing)).
- Moose clues (Tip): a black horizontal back in willow; pale legs; ears flicking; a pale antler or palm catching light; a water ripple at a lake edge.
- Willow flats and wetland edges are open enough to glass; timber is not (Tip).
- Glass a long time: a bedded moose in willow can vanish (Tip).
- When you find a bull, count the tines with the spotting scope or binoculars before you plan the stalk (Tip).
```

```more scouting in detail
- Map first (chapter 7): wetlands, 10 to 30 year cutblocks and 3 to 15 year burns near water, close to a road or trail you can pack out to (Tip).
- Late September to early October: rut peak (90%). Even with a November tag, the rut tells you where bulls live (Tip).
- Walk edges at midday; sit and listen at dawn and dusk (Tip).
- Note every moose seen in the Journal and in the BC Moose Tracker app (99% app exists, [after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill)).
- Check closures and land status for every spot (Tip, [land status](#/s/maps-land-status)).
```

## Spot and stalk

```steps Stalk a moose in 4 steps
mc-moose-stalk-1 | Glass the willow flat from above at first light.
mc-moose-stalk-2 | Mark it: two landmarks near the moose.
mc-moose-stalk-3 | Move tree to tree, wind in your face.
mc-moose-stalk-4 | Rest, count, then wait for broadside.
```

- Wind in your face beats every other trick (80%).
- Close enough to count, not closer (Tip).

```more spot and stalk in detail
- [[Spot and stalk]]: find from far, plan, close the distance unseen (Tip).
- Before you move: note the wind, a route with cover, and two landmarks you will recognise up close (Tip).
- Moose feeding: move when the head is down or screened. Moose staring: freeze (Tip, chapter 3).
- Watch for a cow or calf you did not see first: a second moose blows the stalk or turns it dangerous (Tip).
- At the end: rest the rifle, count one antler twice, wait for broadside with a clear backstop (Tip).
```

## Still hunt or sit

```anim hf-step-cadence A few steps, then a long look.
```

- Slow walk into the wind through edges and timber (Tip).
- Or sit downwind of a trail between timber and water (Tip).

```more still hunting in detail
- [[Still hunting]]: a few quiet steps, a long look, repeat; along willow edges, beaver ponds and cutblock margins (Tip, [moving quietly](#/s/still-hunting)).
- Moose noises help you: a careful, uneven step can sound like a moose; a steady human rhythm cannot (Tip, chapter 3).
- Midday: still hunt slowly toward likely beds in timber next to food, wind in your face (Tip).
- Pros: fits a November season with no rut. Cons: hard to see far in timber; a bedded moose often sees you first (Tip).
```

```more sitting in detail
- Pick a trail or edge with fresh tracks and browse between bedding timber and a willow flat or lake (Tip, chapter 8).
- Sit with the wind blowing from the moose side to you, and remember evening air sinks into the wetland (Tip, chapter 3).
- Pros: quiet, safe, a rested shot, good for a beginner. Cons: patience; one spot only (Tip, [Moose](#/s/moose)).
- Leave the seat in the dark carefully: do not bump moose feeding beside you (Tip).
```

## Calling

```steps Call a moose in 4 steps
mc-moose-call-1 | Set up on a wetland edge, wind in your face.
mc-moose-call-2 | Cow call: a long moan, start soft.
mc-moose-call-3 | Rake brush; grunt if he hangs up.
mc-moose-call-4 | Wait, watch downwind, count antlers.
```

- Best in the rut, late September to October (65 to 90%).
- In November: worth a try when nothing else works (65%).

```video
yt-moose-calling-strategies | Cow calls, bull grunts and raking brush for moose.
```

```more calling in detail
- Outdoor Canada (65%, hunting writer; tactics are Tip): mimic a cow's mournful bawl, which can last nearly 10 seconds, followed by two or three short grunts; start softly in case a bull is close; birch bark or fibreglass horns amplify.
- Rake shrubs with a stick, branch or moose shoulder blade; a few bull grunts can make a hung up bull think a rival is near (65%).
- Call every 20 to 30 minutes, each sequence up to a minute. Some hunters move after about 3 hours without a reply; staying put for days can pay because rut bulls roam (65%).
- Best weather: cool, clear, calm or a light breeze; heavy wind or rain distorts calls (65%).
- Best spots: lake points, ponds, river shores, beaver dams, cutblocks, regenerating burns, ridges with a view (65%).
- A bull may slip in silently or crash in grunting; stay patient and alert (65%).
- Season fit: the main rut is late September and early October (90%). Outdoor Canada says calling can also work "at other times if other tactics fail" (65%). In MU 3-27 your season is 1 to 15 November: treat calling as a bonus (Tip).
```

## Water and boats

```diagram mc-moose-boat
```

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- No hunting from a boat the motor is pushing (99%).
- Never shoot a swimming moose (99%).

```video
yt-cold-water-1-10-1 | Fall in cold water? 1 minute breathe, 10 minutes move, 1 hour.
```

```more water rules and safety in detail
- Unlawful: "To hunt wildlife, other than migratory game birds, from a boat, unless the boat is not being propelled by a motor, or is held stationary at a fixed position in current, tide or wind by the power of the motor." (99%, page 11, [unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts))
- Unlawful: "To hunt, take, wound or kill big game while it is swimming unless it has been previously wounded." (99%, page 11 item 26)
- Retrieval by boat is lawful if nobody aboard has a loaded firearm, or if the boat is not being propelled by a motor (99%, page 12).
- Since 1 October 2026 the BC FVPA (Firearm Violence Prevention Act) bans a loaded firearm in or on a boat, with an exemption for a boat with no motor or one the motor is not propelling (99%, [unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts)).
- No herding or harassing wildlife with a power boat (99%).
- Safety: wear a zipped PFD (personal flotation device); cold water follows 1 minute, 10 minutes, 1 hour (80%, [water safety](#/s/water-safety), [ice and cold water](#/s/ice-cold-water)).
- A moose quarter in a 10 ft boat or a kayak is a heavy, high load: several trips, calm water, everyone in PFDs (Tip).
- A moose shot at the water's edge can walk in and die in deep water: wait for it to be well onto land (Tip, [Moose](#/s/moose)).
```

## Roads

```diagram mc-moose-road
```

- Moose cross at dusk and dark; their eyes sit above your headlights (70%).
- No hunting from a vehicle; nothing loaded inside (99%).

```more roads in detail
- Driving: a moose's eyes are above your headlights, so look for a dark silhouette, not eyeshine (70%, Wildlife Collision Prevention Program via search).
- BC advice: extra care at dusk, dawn and at night; scan tree lines and near water; slow down at wildlife signs; high beams when no traffic (85%, BC Ministry of Transportation 2020).
- Hundreds of moose die on BC highways and rail lines each year, especially along the Yellowhead (90%, BC account).
- Rules: "To hunt wildlife from a motor vehicle" and to have a loaded firearm in or on a vehicle are unlawful (99%, page 11). Shooting near roads: see [road allowance](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts) and [Forest Service roads](#/s/fsr-vehicles-radio).
- Region 3 vehicle limits: ATV (all terrain vehicle) closed for hunting 1 September to 10 December in MUs (Management Units) 3-28, 3-29, 3-30; no motor vehicles above 1,700 m except on existing roads and trails (99%, [Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3)).
- Old hand's rule (opinion): "Park the truck, walk the edge." Moose that live by roads learn them (Tip).
```

## Hunting with your brother

```diagram sk-moose-trips
```

- Agree roles, signals and the pack out before you leave (Tip).
- One moose is many loads for two people (Tip).

```video
yt-quarter-pack | Quarter the animal so you can carry it out.
```

```more partner work in detail
- Roles (Tip, [partner](#/s/hunting-partner)): caller behind, shooter ahead and downwind; or glasser on the knob guiding the stalker by hand signals or radio.
- Zones of fire: agree who may shoot where; never shoot toward your partner's position (Tip, [firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety)).
- Before the shot: both agree it is a spike fork, count twice (Tip).
- Pack plan before you go: game bags, saw, rope, headlamps, a cart or sled if allowed, the trips counted (Tip, [packing out](#/s/packing-out)).
- An unlicensed companion may help pack meat; a Record of Receipt is needed if your brother carries meat you killed (90 to 99%, [Moose](#/s/moose), regs safety notes).
```

## What an old hand does differently

```photo g-moose-bull-front
```

- Hunts food, water and wind, not luck (Tip).
- Lets doubtful bulls walk (Tip).

```more the old hand's habits (opinion, widely taught)
- Spends more days scouting than hunting; knows three wetlands well rather than thirty a little (Tip).
- Checks the wind constantly and gives up a spot rather than hunt it with the wrong wind (Tip).
- Calls less and listens more; is still sitting when the impatient hunter stands up (Tip).
- Counts twice, waits for broadside, and takes no shot near deep water (Tip).
- Plans the kill site: "never shoot a moose you cannot get a truck or a boat within a kilometre of" (opinion, [Moose](#/s/moose) Grandpa's rules).
- Treats every cow with a calf as dangerous and keeps a tree close (85%, chapter 4).
- Ethics: one well placed shot, every effort to recover, all edible meat taken (99% for the legal duties, chapter 10).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-moose-stalk-4
```

- **Rhyme:** "Wind in your face, slow is the pace; count it twice, the shot is nice."
- **Number trick:** 10, 2 to 3, 20 to 30: a 10 second moan, 2 to 3 grunts, call again in 20 to 30 minutes (65%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Plan the pack before the shot."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Motor on, gun is down; motor off, it's hunting town." (Big game from a boat; rules above.)
- **Number trick:** 6 checks: antlers, count, water, backstop, way out, broadside (Tip).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely reported, opinion): "If he goes downwind, you go there first."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "A moose at the water's edge is a moose in the water. Wait."

> [!mistake]
> - Shooting a bull standing in a lake.
> - Calling with the shooter upwind of the caller.
> - Driving roads all day instead of walking edges.

> [!field]
> Practise a cow call and a bull grunt at home 10 times each. Then rake a willow bush with a stick for 30 seconds and listen to how far it carries.
```
