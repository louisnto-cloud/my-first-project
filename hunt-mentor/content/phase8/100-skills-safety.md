## Firearm safety in the field

```diagram mc-skills-acts-prove
```

- ACTS every time you handle it. PROVE it safe every time (85%).
- Load only where you may lawfully shoot. Never loaded in or on a vehicle (99%).

```more the rules in full
- [[ACTS]]: Assume every firearm is loaded; Control the muzzle; Trigger finger off; See that it is unloaded (85%, Canadian Firearms Safety Course).
- [[PROVE]]: Point it safe; Remove all cartridges; Observe the chamber; Verify the feeding path; Examine the bore (85%).
- Federal rules allow loading only where the firearm may lawfully be discharged (99%, transport regulations s. 15).
- BC: no loaded firearm in or on a vehicle; the boat exemption since 1 October 2026 is for boats only (99%, [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety)).
- "Unloaded" means nothing in the chamber and nothing in a magazine attached or inserted (99%).
- Fence crossing alone: unload, PROVE it, pass it under or through, muzzle away, cross well away from the muzzle (75%).
- Crawls and falls: check the barrel for snow, mud or a plug before the next shot (Tip).
```

## Two hunters: zones of fire

```diagram mc-skills-partner-arcs
```

- Each hunter shoots only in about 45 degrees straight ahead (75%).
- Side by side, in sight. The middle and behind: no shot (75%).

```more partner rules in full
- A [[Zone of fire]] is the only slice of ground where you may shoot: about 45 degrees straight ahead (75%, hunter education).
- Walk in a line, side by side, always in sight (75%).
- A deer runs into the gap: no shot (75%). Never swing outside your arc, even on a running deer (75%).
- Lose sight of each other: muzzles up or down, no shooting, call out (75%).
- One gets ahead or behind: both stop and fix the line (75%).
- Blaze orange: BC has no blaze orange rule (95%, [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety)). My lean: orange hat and vest when you are two or more (Tip).
- Whoever kills big game cancels their own licence, never the partner's (99%).
```

## Stop the bleeding

```steps Stop bleeding with your hands
fs-fa-press-1 | Expose: cut clothing, find the source.
fs-fa-press-2 | Press hard with both hands, arms locked.
fs-fa-press-3 | Deep wound in groin or armpit? Pack it.
fs-fa-press-4 | Press on top 3 minutes. Do not peek.
```

- Scene safe, call, then stop the bleeding (75%).
- Press, pack, tourniquet: in that order for most wounds (75%).

```more bleeding control in full
- Check, Call, Care: unload guns and find the danger; call 911 or SOS with location first; stop big bleeding first (75%, [First aid in the field](#/s/field-first-aid)).
- Firm direct pressure stops most bleeding (75%, Canadian Red Cross).
- Deep wounds in groin, armpit or side of the neck, where a [[Tourniquet]] cannot go: pack with gauze and press at least 3 minutes (65 to 70%).
- Do not pack wounds of the chest or belly (65%).
- A limb bleed pressure cannot stop gets a tourniquet (75%).
- Write the times: injury, tourniquet on, changes (75%, Canadian Red Cross).
```

## Your call: the tourniquet questions

```diagram mc-skills-tq-choices
```

- Sources differ on two points. Learn both. Ask your first aid instructor. Your call.
- Both agree: above the wound, heart side, never on a joint, tight, write the time (75%).

| Question | Answer A | Answer B |
|---|---|---|
| How far above the wound? | 5 to 8 cm (2 to 3 in): American Red Cross, Stop the Bleed guides (70%) | 5 to 10 cm (2 to 4 in): Canadian Red Cross skills sheet, 2012 (75%) |
| Loosen it later? | Older Canadian Red Cross sheet (2012): slowly release after 10 minutes if bleeding has dropped a lot (75%) | Newer American Red Cross and Stop the Bleed: never loosen; only medical staff remove it (70%) |

```more both sides in their words
> [!lean]
> Your call. These are open questions in the sources. Take a certified course this winter and use the numbers your instructor teaches. VERIFY.

### How far above
- **5 to 8 cm (2 to 3 in):** American Red Cross (search preview) and several Stop the Bleed guides (70%). Also in [Pack and car kit](#/s/pack-kit).
- **5 to 10 cm (2 to 4 in):** Canadian Red Cross workplace skills sheet, Controlling External Bleeding, 2012 (75%, read directly via [First aid in the field](#/s/field-first-aid)). Also in [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety).
- Close to a joint: go above the joint (70%, Stop the Bleed guidance from fire department pages).

### Loosen or never loosen
- **Loosen after 10 minutes:** the 2012 Canadian Red Cross skills sheet says to slowly release after 10 minutes if bleeding has dropped a lot (75%, dated 2012).
- **Never loosen:** newer American Red Cross and Stop the Bleed material says leave it on; only medical staff loosen or remove it (70%, search preview).
- Still bleeding with the first one tight? A second tourniquet above the first (70%).
- Buy a real windlass tourniquet from a known medical seller; fakes exist (60%).
```

## Cold: hypothermia

```diagram gr-hypo-scale
```

- Stumbles, mumbles, fumbles, grumbles: early signs (75%).
- Shivering that stops is an emergency (75%).

```more hypothermia in full
- You lose heat four ways: conduction (cold ground), convection (wind), radiation, evaporation (wet clothes) (75%).
- Mild: alert, shivering strongly. Moderate: drowsy, shivering slows or stops. Severe: unconscious, no shivering (75%, Wilderness Medical Society via American Family Physician).
- Mild: shelter, insulation, heat to the trunk, food if alert (75%).
- Moderate and severe: gentle, horizontal, no food or drink, wrap, warm the upper trunk, evacuate (75%).
- Warming or rubbing arms and legs first can send cold blood to the core (75%). Trunk first (75%).
- Long glassing sits: a foam pad under you stops conduction (75%). Kamloops Airport November average low is about minus 1°C; hunting ground higher up is colder (75%, [Cold injuries](#/s/cold-injuries)).
- Frostbite: do not rub; rewarm in 37 to 39°C water only if it will not refreeze (75%).
```

## Lost: STOP

```steps Lost? STOP
gr-stop-s | Stop. Sit down. Do not keep walking.
gr-stop-t | Think. Where did you last know where you were?
gr-stop-o | Observe. Map, compass, sun, creeks, roads.
gr-stop-p | Plan. Stay put, or move only if sure.
```

- STOP: Stop, Think, Observe, Plan (75%).
- Doubt means stay. Call early (75%).

```more lost in full
- [[STOP]] is taught by AdventureSmart via the BC Search and Rescue Association (75%).
- Staying put reduces the time and the area searchers must cover (75%). Area grows with the square of the distance: double the walk, 4 times the search (95%, geometry).
- Under cloud in a forest, people walk in circles without knowing it (75%, Max Planck Institute, 2009).
- Do not follow water downhill into ravines in steep country (75%, North Shore Rescue).
- Signal in threes: whistle, mirror, horn, fires (75%). Ground to air: a V or SOS at least 3 m long (75%).
- Phone in an inside pocket: cold kills batteries (70%).
```

## The 10 essentials

```diagram gr-ten-essentials
```

- On you, not in the truck (75%).
- Light, fire, signal, food and water, clothes, navigation and communication, first aid, shelter, knife, sun protection (75%).

```more the list and the hunter's extras
- AdventureSmart's 10 essentials: light, fire making kit, signalling device, extra food and water, extra clothing, navigation and communication aids, first aid kit, emergency shelter, pocket knife, sun protection (75%, via [Lost](#/s/lost-survival-kit)).
- Hunter's extras (Tip): a real tourniquet, bear spray on the belt, flagging tape, game bags, headlamp with spare batteries, a foam sit pad.
- A foil blanket reflects heat but does little against cold ground; an emergency bivy bag is better (65%).
- Full kit with tiers: [Day pack and car kit](#/s/pack-kit).
```

## Who knows where you are?

```diagram mc-skills-comms
```

- Trip plan with someone at home: where, who, when back (75%).
- 911 first on any phone. No signal: satellite SOS (75 to 85%).

```more communication in full
- AdventureSmart's Three Ts: trip planning, training, taking the essentials (75%). Leave a [[Trip plan]] with the truck location, route and return time (75%).
- If you need help, your first action is to call 911; police then call in Search and Rescue (75%, BC Search and Rescue Association).
- 911 can connect through any carrier's tower in range (75%). 911 does not work everywhere in BC (85%, [Vehicles and radio](#/s/fsr-vehicles-radio)).
- iPhone 14 or later can send Emergency SOS via satellite in Canada (85%, [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety)).
- A [[Satellite messenger]] such as a Garmin inReach sends SOS over the Iridium satellite network to a staffed 24/7 response centre and needs an active subscription (65%, Garmin's own description).
- FRS (Family Radio Service) handheld radios need no licence (85%, ISED (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada)). Partner radios are not banned in the app's Rule Book (80%, my reading).
- Agree a turnaround time and a meeting point before you split up (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-skills-partner-arcs
```

- **Acronyms:** ACTS, PROVE, STOP (75 to 85%).
- **Number trick:** 45 degrees each, side by side, in sight (75%).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely taught hunter education wisdom, opinion): "Treat every firearm as if it is loaded."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Press, pack, tourniquet, write the time."
- **Rhyme:** "Stumbles, mumbles, fumbles, grumbles" (75%).
- **Number trick:** 3 of anything means help (75%).
- **Number trick:** 10 essentials, on you, not in the truck (75%).
- **Your call** (opinion): tourniquet 5 to 8 or 5 to 10 cm; loosen at 10 minutes or never. Ask your instructor.
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, AdventureSmart): "Leave a trip plan every time."

> [!mistake]
> - Swinging past your zone of fire on a running deer.
> - A tourniquet in the truck, not in your pocket.
> - Walking on when lost, and calling for help too late.

> [!field]
> Before the season: a first aid course with tourniquet practice, a trip plan template on your phone, and the 10 essentials checklist ticked for you and your brother.
```
