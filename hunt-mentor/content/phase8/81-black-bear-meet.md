## Black bear or grizzly: 4 clues

```steps Black bear or grizzly: 4 clues
sa-bid-1 | Hump: none on a black bear.
sa-bid-2 | Face: straight on a black bear, dished on a grizzly.
sa-bid-3 | Ears: tall on a black bear, short and round on a grizzly.
sa-bid-4 | Claws: short and curved, or long and straight.
```

- Use at least 3 clues. Never one alone (85%).
- No grizzly season anywhere in BC (98%).

```more the clue table
| Clue | Black bear | Grizzly |
|---|---|---|
| Shoulder hump | None; the rump is the high point (90%) | Big muscle hump over the shoulders (90%) |
| Face profile | Straight, forehead to nose (90%) | Dished between the eyes and nose (90%) |
| Ears | Taller, more pointed (85%) | Short, round (85%) |
| Front claws | Short, curved, rarely over about 3.8 cm (1.5 in) (85%) | Much longer, straighter, built to dig (90%) |
| Colour | Black, brown, cinnamon, blond (90%) | Usually not all black; often grizzled tips (85%) |

- BC government account: grizzlies "have a prominent shoulder hump, which is lacking in black bears, and a dish shaped face instead of the straight facial profile of the black" (90%).
- Alaska Department of Fish and Game: black bear claws "rarely exceed 1 ½ inches" (85%).
- Idaho Fish and Game: no single feature is enough; use a combination; do not use colour or size (85%).
- The quick lesson has the drawn ID (identification) card: [Black bear](#/s/black-bear).
```

## Colour is a trap

```diagram mc-black-bear-colour-trap
```

- Interior black bears are often brown or cinnamon (90%).
- Shape, not colour, names the bear (90%).

```gallery
g-bb-brown | Brown, but no hump and a straight face: black bear.
g-bb-cinnamon | Cinnamon black bear eating dandelions.
g-bb-blonde | Pale black bear. Same body shape.
g-bb-sow-cubs | One family: a black cub and a cinnamon cub.
bg-bb-kermode | White (Kermode) phase: never legal.
bg-bb-glacier-cubs | Black sow, two blue grey cubs.
```

```more colour phases in BC
- BC account (90%): "Black bears are not always black, and this variation is most apparent in British Columbia." Colour phases: black, cinnamon, brown, blonde.
- The interior race "is generally larger" and "the brown colour phase is common" (90%). That is your Heffley Creek and Okanagan bear.
- Typical black coat: tan muzzle, sometimes a white V on the chest (90%).
- White (Kermode, Spirit bear) phase: most often on the north central coast, about 10% of bears on Princess Royal and Gribbel Islands (90%).
- Blue (Glacier) phase: far northwest, Tatshenshini area (90%).
- Both protected: see the next screen.
```

## The grizzly: legal or not

```gallery
g-griz-side | Grizzly: hump is the high point. No season.
g-griz-face | Grizzly: dished face, small round ears.
```

- Grizzly: no open season in BC (98%).
- White or blue black bears: never legal (99%).

```video
commons-grizzly-bear | Grizzly: shoulder hump, dished face, short round ears.
```

```more the rules, with links (no new rules)
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- No open season on grizzly bear anywhere in BC (98%, synopsis page 19, via the [quick lesson](#/s/black-bear)).
- Item 15, page 11: unlawful "to hunt the white or blue (Glacier) colour phases of the black bear" (99%, [unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts)).
- Grizzlies live in the North Thompson and Wells Gray; rarer in the dry country near Kamloops, not absent (65 to 75%, quick lesson). Treat every big brown bear as a grizzly until three clues say otherwise (Tip).
- Killed one by mistake? Report it promptly: [[RAPP]] (Report All Poachers and Polluters) 1 877 952 7277 (99%, [after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill)).
```

## Boar, sow and cubs

```diagram mc-black-bear-age-classes
```

```photo bg-bb-yearling
```

- A bear under 2, or any bear with one: never (99%).
- The target: an adult male, a boar (98%).

```diagram sa-bear-age
```

```more the words of the rule and the notice
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Item 14, page 11: unlawful "to hunt a black bear less than 2 years old or any bear in its company" (99%, [unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts)).
- [[Cub]]: first year. [[Yearling]]: second year. Both under 2. The [[Sow]] with them is "in its company" (99%).
- Synopsis page 80, Notice to Black Bear Hunters: "Be patient. Don't shoot the moment you see a bear!" and "select an adult, male bear" (98%, via the [quick lesson](#/s/black-bear)).
- Cubs stay with the mother through their first year and den with her the first winter; they are on their own by the middle or end of their second spring (90%, BC account). So a spring sow can have yearlings the size of a big dog.
- [[Boar]] clues and sow clues are in chapter 9 (Judge 3 of 4).
```

## Size and weight

```diagram mc-black-bear-size-bars
```

- Males 80 to 300 kg; females 40 to 140 kg (90%).
- Shoulder 60 to 90 cm: about waist high (90%).

```more size details and what it means for you
- BC account (90%): adult males 60 to 90 cm at the shoulder, 130 to 190 cm long, 80 to 300 kg. Females 40 to 140 kg.
- Weight "varies greatly according to sex, season, food supply, and geographic area" (90%).
- Alaska: about 20% heavier in fall than after the den (85%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game). Chapter 2 has the fat year.
- A bear looks bigger than it is. A lone bear at 100 m with no reference is easy to overjudge (Tip, chapter 9).
- Meat math: chapter 10.
```

## Six races in BC

```diagram mc-black-bear-races
```

- Interior bears: U. a. cinnamomum. Brown is common (90%).
- Fraser Valley bears: U. a. altifrontalis, mostly black (90%).

```more the six races and why BC has so many
- BC "has more races of black bear than any other part of Canada" (90%, BC account).
- Why: bears came back after the ice from at least two refuges, one south and one near Haida Gwaii, plus BC's varied terrain and island isolation (90%).
- Races (90%, BC account):
  - **cinnamomum:** most of BC east of the Coast Range; larger, narrower teeth; brown phase common.
  - **altifrontalis:** coast from Oregon to Bella Coola, east into Manning Park and Lillooet; mostly black.
  - **vancouveri:** Vancouver Island and big nearby islands; mostly black.
  - **carlottae:** Haida Gwaii; massive skull, big molars; black only.
  - **kermodei:** coastal mainland, Burke Channel to the Nass; white and black phases.
  - **emmonsii:** far northwest, Tatshenshini; the blue Glacier bear.
- Your bases: Heffley Creek and the South Okanagan are cinnamomum country; Mission is altifrontalis country (85%, my reading of the ranges above).
```

## How many, and how hunters do

```diagram mc-black-bear-harvest
```

- BC: often quoted at 120,000 to 160,000 bears (70%).
- MU (Management Unit) 3-27: about 1 hunter in 4 got a bear (90%).

```more numbers, trend and the harvest table
- BC account 2001: "120,000 to 160,000", about a quarter of Canada's black bears; "not currently rare, threatened, or endangered" (90% that the account says it).
- WildSafeBC gives 120,000 to 150,000 (75%). A CBC (Canadian Broadcasting Corporation) report says the province cannot show the data behind these estimates (60%). So: lots of bears, number uncertain (70%).
- Bear numbers are higher in wet zones than dry ones; coast densities are higher because of salmon (90%, BC account).
- Conservation officers kill up to 1,000 conflict bears a year, mostly over garbage (90%, BC account 2001).
- BC big game harvest statistics, resident hunters, black bear (90%, survey estimates; success computed by Hunt Mentor):
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-black-bear-colour-trap
```

- **Rhyme:** "Hump and dish, let it be; straight and tall, black as can be."
- **Number trick:** 3 of 4: hump, face, ears, claws. Three must agree (85%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, built on Idaho Fish and Game's "identify your target"): "Every bear is a grizzly until its shape says it isn't."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Brown is a colour, not a kind."
- **Number trick:** 80 to 300: a boar in kilograms; 40 to 140: a sow (90%).
- **Number trick:** 2: never a bear under 2, never the bear with it (99%).
- **Number trick:** 6 races in BC, more than anywhere in Canada (90%).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely taught hunter education wisdom, opinion): "If you are not sure, you are not shooting."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, echoing the synopsis notice): "The first bear you see is rarely the one to take."

> [!mistake]
> - Calling a brown bear a grizzly, or a black bear, by colour.
> - Judging from one clue in bad light.
> - Shooting the first bear without watching for cubs.

> [!field]
> Next drive at dusk: for every bear you see, say out loud "hump, face, ears, alone, boar or sow".
```
