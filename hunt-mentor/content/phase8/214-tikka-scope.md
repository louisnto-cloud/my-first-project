## A hunting scope, part by part

```diagram tk-scope-parts
```

- Ring size must match the tube: 1 in or 30 mm (85%).
- A 3 to 9x40 covers black tails in timber and cutblocks (Tip).

```more choosing for your hunting
- "3 to 9x40": zoom 3 to 9 times, 40 mm front lens (85%, [scope guide](#/s/scope-guide/0)).
- Fraser Valley black tails are often close, in timber, at dawn and dusk (Tip, see the [mule deer Masterclass](#/s/mc-mule-deer-01) for the coastal race). Low power (2 to 4x) and a bright 40 to 44 mm lens matter more than 15x.
- Second focal plane, plain duplex reticle, capped turrets: simple and hard to bump (Tip).
- Set the diopter once for your eye: [Optics](#/s/optics-mastery/0) shows how (85%).
- Illuminated reticles: whether they count as a light for hunting is not settled in these lessons. VERIFY with a conservation officer before relying on one (55%).
- Thermal and night vision scopes are banned on the hunt (99%, [Rule Book: unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts/0)).
```

## Your rail from above

```diagram tk-rail-top
```

- Picatinny rail, about 122 mm, at the front of the receiver (75%).
- The drum sits behind it, under where the scope passes (95%).

```more the manual on rails and mounts
- "Some Tikka models are equipped with a Picatinny rail (MIL-STD-1913) on top of the receiver, which can be fitted with any standard Picatinny type scope mount. In the Picatinny rail the base screw grooves on the rail will act as recoil stops" (95%, Tikka manual page 11).
- "Before obtaining scope mounts, check their compatibility with the rifle (mounting rail type, etc.) and scope. Please refer to the instructions of the scope mounts for mounting, adjustments and use" (95%, page 11).
- "The rail is permanently fixed and should not be removed by the user. If you intend to remove the Picatinny rail, turn to qualified gunsmith" (95%, page 13).
- The Arctic rail: 122 mm (4.8 in), flush with the receiver front (75%, all4shooters); "5 inch long (shortened to accommodate the rear aperture sight)", zero degree (70%, Recoil).
- Short rail means the rings sit close together. Check that the rings land on the scope tube, not on the turret or the bells (Tip, [scope guide](#/s/scope-guide/0)).
```

## Clear the drum: 2 options

```diagram tk-clearance
```

- A: drum on, high rings or a cantilever mount (70%).
- B: drum off by the manual's steps, lower rings (95%).

```more options, pros, cons, and my lean
| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| A: drum stays on, high rings | Iron sights ready if the scope fails (Tip) | Scope sits high: cheek off the stock, harder to hold steady (Tip) |
| A2: cantilever one piece mount | Puts the scope where eye relief wants it on a short rail; Recoil's test mount sat about 1/4 in above the drum (70%) | Costs more; still high |
| B: drum off, lower rings | Lower, steadier cheek weld (Tip) | No quick iron sights; zero both again when you swap |

- Taking the drum off is in the manual: loosen the set screws and windage screws, slide it off the back of the 17 mm rail; refit at 0.8 Nm (95%, Tikka manual page 11).
- Measure before you buy (Tip): height from the rail top to the top of the drum, in mm. Your ring centre height minus half the eyepiece width must be bigger than that. VERIFY on your rifle.
- Ring height example: Leupold BackCountry cross slot 30 mm high rings are 1.14 in (29 mm) tall; 1 in medium rings are 0.9 in (23 mm) (95%, Leupold product pages, 2026-10-23).
- Bring the rifle to the store and dry fit scope and rings before you pay (Tip).

> [!lean]
> My lean: option B. Keep the drum in a labelled bag with its screws and a note of the windage clicks. Zero the irons first (chapter 3) so you can refit them in camp if the scope breaks.
```

## Mount the scope, step by step

```steps Mount the scope in 6 steps
tk-mount-1 | Prove it unloaded. Bolt out. Rail clean and dry.
tk-mount-2 | Rings on the rail, pushed forward in the slots.
tk-mount-3 | Scope in the bottom rings. Caps loose.
tk-mount-4 | Full picture on top power. Bolt must clear.
tk-mount-5 | Crosshair square to the rifle.
tk-mount-6 | Caps in a cross pattern, to the maker's torque.
```

- Follow your ring maker's sheet first (95%).
- Paint pen line on every screw after (Tip).

```more the steps in full
1. Prove the rifle unloaded, bolt and magazine out (95%, Tikka manual page 11).
2. Degrease the rail, ring bases and screws, unless your ring maker says otherwise (Tip).
3. Rings on the rail. Push each base forward in its slot toward the muzzle before you tighten, so recoil cannot shift it (Tip; the Picatinny grooves act as recoil stops, 95%, page 11).
4. Lay the scope in the bottom halves; caps on loosely.
5. Eye relief: rifle mounted as if to shoot, scope on its highest power, slide until the picture is full with no dark ring (85%, Vortex). Lift the bolt: the handle must not touch the eyepiece (Tip).
6. Level the crosshair to the rifle, not to the floor or a sloped bench (Tip).
7. Tighten cap screws a little at a time in a cross pattern, keeping the gaps even (Tip), then to the ring maker's number with a torque driver.
8. Lapping (grinding the ring insides to match): the Tikka manual does not ask for it (95%). Follow your ring maker; most modern machined rings are sold ready to use (Tip). VERIFY on your ring maker's sheet.
9. Vortex says do not use thread locker on ring screws: it acts as a lubricant and leads to over torquing (85%, Vortex). Your ring maker's sheet rules.
```

## Eye relief

```diagram tk-eye-relief
```

- About 9 cm (3.5 in) or more on a .308 (65%).
- Check it prone and sitting: your head creeps forward (Tip).

```more eye relief in full
- Eye relief is the distance from your eye to the eyepiece for a full picture (85%, [Optics](#/s/optics-mastery/0)).
- Vortex: at least about 3 in (7.6 cm) on a rifle scope for safety; it often shrinks as power goes up (85%).
- A .308 kicks. Too close and the eyepiece cuts your brow: the "scope bite" (Tip).
- Long eye relief also helps on a short rail: it lets the scope sit further forward (Tip).
```

## Zero the scope

```diagram tk-scope-clicks
```

- Bore sight at 25 to 30 m, confirm at 100 m (95%).
- 1/4 MOA click: about 0.73 cm at 100 m (70%).

```more the manual's method with a scope
1. Rifle solid on a rest, bolt out (95%, Tikka manual page 12).
2. Target at 25 to 30 m. Centre it in the bore.
3. Without moving the rifle, turn the turrets until the reticle is on the same spot.
4. At 100 to 150 m, from a solid rest, fire 3 shots. Unload, check with binoculars or a spotting scope.
5. "Adjust the sight (elevation and windage) so that reticle moves to the center of the shot grouping" (95%, page 12, figure 15). Repeat.
- Turret arrows: "UP" moves the shots up. Count clicks; read the click value on YOUR turret cap: 1/4 MOA (about 0.73 cm at 100 m) or 0.1 mil (1 cm at 100 m) (70%, [sighting in](#/s/sighting-in/0)).
- Which zero: 100 m or a longer zero. See [.308 ballistics](#/s/ballistics-308/0). For black tails in timber a 100 m zero keeps it simple (Tip).
- Check zero after any knock, flight, or rough ATV ride (Tip). The manual's yearly service includes checking scope and sight mounts (95%, page 19).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram tk-clearance
```

- **"Clear the drum, or drum's off."**
- **"Forward in the slot."** Ring bases pushed toward the muzzle.
- **"Their sheet, their number."** Ring torque only from the ring maker.

```more more hooks, mistakes and the field task
- **"Three and a half inches of peace."** About 9 cm of eye relief.
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "A scope you cannot trust is worse than iron sights."

> [!mistake]
> - Buying rings before measuring the drum height.
> - Cranking ring screws with no torque driver.
> - Setting eye relief on low power, then getting bitten on high power.

> [!field]
> Dry fit: rifle proved unloaded, scope in loose rings, lie prone on the floor, then sit. Slide the scope until both positions give a full picture. Then torque it.
```
