## The 4 marks

```diagram mbt-id-card
```

- Tail: wide, dark brown or black on top for most of its length (90%).
- Small white rump patch, ears about 3 cm shorter than a mule deer's (90%).

```more the full ID list
- **Tail:** dark brown or black for most of its length, not just the tip (90%, BC deer account 2000). WDFW (Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife): broad, dark brown on the back, black near the tip (80%).
- **Alarm:** a raised tail shows the broad white underside (80%, WDFW).
- **Rump:** a small white rump patch; the mule deer's is large (90%).
- **Ears:** smaller than a mule deer's, about 3 cm shorter (90%, Nyberg and Janz 1990).
- **Body and coat:** smaller and slightly darker than a mule deer; a browner winter coat; smaller glands on the hock (90%).
- **Antlers:** branch like a mule deer's: each beam forks, then forks again (90%). Chapter 1, "Antlers by age", has the detail.
- Sitka black tails (north coast, Haida Gwaii) are smaller and darker again. Not near Harrison (90%).
```

## Look alikes: legal or not

```diagram mbt-id-flow
```

- White tailed deer: no season anywhere in Region 2 (99%).
- Not sure? No shot (Tip).

```more the look alike table
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

| Look alike | Tell it by | Legal point |
|---|---|---|
| Mule deer or hybrid | Big white rump, thin white tail with a black tip (90%) | One species in law: "mule (black-tailed) deer" (99%, page 27). Same licence (99%, page 8) |
| White tailed deer | Wide brown tail, white flag; tines rise off one main beam (85%) | No season in Region 2 (99%, pages 14 and 27). Some live at Herrling Island near Hope (99%, page 27) |
| Elk | Much bigger, dark neck, pale yellow rump (85%) | No Region 2 general season row (99%, page 28) |
| Doe or fawn | No visible antlers | No antlerless season in MUs (Management Units) 2-8, 2-18, 2-19 (99%, page 28) |

- Coast and interior deer interbreed where their ranges meet, so some valley deer look mixed (90%, BC deer account). In law they are one species, so a hybrid buck uses the same licence (85%, my reading).
- More rules: [Your Harrison deer hunt](#/s/local-harrison-deer), [Rule Book: Region 2](#/s/rb-region-2).
```

## Antlers by age

```diagram mbt-antler-ages
```

- Yearlings: about 90% are spikes (90%).
- A spike is a buck. Legal in 2-8, 2-18, 2-19 (99%).

```gallery
g-bt-victoria | Young buck, Victoria: wide dark tail.
bg-md-blacktail-vi | Young buck, Vancouver Island: short velvet antlers.
g-bt-buck-velvet | Older buck in velvet: forks on forks.
```

```more antlers, age and the law
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- South coast data (90%, Nyberg and Janz 1990):
  - Male fawns grow no antlers; you may see the bumps (pedicels) up close.
  - Yearlings: about 90% are spikes with long single point antlers; about 10% have one more point a side.
  - By 2.5 years most bucks have forks, 2 points a side.
  - By 4.5 years, on good range, usually 3 or more points per antler. On poor range many adult bucks only grow forks.
- BC deer account (90%): yearlings almost always spikes; two year olds mostly small 2 points, sometimes spikes; 3 years and older 2, 3 or 4 points a side. Points are "not a reliable way to determine the age".
- The law: a buck is a deer "bearing visible bony antlers" (99%, page 4). The rows for MUs 2-8, 2-18 and 2-19 say Bucks, not 4 point bucks (99%, page 28). So a spike is a legal buck there (95%, my reading).
- Harvested bucks on Vancouver Island (Campbell River check, 1976 to 1980): average age 2.5 years; only 4% older than 5.5 (90%, Nyberg and Janz).
```

## Size: a small deer

```diagram mbt-size-bars
```

- Buck 48 to 90 kg, doe 40 to 65 kg (90%).
- The vital zone is small. Short, sure shots (Tip).

```more size details and the Fraser Valley
- BC deer account (90%): adult males in good condition about 48 to 90 kg, females 40 to 65 kg. Mule deer bucks 68 to 113 kg.
- South coast (90%, Nyberg and Janz 1990): adult does 35 to 45 kg in summer and fall, 50 to 60 kg on excellent range. Males 50 to 110 kg, "with the largest ones coming from the fertile lower Fraser Valley" and from migratory deer that feed high in summer.
- WDFW (80%): bucks 140 to 200 lb (64 to 91 kg), does 90 to 130 lb (41 to 59 kg).
- Chest height of an adult on Vancouver Island: about 57 cm (90%). That is knee to hip on most adults (Tip).
- Bucks are heaviest about mid November to early December, then lose 20 to 25% over most winters (90%). Chapter 4 has the rut year.
- Pack out: a field dressed buck is still a load. Chapter 6.
```

## Where they fit in BC

```diagram mmd-bc-subspecies
```

- Coast, west of the Coast and Cascade summits: black tails (90%).
- The Fraser Valley is black tail country (90%).

```gallery
bg-md-sitka-haida | Sitka black tailed doe, Haida Gwaii: smaller, redder.
g-bt-buck | Columbian black tailed buck and doe, Washington.
```

```more three races and numbers
- One species, Odocoileus hemionus, three races in BC (90%, BC deer account): mule deer (interior), Columbian black tailed deer (coast, Vancouver Island, Fraser Valley, Sechelt Peninsula), Sitka black tailed deer (north coast, introduced to Haida Gwaii).
- The Coast Range largely separates black tails and mule deer, mostly in winter. They interbreed where the ranges meet (90%).
- Black tails spread north from Washington as the ice left, 15,000 to 10,000 years ago. Long isolation made them "a species in the making" (90%).
- BC black tails: 150,000 to 250,000 in recent decades (90%, BC deer account 2000).
- Black tails swim well and live on most coastal islands (90%).
```

## Numbers near Harrison

- 2-18 (east side, Agassiz): about 25 hunter days per deer (90%).
- Region 2 average: about 13 days. Plan several trips (Tip).

## Memory hooks

```diagram mmd-tail-compare
```

- **Rhyme** (Tip): "Black on top, the whole way down: coast deer in a forest gown."
- **Number trick:** 90, 2, 57: 90% of yearlings are spikes, forks by 2.5, chest 57 cm (90%).

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom, not law): "Tail, then antlers, then the backstop. In that order, every time."

> [!mistake]
> - Calling every Fraser Valley deer a mule deer and hunting it like open country.
> - Passing a spike because "it is too small to be legal". In 2-8, 2-18 and 2-19 a spike is a buck.
> - Shooting at a flag tail in the brush. White tails have no season in Region 2.

> [!field]
> Next drive to Harrison at dusk: for every deer, say out loud "tail, rump, antlers, buck or doe".
