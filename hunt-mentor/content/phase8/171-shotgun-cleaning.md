- Every strip starts: point it safe, finger off, unload and prove (95%).
- Eye protection: springs and solvent (95%).

```more before any cleaning
- Remington, Beretta and Browning all say: check the gun is completely unloaded before you take it apart (95%).
- Browning: keep all ammunition away from the cleaning area and "never test the mechanical function of your shotgun with live ammunition" (95%).
- Wear eye protection: "springs, spring-tensioned parts, solvents" (95%, Citori manual).
- Good ventilation, wash your hands after: lead and solvent exposure (95%, Browning and Remington).
- General care, wet and cold weather, storage law: [Firearm care](#/s/firearm-care/8). This chapter is the strip for each action.
- These are general steps from three makers' manuals, as examples. Your gun's manual wins (Tip).
```

## Pump: Remington 870

```steps Field strip a pump in 5 steps
sg2-pc-1 | Safety on. Lock, open, prove empty.
sg2-pc-2 | Fore end halfway. Magazine cap off.
sg2-pc-3 | Pull the barrel forward and out.
sg2-pc-4 | Carrier up, left shell latch in, fore end and bolt off.
sg2-pc-5 | Clean, thin oil, reassemble, check.
```

- Clean the bore from the chamber end when you can (95%).
- The barrel off is enough for most cleans (Tip).

```more the 870 manual in full
1. Safety on. Push the action bar lock, pull the fore end back, check chamber and magazine (95%).
2. Slide the fore end about halfway forward. Unscrew the magazine cap (95%).
3. Hold the barrel ahead of the magazine tube and pull it from the receiver (95%).
4. Push the carrier up, press and hold the left shell latch, slide the fore end forward off the magazine tube; lift the bolt and slide off the action bars (95%).
5. Trigger plate: the manual shows tapping out two pins. Keep the safety on so the hammer does not snap forward (95%). Tip: most owners leave this to a gunsmith or the yearly service.
- Clean: brush with solvent, patches until clean, remove residue from the barrel's locking notch; choke threads cleaned and oiled; bolt "as a unit"; receiver brushed and wiped, "There may be sharp edges inside the receiver" (95%).
- Lubrication: "Over-lubrication should be avoided at all times. A thin coat... is all that is needed" (95%).
- Reassembly is in the manual, with the shell latches in order. Go slow the first time (95%).
- Remington recommends a professional service every year (95%).
```

## Gas semi auto: Beretta A300

```steps Field strip a gas semi auto in 5 steps
sg2-gc-1 | Prove empty. Carrier button down. Bolt open.
sg2-gc-2 | Fore end cap off. Fore end off.
sg2-gc-3 | Barrel out. Hold the piston in its cylinder.
sg2-gc-4 | Ease the bolt forward. Handle out. Bolt off.
sg2-gc-5 | Clean piston, seal, tube, cylinder. Dry.
```

- The gas parts are cleaned but not oiled (95%).
- Never take apart the exhaust valve (95%).

```more the A300 manual in full
- Barrel: carrier stop button fully down; bolt back and open; unscrew the fore end cap anticlockwise; pull the fore end off toward the muzzle; pull the barrel out "while holding the piston inside the gas cylinder" (95%).
- Bolt: finger on the cocking handle, press the release and let the bolt go forward slowly; pull the cocking handle out "forcefully"; slide the connecting rod and sleeve off the magazine tube and the bolt comes out (95%).
- Trigger plate: "only needs to be disassembled in order to clean the trigger mechanism" (95%).
- Routine clean at the end of a shooting day: barrel with a soft cloth or bronze brush and gun oil, then a dry cloth, then a lightly oiled cloth (95%).
- Gas parts: spray the piston, its seal and the magazine tube with oil, clean, brush the cylinder walls, wipe clean. "The components specified above must not be lubricated." The piston must glide freely (95%).
- Reassembly: piston into the cylinder first (squeeze the seal with your fingers), then the barrel onto the magazine tube (95%).
- "Excess oil or grease obstructing the barrel, even partially, is very dangerous when firing" (95%).
- Store it taken apart in its case, dry. Not in a leather or fabric case: they hold moisture (95%).
```

## Inertia semi auto: Stoeger M3000

```steps Field strip an inertia semi auto in 4 steps
sg2-ic-1 | Prove empty. Magazine cap off.
sg2-ic-2 | Barrel and fore end forward, off the tube.
sg2-ic-3 | Handle off. Hold the bar. Bolt out slowly.
sg2-ic-4 | Wipe, oil the rails and tube, spring back in.
```

- No gas system to clean (95%).
- The firing pin is under spring pressure if you strip the bolt (95%).

```more the M3000 manual in full
- Unscrew the magazine cap; butt on your hip, pull the barrel and fore end forward off the tube; rotate the fore end off the barrel ring (95%).
- Pull the bolt handle off while holding the action slightly open; hold the action bar against the recoil spring, press the carrier release and ease the bolt forward and out (95%).
- Bolt strip (firing pin, locking head, inertia spring) is shown in the manual. "Always make sure that the inertia spring... is positioned between" the head and the bolt when you rebuild (95%).
- Clean fouling from the receiver; oil the bolt rails and the outside of the magazine tube; clean the trigger assembly (95%).
- Leave the choke in when you clean the bore so residue does not get into the threads (95%).
- Tighten the magazine cap firmly after reassembly, and again after a few shots: it holds the barrel on (95%).
- Function check unloaded, as the manual lists (95%).
```

## Over under: Browning Citori

```steps Field strip an over under in 3 steps
sg2-oc-1 | Prove empty. Close it. Latch out, forearm off.
sg2-oc-2 | Open it. Lift the barrels off the hinge pin.
sg2-oc-3 | Clean bores, grease the hinge, no oil in wood.
```

- Clean after every day of shooting (95%).
- "Never attempt to take your firearm apart further" than the manual shows (95%).

```more the Citori manual in full
- Forearm: "Close the action, anchor the buttstock against your upper leg and lift outward on the forearm takedown latch," then pivot the forearm away (95%).
- Barrels: open the action, disengage the barrel lug from the hinge pin, lift the barrels out (95%).
- Bores: patch from the breech end; look for lead or plastic streaks near the chambers and muzzles; brush with a brass brush if heavy; dry patch, then a lightly oiled patch (95%).
- Grease: the hinge pin recess, barrel lugs and forearm bracket "must always be coated with a film of high quality grease" (95%).
- "Do not place large quantities of oil into the action. Excess oil will run back into the wood of the stock and soften the wood" (95%).
- Check no patch is left in a barrel (95%).
- For storage, put the forearm back on the barrels: two units (95%).
```

## Oil, grease or dry

```diagram sg2-lube
```

- Thin oil on steel. Grease on an over under hinge. Nothing on the A300 gas parts (95%).
- Dry patch through the bore before you shoot (95%).

```more why
- Remington: chamber and bore "must be thoroughly wiped dry" before use (95%).
- Cold days: oil thickens; less is better (Tip, [Firearm care](#/s/firearm-care/10)).
- Wet days: dry, then the full clean the same day (Tip, [Firearm care](#/s/firearm-care/9)).
- Log each clean: date, rounds fired, what you found (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Clean it the night you shoot it. Tomorrow it is rust.

> [!mistake]
> - Live shells on the bench while you clean.
> - Oil poured into the action "to be safe".
> - Taking a gun apart past what the manual shows.

> [!field]
> After every duck day: barrel off, bore clean, choke out and threads oiled, gun dry, cased open, out of the truck.
