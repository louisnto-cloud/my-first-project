> [!why]
> Elk leave a lot behind: big tracks, piles of pellets, torn trees, stinking wallows and noise. Read it and you know if the herd is here today, last week or never.

## Tracks to scale

```diagram mc-elk-track-scale
```

- Front track about 7.6 to 12.4 cm (70%).
- Rounder and blunter than deer (65%).

```gallery
hab-elk-tracks-shore | A herd churned this shore.
hab-elk-trail-snow | Single file through spring snow.
ts-elk-tracks-cmr | One elk print in sand: round and blunt.
```

```more tracks in detail
- Front tracks 7.6 to 12.4 cm long; hind 6.4 to 11.4 cm (70%, field guide via iNaturalist). Missouri Department of Conservation: 3.5 to 5 inches (9 to 13 cm), front slightly larger (70%).
- Shape: a rounded heart, split down the middle; tips blunter than a deer's (65%).
- Walking step 45 to 90 cm (1.5 to 3 ft); trail width 18 to 33 cm (70%).
- Moose tracks are longer and more pointed, 11 to 18 cm (65%, Hunt Mentor hoof grid). Cattle: round and blunt, no point (65%).
- Telling bull from cow by track: a common claim (bull front foot wider) but unconfirmed (VERIFY). Do not judge legality from a track.
```

## Droppings

```diagram sg-scat-hoofed
```

- Pellets bigger and rounder than deer (70%).
- Soft clumps on rich green feed (65%).

```photo hab-elk-pellets
```

```more droppings in detail
- Elk pellets: about 1.3 to 2.5 cm long and 1.1 to 1.7 cm across; bigger and more oval than deer pellets (70%, field guides). Often dimpled on one end (65%).
- On lush summer feed they clump or form soft patties (65%, quick lesson).
- Moose pellets are larger, 2.5 to 4 cm long (65%).
- Fresh: shiny, dark, moist, soft. Old: dull, dry, pale, crumbly (Tip, see the freshness ladder below).
- Lots of piles of different sizes: a herd with calves (Tip).
```

## Beds

```diagram mc-elk-bed-site
```

- Flattened ovals together in timber near feed (Tip).
- Summer beds: shade, north slopes (75%).

```more beds in detail
- Elk bed close to cover after feeding (80%, BC habitat model).
- A herd bed site: several flattened ovals, pellets and hair, often on a bench or gentle slope in timber (Tip). Elk use slopes of 15 to 30% most (80%).
- Fresh bed: grass still pressed and damp underneath; in frost or snow, melted and not refrozen (Tip).
- Found warm beds? The herd is close and may have heard you. Stop, check the wind, glass ahead (Tip).
```

## Rubs and thrashed brush

```photo hab-elk-rub
```

- Bulls rub and thrash trees in the rut (90%).
- Higher and rougher than deer rubs (Tip).

```more rubs in detail
- In the rut, bulls thrash vegetation and rub saplings and small trees (90%, BC account).
- Rubbed or debarked trees often line trails leading to wallows (70%, RMEF (Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation)).
- Elk rubs: bark stripped high, shredded, sometimes on trees thicker than a deer would use (Tip).
- In late summer bulls rub off velvet; shreds of velvet can hang on a rubbed tree (Tip).
- Winter: elk also strip bark from aspen with their teeth, leaving dark scars (65%, RMEF lists bark in winter diet; scar pattern Tip).
```

## Wallows

```diagram mc-elk-wallow-read
```

- Bulls urinate and roll in mud pits in the rut (85 to 90%).
- Fresh: muddy water, splash, urine smell (Tip).

```photo hab-elk-wallow
```

```more wallows in detail
- Bulls wallow, spray urine and roll to coat themselves with a strong scent that advertises them to cows and rivals (90%, BC account; 85%, RMEF (Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation)).
- Wallows start about two weeks before cows come into heat and fade as the rut ends; bulls may visit any hour (70%, RMEF).
- Where: springs, seeps, creeks, wet meadows, pond edges; in dry country even stock tanks; where water is short, bulls urinate on loose soil and roll (70%).
- A long used wallow is dished out, eroded, with little plant cover; check for rubs on trails leading in (70%).
- Fresh signs: cloudy water, mud splashed on plants, fresh tracks, a strong urine smell (Tip).
- Hunting a wallow: chapter 9.
```

## Sounds are sign too

```diagram mc-elk-sounds
```

- Bugle: a bull in the rut (90%).
- Mew and chirp: cows and calves. Bark: alarm (65%).

```video
yt-elk-bugle | A bugle: low roar, high whistle, grunts.
yt-elk-cow-call | A cow mew: the most common elk sound.
```

```more sounds in detail
- Bugle: starts as a low chesty roar, glides into a high bugling sound and ends in grunts (90%, BC account). It can be heard 1.6 km or more (80%).
- Bugle whistles can rise above 2,000 Hz (2 kHz) (70%, research citing Feighny and others 2006).
- Mews: the most common cow call, between cows and calves (65%).
- Chirps: short contact calls in a moving herd (65%).
- Barks: alarm (65%).
- Splashing, grunting or whining at a wallow: a bull is there now; close in fast while he is busy (70%, RMEF (Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation)).
- Listen at dawn and dusk from a ridge in September before you walk (Tip).
```

## How fresh: put it together

```diagram sg-fresh-ladder
```

- Minutes to hours: wet, warm, crisp (Tip).
- Hunt near the freshest sign at dawn and dusk (Tip).

```more freshness in detail
| Sign | Fresh (hours) | Old (days or more) |
|---|---|---|
| Track | Sharp edges, crumbs of soil on top, no debris inside | Rounded, rained on, leaves in it |
| Pellets | Shiny, moist, soft | Dull, dry, crumbly |
| Wallow | Cloudy water, wet splash, strong smell | Clear water, dry mud |
| Rub | Wet white wood, fresh shavings | Grey, dry wood |
| Bed | Grass still pressed, damp | Grass springing back |

- All Tip. Weather changes everything: rain resets the clock (Tip).
```

## How to follow a herd

```diagram mc-elk-follow
```

- Follow the freshest tracks, check the wind, look ahead (Tip).
- Trail climbs to timber: slow way down (Tip).

```more following in detail
1. Pick the freshest tracks and note the number and sizes (Tip).
2. Check the wind before every move. If the herd's direction puts your scent on them, circle (Tip).
3. Look ahead, not down. Glass openings and timber edges 100 to 300 m in front (Tip).
4. When the trail climbs into timber or slows, beds are close (Tip).
5. Fresh beds or steaming droppings: stop, glass, wait (Tip).
6. Snow makes it easy to follow and easy to be seen. Move only when heads are down (Tip).
- Following a wounded elk is different: [blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing) and chapter 10.
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-elk-sign-grid
```

- **Rhyme:** "Splash, grunt, stink and bark: the herd is near, before it is dark."
- **Number trick:** 8 to 12: cm of an elk front track; a deer track fits inside (65 to 70%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Old sign tells you where. Fresh sign tells you when."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Rub, wallow, track and pile: bull was here a little while."
- **Number trick:** 2 weeks: wallows start about two weeks before cows come into heat (70%).
- **Number trick:** 1.6 km: a bugle carries that far or more (80%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "A warm bed means stop walking."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Never stand in the wallow you want to hunt."

> [!mistake]
> - Walking straight to a wallow and leaving your scent all over it.
> - Following a herd trail with the wind at your back.
> - Calling a track "a bull" from its size.

> [!field]
> On your next hike, measure five hoof tracks with a ruler and photograph them beside the ruler. Practise guessing before you measure.
```
