> [!why]
> A goose is a flying engine wrapped in muscle, bone and feathers. Know where the engine sits and you know why steel BB, close range and the head matter.

- Big breast muscle on a deep keel: that is flight, and your meat (80%).
- Hollow bones join the air sacs (80%).

## Built to fly

```diagram mgs-skeleton
```

- The keel (breastbone) anchors the breast muscles (80%).
- Legs sit far back: a goose walks and swims well (Tip).

```more muscle and bone
- A bird's skeleton is light: some bones are hollow and linked to the air sacs; others are dense where strength matters (80%, Stanford Birds).
- Air spaces in bones are more extensive in large birds than small ones (70%, search preview of a bird anatomy handout).
- Flight power comes from the breast muscles on the keel. That is why a goose breast is so big (80%).
- Wing bones and the thick breast stand between a pellet from below and the vital organs (Tip, 65%).
- Geese fly in V formation (80%, Audubon) and can travel more than 1,000 km in a day (80%, Hinterland Who's Who).
```

## Breathing: air sacs

```anim mgs-air-sacs Fresh air crosses the lungs on the way in and the way out.
```

- Birds have 9 air sacs and one way air flow (85%).
- The breathing system fills about one fifth of the body (80%).

```more how a goose breathes
- The 9 air sacs: one interclavicular, two cervical, two front thoracic, two rear thoracic, two abdominal (85%, Weber State ornithology lecture).
- The sacs do not trade oxygen. They are bellows that push air one way through stiff lungs (85%).
- Breath in: most fresh air goes to the rear sacs. Breath out: the rear sacs push it through the lungs. Used air moves to the front sacs and leaves on the next breath out (80%).
- About one fifth of a bird's body volume is breathing system, versus about one twentieth in a mammal (80%, Stanford Birds).
- A bird takes about one breath every 6 to 10 heartbeats; a mammal about one every 4.5 (80%).
- Air sacs also help shed heat in hard flight (70%).
- What it means for you: a pellet through the body cavity can collapse sacs, but a goose hit only in the sacs can still fly far (Tip, 60%).
```

## Heart and big vessels

```diagram mgs-heart-lungs
```

- Heart: low and forward, under the base of the neck (80%).
- Lungs: tight against the back ribs (80%).

```more heart and blood
- Birds have relatively large four chamber hearts (80%, University of Connecticut lecture notes; Stanford Birds).
- Resting heart rate in birds: about 150 to 350 beats a minute, higher in flight (80%).
- Blood pressure can reach 300 to 400 mm of mercury (75%).
- Big vessels run from the heart up the neck to the head. A neck hit cuts them (Tip, 65%).
- Heart and lungs sit in the front half of the body, shielded by the keel and breast below and the back ribs above (80%).
```

## The gut and gizzard

```diagram mgs-gut
```

- Grit in the gizzard grinds grass (85%).
- Grass passes in about 1.5 hours (70%). They eat most of the day.

```more digestion in full
- Path: bill, gullet, glandular stomach (proventriculus), muscular gizzard, intestine with two blind pouches (ceca), vent (85%, FAO poultry digestion chapter).
- The gizzard is the grinding mill. It needs grit, small stones the goose swallows (85%).
- A true crop in geese: sources disagree. VERIFY. Hunters often find grain packed in the gullet (Tip).
- Brent geese passed food in about 1.5 hours (70%, Wildfowl journal). Grazing upland geese digest only 27 to 34% of grass organic matter (70%).
- One barnacle goose study estimated about 160 droppings a day (60%, crude estimate). Lots of droppings is good sign for you. See [Chapter 8](#/s/mc-geese-08).
- Geese often feed 12 hours a day or more (80%, Hinterland Who's Who).
- Canada geese on rougher, high fibre food ate less, held it longer and grew bigger guts (70%).
```

## Body heat

```diagram mgs-body-heat
```

- Birds run warmer than you: about 38.5°C resting, 41°C active (85%).
- Down traps heat; feet feel less cold (80%).

```more cold and heat
- Mean bird body temperature: 38.5°C resting, 41.0°C active, 43.9°C in hard activity (85%, Prinzinger and others 1991, search preview).
- Waterfowl feet have fewer nerves, so they stand on ice and snow for long spells (80%, Ducks Unlimited senses article).
- Cold changes behaviour: below about minus 9°C (15°F) at sunrise, a big wintering flock usually skipped its morning flight (80%, Raveling and others 1972). See [Chapter 6](#/s/mc-geese-06).
- A shot goose holds heat in its feathers. Cool it fast (Tip). See [Chapter 10](#/s/mc-geese-10).
```

## The fat cycle

```diagram mgs-fat-cycle
```

- Hens store reserves for about a month of sitting (80%).
- Fall: they fatten on grain (Tip).

```more the year in reserves
- Before nesting, geese feed hard to store reserves that carry the hen through about one month of incubation (80%, Hinterland Who's Who).
- The hen sits 25 to 28 days and eats little (80%). She is at her leanest after hatch (Tip).
- Moult costs: a study of Illinois geese found the largest nutrition costs of moult from July to November (70%).
- The chart shape is a Tip, built from these facts, not a measured curve.
- For the table: fall birds on grain are often fatter than early September birds (Tip).
```

## How long they live

| Age | What it means (80%, Hinterland Who's Who) |
|---|---|
| Gosling | Flies at 6 to 9 weeks |
| Year 1 | Stays with parents all year |
| Year 2 | Finds a mate |
| Year 2 or 3 | First nest |
| 10 to 24 years | Old hand of the flock |

- Most that survive year one live 10 years or more, up to about 24 (80%).
- About half of goslings survive to fly (80%).

```more longevity records
- A Canada goose banded at the Jack Miner sanctuary in Ontario was at least 23 years old when found (70%, Auk 1955, search preview).
- Older sources list longer records; the current official banding record could not be read here. VERIFY.
- Old birds have seen many hunters. They are often the wary ones (Tip). See [Chapter 4](#/s/mc-geese-04).
```

## What the body means for the shot and the meat

```diagram mgs-vitals
```

- Head, neck, front body: clean kills (Tip, 65%).
- Rear body hits: cripples that fly on (Tip).

```more shot and meat notes
- Thick feathers, big muscle and strong wing bones stop small pellets. Use steel BB for big Canada geese (70%, quick lesson; Ducks Unlimited).
- Pattern: at least 60 pellets in a 76 cm (30 in) circle at your longest range (65%, Ducks Unlimited).
- On close decoying birds aim at the head, which is about the size of a dove's body (65%, Ducks Unlimited). Fewer pellets end up in the breast (65%).
- Max range for you this season: 35 m (38 yd) (Tip, from the quick lesson).
- Meat: the breast is the prize, about 1 kg boned from a big Canada goose (60%). Legs are worth slow cooking (Tip).
- Full detail: [Chapter 10](#/s/mc-geese-10) and [Shot placement](#/s/shot-placement).
```

> [!tip] Memory rhymes (made for this app)
> - "Keel and breast, the engine's chest."
> - "Nine sacs breathe one way: air in, air through, air away."
> - "Heart up front and low: aim the head, and down they go."

- Number trick: **9, 41, 1.5**. 9 air sacs, about 41°C active, about 1.5 hours from bill to dropping.

> [!rule] Old hand's rule (tradition, opinion)
> Shoot the head, not the goose. Widely taught by goose guides: aim at the white cheek, not the whole bird.
