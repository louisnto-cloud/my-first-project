> [!why]
> A semi auto reloads itself. That is softer on your shoulder with heavy steel, and less to think about when ducks come in. It also has more parts to keep clean.

```diagram sg2-semi-parts
```

- Steps follow the Beretta A300 Outlander manual (gas). Inertia notes follow the Stoeger M3000 manual (95%).
- Your own gun's manual wins (Tip).

```more two kinds of semi auto
- Gas: a little gas from the barrel pushes a piston that works the action (95%, Beretta A300 manual).
- Inertia: the gun recoils, a stiff spring in the bolt stores that energy and works the action (95%, Stoeger M3000 manual).
- Which to buy: [Chapter 12](#/s/mc-shotgun-12). Short version: gas is softer, inertia runs cleaner (70%, [Choosing your shotgun](#/s/shotgun-guide/2)).
```

## Gas: how it cycles

```diagram sg2-semi-gas
```

- Gas, piston, bolt back, spring forward (95%).
- The A300 vents extra gas from heavy loads by itself (95%).

```more the A300 gas system
- Beretta: a "self-cleaning cylinder and piston" with an exhaust valve, so excess pressure from the most powerful loads is vented (95%, A300 Outlander manual).
- Shoots 70 mm (2 3/4 in) 24 g (7/8 oz) to 76 mm (3 in) 57 g (2 oz) loads without adjustment (95%).
- "NEVER STRIP the spring of the valve or the valve retaining nut... If necessary, contact a competent gunsmith" (95%).
- Gas spreads recoil over time, which is why it feels softer (70%, [Choosing your shotgun](#/s/shotgun-guide/2)).
```

## Inertia: how it cycles

```diagram sg2-semi-inertia
```

- No gas, no piston. Recoil and a spring do the work (95%).
- It needs real recoil. Light target loads may not cycle (95%).

```more the M3000 inertia system
- Stoeger: a spring sits between the locking head and the bolt. On firing the bolt moves about 4 mm relative to the gun and compresses it; the spring then cycles the bolt (95%, M3000 manual).
- "The inertia system requires shells that generate enough kinetic energy (recoil) to fully cycle the action": at least 1 oz (28 g), 3 dram loads (95%).
- Stoeger says inertia avoids gas system problems that need "frequent cleaning" (95%, maker claim).
- Because the gun must recoil to cycle, inertia guns tend to feel sharper (70%, [Choosing your shotgun](#/s/shotgun-guide/2)).
```

## Load, step by step (gas, A300)

```steps Load a gas semi auto in 3 steps
sg2-semi-load-1 | Point it safe. Bolt back and locked. Safety on.
sg2-semi-load-2 | One shell in the port. Fingers clear. Release.
sg2-semi-load-3 | Shells through the gate into the magazine.
```

- A300: the safety only goes on when the hammer is cocked (95%).
- Factory reducer: 2 in the magazine, 3 in the gun (95%).

```more the manual's words
- Check the carrier stop button is pressed fully down. Pull the bolt back with the cocking handle until it locks open. Push the safety so the red ring is hidden (95%, A300 manual).
- Put the first shell in the chamber through the ejection port. "Keeping your hands away from the ejection port, press the release button" (95%).
- Push more shells through the loading gate so they catch on the retaining tooth (95%).
- Beretta fits a reducer at the factory: "no more than three shots (two in the magazine, and one in the cartridge chamber)" (95%). That matches the bird law (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-migratory-birds/32)).
- After the last shot the bolt stays open, showing the magazine is empty (95%).
```

## Unload, step by step (gas, A300)

```steps Unload a gas semi auto in 3 steps
sg2-semi-unload-1 | Point it safe. Safety on. Handle back: chamber shell out.
sg2-semi-unload-2 | Lift the carrier, release the magazine shells one by one.
sg2-semi-unload-3 | Look, feel, count. Bolt stays open.
```

- Count what you loaded. Count what came out (Tip).
- Inertia guns unload a little differently: read the next screen (95%).

```more the manual's words, both guns
- A300: safety on, pull the bolt back to throw out the chamber shell, let it lock, raise the carrier, push the front shell lightly into the magazine and press the release, guiding the shells out (95%).
- A300 then: check the magazine and receiver are empty, safety off, bolt held back 2 cm, pull the trigger to let the hammer down (95%). Only after you have proved it empty.
- M3000: safety on, butt on your hip, pull the bolt back to throw out the chamber shell, let it close; turn the gun over, push the carrier down and press the front of the carrier latch; each press releases one shell (95%, M3000 manual).
- M3000 warns the magazine spring "can yield enough force to cause injury" (95%).
```

## What goes wrong

```diagram sg2-semi-fails
```

- Dirt, too much oil, light shells: most stoppages (70%).
- Bolt slams shut hard. Fingers out of the port (95%).

```more fixes in full
- Inertia gun with light target loads: may not cycle. Use at least the maker's minimum load (95%, M3000 manual).
- Gas gun: residue builds up on the piston, its seal and the magazine tube. Clean them (95%, A300 manual). How: [Chapter 11](#/s/mc-shotgun-11).
- Piston put back wrong: Beretta says put the piston into the cylinder first, then slide the barrel on (95%).
- Gun does not fire: keep it pointed safe, safety on, wait at least one minute, then unload (95%, A300 manual).
- Oil in cold weather thickens. Thin film only (Tip; see [Cold weather](#/s/firearm-care/10)).
- Riding the bolt release with a thumb, or a loose hold, can cause a short cycle (Tip, 55%).
```

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): An automatic does what you tell it, fast. Tell it the right thing slowly first.

> [!mistake]
> - Fingers in the port when you press the bolt release.
> - Target loads in an inertia gun, then blaming the gun.
> - Oiling the gas piston "to help". Beretta says those parts must not be lubricated.

> [!field]
> Shoot 5 of your hunting loads at the range before the opener. Every one must cycle. If not, clean it and try again.
