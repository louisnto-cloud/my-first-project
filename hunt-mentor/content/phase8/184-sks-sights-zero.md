> [!why]
> A surplus SKS (Simonov self loading carbine) was zeroed for a soldier you will never meet. Make it hit where you look before you aim at a deer.

```diagram sks-sights
```

- Rear: an open notch on a leaf. Front: a hooded post (99%).
- You zero by moving the front post (95%).

```more the sights in the manual
- RCMP description: "hooded adjustable post front sight and tangent notch rear sight" (99%, Firearms Reference Table).
- "The front sight is adjustable for zero" (95%, US Army TC 9-56, 1969).
- Zeroing basics, targets and the 3 shot group method are in [sighting in](#/s/sighting-in/0). Not repeated here.
- [[Zero]]: the distance where the bullet hits your point of aim.
```

## The rear sight leaf

```diagram sks-rear-leaf
```

- Numbers are hundreds of metres: 1 is 100 m, 10 is 1000 m (95%).
- First mark: the battle setting, for up to 300 m (95%).

```more setting the rear sight
- "Set the rear sight for the desired range by depressing the slide catch and moving the sight bar along the leaf until the front edge of the bar is aligned with the line below the number that corresponds to the range in hundreds of meters" (95%, TC 9-56).
- "The first character on the sight leaf is a battle sight setting that is used in combat, for shooting at ranges up to 300 meters" (95%).
- For deer at hunting range, set the leaf to the 1 (100 m) mark after you zero, and leave it there (Tip).
- Marks out to 1000 m are military. Your ethical range is far shorter ([chapter 7](#/s/mc-sks-07)).
```

## Sight picture

```diagram sks-sight-picture
```

- Post centred in the notch, top level with the notch top (85%).
- Focus on the front post, not the deer (85%).

```more sight picture details
- Same rule as any open sight: equal light either side of the post, post top level with the notch shoulders (85%, hunter education).
- Your eye can focus on one distance. Keep the post sharp; the target and rear notch go a little soft (85%).
- Hold: put the post top where you want to hit at 100 m (Tip). Practise the same hold every time.
- Low light: an open post disappears early on a dark animal. Legal light still applies (99%, [hunting hours](#/s/rb-legal-methods/0)).
```

## Zero up and down: the front post

Point in a safe direction. Finger off the trigger. Unloaded and proved.

```diagram sks-front-elev
```

- Post screws down: the rifle hits higher (95%).
- Post screws up: it hits lower (95%).

```more elevation in the manual
- "Slip combination wrench over the front sight post. Turn post clockwise to raise the strike of the bullet; turn counterclockwise to lower the strike of bullet. Remove tool from sight post" (95%, TC 9-56 training packet).
- Rule for any front sight: move the front sight the opposite way to where you want the hit to go (85%, hunter education). Lower post, higher hits.
- Small turns. Shoot 3 more rounds after each change (Tip).
- The tool is in the butt trap ([chapter 5](#/s/mc-sks-05/4)).
```

## Zero left and right

```diagram sks-front-windage
```

- The front sight drum drifts sideways in its base (70%).
- Sight left, hits right. Needs a sight tool (70%).

```more windage
- TC 9-56 says the front sight adjusts for zero but does not describe the windage method (95%, my reading).
- On most SKS the front sight post sits in a drum that drifts sideways; owners use a dedicated SKS and AK front sight tool (70%, owner and armourer guides). Tip: hammering a punch can mar the drum. A gunsmith does it in minutes.
- Same rule: move the front sight opposite to the miss (85%).
- Paint pen line across the drum and base after zero, so you see if it moved (Tip).
```

## Zero plan for deer

```diagram sks-zero-plan
```

- Zero at 100 m with your hunting ammunition (Tip).
- Then check at 50 and 150 m. No deer shots past 150 m (Tip).

```more the plan in steps
1. Clean, dry bore. Gunsmith check done ([chapter 1](#/s/mc-sks-01)).
2. Rear sight on 1 (100 m) (95% for the setting).
3. Rest the rifle. 3 shots at 25 m to get on paper (Tip).
4. 3 shot groups at 100 m. Move the front post until the group centres on your aim (95% for the method, via [sighting in](#/s/sighting-in/0)).
5. Same ammunition you will hunt with. Different loads hit in different places (85%).
6. Shoot at 50 and 150 m and write down where it hits (Tip).
- Hornady publishes its 123 grain SST load as +3.5 in high at 100 yd with a 200 yd zero (75%, maker data via a retailer). A 100 m zero keeps it simpler for close cover deer (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Chase the group, not the shot.

> [!mistake]
> - Zeroing with cheap surplus and hunting with soft points.
> - Moving the front sight the same way as the miss.
> - Leaving the leaf on the battle mark and wondering why deer shots go high.

> [!field]
> Range day: 15 rounds of your deer load. 3 to get on paper, 9 to zero at 100 m, 3 at 150 m. Write it on a card in the butt trap.
