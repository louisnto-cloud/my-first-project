## The two sights

```diagram tk-iron-parts
```

- Rear: a drum peep sight on the 17 mm rail (95%).
- Front: a post on the muzzle thread, guarded by two ears (95%).

```more the parts in the manual's words
- Rear sight: "attached to the top rail of the receiver right behind the picatinny rail" with four securing set screws (A) and windage screws (B) (95%, Tikka manual page 11, figure 16).
- Front sight: "attached on the muzzle thread", with a guiding screw (A), an attachment screw (B) and an elevation screw (C) (95%, page 11, figure 17).
- "Do not open the screw on the center of the rear sight drum. This may cause the internal spring loaded parts to be thrown away thus making the sight defective" (95%, page 12).
- The drum has six apertures (peep holes) of different sizes, one per range step, and two protective ears behind it (75%, all4shooters test).
- "All Tikka rifles fitted with open sights are factory adjusted to zero at 100m" (95%, page 11). Still check it: you, your eyes and your ammunition are not the factory's (Tip).
```

## The sight picture

```diagram tk-sight-picture
```

- Look through the peep. Focus on the post (Tip).
- Top of the post on the spot you want to hit (Tip).

```more how to use a peep sight
- Tip (widely taught): your eye centres the post in the round peep by itself. Do not try to see the ring sharp; look through it.
- Tip: your eye can only focus on one thing. Make it the front post. The target can be a little soft.
- Tip: keep the same cheek spot on the stock every shot. A different cheek spot is a different zero.
- Tip: the smallest aperture is sharpest in bright light; a larger one is faster and brighter in dim light. Which drum step has which aperture size on your rifle: VERIFY by looking.
- Legal light only: the hunting day ends at legal shooting time, and iron sights fade before a scope does. See the [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods/0).
```

## Six range steps

```diagram tk-drum-steps
```

- 100 to 600 m in six steps (95%).
- Zero and hunt on the 100 m step (Tip).

```more what the steps really mean
- "The rear sight can be adjusted to six different ranges (from 100 m to 600 m). Desired range selection is done by rotating the drum" (95%, Tikka manual page 12).
- The manual does not say which bullet the steps were set for. Assume nothing: shoot each step you might use on paper before you trust it (Tip). VERIFY with your load.
- Assumption: the steps are marked 1 to 6 for 100 to 600 m. Check the markings on your drum.
- Deer with iron sights: a beginner should keep shots short. Your limit is the distance where you put 5 of 5 shots in a paper plate from a field position (Tip; see chapter 5 and [shooting skills](#/s/shooting-skills/0)).
- With a 100 m zero, a 150 gr .308 load is about 4 cm low at 150 m, 12 cm low at 200 m and 27 cm low at 250 m: see the drop table in [.308 ballistics](#/s/ballistics-308/0) (75%). Iron sight steps aside, that is why short shots win.
```

## Up and down: the front screw

```diagram tk-front-elev
```

- Clockwise: post down, shots up (95%).
- 1 click: about 7 mm at 100 m (95%).

```more the manual's elevation note
- "Turn the elevation screw (C) clockwise to move the bead downwards and the point of impact upwards, and vice versa. One click moves the point of impact about 7 mm in 100 m distance (0.26 in @ 100 yds)" (95%, Tikka manual page 11).
- Memory: move the front sight the opposite way you want the shots to go (Tip; matches the manual: post down, shots up).
- Count clicks out loud and write them in your log. If you get lost, you can count back (Tip).
```

## Left and right: the rear screws

```diagram tk-rear-wind
```

- Loosen one side, snug the other (95%).
- Move the rear sight the way you want the shots to go (85%).

```more the manual's windage note
- "First, loosen the windage screw (B) with a slotted driver from the side where the bead is desired to move. Tighten the screw on the other side to loosen the slack. Do not overtighten! One click moves the point of impact approx. 7 mm in 100 m distance" (95%, Tikka manual page 12).
- Direction: for Tikka standard open sights the manual says "To move the point of impact to the right, adjust sight blade to the right" (95%, page 11). The same rule for the Arctic drum is my reading (85%): rear sight right, shots right.
- Use a good fitting slotted screwdriver. A slipping blade chews the screw heads (95%, page 18 says use tools of the right size).
```

## Click math

```diagram tk-iron-clicks
```

- Clicks = centimetres off ÷ 0.7 at 100 m (95%).
- Measure from the group centre, not one shot (95%).

```more more examples
- 7 mm per click at 100 m (95%, Tikka manual). So:

| Group off at 100 m | Clicks |
|---|---|
| 2 cm | 3 |
| 3.5 cm | 5 |
| 7 cm | 10 |
| 10 cm | 14 |

- At 50 m each click moves half as much on paper, so you need twice the clicks (95%, arithmetic).
- A scope click is a different size: usually 1/4 MOA (minute of angle), about 0.73 cm at 100 m (70%). Chapter 4.
```

## Zero the irons, step by step

```diagram tk-iron-zero
```

- Bore sight close, confirm at 100 m (95%).
- 3 shot groups from a solid rest (95%).

```more the manual's sighting in steps
- The manual's generic method, for sights or scope (95%, Tikka manual page 12):
  1. Rifle solid on a bench rest or sandbags. Remove the bolt.
  2. Target at about 25 to 30 m. Move the rifle until you see the target through the bore. Keep the rifle still.
  3. Adjust windage and elevation so the sights point at the same spot you see through the bore.
  4. Final sighting in at 100 to 150 m, from a solid rest.
  5. Aim at the bull and fire 3 shots. Unload. Check the group with binoculars or a spotting scope.
  6. Adjust so the point of aim moves to the centre of the group. Repeat until the group is near the bull.
- Let the barrel cool between groups (Tip).
- Use the exact load you will hunt with (75%, see [ammunition](#/s/mc-tikka-06)).
- The full range day, minute by minute: [chapter 10](#/s/mc-tikka-10).
```

## Refitting the sights

```diagram tk-sight-fit
```

- Rear set screws: 0.8 Nm. Front: 5 Nm (95%).
- Moving a sight means zeroing again (Tip).

```more the manual's fitting steps
- Rear sight (95%, Tikka manual page 11): loosen the set screws (A) and windage screws (B) slightly; slide the sight on "from the back of the receiver to the 17 mm rail"; tighten the four set screws with a small slotted screwdriver to 0.8 Nm (7 in lb). Removal is the reverse.
- Front sight (95%, page 11): thread protector off; turn the sight clockwise to the end of the thread; back it off counterclockwise until the guiding screw (A) drops into the slot on the end of the barrel, leaning on the left wall (shooter's view); tighten the attachment screw (B) to 5 Nm (44 in lb).
- So taking the rear sight off is a user job in the manual. Chapter 4 uses that for a lower scope.
- The front sight sits on the muzzle thread. That thread is where a muzzle brake would go. A suppressor is a prohibited device in Canada (99%, Criminal Code s. 84(1)). See [chapter 9](#/s/mc-tikka-09).
- Paint pen line across each screw head after tightening: a broken line means it moved (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram tk-front-elev
```

- **"Front opposite, rear follows."** Front post down, shots up; rear right, shots right.
- **"Seven per click."** 7 mm at 100 m, both screws.
- **"Through the hole, eye on the pole."** Look through the peep, focus on the post.

```more more hooks, mistakes and the field task
- **"Point eight and five."** 0.8 Nm rear set screws, 5 Nm front attachment.
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Irons first, glass second." A shooter who can hit with irons shoots a scope better.

> [!mistake]
> - Opening the centre screw on the drum. Spring parts fly out.
> - Adjusting from one lucky shot instead of a 3 shot group.
> - Cranking the windage screws tight and stripping them.

> [!field]
> Dry, at home, rifle proved unloaded: 20 slow presses with a snap cap, focus on the front post, post steady on a dot on the wall. Chapter 5 has the full drill.
```
