## Notches: lead versus steel

```diagram sg2-notches
```

- Rim notches name the choke for lead (95%).
- With steel the same tube shoots one step tighter (95%).

```more what the makers say
- Browning Invector: one notch full, two improved modified, three modified, four improved cylinder, five skeet, none cylinder. "Rim notches refer specifically to lead shot" (95%, Citori manual).
- Browning chart with steel: improved modified and modified tubes pattern full; improved cylinder patterns modified; skeet patterns improved cylinder (95%).
- Browning: full "Not for use with steel shot. Using an over-tight choke constriction with steel shot will result in an ineffective 'blown' pattern" (95%).
- Beretta Mobilchoke: stars instead of names; full (one star) and improved modified (two stars) "Not recommended for steel shot" (95%, A300 manual).
- Beretta: with steel, "the best results are obtained by using open chokes" (95%).
- Stoeger M3000: "Use of Full or Improved Modified choke tubes is not recommended with steel shot" (95%).
- Remington: "NEVER fire the firearm without the choke tube installed" (95%, 870 manual).
```

## Change a choke

- Unloaded and open first. Wrench, anticlockwise to remove (95%).
- Clean and lightly oil the threads before it goes back (95%).

```more the steps
1. Point it safe. Unload. Action open, chamber and magazine empty (95%, Remington, Beretta, Browning).
2. Wrench in the tube, turn anticlockwise, then finger twist it out (95%, Browning).
3. Clean the threads in the barrel and on the tube. Lightly oil them (95%). Browning: dirty threads can make the tube seize (95%).
4. Screw the new tube in by hand, then snug it with the wrench. Remington: "DO NOT OVERTIGHTEN" (95%).
5. Check it is still tight now and then, gun unloaded (95%).
- Beretta: never look through the barrel from the muzzle, and never change a choke with the gun loaded, even on safe (95%).
```

## Pattern your gun, step by step

```steps Pattern a shotgun in 6 steps
sg2-pat-1 | Big sheet, aim mark, safe backstop.
sg2-pat-2 | 40 yd (37 m), then your real range.
sg2-pat-3 | Padded rest, head down. One shot per sheet.
sg2-pat-4 | Circle the densest 30 in (76 cm).
sg2-pat-5 | Count inside. Divide by pellets in the shell.
sg2-pat-6 | Five sheets per choke and shell. Average.
```

- Standard test: 30 in (76 cm) circle at 40 yd (37 m) (85%).
- Hunt test: same thing at your real range, 35 m ducks, 25 m grouse (Tip).

```more Trulock's method in full
1. Target about 4 ft (1.2 m) square. Butcher or flooring paper works (85%, Trulock).
2. Mark the centre. Shoot from a padded bench with your head and eye where they are when you shoot standing (85%).
3. At least 5 shots, each on its own sheet, for each shell and choke (85%).
4. Draw a 30 in (76 cm) circle around the densest part of each pattern. Count the pellets inside and divide by the pellets in the shell (85%).
5. Average all sheets (85%).
6. Look for holes a bird could slip through. "The goal is multiple hits" (85%).
- Cylinder and skeet are normally patterned at 25 yd, the rest at 40 yd (85%, Trulock).
- Pellets in the shell: shot weight times pellets per ounce, or cut one open and count (Tip). Steel No. 2: about 125 per ounce (70%, [Shotgun mastery](#/s/shotgun-mastery/6)).
- Ear and eye protection. A safe backstop. A range that allows shotguns (Tip). Practice ranges: [Hunt Map](#/map).
```

## Read the sheet

```diagram sg2-pat-read
```

- Off to one side: check gun fit and mount first (70%).
- Patchy with holes: try another choke or shell (70%).

```more reading in full
- Benchmarks at 40 yd, lead: cylinder 40%, improved cylinder 50%, modified 60%, full 70% (85%, Trulock, "a starting point only").
- Big ducks need about 100 pellets in the 76 cm circle at your longest range (70%, [Shotgun skills](#/s/shotgun-skills/5)).
- Steel moving faster that patterns poorly: Trulock suggests opening the choke by .005 in (85%).
- Once you find a choke and shell that print even at your range, write it down and stop changing things (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): The paper never lies and never flinches. Believe it over the box.

> [!mistake]
> - Choosing a choke by its name, never by a pattern.
> - Full choke with steel because "tighter reaches farther".
> - Patterning one sheet and calling it done.

> [!field]
> Before the opener: two chokes, two shells, five sheets each at 35 m. Keep the best sheet in your truck.
