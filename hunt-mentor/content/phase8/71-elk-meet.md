## Bull, cow, calf

```gallery

g-elk-cow-rear | From behind: the big cream rump patch.

```

- Tan body, dark mane, cream rump patch (90%).
- Bull: antlers. Cow: none. Both have ivories (90%).

```diagram mc-elk-id
```

```more ID by sex and age, the full list
- **Coat:** brownish body, heavy dark mane on the neck, a yellowish white rump patch bordered by a dark stripe (90%, BC elk account 2000).
- **Ivories:** large rounded upper canine teeth ("elk tusks") in both sexes (90%).
- **Bull** (male): antlers grow April to August in velvet, hard by September, drop late February to early April (90%). Bulls have a lighter, creamier body than cows (90%).
- **Mature bull** (4 years and older): rich brown antlers with ivory tips; beams 110 to 160 cm long sweep up and back over the shoulders; usually 5 tines plus the beam tip (90%).
- **Cow** (female): no antlers, slimmer neck, same colours (90%).
- **Calf** (under 12 months in the rules): one spotted calf born late May or early June; spots gone by fall (90%). The Rule Book defines a calf as "less than twelve months of age" (99%, [definitions](#/s/rb-definitions)).
- **In the law** a "bull" is an elk "bearing visible bony antlers", not a calf (99%, page 3).
- Elk are the only North American deer, other than caribou, that commonly form large groups (90%).
```

## Age a bull on the hoof

```diagram mc-elk-bull-ages
```

- Spike, then 3 or 4, then 4 or 5, then about 6 a side (90%).
- Many 6 point bulls are 4 or older (80%).

```gallery
g-elk-bull-rear | Count one antler. Then count it again.
g-elk-bulls-distance | At range, body size and neck tell age first.
```

```more antlers, body and the 6 point count
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- BC elk account (90%):
  - Yearling bulls usually carry unbranched spikes.
  - Two year olds: a slender rack, 3 or 4 points a side. Hunters call them "raghorns".
  - Three year olds: a heavier set with 4 or 5 points.
  - Mature bulls (4 plus): usually 5 tines plus the tip.
- US Park Service, Yellowstone (80%): yearlings grow 10 to 20 inch (25 to 50 cm) spikes; two year olds usually 4 to 5 points; 4 year olds and older typically 6 a side; the heaviest antlers often at 11 or 12 years.
- The two sources differ by a point at age 2. Point count is a guide, not an age certificate (Tip).
- Body clues for an older bull: deep chest, thick neck, mane blending into the shoulders, a heavy, slow walk (Tip, hunter consensus).
- Legal count: a [[6 point bull]] has at least 6 tines on ONE antler. The [[Brow tine]] counts. Each [[Tine]] must be at least 2.5 cm and longer than it is wide (99%, pages 3 and 4, [definitions](#/s/rb-definitions)).
```

## Look alikes: legal or not

```diagram sa-elk-lookalike
```

- Moose: dark, long nose, bell. Mule deer: a third the size (85%).
- Not sure? No shot (Tip).

```more the look alike table and the legal point
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

| Look alike | Tell it by | Legal point |
|---|---|---|
| Moose | Dark brown to black all over, long drooping nose, a bell under the throat, palm shaped antlers (85%) | Separate licence and seasons ([Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3)) |
| Mule deer | Much smaller, grey brown, big ears, black tipped tail (90%) | Separate licence; elk are 2 to 3 times the size (Tip) |
| Caribou | Cows can carry antlers too (99%, the Rule Book bull definition for caribou says male) | Not your areas |
| Elk cow vs young bull | A spike or raghorn can hide in a herd of cows (Tip) | Region 8 general season: 6 point bulls only (99%) |
| Domestic elk or cattle | BC did not permit elk ranching in 2000 (90%, BC account) | Fences, ear tags and farms: not wild game (Tip) |

- Region 3 (Heffley Creek) and Region 2 (Mission) have no general open season for elk (99%, pages 34 and 28). An elk there is a Journal note, not a target (Tip).
```

## Size and weight

```diagram mc-elk-size-bars
```

- Bull 265 to 410 kg; cow 190 to 270 kg (90%).
- About 3 to 4 times a mule deer buck (90%, computed).

```more size details and what it means for you
- BC numbers (90%, BC elk account 2000):
  - Bulls about 140 cm at the shoulder, 265 to 410 kg.
  - Cows about 130 cm, 190 to 270 kg.
  - Calf at birth about 13 kg.
- Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation (85%): bull about 315 kg, 1.5 m at the shoulder, 2.4 m nose to tail; cow about 225 kg; calf 16 kg. Roosevelt elk run heavier.
- Hinterland Who's Who (80%): some big bulls near 500 kg in late summer.
- Mature antlers alone can weigh up to about 18 kg (40 lb) (85%, RMEF).
- Mule deer buck for scale: 68 to 113 kg (90%, BC deer account).
- What it means for you: one bull is several loads on your back. Plan the pack out before the shot (chapter 10).
```

## Two races in BC



- Coast: Roosevelt elk. Interior: Rocky Mountain elk (90%).
- They look alike. Range tells them apart (90%).

```video
commons-roosevelt-elk | Roosevelt elk: the coastal race. Watch the herd hold together.
commons-elk-bull-yoho | A Rocky Mountain bull in Yoho, BC.
```

```more the two races, history and transplants
- Elk (wapiti) are the same species as European red deer, but North American races have been apart for at least 15,000 years (90%, BC elk account).
- **Roosevelt elk** (C. e. roosevelti): slightly larger and darker; antlers sometimes end in a crown of 3 or 4 points (90%). Vancouver Island, the Sunshine Coast and, since translocations, Lower Mainland valleys (80 to 90%). Blue listed (vulnerable) since 1998 (90%).
- **Rocky Mountain elk** (C. e. nelsoni): most numerous in the east and west Kootenay, north to about Golden, west to Grand Forks; also the east slope of the Rockies to the Liard River (90%). More migratory (90%).
- Elk once roamed the interior more widely; numbers fell sharply in the mid to late 1800s for unknown reasons (90%).
- Transplants since 1917, starting with 10 elk near Lillooet; over 600 released. New herds include Princeton, Lytton, Okanagan Lake, Granby River, Sechelt and Powell River (90%).
- Your bases: Heffley Creek and the Okanagan are Rocky Mountain elk country; Mission is Roosevelt elk country (85%, my reading of the ranges above).
```

## How many, and the trend

```diagram mc-elk-numbers
```

- BC: 35,000 to 71,500 elk, 2022 (90%).
- Your regions: Thompson 300 to 500; Lower Mainland 2,200 to 2,800; Okanagan 2,500 to 3,500 (90%).

```more numbers, trend and history
- 2022 provincial estimate table (90%, BC government, read directly):
  - Region 2 Lower Mainland: 2,200 to 2,800, increasing.
  - Region 3 Thompson: 300 to 500, increasing.
  - Region 8 Okanagan: 2,500 to 3,500, stable to increasing.
  - Biggest herds: Peace (7B) 15,000 to 35,000 and Kootenay 10,000 to 20,000.
- Elk are counted by aerial surveys per MU (Management Unit), aiming at each important MU once every 5 years (90%).
- History: interior Rocky Mountain elk rose from about 15,000 in the mid 1970s to about 40,000 by 2000; only about 1,350 in the Thompson Okanagan then (90%, BC account).
- Lower Mainland Roosevelt elk grew from about 315 in 2000 to about 1,600 in 2015 after translocations (85%, Habitat Conservation Trust Foundation).
- Main causes of death: severe winter malnutrition, then predators (wolves, cougars, bears), hunting, roads and railways (90%, BC account).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-elk-id
```

- **Rhyme:** "Dark neck, light back, cream seat, swept back rack."
- **Number trick:** 1, 3 or 4, 4 or 5, 6: spike, raghorn, three year old, mature (90%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Count one side, count it twice, then count the brow."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Moose is a shadow, elk is a lamp": dark all over versus a pale body that glows at dawn (Tip).
- **Number trick:** 140 and 130: shoulder heights in cm, bull and cow (90%).
- **Number trick:** 4 and 195: Region 8 success about 4% and about 195 hunter days per elk (90%, computed).
- **Number trick:** 2 races: Roosevelt on the coast, Rocky Mountain inside (90%).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely taught hunter education wisdom, opinion): "If you are not sure, you are not shooting."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Elk are big animals, not big targets."

> [!mistake]
> - Counting both antlers together for a 6 point bull. Count one side.
> - Treating an elk near Heffley Creek or Mission as huntable. No general season there.
> - Mistaking a moose cow at dusk for an elk. Look for the cream rump.

> [!field]
> Next time you watch an elk video, call out "bull or cow, age guess, points on one side" before the narrator does.
```
