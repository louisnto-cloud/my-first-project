## Alarm signals

```diagram fs-bc-warning
```

- Jaw popping, woofing, moaning, stomping, a bluff charge (75%).
- A stressed, defensive bear. Give it room (75%).

```more alarm signals in detail
- WildSafeBC lists the agitated bear signs: "jaw popping, moaning, woofing, stomping the ground, or a bluff charge" (75%). Speak calmly, back away, avoid direct eye contact, ready your spray.
- A woof or huff from a sow is often the signal that sends cubs up a tree (Tip, next screens).
- Ears laid back and head low go with stress (Tip, [bear safety](#/s/bear-cougar-encounters)).
- Standing up is curiosity, not attack (Tip, chapter 3).
```

## Flight, and the rare attack

```diagram fs-bc-attack-types
```

- Fatal black bear attacks in BC: under 1 every 3 years (75%).
- Dogs were part of over half of injuries in one review (70%).

```more what a bear does when disturbed
- Most black bears that notice a person leave, often fast and quietly (Tip, hunter consensus).
- A defensive bear (surprised, guarding cubs or food) comes noisy and fast; a predatory bear is quiet, stalks and keeps coming (75%, WildSafeBC, [bear safety](#/s/bear-cougar-encounters)).
- WildSafeBC: fatal black bear attacks average less than one every three years in BC (75%). Hristienko and Herrero (2014) found dogs involved in over 50% of black bear injuries to people (70%, via WildSafeBC).
- A gut pile or a kill draws bears, which may then defend it (75%, [bear safety](#/s/bear-cougar-encounters)).
```

## Sows and cubs

```anim mc-black-bear-cubs-up Sow woofs, cubs climb.
```

- Sow with cubs is never legal (99%).
- Cubs often climb when alarmed; she stays near (Tip).

```photo g-bb-sow-cubs
```

```more family life in detail
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Black bears are "mostly solitary. The only common social groups are the sow with her cubs and the mating pair" (90%, BC account).
- Cubs stay with the mother their whole first year, den with her, and leave by the middle or end of the second spring when she drives them off to breed again (90%).
- She "protects them and teaches them how to survive" (90%). Daughters may set up ranges that overlap hers; sons disperse (90%).
- Females with cubs avoid open areas when moving between feeding sites (90%, BC account). So a sow may feed at the edge while cubs wait in cover (Tip).
- Item 14, page 11: no bear under 2 and no bear in its company (99%, [unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts)).
```

## Boars

```diagram mc-black-bear-home-range
```

- Boars roam 25 to 150 km2; sows 5 to 25 (90%).
- Big boars rub trees and push smaller bears off food (65%).

```more boars in detail
- Home range "of adult males, typically 25 to 150 km2, are larger than those of females, which vary from 5 to 25 km2" (90%, BC account).
- Mating season, early June to mid July: boars travel widely looking for sows (90% timing; Tip on travel).
- Boone and Crockett (Jim Shockey): boar behaviours include rubbing on trees, straddling and breaking saplings, chasing other bears and dominating smaller bears; big bears hold the best habitat at prime feeding times; small bears stay near escape trees (65%).
- Adult males sometimes kill cubs (sexually selected infanticide); sows with young cubs avoid males in mating season. In an Alberta study of hunted black bears the effect on numbers was weak (65%).
- An old boar is the bear most likely to feed only at last light or at night where people are (Tip).
```

## Learning and memory

```anim mc-black-bear-scent-cone The lesson a bear learns fastest: your smell.
```

- Big brain for its size; excellent long term memory (70%).
- Learns where food and danger are, and when (Tip).

```more learning in detail
- North American Bear Center: bears have a large brain for their body size and excellent long term memory; navigation better than ours (70%).
- Bears that learn garbage, fruit trees or coolers return to them; conflict bears are often destroyed (90%, BC account).
- Near people, bears shift feeding to night (75%, WildSafeBC; 65%, a Massachusetts GPS (Global Positioning System) collar study).
- Pressure: a bear that is bumped at a berry patch may switch to dark only use of it (Tip). Disturb a feeding area once, then leave it alone (Tip).
```

## Predictable habits you can use

| Habit | Use it |
|---|---|
| Follows the richest food in season (90%) | Hunt the food, not the bear |
| Steps in the same prints on old trails (90%) | Find worn trails near food |
| Uses cover between feeding sites (90%) | Watch the edge where cover meets food |
| Comes back to a good food source (Tip) | Glass it again at the same hour |
| Feeding bear is noisy, focused (Tip) | Move while it feeds |

```more the sources for the habits
- BC account (90%): bears "seek out concentrated food sources such as spawning fish and dense berry patches"; "home ranges are usually made up of several feeding areas connected by travel routes... often located in wooded areas that provide cover"; bears "often step in the same footprints of other bears, thus creating well worn paths that they use year after year".
- The rest are hunting tips, not measured facts.
```

## Bear safety and bear spray

```anim gr-bear-spray Aim low, sweep up.
```

- Spray when the bear is about 5 to 10 m away (75%).
- Spray stopped black bears about 90% of the time in Alaska (80%).

```diagram fs-bc-spray-ranges
```

```more spray and safety in detail
- WildSafeBC: carry spray where you can reach it fast, use it at about 5 to 10 m (75%).
- Smith, Herrero and others (2008), Alaska incidents 1985 to 2006: spray stopped undesirable behaviour in 92% of brown bear, 90% of black bear and 100% of polar bear cases; 98% of people with spray were uninjured in close encounters; wind interfered in 5 of 71 cases (80%, journal abstract via search preview). Only 20 black bear cases: a small sample (Tip).
- Spray versus firearms: see [bear safety](#/s/bear-cougar-encounters) (70%).
- Defensive contact: lie on your stomach, hands over the back of your neck, legs apart. Predatory attack: fight back at the face (75%, WildSafeBC).
- Hunters are at higher risk than hikers: quiet, early, late, in thick cover, with a carcass (75%, [bear safety](#/s/bear-cougar-encounters)).
- Bear bells: WildSafeBC does not recommend them (75%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram fs-bc-warning
```

- **Rhyme:** "Pop and woof, give it room; quiet and coming, spray it soon."
- **Number trick:** 5 to 10: metres to spray (75%). 90: percent of black bear cases stopped (80%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, the quick lesson, built on the synopsis "Be patient"): "Watch a bear five minutes. Cubs show up in the fourth."

```more more hooks
- **Number trick:** 25 to 150 and 5 to 25: boar and sow home ranges in km2 (90%).
- **Rhyme:** "Cubs up a tree, sow close by: keep your safety on, walk on by."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, the synopsis notice): "Be patient. Don't shoot the moment you see a bear."

> [!mistake]
> - Thinking a woofing, jaw popping bear is about to attack. It is warning you.
> - Carrying spray in the pack.
> - Bringing a loose dog into bear country.

> [!field]
> Practise with an inert spray can: draw, safety clip off, fire low, 10 times.
```
