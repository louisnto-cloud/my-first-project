> [!why]
> Elk give you two short windows a day. Be in the right seat when they open, and do not burn the day walking through their beds.

```diagram mc-elk-day-clock
```

- Two peaks: after dawn and at dusk (80%).
- Midday: bedded near cover (80%).

```more what this chapter covers
- Bed, feed, water; the midday bed; weather (wind, rain, snow, cold fronts, barometer); the moon; hunting pressure; and how strong the evidence is for each.
- An Alberta GPS (Global Positioning System) collar study: elk were crepuscular, with activity peaks after sunrise and at the end of evening twilight; the morning peak came almost 2 hours after first light; Canadian elk were mostly active by day; more active in summer; weakest pattern in winter (80%, Ensing and others 2014).
- The authors put the daily pattern down mostly to human disturbance (80%).
- Times on the clock are a sketch for late September (Tip).
```

## Bed, feed, water

```diagram mc-elk-day-route
```

- Dusk: out to feed. Morning: back to timber (80%).
- Most elk stay within 183 m of cover (80%).

```diagram sa-elk-day
```

```more the daily loop in detail
- Elk feed in openings next to cover: meadows, burns, cutblocks, avalanche tracks (80%, BC habitat models).
- Most elk occur within 183 m (200 yd) of cover (80%, BC habitat model citing Black 1979).
- Where people disturb them, elk fed within 100 to 200 m of cover by day and went farther into the open at night (80%, Morgantini 1979, cited in a BC habitat model).
- Summer: elk bed wherever they finish feeding, but close to cover (80%).
- Water: drink at springs, creeks and ponds; bulls wallow in the rut (70%, RMEF (Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation); Tip for drinking times).
- Mineral licks: elk visit licks for minerals (85%, RMEF). A lick is a natural sit spot (Tip). Baiting rules: [unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts).
```

## Midday: the bedded elk

```diagram mc-elk-bed-site
```

- Summer beds: north slopes, dense shade (75%).
- Slopes of 15 to 30% used most (80%).

```more beds in detail
- Black Hills study: summer day beds were on north aspects, cooler than random spots, with over 54% canopy closure (75%, Millspaugh and others 1998).
- Elk use slopes up to about 30 to 40%, most often 15 to 30%; use drops sharply past 40 to 50% (80%, BC habitat model).
- Cover that hides 90% of a standing elk at 61 m is "security cover" (80%).
- Bedded herds have sentries awake (Tip). Walking into beds at midday usually sends the herd to the next drainage (Tip).
- Better: glass the edges, wait for the evening move, or still hunt slowly on a perfect wind (Tip, chapter 9).
```

## Wind, rain, snow, cold

```diagram mc-elk-evidence
```

- Strong evidence: dawn and dusk, people, deep snow (80 to 95%).
- Weak: barometer and moon (no elk study found).

```more weather in detail
- **Wind:** wind does not stop elk, but swirling wind makes them hard to approach (75%, Colorado Parks and Wildlife). Steady wind is your friend (Tip).
- **Rain:** light rain dampens your noise and scent; elk often feed longer on grey days (Tip, hunter consensus, no study found).
- **Snow:** deep snow pushes elk down and changes what they eat (80%, chapter 5). In Wyoming, collared elk gathered in heavy snow (70%, Wyoming Game and Fish news).
- **Heat:** elk pick shade and north slopes on hot days and move more at night (75%).
- **Cold fronts and barometer:** hunters often report more elk movement before a storm; no elk study tested it (Tip).
- **Summer to winter:** elk are most active in summer and least patterned in winter (80%).
```

## The moon and the evidence ladder

```diagram mc-elk-evidence
```

- No elk study links the moon to daytime activity (VERIFY).
- Deer studies found no moon effect (70%).

```more the evidence ladder
| Claim | Evidence | % |
|---|---|---|
| Peaks after dawn and at dusk | GPS (Global Positioning System) collars, Alberta | 80 |
| People and vehicles move elk away | Field experiments, Oregon | 85 |
| Hunting pushes elk into refuges | Collared elk, Montana | 80 |
| Deep snow pushes elk down | Field studies via BC models | 80 |
| Heat pushes elk to shade | Habitat selection, mixed effects | 75 |
| Storms and fronts get elk moving | Hunter reports only | Tip |
| Full moon means less daytime movement | Hunter claim; deer studies found no moon effect | Tip |

- Deer evidence: an Oklahoma GPS study found the moon phase had no effect on deer movements; weather effects were inconsistent (70%, search summary). Elk specific: VERIFY.
```

## Hunting pressure and the day

```anim hf-pressure-shift Pressure pushes animals into thick cover.
```

- Pressure shifts elk to night and to refuges (80 to 90%).
- After disturbance, elk moved more at sunrise and sunset (85%).

```more pressure in detail
- Hunted elk become wary, secretive and nocturnal (90%, BC account).
- In the Starkey study, on days with ATV (all terrain vehicle), bike, horse or hiking activity, elk moved more around sunrise and sunset even when no people were out, as if pushed from their best spots (85%).
- Refuge use doubled during a Montana hunt (80%).
- So: on pressured weekends, hunt far from roads and trails, or the edge of a refuge at first and last light (Tip). Respect private land; ask first ([landowner permission](#/s/landowner-permission)).
```

## How the day shifts through the fall

| When | Day pattern | % |
|---|---|---|
| Early Sept | Rut starts; bugles at dawn and dusk; wallows midday | 70 to 90 |
| Mid Sept | Peak rut; bulls active all day, vocal at night | 70 |
| Early Oct | Second heat; quieter; feed and bed pattern returns | 90 for heat, Tip for pattern |
| Late Oct | Pressure, snow; timber by day, open at night | 80 to 90 |

- Rut bulls are most vocal at dawn, dusk and through the night (70%, quick lesson).

```more notes
- Bulls at wallows: may be any hour (70%, RMEF (Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation)).
- Legal hunting hours: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (99%, page 11, [legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)).
- The morning peak can come almost 2 hours after first light (80%). Do not leave your seat at sunrise (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-elk-day-clock
```

- **Rhyme:** "Out at dusk, in by nine, beds in timber, shade and pine."
- **Number trick:** 183: metres most elk stay from cover (80%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Be on the glass before the light, and stay after the sun is up."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Strong to weak: light, people, snow; moon and pressure gauge, nobody knows."
- **Number trick:** 2 hours: the morning peak can lag first light by almost 2 hours (80%).
- **Number trick:** 15 to 30: the slope percent elk use most (80%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Never walk through the beds at noon."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "The worse the weather, the better the elk hunting." Tip only, no study.

> [!mistake]
> - Leaving the seat at sunrise because nothing showed.
> - Bumping the bedding timber at midday.
> - Planning the hunt around the moon instead of the wind.

> [!field]
> Next trip, log the time of every elk you see or hear. After a season you will have your own day clock.
```
