> [!why]
> Ducks leave little sign on the land. Their best sign is in the air: where they fly at first and last light.

```diagram mdk-shore-sign
```

- Five pond clues: droppings, feathers, muddy water, lanes, tracks (Tip).
- Many clues, fresh: ducks use it now (Tip).

```more reading a pond
- Droppings on logs, rocks and beaver dams where ducks loaf (Tip).
- Feathers along the edge, most in late summer and early fall when birds moult (Tip).
- Muddy, stirred water in the shallows where puddle ducks tipped up (Tip).
- Open lanes swum through duckweed or floating leaves (Tip).
- Webbed tracks in soft mud at the edge (80%).
- The best proof is still your eyes: ducks on the water at first or last light (Tip).
```

## Tracks

```diagram mdk-track
```

```photo hab-duck-tracks Webbed tracks in mud: 3 forward toes joined by skin.
```

- Three toes forward, joined by web, a tiny hind toe (80%).
- A mallard track is about 6 cm long (60%).

```more tracks in detail
- Duck feet: three forward toes joined by webbing, and a short hind toe that often barely prints (80%).
- A small sample of fresh mallard tracks in silt averaged about 62 mm long and 66 mm wide (60%, one source, 4 tracks).
- Goose and gull tracks look like duck tracks; geese are much bigger (75%).
- Coots have long toes with lobes, not a full web (80%).
- Lots of overlapping tracks at one spot of shore: a loafing or feeding spot (Tip).
- More on reading tracks: [Tracks](#/s/tracks).
```

## Droppings and feeding sign

| Sign | What it means (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Fresh droppings on a log | Birds loafed here today or yesterday |
| Muddy water in the shallows | Puddle ducks fed here recently |
| Lanes through duckweed | Birds swam here; fresh lanes close fast |
| Grain spilled by a field pond | Mallards may feed here at dawn |

- Fresh is wet and shiny; old is dry and white (Tip).

```more how to age sign (opinion)
- Droppings: wet and dark green or brown is fresh; dry, crusted and white is old (Tip).
- Muddy water settles within hours on a calm pond, so muddy water means birds were there very recently (Tip).
- Duckweed lanes close up within a day (Tip).
- These are field judgements, not measured rates. Confirm with a dawn or dusk watch (Tip).
```

## Feathers

```diagram mdk-feathers
```

- A coloured wing feather names the duck (80%).
- Down and body feathers in late summer: a moult spot (Tip).

```more feathers in detail
- Wing feathers with the speculum colour tell the species (80%). Keep one for your ID kit.
- Ducks drop all flight feathers at once in summer, so in July and August shore spots can be littered with feathers (85% for the moult, Tip for the litter).
- A pile of plucked feathers and a few bones: an eagle, hawk or mink kill (Tip).
- Feathers on the water at dawn after a calm night: birds roosted there (Tip).
```

## Calls: the sign you hear

```video
commons-mallards | Mallards up close: hear the hen and the drake.
yt-duck-call-quack | The basic quack, slowly.
```

- Loud quack: mallard hen. Soft raspy call: drake (75%).
- Whistles: wigeon, pintail, teal drakes (70%).

```more who says what
| Sound | Who (70 to 80%) |
|---|---|
| Loud, repeated quack | Mallard hen (the classic duck sound) |
| Soft, raspy "rab rab" | Mallard drake |
| Three note whistle | Wigeon drake |
| High "peep" or trill | Green winged teal drake |
| Soft whistle | Pintail drake |
| Whistling wings, no voice needed | Goldeneyes |

- Listen at dawn from the truck or a high point before you walk in (Tip).
- Mallard voices sit at 1 to 3 kHz, the band ducks hear best (75%).
```

## The flight line: the best sign of all

```anim mdk-flightline Scout: watch where they go at last light.
```

- Watch from far away; never walk in (Tip).
- Mark where they land. Be there at dawn (Tip).

```more how an old hand scouts (opinion, widely taught)
- Sit far off with binoculars at last light. Watch which way the birds leave the roost and where they drop (Tip).
- Field hunting depends on finding the field they used yesterday (99% that permission is needed on private fields; the rest is Tip).
- Watch two evenings if you can. Ducks often use the same field while the food lasts (Tip).
- Do not drive or walk into the field to look closer. You will push them (Tip).
- Mark it on the Hunt Map with a waypoint and the time (Tip).
```

## How fresh is it?

| Clue | Fresh | Old (Tip) |
|---|---|---|
| Ducks seen | Last light yesterday | Last week |
| Droppings | Wet, shiny | Dry, white, crusted |
| Feathers | Clean, dry, fluffy | Wet, matted, dirty |
| Water | Muddy, stirred | Clear, settled |

- Sign tells you "maybe". Birds in the air tell you "yes" (Tip).

```more why sign is weak for ducks
- Ducks spend most of their time on water, where tracks do not last (Tip).
- Pressure moves them fast: a pond full on Friday can be empty on Saturday (80% that pressure moves them, Tip for the timing).
- Old hand's rule (opinion): "Scout with your eyes, not your boots."
```

## Find a downed duck

```diagram hf-fetch-plan
```

- Mark every fall against a tree or rock (Tip).
- Cripples swim for the shore and cover. Check the bank (65%).

```more after the fall
- Wounded ducks often dive and stay under longer with each dive (65%).
- Most cripples head for shore; check the edge cover (65%).
- If a duck's head is still up, shoot again before you go after it (65%, dog trainer advice).
- Mark all birds. If you cannot mark them all, shoot less (65%).
- Retrieval is the law: you must have a way to retrieve, and must make the effort (99%). [Rule Book](#/s/rb-migratory-birds/21).
- More in [Masterclass 10](#/s/mc-ducks-10).
```

> Old hand's rule (opinion, widely taught): "Hunt where they WANT to be tomorrow, not where they were last week."
