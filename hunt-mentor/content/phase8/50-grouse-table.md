> [!why]
> A clean shot, a fast follow up and cold meat make grouse one of the best meals in the bush. The rules on wings and breasts travel with you all the way home.

```diagram mgr-shot-zones
```

- .22: head or upper neck. Shotgun: pattern on the head and front (Tip, 70%).
- All the vitals are in the front half (80%).

```more shot placement and range
- Brain, spine, heart and lungs sit in the head, neck and front of the body (80%). See [The body](#/s/mc-grouse-02).
- .22 head shots: inside about 25 m (27 yd) for most shooters, with a rest and a dirt backstop (Tip, 55 to 60%).
- Shotgun: most grouse shots are inside about 25 m; improved cylinder and No. 7.5 or 6 lead (Tip, 60%).
- Big game vitals diagrams do not apply to birds; see [Shot placement](#/s/shot-placement) for deer.
```

## After the shot

```diagram mgr-mark-fall
```

- Keep your eyes on the spot, pick a mark, walk straight there (Tip).
- A head shot bird may flap hard on the ground; that is a reflex (Tip, 60%).

```more reactions and recovery
- A grouse hit hard falls straight; a wing hit glides or drops and runs (Tip).
- A runner hides under logs, brush and roots. Search slowly in growing circles from the mark (Tip).
- A dog finds wounded birds you will not (Tip).
- Feathers floating but the bird flew on: watch it out of sight; hit birds often land and stay down (Tip).
- Reload, then go. Do not stop to celebrate (Tip).
- Dispatch a live bird quickly and humanely; ask an experienced hunter to show you (Tip).
```

## Field care in 4 steps

```steps Grouse field care
mgr-care-1 | Note the species. Leave one feathered wing on.
mgr-care-2 | Pull the crop and the guts soon.
mgr-care-3 | Spread birds out in the shade. No warm piles.
mgr-care-4 | Into a cooler on ice, under 4 C.
```

- Cool fast; heat spoils birds (80%).
- One feathered wing stays attached (99%).

```more the care details
- Remove the entrails and the crop soon after the kill; food in the crop can sour (80%, Clemson Cooperative Extension).
- Cool quickly; above 4 C (40 F) is the main threat to meat quality (80%).
- Wipe the cavity with a clean cloth or paper towel, not grass or snow (80%).
- Air around each bird; in hot weather bag each one and keep it on ice; no warm birds piled together (80%).
- Transport in a cooler; if none, a ventilated car interior, not the trunk (80%).
- Note species, MU (Management Unit), date and time in the Journal: Region 3 bag limits are per species (99%).
```

## The wing and the breasts: the law

```diagram rb-evidence-birds
```

- One feathered wing stays attached until home (99%).
- Both breasts are edible portions: take them home (99%).

```more the exact words
> [!law] Possession and Transportation, Game Birds, page 15
> "Anyone who possesses or transports a game bird, other than a migratory game bird, must leave attached to the carcass one feathered wing and for turkey, also the beard, if harvested during a bearded turkey only season."

> [!law] Definitions, page 3
> "EDIBLE PORTIONS - ... with respect to game birds, means the edible portions of both breasts of the bird."

- Breasting in the field: keep the wing attached to the breast you carry (85%, my reading in the [Rule Book](#/s/rb-after-the-kill/3)).
- Item 44: you must take the edible portions to your home, a meat cutter or a cold storage plant (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts/18)).
- Possession while hunting or going home: 3 times the daily limit; sharp tailed grouse in Region 3: 2 times (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-bag-limits/5)).
```

## Meat and cooking

```diagram sb-bird-yield
```

- A ruffed grouse gives about 200 to 280 g of meat (Tip, 60%).
- Cook to 74 C (165 F) in the thickest part (80%).

```more cooking notes
- Fridge at 4 C or colder, use within 3 days; freeze at -18 C (0 F), use within 6 months (80%, Clemson).
- Food safety guidance for game birds: 74 C (165 F), checked with a thermometer, since colour is not reliable (80%, extension services citing USDA, the United States Department of Agriculture).
- Grouse breast is lean white meat that dries out fast; many cooks brine it, wrap it, or cook it in a sauce so it stays moist at a safe temperature (Tip).
- Spruce grouse on a needle diet in late fall can taste of pine; brine or braise (Tip).
- Pluck for roasting, skin for speed; the legs are small but worth it (Tip).
- Pick out shot pellets; a pellet hole that looks dark can carry feather bits (Tip).
- More: [Cooking wild game](#/s/cooking).
```
