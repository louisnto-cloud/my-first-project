> [!why]
> A covey keeps a tight daily routine: roost, call, feed, grit, dust, rest, feed, call, roost. Be where they will be, at the hour they will be there.

```diagram mql-day-clock
```

- Two feeds: just after dawn and late afternoon (70%).
- Midday: loaf in cover, dust bathe, drink (75%).

```more the day in full
- "During the non breeding season, quail feed twice a day, in the morning just after dawn and again in late afternoon." (70%, Edgewood notes from Calkins et al.)
- Birds leave the night roost, generally off the ground in trees, between first light and sunrise; assembly calls start the move (70%).
- They pick grit at road and trail edges before the morning and afternoon feeds (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife).
- Midday: rest in moderate to heavy cover, dust bathe, or take water in warm, dry weather (75%).
- Dusk: a chorus of calls as they gather to roost (75%).
- Legal hunting hours: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (99%, Rule Book).
```

## Roost to feed

```steps From roost to breakfast

mql-follow-2 | They walk out to lane edges for grit and seeds.
hf-quail-cover | They feed along brush that sits beside food.
mql-dust-bowl | By midday they dust and rest near cover.
```

- Be in place, quiet, at first light (Tip).
- Listen before you walk (Tip).

```more mornings in full
- Roosts: dense trees or shrubs, not on the ground (70%, Birder's Handbook).
- The morning feed starts just after dawn (70%). Grit before feeding (75%).
- Feeding stays close to cover: seldom more than 15 to 30 m out (80%).
- Tip: on a frosty morning, birds may wait for sun on the brush before moving. A late start can pay.
- Note the legal start: 1 hour before sunrise (99%). It is often too dark to name a quail then. No shot until you can see the plume (Tip).
```

## Midday: dust, water, shade

```diagram mql-heat
```

- Hot, dry day: they need water daily (80%).
- Midday they rest in thick cover and are hard to flush (60%).

```more midday in full
- In hot weather quail need free water every day; in cool weather green plants, insects and dew can be enough (80%, California habitat records).
- They gather near water in dry weather and spread out when green plants and water are plentiful (80%).
- "During the middle of the day, especially when it's hot, they tend to hold tight in thicker cover." (60%, Field and Stream, search preview)
- Chukar: feed through the morning, then move to shady slopes and draws, dusting sites and water holes at midday (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife). They spend the hottest part of the day in shade (80%, FEIS).
- Tip: a midday break for you too. Clean birds, rest the dog, then hunt the last 2 hours.
```

## Weather: what changes the day

```diagram mql-evidence-bars
```

- Storms: they feed in the breaks (70%).
- Cold fronts, pressure and moon: no good data, Tip only.

```more weather in full, with evidence
- Rain and storms: "During storms they feed sporadically throughout the day during breaks in the storm." (70%, Edgewood notes)
- Heat and drought: water every day; birds gather near water (80%).
- Wind: Huns take shelter behind tree lines, fence rows, boulders, buildings, in narrow draws and on lee hillsides (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife). For quail, wind makes birds hard to hear and flighty: Tip.
- Snow: chukar gather where snow is thin; south slopes after cold nights (75%). Quail move to sunny slopes and bare ground (Tip, 60%).
- Cold fronts and barometric pressure: many hunters say birds feed hard before a front. No quail study found: Tip, VERIFY.
- Moon: no quail data found. Quail are day birds and roost at night (70%), so moon effects are unlikely to matter much (Tip).
- Cooper's hawk about: quail may skip the second feeding and stay close to cover (70%, Edgewood notes).
```

## Hunting pressure and the day

```diagram mql-pressure
```

- Pressured birds run more and flush wilder (60 to 75%).
- Pushed birds near trees may perch up out of reach (75%).

```more pressure in full
- Huns flush farther each time they are pushed (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife).
- Quail flushed near trees may fly up and perch. Washington Fish and Wildlife suggests walking away and coming back 30 to 60 minutes later, when they may be back on the ground (75%).
- Do not shoot sitting birds in trees: it is not sporting and a low shot into branches is a safety risk (Tip).
- On busy weekends, hunt a weekday (Tip, from the trip plan).
```

## Chukar and Hun days

| Hour | Chukar (75 to 80%) | Gray partridge (75%) |
|---|---|---|
| Dawn | South slopes after cold nights | Edges of grain fields |
| Morning | Feed, often toward water | Feed in weedy patches |
| Midday | Shade, dust, water | Rest in hay, tall grass, fence lines |
| Late day | Climb back to steep roosts | Feed again (Tip) |

- Chukar: be above them late in the day (Tip).

```more chukar and Hun days in full
- Chukar forage in early morning and late afternoon, usually on the way to water (80%, FEIS). Roost on the ground under sage, juniper or rock outcrops, often in tail to tail coveys (80%).
- "They'll usually begin moving back toward steeper roosting areas late in the afternoon." (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife)
- Huns feed around grain field edges and weedy patches; they roost, hide and rest in hay fields, tall grass, brush and fence lines (75%).
- Hun afternoon pattern: Tip, no source.
```

## Best hours, my lean

```diagram hf-quail-day
```

> [!lean]
> First 2 hours after legal light you can see in, and the last 2 hours before sunset. Calm, cool days. Midday: rest and clean birds.

- This matches the bird guides' two feeds (70%).

```more why this lean
- Two feeds a day, morning and late afternoon (70 to 75%).
- Morning and late afternoon calling helps you find coveys (75%).
- The quick lesson already says: "Best time, my opinion: the first 2 hours after legal light and the last 2 hours before sunset, on calm days." (Tip)
- Give coveys time to regroup before dark: stop a little early (Tip, Tall Timbers manners rule, 60%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mql-day-clock
```

- **Rhyme:** "Call at dawn, grit and feed; dust at noon, then feed at need." (Tip)
- **Number trick:** "2 feeds, 2 hours, 2 ends": feed twice a day (70%); hunt 2 hours at each end of the day (Tip).

```more more hooks
- **Weather hook:** "Hot means water, wind means lee, storm means breaks." (70 to 80%)
- **Old hand's rule (opinion):** "Walk away from a covey in the trees. Come back in an hour." From Washington Fish and Wildlife advice (75%).
```
