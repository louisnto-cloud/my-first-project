## Three needs

```gallery
hab-moose-willows | Willow: the main winter food.
hab-beaver-lodge | Beaver pond: water, willow, water plants.
hab-douglas-fir-stand | Conifer: shade and shelter.
```

- Food: dense browse, mostly willow (90%).
- Cover: to hide, for shade and for blizzards (90%).

```more the needs in detail
- BC account (90%): "In British Columbia, as elsewhere, Moose depend primarily on willows for winter food."
- "Moose need nearby cover to hide from predators and hunters, for shade in hot weather, and for shelter during blizzards." Removing too much forest cover can hurt moose even where browse is plentiful (90%).
- Water: lake shores, swamps and beaver ponds pull moose in summer, especially on plateaus (90%).
- Edges: willow flats and cutblocks against conifer (75%, [edges](#/s/edges)).
- Snow: winter ranges are where browse sits in snow moose can handle (90%, chapter 2).
```

## Wetlands and beaver ponds

```gallery
rl-beaver-pond-manning | Beaver pond: flooded meadow and willow.
hab-cattail-marsh | Marsh edge: moose feed along it.
```

- Lake shores, swamps, beaver ponds: summer magnets (90%).
- Fens and swamps grow willow and dogwood (95%).

```more wetlands in detail
- BC wetland classes (95%, [wetland country](#/s/wetland-country)): shallow open water (pondweeds; moose in summer), marsh (cattail, sedges, horsetail), fen (sedges, willow, scrub birch, red osier dogwood; moose browse), swamp (trees or tall shrubs; Interior spruce or willow).
- Beaver ponds flood timber and grow willow, a moose favourite (85%).
- On plateaus in winter moose concentrate in "burns, logged areas, and wetland complexes" (90%, BC account).
- Safety: soft muskeg, beaver dams and lake edges are hard places to recover a moose. Chapter 9 covers water.
- The Hunt Map has a Wetlands layer (99%, [Hunt Map pro](#/s/hunt-map-pro)).
```

## Burns

```diagram rp-burn-who
```

- Moose use burns roughly 3 to 15 years after fire (60 to 75%).
- Best in low and medium severity, wet patches (75%).

```photo hab-north-thompson-burn
```

```more burns in detail
- BC account (90%): moose habitat projects used controlled burns "to produce early successional stages" and more browse.
- Mount McAllister burn, north central BC: moose used low and medium severity patches, especially wet sites (75%, McNay and others 2021, [burn country](#/s/burn-country)).
- Not every burn means moose: boreal moose made low use of burns up to 40 years old in one study (65%).
- Local: the Bonaparte moose study area (MUs 3-29 and 3-30) had wildfires in 2017 and 2021, including the Sparks Lake fire (90%, BC moose research report 2022).
- The Hunt Map has a Burns by year layer: grey before 2010, orange 2010 to 2016, red orange 2017 and later (99%, app).
```

## Cutblocks

```diagram rp-moose-cut-age
```

- One BC study: blocks 10 to 30 years old selected (65%).
- Fresh blocks under 10 years avoided (65%).

```anim rp-cut-greenup A block greens up: shrubs, then tall shrubs and moose.
```

```more cutblocks in detail
- BC account (90%): "Logging may temporarily benefit Moose when willows and other shrubs become abundant in cutblocks, but removing too much forest cover can be detrimental."
- One BC thesis, 46 GPS collared moose, 2004 to 2019: cutblocks 0 to 9 years old avoided, 10 to 30 selected, over 30 avoided (65%, [cutblock country](#/s/cutblock-country); study area not confirmed).
- BC stages: tall shrub (2 to 10 m) is usually under 40 years after cutting (90%). That is moose browse height.
- The flip side: new roads into fresh cuts made moose easier to kill (90%, chapter 4).
- The Hunt Map has a Cutblocks by age layer (99%).
```

## Elevation by month

```diagram mc-moose-elevation
```

- Summer high; winter in valleys; stays high late in fall (90%).
- Some moose stay low all year (90%).

```more movement in detail
- BC account (90%): in mountains moose "usually migrate between winter spring ranges in valley bottoms and higher elevation summer ranges, but some Moose remain in the valleys year round".
- They stay high in autumn longer than elk, deer or mountain sheep, and drift down gradually; with light snow they stay mid elevation until late winter (90%).
- Plateaus: summer spread out near lakes and swamps; winter concentrated along river valleys, burns, cutblocks and wetlands (90%).
- Seasonal home range seldom exceeds 5 to 10 km²; migratory moose have larger yearly ranges (90%).
- Alaska: seasonal moves of a few miles up to about 60 miles (about 100 km) (85%).
- BEC (Biogeoclimatic Ecosystem Classification) zones: September and early October moose are often in the MS (Montane spruce) and ESSF (Engelmann spruce and subalpine fir) zones (Tip, [BEC zones](#/s/bec-zones)).
```

## Heffley Creek, Region 3

```diagram mc-moose-three-areas
```

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Moose live on the forested plateaus, not the dry valley (85%).
- MUs (Management Units) 3-27 and 3-28: spike fork bulls, 1 to 15 November (99%).

```more Heffley Creek in detail
- BC account (90%): moose are common in mountain valleys "except for a few dry southern valleys like the Thompson and Okanagan". Near Kamloops, look up on the forested plateaus with lakes, swamps, burns and cutblocks (85%, my reading).
- MU 3-27 covers the Heffley Creek east bank, Sun Peaks, Heffley Lake, Paul Lake, Pinantan and Louis Creek; MU 3-28 the west bank from Westsyde to Barriere, Skull Mountain and McQueen Lake (85 to 90%, [Heffley Creek](#/s/local-heffley)).
- Closures to know: Sun Peaks No Shooting or Hunting Area 400 m from ski lifts, runs and facilities (MU 3-27); McQueen Lake No Shooting Area (MU 3-28); Paul Lake Park, treat as closed (95 to 99%, [Heffley Creek](#/s/local-heffley), [Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3)).
- Vehicles: no ATV (all terrain vehicle) for hunting 1 September to 10 December in MUs 3-28, 3-29 and 3-30; Skull Mountain area in 3-28 open roads only 1 September to 10 December; no motor vehicles above 1,700 m in Region 3 except on existing roads and trails (99%, [Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3)).
- Bonaparte Plateau (MUs 3-29 and 3-30), just west, is a provincial moose study area since 2012; 0.25 to 0.30 moose per km² (90%).
- Success: about 8% of MU 3-27 moose hunters, about 77 days per moose (90%, chapter 1).
```

## Fraser Valley and South Okanagan

```photo hab-wells-gray-cutblock
```

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Region 2: 70 to 100 moose; no general open season near Mission (90 to 99%).
- Region 8: 4,000 to 5,000 moose; spike fork bulls, 1 to 15 November (90 to 99%).

```more Fraser Valley in detail
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- 2022 estimate for Region 2 Lower Mainland: 70 to 100 moose, stable (90%).
- "Not open near Mission: antlerless deer, white tailed deer, wolf, moose and elk" (99%, page 28, [Region 2](#/s/rb-region-2)).
- Moose are usually absent from the coast, reaching tidewater only at the heads of some inlets from Bute Inlet north (90%, BC account).
- So from Mission, moose hunting means a drive to Region 3 or Region 8 (Tip). See [Mission](#/s/local-mission).
```

```more South Okanagan in detail
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- 2022 estimate: Okanagan 4,000 to 5,000, stable to increasing (90%).
- Season: MUs 8-1 to 8-15 and 8-21 to 8-26, spike fork bulls, 1 to 15 November, bag 1 (99%, page 68, [Region 8](#/s/rb-region-8)).
- The dry South Okanagan valley is not moose habitat (90%, BC account on dry valleys). Moose there live on the higher forested plateaus with lakes and wetlands (75%, my reading).
- Region 8 hunters killed 181 to 283 moose a year from 2020 to 2024 (90%, harvest statistics).
- Which MU covers your quail spot and whether it holds moose: VERIFY on the map.
```

## Find them on the Hunt Map

```steps Find moose country on the Hunt Map
mc-moose-map-1 | Layers: Moose by month, wetlands, winter range.
mc-moose-map-2 | Layers: burns by year, cutblocks by age.
mc-moose-map-3 | Slope steepness: find gentle wet benches.
mc-moose-map-4 | What can I see: a knob above a wetland.
mc-moose-map-5 | Wind: keep the scent cone off the swamp.
```

- Open the [Hunt Map](#/map). Pick November in the layers panel (Tip).
- Then check every spot on foot (Tip).

```more step by step in words
1. **Layers:** turn on "Moose by month (general pattern)", "Moose winter range", "Wetlands", and the land status layers (private land, parks, closures). The seasonal layer is a general pattern from studies, not mapped corridors (99%, app layer note).
2. **Layers:** add "Burns by year" and "Cutblocks by age". Look for 10 to 30 year blocks and 3 to 15 year burns near wetlands (65 to 75%, Tip).
3. **Slope steepness:** moose favour gentle, wet ground: benches, basins and valley floors, not cliffs (Tip).
4. **What can I see from here (viewshed):** drop a pin on a knob above a willow flat or lake; pick seats that see a lot of edge (Tip).
5. **Weather and wind, scent cone:** draw your cone so it misses the swamp; remember evening air drains into low ground (Tip, chapter 3).
6. Add waypoints for seats and for possible kill sites near a road or trail: you must pack a moose out (Tip, chapter 10).
- Map tools: [Hunt Map pro](#/s/hunt-map-pro).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-moose-habitat
```

- **Rhyme:** "Willow, water, cover near: that's where moose live all year."
- **Number trick:** 3 to 15 and 10 to 30: burn years and cutblock years moose like (60 to 75%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Find the groceries and you find the moose."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Dry valley, no moose; wet plateau, cut them loose."
- **Number trick:** 0.25: moose per km² on the Bonaparte; one moose in 4 km² (90%).
- **Number trick:** 5 to 10: seasonal home range km² (90%).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely reported, opinion, [habitat tour](#/s/habitat-big-game)): "Hunting dry timber for moose is hunting for nothing."

> [!mistake]
> - Hunting the grass benches near Kamloops for moose.
> - Picking a spot you cannot get a moose out of.
> - Trusting a map layer without walking the ground.

> [!field]
> On the Hunt Map, find one wetland within 1 km of a road in MU 3-27 or 3-28 with a 10 to 30 year cutblock beside it. Walk its edge and look for browse and tracks.
```
