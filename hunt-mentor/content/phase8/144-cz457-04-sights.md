> [!why]
> A rifle that is not zeroed misses where you aim. A 50 m zero turns the CZ into a point and shoot tool for grouse.

```diagram cz-rear-sight
```

- Factory zero for iron sights: 50 m (95%).
- Rear sight: windage. Front sight: elevation (95%).

```more iron sights in full
- "The CZ 457 rimfire rifle enables rear sight adjustment for windage and front sight adjustment for elevation ... The sights have been zeroed at 50 m in the factory" (95%, CZ manual page 8).
- "The folding rear sight ... is provided with marks corresponding to 25 m for the shooting distance from 25 to 200 m. When you press the button, use the slider to set the required shooting distance (the distances are only indicative and may vary depending on the ammunition used)" (95%).
- Windage: "unscrewing the screw on the side of the rear sight plate to which you wish to shift the mean point of impact to and ... tightening the screw on the other side" (95%, Fig. 8).
- Not every CZ 457 has iron sights; the American has none (85%, CZ USA). VERIFY yours.
```

## Front sight and which way to move

```diagram cz-front-sight
```

- Rear sight: move it the way you want the hits to go (90%).
- Higher front blade: hits go lower (90%).

```more the elevation rule
- "Elevation adjustment of the front sight can be carried out using the pair of front screws (Fig. 9)" (95%, CZ manual page 8).
- Front and rear sight rules are geometry: moving the rear sight up or the front sight down raises the point of impact (90%, standard iron sight practice).
- Change one thing at a time, then shoot a new group (Tip).
```

## The 11 mm dovetail

```diagram cz-dovetail
```

- Rings clamp straight onto the receiver (95%).
- Or fit a Weaver rail (95%).

```more mounts in full
- "The rifle scope is to be installed on an 11 mm dovetail on the receiver using rifle scope mounts or via a Weaver rail" (95%, CZ manual page 8).
- CZ: "Mounting of the riflescope and sighting-in of the rifle should be entrusted to a qualified gunsmith's workshop" (95%). Many owners mount their own with care and a torque driver (Tip). My lean: first time, do it at the gun shop counter and watch.
- Buy rings made for an 11 mm rimfire dovetail, and match ring size to the scope tube: 1 in (25.4 mm) or 30 mm (80%, ring makers' listings).
- One ring maker's spec for its CZ 457 rings: 2 to 2.5 Nm (newton metres) (65%, CZ Parts online store tutorial). Use your ring maker's number first.
```

## Mount a scope, 5 steps

```steps Mount a scope on the CZ in 5 steps
cz-scope-1 | Ring bases on the dovetail, torqued.
cz-scope-2 | Scope in, tops loose. Slide it fore and aft.
cz-scope-3 | Set eye relief in your shooting position.
cz-scope-4 | Level the crosshair with the rifle level.
cz-scope-5 | Tighten the top screws in a cross pattern.
```

- PROVE it unloaded first (85%).

```more mounting in full
- Degrease the dovetail and ring bases so they grip (65%, CZ Parts online store tutorial).
- Eye relief: in your real shooting position, slide the scope until you see a full picture with no black ring, then stop (Tip). More: [optics mastery](#/s/optics-mastery) and [choose a scope](#/s/scope-guide).
- [[Eye relief]] on rimfire scopes is often long; a .22 has almost no recoil, so eye relief is about comfort, not scope bite (Tip).
- Level: rifle level in a rest, vertical crosshair straight up and down (Tip).
- Tops: tighten a little at a time, corner to corner, keep the gaps even on both sides (Tip, common ring maker instruction).
- Check the bolt handle clears the eyepiece at the full 60 degree lift (Tip).
```

## Zero at 25 m, then 50 m

```diagram cz-zero-chart
```

- First shots at 25 m get you on paper (Tip).
- Final zero at 50 m: dead on from 15 to 55 m (75%).

```more zeroing step by step
1. PROVE it, then bore sight: bolt out, rifle in a rest, look through the bore from the chamber at a target 25 m away, then move the crosshair onto the same spot (Tip).
2. At 25 m: 3 shots from a solid rest. Move the group onto the aim point (Tip).
3. At 50 m: 5 shot groups. Fine tune until the group centre is on the aim point (Tip).
4. Confirm at 25, 40 and 75 m to learn your holds (75%, Hunt Mentor model).
- Model numbers for a 40 grain bullet at 1,235 fps (feet per second), scope 4 cm over the bore: 50 m zero is 6 cm low at 75 m and 19 cm low at 100 m (75%, from [the .22 rimfire](#/s/rimfire-22)).
- Same steps for iron sights, starting from the factory 50 m zero (95%, CZ page 8).
- Full method: [sighting in](#/s/sighting-in).
```

## Move the group with clicks

```diagram cz-clicks
```

- Close range means more clicks (95%).
- Half the distance: twice the clicks (95%).

```more click math
- A [[MOA]] (minute of angle) is about 2.9 cm at 100 m, so a quarter MOA click is about 0.73 cm at 100 m, 0.36 cm at 50 m and 0.18 cm at 25 m (95%, geometry).
- Example: group 3.6 cm low at 50 m with quarter MOA clicks: 3.6 / 0.36 = 10 clicks UP (95%, math).
- Some rimfire scopes have different click values or are set for 50 yd parallax. Read your scope's manual (Tip).
- Turret arrows say which way moves the hits. Trust the arrow, then check on paper (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram cz-zero-chart
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Zero with the ammo you hunt with. Change ammo, zero again.

> [!mistake]
> - Zeroing at 25 m and calling it done. At 50 m you are about 1 cm low (75%).
> - Overtightening ring screws and crushing the scope tube.
> - Cranking turrets after every shot instead of after a group.

> [!field]
> Write your zero, ammo and date on tape inside the scope cap.
