> [!why]
> Elk learn. A herd that meets one careless hunter moves a mountain over and changes its hours. Know how they think and you hunt the herd nobody spooked.

```photo g-elk-herd
```

- Elk are intelligent and learn from experience (90%).
- Hunted elk turn wary, secretive and nocturnal (90%).

```more what this chapter covers
- Alarm signals, flight distance, how elk learn and remember pressure, herd life and hierarchy, cows and calves, old bulls and young bulls, and the mistakes elk make that you can use.
- BC elk account (90%): "Elk are intelligent animals that learn from experience and adapt to changing conditions." Hunted elk "become wary, secretive, and nocturnal and learn the location of protective reserves."
```

## Alarm signals

```diagram mc-elk-alarm
```

- Head up, ears forward, stare, bark, run (65 to 85%).
- Freeze at step 1 or 2 (Tip).

```more alarm signals in detail
- Alarmed elk rotate their ears to listen; agitated elk flare their nostrils (85%, RMEF (Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation)).
- A cow's bark is the alarm call; the herd may run (65%, hunting guides).
- Elk check a shape or sound with their other senses before running (75%, Colorado Parks and Wildlife).
- A spooked herd often bunches, then leaves at a trot or run toward timber, frequently over a ridge into the next drainage (Tip, hunter consensus).
- Unlike a mule deer, an elk rarely stops to look back for long (Tip). Do not count on a second chance.
```

## Flight distance and pressure

```diagram mc-elk-flight-rings
```

- Elk kept about 560 to 880 m from people in a trail study (85%).
- ATVs (all terrain vehicles) pushed elk farthest; hikers least (85%).

```more the Starkey experiments
- Starkey Experimental Forest, northeast Oregon, US Forest Service: ATV, mountain bike, horseback and hiking were run on trails through a large elk enclosure (85%).
- Flight probability: little effect when hikers were beyond 500 m; higher probabilities beyond 750 m from horse riders and beyond 1,500 m from mountain bikes and ATVs (85%, Wisdom and others 2004).
- Elk moved fastest during ATV riding, then mountain biking, then hiking and horseback (85%).
- Elk kept an average minimum distance of 558 to 879 m from people, and shifted out of view and away from trails, coming back only when people were gone (85%, Wisdom and others 2018).
- On treatment days elk moved more at sunrise and sunset too, as if pushed out of their best feeding and security areas (85%, 2004).
- Mule deer changed far less than elk in the same tests (85%).
- What it means: your ATV is an elk alarm. Park it far back and walk (Tip). Rules for ATVs while hunting: [ATV rules](#/s/atv-rules).
```

## How it learns and remembers

```diagram mc-elk-refuge
```

- Hunted elk find places with no hunting (80 to 90%).
- They feed at night and hide by day (90%).

```more learning in detail
- When a Montana late season hunt opened, the odds of elk being in no hunting refuges more than doubled, and elk shifted onto private land closed to hunting (80%, Proffitt and Garrott 2009, abstract).
- In two western Montana herds, refuge use began with a few elk in the hunting season, then the share of the herd using refuges and the time spent there grew every year (80%, Burcham and others, abstract).
- Unhunted elk in parks graze on highway verges and golf courses and can be belligerent to people (90%, BC account). The same species learns the opposite lesson where it is hunted.
- Elk are among the most easily domesticated American hoofed animals (90%).
- What it means: the herd near the road on opening morning will not be there on day three. The herd behind the locked gate learns the gate (Tip).
- Pressure skills: [pressure push](#/s/pressure-push), [weather and moon](#/s/weather-moon).
```

## Social life

```diagram mc-elk-social
```

- Cow groups: up to 20 or more cows, calves, yearlings (90%).
- Bulls live apart until the rut (90%).

```more social life in detail
- Up to 20 or more cows, calves and yearlings live in groups that stay apart from smaller groups of bulls, except during the autumn rut (90%, BC elk account).
- Both groups have a pecking order kept by threats and displays rather than serious fights. Front hooves are the usual weapon; bulls also use antlers (90%).
- Fights flare over salt licks, beds, wallows or the best feed; big animals push small ones aside (90%).
- Bulls of equal size spar: lock antlers, push and twist, then let go with no winner. Not a real fight (90%).
- In forest and parkland, groups average 6 or 7 (80%, Hinterland Who's Who). Big open country herds are larger (Tip).
- Bulls live in [[Bachelor group|bachelor groups]] or alone (85%, RMEF (Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation)).
- In the rut, cows and calves form harems with one or two mature bulls (85%, RMEF). The herd bull holds them; younger bulls hang on the edges (Tip).
```

## Mother and calf

```diagram mc-elk-calf-hide
```

```photo bg-elk-calf
```

- Calf hides 2 to 3 weeks, then joins the cow group (90%).
- Bears take many calves in the first weeks (80%).

```video
commons-elk-cow-calf | A cow and calf: a calf follows close by fall.
```

```more cows and calves in detail
- Before birth, a cow finds a secluded spot in dense cover, sometimes an island in a river (90%, BC account).
- Most cows have one spotted calf of about 13 kg; twins are rare (90%).
- The cow hides the calf, feeds nearby and comes back to nurse. After two or three weeks the cow and calf rejoin the matriarchal group (90%).
- Calves are born spotted and scentless and spend the first weeks hiding motionless (85%, RMEF (Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation)).
- In some areas black bears may kill up to half the calves, mostly in the first 2 or 3 weeks (80%, Hinterland Who's Who).
- Found a calf alone in June? It is not abandoned. Leave it (Tip, widely given wildlife agency advice).
- Matriarchal: the cow groups are led by older cows (90%, BC account calls them matriarchal). The lead cow often decides when the herd leaves (Tip).
```

## The rut mind

```diagram mc-elk-rut-calendar
```

```photo bg-elk-sparring-jasper
```

- Bulls gather harems, bugle, wallow, thrash (90%).
- Most bulls do not breed until 4 or 5 (90%).

```anim sa-elk-bugle Low start, high scream, grunts.
```

```more the rut mind in detail
- As mid September nears, bulls become more active and aggressive; they find cow groups and gather harems that they herd and guard (90%, BC account).
- To show off to cows and scare rivals they bugle, wallow, spray urine, thrash bushes and more (90%).
- Adult bulls fight head to head; sometimes the winner gores the loser (90%).
- When a cow is ready, the bull switches from herding to gentler courtship (90%).
- Bulls can breed as yearlings but usually do not get the chance until 4 or 5 (90%).
- Harem size: "several cows" (90%, BC account); up to 20 (80%, US Park Service); 20 or 30 for large bulls (80%, Hinterland Who's Who).
- Rut timing and dates: chapter 5.
```

## Old bull, young bull

```diagram mc-elk-old-young
```

```gallery
bg-elk-spike-bc | Young bull: spikes in velvet.
bg-elk-spikes | Young bulls together: spikes.
```

- Herd bulls move cows away from trouble (Tip).
- Young bulls come in loud and fast (Tip).

```more old and young in detail
- Hunter lore, not studies (Tip):
  - A herd bull with cows often answers a bugle but pushes his cows away rather than coming to fight.
  - Satellite bulls without cows are curious and come to calls more readily.
  - Old bulls often slip in quietly and circle downwind to scent check the call.
- Facts behind it: bulls rarely breed before 4 or 5 years, so most legal 6 point bulls are mature, experienced animals (80%, my reading of the BC account and US Park Service antler figures).
- A mature bull grows his heaviest antlers around 11 or 12 years (80%, US Park Service).
```

## Predictable mistakes elk make

```diagram mc-elk-sense-check
```

- Rut bulls answer calls and challenge rivals (90%).
- Elk return to the same wallows and winter ranges (85 to 90%).

```more mistakes you can use
- In the rut, bulls go looking for cows and rivals; a good cow call or bugle can bring one in (90% for the rut behaviour; calling success is Tip).
- Bulls return to wallows through the rut (70%, RMEF (Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation)).
- Elk use traditional winter ranges year after year; autumn snow pushes them back to them (90%, BC account).
- A herd that is not pressured feeds in the open in daylight and repeats its routine (Tip). Find it, do not bump it, and hunt it on the right wind.
- A curious elk sometimes stops after a short run to look (Tip). A soft cow call can stop a walking elk for a shot (Tip, chapter 9).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-elk-flight-rings
```

- **Rhyme:** "Push them once, they learn; push them twice, they are gone."
- **Number trick:** 500, 750, 1,500: metres of flight risk for hiker, horse, bike or ATV (all terrain vehicle) (85%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Leave the road hunters behind. The elk did."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "A bark is a goodbye."
- **Number trick:** 20: cows, calves and yearlings in a big cow group (90%); 2 to 3 weeks: a calf in hiding (90%).
- **Number trick:** 4 or 5: the age bulls usually start breeding (90%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Never spook the herd you want to hunt tomorrow."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "The bull that bugles most is rarely the one with cows."

> [!mistake]
> - Riding the ATV close to the elk you want to hunt.
> - Bumping a herd at midday to "see what is there".
> - Assuming the herd will be back in the same meadow after a shot.

> [!field]
> Watch a herd for an hour with binoculars from far away. Note who leads when they move, and who barks first when something spooks them.
```
