> [!why]
> Being lost is a problem you can solve. Panic and wandering are what turn it into an emergency. Sit down first; think second; walk last, if at all.

```steps Lost? STOP
gr-stop-s | Stop. Sit down. Do not keep walking.
gr-stop-t | Think. Where did you last know where you were?
gr-stop-o | Observe. Map, compass, sun, creeks, roads.
gr-stop-p | Plan. Stay put, or move only if sure.
```

- STOP: Stop, Think, Observe, Plan (75%).
- "It's always harder for search teams to find you if you move" (75%).

```more what this chapter adds
- The quick version: [Lost and the survival kit](#/s/lost-survival-kit) (STOP, circles, stay or move, calling, signals, being found). This chapter goes deeper: the first 10 minutes, the decision in detail, what to say on the call, signalling by mirror, and what searchers want from you.
- Previous: [Chapter 6](#/s/mc-nav-06). Next: [Chapter 8, Surviving the night](#/s/mc-nav-08).
- [[STOP]] is taught by AdventureSmart for when you are lost, disoriented or injured (75%, Global News interview with AdventureSmart, 2023).
- AdventureSmart: moving "more often than not" puts you in a more dangerous situation; "this time, you want to be found" (75%).
```

## The first 10 minutes

```diagram mc-nav-first10
```

- Sit. Drink. Eat something. Then think (Tip).
- Panic makes safe decisions harder (75%).

```more the first 10 minutes in full
- North Shore Rescue: "Stay Calm if You're Lost: panicking makes safe decision making harder" (75%, read directly).
- Minutes 0 to 3 (Tip): sit down, unload if you are carrying a firearm and are no longer hunting, drink, eat a bar. Say out loud: "I am not lost yet. I am working out where I am."
- Minutes 3 to 6 (Tip): Think. When were you last sure? What time was it? Which way did you go after that? How long did you walk? At 4 km an hour off trail that sets the radius (75% for the speed).
- Minutes 6 to 10 (Tip): Observe. Orient the map. Name what you can see and hear: a road, a creek, a ridge, traffic, a chainsaw.
- Then Plan, on the next screen.
- Check the light: how long until dark? Mid October sunset at Heffley Creek is about 18:08 (85%).
```

## Stay or move?

```diagram mc-nav-stay-move
```

- Hurt, nearly dark or any doubt: stay (75%).
- Move only to a safer spot nearby, or back on a route you are sure of in daylight (Tip).

```more the decision in full
- Stay put: it reduces the time and the area searchers must cover (75%, BC Search and Rescue Association via [Lost](#/s/lost-survival-kit)).
- North Shore Rescue: "Stay Put Once Lost: wandering usually takes you farther from your last known point" (75%).
- AdventureSmart warns against resting in hazardous spots: swift water, under or on a cornice, near slippery ledges (70%, Global News, search preview).
- Moving can be right (Tip): you are sure of the way back to your last known point and can get there in daylight; you can follow your own fresh tracks in snow; your spot is dangerous (rising water, fire, avalanche path).
- If you move: leave a note or a flagged branch with time and direction, mark your route, and stop at the first doubt (Tip).
- Your phone works? Calling and then staying is almost always the best move (Tip, NSR call early advice 75%).
```

## Why searchers want you to stay

```diagram fs-lost-search-area
```

- Double the distance, four times the area to search (95%).
- Lost hunters tend to drift downhill and off trail (65%).

```more the geometry and the hunter pattern
- Search area grows with the square of the radius: double the distance, 4 times the area; triple it, 9 times (95%, geometry).
- Koester's lost person data: hunters tend "to go around 100 feet downhill and to take that journey off trail" (65%, Popular Science, 2024).
- North Shore Rescue: avoid going downhill into ravines; they are steep, unstable and hard to climb out of (75%).
- Searchers start at your last known point from the trip plan and work outward using statistics like these (65%, Koester via Popular Science).
- So: stay near where the trip plan says you are, and up high and in the open rather than down in a draw (Tip).
```

## Downhill is the trap

```diagram mc-nav-downhill
```

- "Follow water down" can lead into canyons and cliffs (75%).
- In this country, the road is often up on a bench, not down in the creek (Tip).

```more why downhill feels right and is wrong
- Going down feels easy and hopeful. Draws narrow, steepen and end in creeks, blowdown and cliffs (75%, North Shore Rescue on ravines; Tip for the detail).
- Around Heffley Creek, many forest roads run along slopes and benches above the creeks (Tip; check your area on the Hunt Map).
- Check the map: where is your baseline? Uphill or downhill from you? (Tip, [Chapter 2](#/s/mc-nav-02))
- At night, never cut down a dark draw to save time ([Night navigation](#/s/night-navigation)).
```

## Call early: what to say

```diagram mc-nav-call-script
```

- Dial 911 and ask for police (75%). Say where you are first (Tip).
- Then save the battery and keep agreed check in times (Tip).

```more the call in full
- North Shore Rescue: "For a search and rescue emergency dial 911 and ask for police"; call "as soon as you know you're lost or injured" (75%).
- BC Search and Rescue Association: your first action is to call 911; police request search and rescue (75%, via [Lost](#/s/lost-survival-kit)).
- A phone can reach 911 through any carrier's tower in range (75%).
- Script (Tip): 1 where (coordinates from the Hunt Map: tap the elevation readout, then copy; say the format); 2 who and how many; 3 what happened and any injuries; 4 what you have (light, fire, food, warm layers, battery percent); 5 the plan (staying put, next check in time).
- No signal: Emergency SOS via satellite on iPhone 14 or later from open sky, or a satellite messenger (95% Apple, 65% Garmin; [Chapter 6](#/s/mc-nav-06)).
- After the call: stay where you told them you are (Tip). Phone in a warm pocket.
- There is no charge for rescue in BC (95%, BCSARA report; 75% North Shore Rescue).
```

```steps Call for help in 4 steps
fs-lost-call-1 | Call 911 early. Any tower works.
fs-lost-call-2 | Give your coordinates first.
fs-lost-call-3 | No signal? Satellite SOS from open sky.
fs-lost-call-4 | Then save battery. Check in on the hour.
```

## Signal: three of anything

```diagram mc-nav-signals
```

- Whistle, mirror, light or fire, in threes (75%).
- A V or SOS on the ground, at least 3 m long (75%).

```more signals in full
- BC Search and Rescue Association: whistle blasts, mirror flashes, horn blasts, signal fires or rock piles, in threes (75%).
- North Shore Rescue: "use a whistle (three blasts for help), mirror, flashlight, or fire to attract attention" (75%).
- AdventureSmart: shelter and fire also make you more noticeable; smoke by day, light by night; bright gear laid out in the open (70%, Global News, search preview).
- Ground to air: a V or SOS at least 3 m long, high contrast (75%, via [Lost](#/s/lost-survival-kit)).
- Three shots is known among hunters as a call for help (Tip). Keep ammunition for the walk out; a whistle is better and free (Tip).
- Fire rules and bans: check before any fire ([Fire in wet weather](#/s/fire-wet-weather), [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety)). In a true emergency, safety first (Tip).
```


## Mirror flash: 3 steps

```steps Aim a signal mirror without a sight
mc-nav-mirror-1 | Two fingers in a V on the target.
mc-nav-mirror-2 | Tilt the mirror so the light hits your V.
mc-nav-mirror-3 | Flash in threes. Repeat.
```

- Any shiny thing works: a phone screen, a knife, foil (Tip).
- Sun behind the target is hard; sweep anyway (Tip).

```more mirror tips
- A sighting mirror on a compass doubles as a signal mirror (Tip).
- The V finger method is a common field technique (Tip). Signal mirrors with a sight hole are easier: follow the maker's steps.
- Sweep the flash slowly across a distant ridge or road where people may be (Tip).
- Mirror flashes in threes count as a distress signal (75%, BCSARA).
```

## Help searchers find you

```steps Be found in 3 steps
fs-lost-found-1 | Settle at the edge of an opening.
fs-lost-found-2 | Spread orange and bright gear in the open.
fs-lost-found-3 | Hear searchers? Whistle 3 times. Stay put.
```

- Answer every call, whistle or engine you hear (70%).
- Do not hide from searchers out of embarrassment (Tip).

```more what search and rescue wants from you
- Stay put, keep warm and dry, answer searchers' calls (70%, Hug a Tree program, Canadian Geographic Education).
- Dense cover keeps you warm but hides you. Be visible outside the shelter (65%, via [Lost](#/s/lost-survival-kit)).
- Blaze orange is already on you: hang the vest in the open (Tip).
- Searchers may use two long whistle blasts so you know it is them (60%, forum post via [Hunting with a partner](#/s/hunting-partner)). Reply with three (Tip).
- Lost hunters are known to avoid searchers out of embarrassment (55%: Tip, secondary summary). Do not be that hunter. Nobody in search and rescue cares; they want you home (Tip).
- If you leave a spot, leave a note: time, direction, name (Tip).
```

## "Hug a tree" is not only for kids

| Hug a Tree (kids program) | Adult version |
|---|---|
| Stay put | STOP, then stay put |
| Keep warm and dry | Shelter, fire, dry layers |
| Answer searchers | Whistle 3, lights, orange |
| Don't hide | Be seen in the open |

- Same core ideas, plus calling 911 and signals (70 to 75%).

```more where these come from
- Hug a Tree and Survive: a program that teaches children to stay put, keep warm and dry and answer searchers (70%, Canadian Geographic Education).
- Adults: STOP, stay put, call 911 early, signal in threes (75%, AdventureSmart, BCSARA, North Shore Rescue).
- The adult difference: you have a phone, a map, a compass, fire and a trip plan. Use them before you need the hug (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-nav-stay-move
```

- **Rhyme:** "Doubt means stay. Help is on the way."
- **Number trick:** 3 of anything means help. 3 m for a ground V (75%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, short form of AdventureSmart's "you want to be found"): "When lost, become easy to find."

```more more hooks
- **STOP:** Stop, Think, Observe, Plan (75%).
- **Number trick:** double the walk, 4 times the search (95%).
- **Rhyme:** "Down a draw, into the jaw" (ravines, 75% NSR).
- **Rhyme:** "Where first, then who, then what."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, from [Lost](#/s/lost-survival-kit) Grandpa's rule): "The minute you wonder where you are, sit down."

> [!mistake]
> - Walking faster and further when unsure.
> - Following a creek downhill into a canyon.
> - Hiding in thick timber where searchers cannot see you.

> [!field]
> On your next trip, practise the first 10 minutes on purpose: sit at a random spot, say STOP out loud, find yourself on the map, and say what you would tell 911. Full drill with your brother: [Chapter 10](#/s/mc-nav-10).
```
