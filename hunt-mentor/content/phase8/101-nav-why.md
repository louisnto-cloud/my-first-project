## Search and rescue in BC: the numbers

```diagram mc-nav-sar-bars
```

- BC ground search and rescue had 1,510 tasks in 2022 (95%).
- Hiking was the biggest slice, about 41% (80%).

```more the 2022 numbers in full
- BCSARA (BC Search and Rescue Association) 2022 Year in Review: 1,510 GSAR (ground search and rescue) tasks, 2,087 team deployments, 67 callouts longer than 24 hours (95%, read directly).
- 1,019 lives saved in 2022; more than 10,000 in the past 10 years (95%).
- "12% involved subjects who would have died without urgent GSAR intervention and 43% involved subjects who would have ultimately not survived" without it (95%).
- Activity chart: hiking 41%, other recreational activities 26%, biking 11%, driving 8%, skiing 7%, sledding 7% (80%, my reading of the chart labels).
- Hunting has no slice of its own in that chart (95%). It sits inside "other" (Tip, my reading).
- Calls have grown: just under 1,000 in 2003, a record 2,100 in 2021, about 1,500 in 2022 (75%, Global News, 2023).
- BC rescue volunteers do not charge you: "you don't pay to be rescued" (95%, sponsor quote in the BCSARA report). North Shore Rescue says the same: "there is no cost for rescue" (75%).
- GSAR teams are called out by agencies such as police, BC Ambulance and fire services, not by you directly. You call 911 (95%).
```

## What search and rescue says goes wrong

```diagram mc-nav-worst10
```

- BCSARA's top 10 worst things you can do (95%).
- Four of them are pure navigation and planning: no trip plan, only a phone, no "what if", not knowing your location.

```more the list word for word
BC Search and Rescue Association, 2022 Year in Review, "Top 10 worst things you can do" (95%, read directly):
1. Wearing inappropriate footwear (causing slips, trips, falls and broken bones).
2. Not leaving a trip plan with a trusted emergency contact.
3. Assessing your skills wrongly and recreating above your abilities.
4. Thinking easy access to a trail equals an easy hike.
5. Going into the mountains with ONLY a cell phone and "maybe" some water.
6. Not thinking "what if" (what if I break my ankle, get lost or have to spend the night).
7. Not knowing your location (such as latitude and longitude).
8. Relying on search and rescue volunteers for incidents that could be preventable.
9. Being over confident, not aware of risks.
10. Being under prepared and misinformed.

- News coverage of BCSARA data: the top three hiking emergencies were getting lost or having no navigation device, poor footwear, and not knowing how hard the trail is; together 271 calls in 2022 (70%, CTV and North Shore News, search preview).
```

## How hunters get lost

```diagram mc-nav-hunter-lost
```

- Hunters usually get lost following an animal or a blood trail through thick brush (65%).
- Eyes on the game, not on the way back.

```more what the lost person research says
- Robert Koester built the ISRID (International Search and Rescue Incident Database), now about 300,000 incidents (65%, Popular Science and Undark, 2024).
- Hunters "usually get lost because they followed an animal intently through dense brush" (65%, same article quoting Koester's book Lost Person Behavior).
- Koester: the most common reason is "spatial disorientation, often related to intense concentration following game or a blood trail" (60%, search preview of the same reporting).
- Lost hunters tend to head downhill and off trail (65%). More in [Chapter 7](#/s/mc-nav-07).
- Under cloud in a forest, people walk in circles without knowing it (75%, Max Planck Institute, 2009, see [Lost](#/s/lost-survival-kit)).
- Hunter specific risks (Tip): starting in the dark, finishing in the dark, leaving the trail on purpose, chasing a wounded animal, hunting alone, carrying a heavy pack late in the day.
- Every blood trail needs a plan for the way out: flag the last blood, drop a waypoint, check the time ([After the shot](#/s/mc-skills-08)).
```

## Small problems stack up

```diagram mc-nav-chain
```

- Most bad nights start as small slips that pile up (Tip).
- Break one link early and the chain fails.

```more how a normal hunt turns bad
- Example chain (Tip): a late start, so you hurry; a wrong turn while chasing a deer; dark comes; you are sweaty and the wind picks up; you slip on a wet log in the dark.
- Each link is small. Together they make a search and rescue call (Tip).
- BCSARA's own list matches: no "what if" thinking, poor footwear, over confidence (95%).
- The cheapest link to break is time: a turnaround time set at home (Tip, see [Planning a route](#/s/route-planning)).
- The second cheapest: a trip plan, so the night is short even if it goes wrong (75%, AdventureSmart).
```

## Six traps in your head

```diagram mc-nav-facets
```

- Avalanche research found six thinking traps in accidents: FACETS (80%).
- They fit hunting too. Name the trap, then decide (Tip).

```more FACETS and the hunter version
- Ian McCammon studied recreational avalanche accidents and named six "heuristic traps" (80%, avalanche.org encyclopedia):
  - **Familiarity:** "Parties traveling in familiar terrain tend to make riskier decisions."
  - **Acceptance:** people take more risk to be accepted by their group.
  - **Consistency:** parties "highly committed to a goal", even in worsening weather.
  - **Expert halo:** a de facto leader, older or more experienced, whom others follow.
  - **Tracks or scarcity:** more risk when "racing a closing window of opportunity".
  - **Social facilitation:** groups get bolder when other parties are around.
- The hunter versions below are my application, not from the research (Tip):
  - Familiarity: "I hunted this draw last year, no need for the map."
  - Acceptance: "I won't tell my brother I'm unsure."
  - Consistency: "One more ridge, we came this far." Or "keep following the blood."
  - Expert halo: "He's been here before, I'll just follow."
  - Scarcity: "Last day of the season, last hour of light."
  - Social: "Other trucks are parked up here, so the road must be fine."
- McCammon's work shows links, not causes, because it is based on accident data (75%, search preview).
- Avalanche risk itself: [Avalanche awareness](#/s/avalanche-hunters).
```

## The trip plan: who knows where you are?

```diagram mc-nav-tripplan
```

- Leave a trip plan with someone not on the trip, every time (75%).
- Add coordinates for the truck and a "call 911 if" time (Tip).

```more the trip plan in full
- AdventureSmart's Three Ts: trip planning, training, taking the essentials (75%, via [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety)).
- BCSARA's number 2 worst thing: no trip plan with a trusted emergency contact (95%).
- North Shore Rescue: "Leave a Trip Plan with a Friend, so someone will notice if you're overdue" (75%, read directly).
- What to write (75% for the core items, Tip for the extras):
  - Who: names, phone numbers, any medical issues.
  - Vehicle: make, colour, plate. Trailer, ATV or boat if you bring them.
  - Where parked: road name and kilometre sign if there is one, plus coordinates from the Hunt Map (tap the elevation readout for Map centre and copy them, 99%, app).
  - Route: plan A and plan B for the other wind.
  - Back by: a clock time. Call 911 if: a later clock time.
  - Gear: satellite SOS on the phone or a messenger, colours of your jacket and pack.
- AdventureSmart has a free trip plan tool (75%, see [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety)).
- Hunt Mentor's [Plan a hunt](#/plan) screen helps you build the day.
```

```checklist mc-nav-tripplan-check
Who, phone numbers, medical notes
Vehicle, colour, plate
Where parked, with coordinates
Route: plan A and plan B
Back by time
Call 911 if no word by time
Jacket and pack colours
```

## When should home call 911?

```diagram mc-nav-call-time
```

- Pick the "call 911 if" time before you leave (Tip).
- You call too, early, as soon as you know you are lost or hurt (75%).

```more the calling rules
- North Shore Rescue: "dial 9-1-1 as soon as you know you're lost or injured"; "early callouts help NSR find you before conditions worsen" (75%, read directly).
- For a search and rescue emergency, dial 911 and ask for police (75%, North Shore Rescue).
- A BCSARA story: a lost hiker hesitated, thinking 911 was only for people in immediate danger. The advice was "call for help and call early" (95%, BCSARA 2022 review).
- Example evening for mid October at Heffley Creek (Tip): turn around 16:15, at the truck 17:40, text home 18:00, home calls 911 at 21:00 with no word.
- Mid October sunset there is about 18:08 (85%, my calculation). Legal light for big game ends 1 hour after sunset (99%, [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)).
- The person at home needs the plan, your plate, and the instruction "call 911, ask for police, read them this" (Tip).
- Rules for when to stop hunting: Rule Book only. This screen adds no rules.
```

## What a 40 year hunter does differently

| Beginner | Old hand (Tip) |
|---|---|
| "Just up the road", no plan | Trip plan every time |
| Phone only | Phone, paper map, compass |
| Follows the blood, eyes down | Flags, pins, looks back |
| Pushes for one more ridge | Turns around on time |
| Calls when it is dark | Calls when unsure |

- Every line is hunter and search and rescue wisdom, labelled Tip.

```more where these come from
- Trip plan every time: BCSARA top 10 and AdventureSmart (75 to 95%).
- Phone plus paper and compass: BCSARA "only a cell phone" (95%); [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety) (75%).
- Flag and pin a blood trail: [After the shot](#/s/mc-skills-08) (Tip).
- Turnaround time: North Shore Rescue "set a clear turnaround time" (75%).
- Call early: North Shore Rescue (75%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-nav-chain
```

- **Rhyme:** "Eyes on the deer, lost in a year. Eyes on the way, home for the day."
- **Number trick:** 1,510 tasks in 2022. About 1 in 10 would have died without urgent help (12%, 95%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, short form of AdventureSmart's trip plan advice): "Tell someone, every time."

```more more hooks
- **FACETS:** Familiarity, Acceptance, Consistency, Expert halo, Tracks or scarcity, Social facilitation (80%).
- **Rhyme:** "Call when unsure, not when it's dark."
- **Number trick:** 2 times: back by, and call 911 if. Both on the plan (Tip).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, short form of North Shore Rescue's "call for help early"): "Pride costs more than a phone call."

> [!mistake]
> - No trip plan because it is "only up the road".
> - Following the blood trail without a single look back.
> - Waiting until full dark to call.

> [!field]
> Tonight: write a trip plan template in your phone notes with every line on the card. Send one for your next scouting trip, even a short one.
```
