## Buck, doe, fawn

```gallery
g-wt-buck-front | Buck: tines rise off one main beam.
g-wt-doe | Doe: no antlers, wide brown tail.
g-wt-doe-flag | Alarm: the white flag goes up.
g-wt-run | Runs low and smooth, no bounce.
```

- Buck: antlers September to winter (85%).
- Doe: no antlers. Fawn: spots fade by fall (75%).

```more ID by sex and age
- **Buck** (male): hard antlers from about September until they drop, January to March (75%, see chapter 5).
- **Doe** (female): no antlers. Longer, slimmer face (Tip).
- **Fawn** (under 1 year): born late May or June with white spots (85%, BC account). Spots fade with the first fall coat (75%).
- **Button buck**: a male fawn with two small bumps, not antlers. Counts as antlerless (99%, synopsis page 3, via the [quick lesson](#/s/white-tailed-deer)).
- **Yearling buck** (about 1.5 years): often a spike or small fork, thin neck, "a doe with antlers" (65%, National Deer Association via Field and Stream).
```

## The tail tells you

```diagram mwt-tail-compare
```

- White tail: wide brown tail, white only underneath (85%).
- Up and waving = the **flag** (85%).

```more tail, rump and run
- White tail: tail is wide, brown on top, bright white underneath and on the edges (85%, Alberta Institute for Wildlife Conservation).
- Rump: only a small white patch, hidden when the tail is down (80%).
- Alarm: tail goes straight up and waves (85%).
- Mule deer: thin white rope tail with a black tip on a big white rump (85%).
- The run: white tails gallop low and smooth to cover. Mule deer **stot**, bouncing on all 4 feet (85%).
```

## Antlers: one beam, tines rise

```diagram mwt-antler-parts
```

- One main beam sweeps forward. Tines rise off it (85%).
- Mule deer antlers fork, and fork again (85%).

```more how to read white tail antlers
- **Main beam**: grows back from the base, then curves forward and in (85%).
- **Brow tine** (G1): short tine just above the base. Most white tail bucks have one (70%).
- **Tines** G2, G3, G4: rise straight up from the main beam, like teeth on a comb (85%).
- **Counting points**: hunters count each tine about 2.5 cm (1 in) or longer, plus the beam tip (Tip, scoring custom). For legal antler definitions use the [Rule Book](#/s/rb-definitions) only.
- Antler size grows with age and food, but a young buck on good feed can fool you. Judge age by the body (next screen) (65%).
```

## Age a buck by its body

```diagram mwt-age-bodies
```

- Body first, antlers second (65%).
- Yearling: thin neck, long legs, "a doe with antlers" (65%).

```more aging on the hoof
- **Yearling** (1.5 years): lanky, clear break between neck and shoulders, narrow waist (65%).
- **2 to 3 years**: chest deepens, neck swells in the rut, hindquarters and shoulders even out (60%).
- **4 years plus**: deep chest, belly sags, thick neck blends into the shoulders, legs look short (65%).
- Old bucks may show loose skin on the face and neck and a sway back (60%).
- Hindquarters bigger than the shoulders: almost always a 1 or 2 year old (60%).
- Not an exact science. Get more than one view (65%, Kip Adams, National Deer Association, via Field and Stream).
- Easiest in late pre rut and the weeks after, when bucks are in peak shape (60%).
```

## Look alikes: legal or not

```diagram mule-vs-whitetail
```

- Mule deer: separate species, separate licence and seasons (99%).
- Button buck = antlerless. Know which class is open (99%).

| Looks like | Tell | Legal note |
|---|---|---|
| Mule deer | Rope tail, black tip, forks | Own seasons ([Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-3)) |
| Doe | No bumps on head | Antlerless season only |
| Button buck | Two bumps | Antlerless (99%) |
| Hybrid | Mixed traits | VERIFY before you shoot |

```more look alikes in detail
- **Mule deer**: big ears, rope tail with a black tip, forked antlers, stots (85%). A separate species licence and separate seasons (99%, synopsis page 8 and region tables).
- **Button buck**: a male fawn. Its skin or hair covered bumps are not antlers, so it is antlerless (99%, synopsis page 3, [Rule Book: definitions](#/s/rb-definitions)).
- **Hybrids**: mule deer and white tails do cross. A genetic study on the western Canadian prairies found about 1% hybrids (85%, Russell and others 2021). Mixed tail and antler traits. If you cannot tell, do not shoot (Tip).
- **Elk calf**: much bigger, pale rump, dark neck (80%). Never confuse at legal range if you look (Tip).

## Size and weight

```diagram mwt-size-bars
```

- Average buck about 91 kg (200 lb), doe about 57 kg (125 lb) (80%).
- BC weights: not found. VERIFY.

```more size detail
- Idaho Fish and Game: bucks average 200 lb (91 kg), does 125 lb (57 kg) (80%).
- Montana State University Extension: mature bucks 200 to 300 lb (91 to 136 kg); does 25 to 40% less, about 120 to 180 lb (54 to 82 kg) (80%).
- Fawns weigh 5 to 8 lb (2 to 4 kg) at birth (80%) and can double that in 2 weeks (80%).
- Northern deer run bigger than southern deer (85%, US Forest Service species review: largest subspecies in the Northeast and Great Lakes, Florida Key deer the smallest).
- White tails are smaller than mule deer on average in Idaho (80%).
- Does stop growing at 3 to 4 years, bucks at 4 to 5 (85%, US Forest Service species review).
- Tip: a big white tail buck plus your pack is more than one trip. Plan the carry (Tip).
```

## Who they are in BC

```diagram mwt-harvest-bars
```

- Most BC white tails: Kootenay, Okanagan, Peace (90%).
- Region 2 Lower Mainland: no white tail season (97%).

```more populations and subspecies
- Southern Interior white tails are the **northwest white tail**, Odocoileus virginianus ochrourus (75%, older BC publication via search preview).
- Peace River white tails may be the prairie (Dakota) race (60%, VERIFY).
- Historic core: the southeast (Kootenay) and northeast (Peace). They have spread into the south central Interior, including the Thompson (80%, BC provincial review, search preview).
- Snow depth sets where they can live. They prefer valley bottoms with farmland (85%, BC account).
- Resident hunter kills in 2024: Region 4 Kootenay 3,971; Region 8 Okanagan 2,225; Region 7B Peace 1,542; Region 3 Thompson 1,091; Region 5 Cariboo 488; Region 7A Omineca 308; Region 6 Skeena 85 (90%, BC Big Game Harvest Statistics, sample survey estimates).
- Region 2: the page 14 table gives white tailed deer 0 for Region 2, and Herrling Island near Hope (MU, Management Unit, 2-3) has no open season (97%, synopsis pages 14 and 27).
```

## Numbers and trend in BC

```diagram mwt-bc-trend
```

- About 40,000 (1987) to over 113,000 (2011) (80%).
- 2022 estimate: 35,000 to 71,500 (75%). Methods differ.

```more the numbers and why they disagree
- BC provincial review: about 40,000 in 1987, rising to more than 113,000 by 2011 (80%, search preview).
- Numbers dropped after the severe 1996 to 1997 winter, then recovered and spread (80%).
- Kootenay buck harvest and hunter success were lowest in 1997 and peaked in 2015 (75%, BC white tail questions and answers, 2017, search preview).
- BC conservation status record: 2022 provincial estimate 35,000 to 71,500 (75%, search preview).
- These two figures are far apart and no source explains why. Treat the true number as uncertain (VERIFY).
- Near you: MU (Management Unit) 3-27 (Heffley Creek) residents killed 116, 97, 111, 145 and 133 white tails in 2020 to 2024, from about 600 to 790 hunters a year (90%, harvest statistics).
- Hunter success in MU 3-27, 2024: about 17% (computed from 133 kills and 771 hunters) (90%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mwt-numbers-card
```

- **Rhyme:** "Brown on top, white below; flag goes up, watch it go."
- **Number trick:** 1 beam, many tines. 2 deer in Region 3 (99%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Body first, antlers last."

```more more hooks, mistakes and field drill
- **Rhyme:** "Forks for mulies, combs for white; rope tail black, wide tail white."
- **Number trick:** 40, 113, 35 to 71: thousands of BC white tails, 1987, 2011, 2022.
- **Old hand's rule** (widely taught hunter wisdom, opinion): "If you have to ask if it is legal, it is not." Echoes hunter education advice to be certain of your target.
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "A flag is a goodbye, not a target." Matches the quick lesson's rule on running deer.

> [!mistake]
> - Calling a deer from one feature, like the tail, when it is half hidden.
> - Judging age by antlers alone.
> - Forgetting a button buck is antlerless and only legal in an antlerless season.

> [!field]
> Next drive along the North Thompson: name every deer you see by tail, run and antlers. Say it out loud before you lift the binoculars.
```
