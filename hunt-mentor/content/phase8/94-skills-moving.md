> [!why]

## Still hunting: the pace

```anim hf-step-cadence Two or three steps, then a long look.
```

- Two or three steps, then stand and look 20 seconds or more (Tip).
- A few hundred metres an hour is normal (Tip).

```more pace in detail
- Look much more than you walk (65%, [Moving quietly](#/s/still-hunting)).
- Look near as well as far: a bedded deer may be very close (65%).
- Before each step, pick where the foot goes next (Tip).
- People walk at a steady beat. Deer step, stop, feed, step again. Break the rhythm (Tip).
- Snap a stick? Stop and wait a few minutes. One crack can pass for an animal; a steady series cannot (Tip).
- Best days: wet ground after rain, soft new snow, a breeze in the trees (Tip).
- Grandpa's rule (wisdom): "If you think you are going slow enough, halve it."
```

## Each quiet step

```steps The fox walk
fn-ft-fox-1 | Weight on the back foot. Lift the front.
fn-ft-fox-2 | Outside edge of the ball down, lightly.
fn-ft-fox-3 | Roll in, then lower the heel.
fn-ft-fox-4 | Quiet? Shift your weight. Crack? Lift and place again.
```

- Weight stays back until the new foot is quiet (65%).
- Heel first to roam, edge first to close (Tip).

```more footwork in detail
- The fox walk lets you feel the ground and slowly compress leaves and sticks (65%, Princeton outdoor action guide via [Stalking footwork](#/s/stalking-footwork)).
- Edge first is quieter but very slow (60%, forum); use it inside about 200 m of the animal (Tip).
- Short strides keep your balance over the back foot, so you can freeze on one leg (Tip).
- Over logs, never on them: dry bark cracks (Tip).
- "A good stalker will take a long time to silently move 50 metres" (65%, Camp Kawartha via [Stalking footwork](#/s/stalking-footwork)).
```

## Ground decides your speed

```diagram mc-skills-ground-noise
```

- Wet moss, soft snow, packed trails: quiet. Dry leaves, crust, gravel: loud (Tip).
- Loud ground? Go around it or wait for wind (Tip).

```more ground by ground
| Ground | Do this (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Dry leaves | Edge first, slowest, or go around |
| Crusty snow | Wait for midday softening or wind |
| Soft new snow | Still hunt it: quiet |
| Gravel and shale | Step on larger, stable rocks |
| Wet moss, grass | Quietest: use it |
| Game trails | Packed and quiet, but deer watch them |

- Deer hear about 0.25 to 30 kHz in one study, best at 4 to 8 kHz; people hear to about 20 kHz (75%, [Moving quietly](#/s/still-hunting)).
- Sharp, high sounds give you away: zippers, Velcro, nylon scrape, metal on metal (Tip).
- Jump up and down at the truck. Fix anything that rattles (Tip).
```

## The stalk: use dead ground

```diagram mc-skills-dead-ground
```

- Fast where it cannot see you, slow where it can (Tip).
- Dead ground: low ground the animal cannot see into (Tip).

```more the stalk in detail
- Plan from where you glassed: two landmarks near the animal, the wind, and a route through dead ground (Tip, [Stalking footwork](#/s/stalking-footwork)).
- Check What can I see on the [Hunt Map](#/map): stand where the animal is and see what it can see (99%, app tool).
- Stalking is "a chess game", alternating fast and slow movement (65%, Princeton guide).
- Landmarks look different up close. Look back often and take a phone photo of the view from the glassing seat (Tip).
- Crawl the last metres over a crest: rifle first, body after, muzzle clear of dirt (Tip). Check the barrel for snow and mud after any crawl (Tip).
- Peek around a rock or tree, not over it (Tip).
```

```steps A stalk, start to finish
sa-stalk-1 | Glass from high ground at first light.
sa-stalk-2 | Pick 2 landmarks near the deer.
sa-stalk-3 | Check the wind before every move.
sa-stalk-4 | Close in out of sight, behind cover.
sa-stalk-5 | Get a rest. Wait for broadside.
```

## The six S's of camouflage

```steps Shape, shine, shadow, silhouette, spacing, movement
fn-cam-s1 | Shape: break up your outline with brush.
fn-cam-s2 | Shine: kill the flash from face, glasses and gun.
fn-cam-s3 | Shadow: sit in shade, watch where yours falls.
fn-cam-s4 | Silhouette: never on the skyline.
fn-cam-s5 | Spacing: two hunters, one shape at a time.
fn-cam-s6 | Movement: head down, move. Head up, freeze.
```

- The words come from military concealment teaching (70%).
- Movement is the big one (75%).

```more the six in detail
- The Australian Army lists shape, shine, shadow and silhouette among its "Seven S's of Tactical Concealment"; older guides add spacing and movement (70%, via [Stay unseen](#/s/stay-unseen)). Using them for hunting is Tip.
- Deer have "an amazing ability to detect movement" (75%, National Deer Association).
- "The actual color of the fabric is relatively unimportant as long as the pattern blends with your surroundings" (75%, National Deer Association).
- Sit in front of something bigger than you, in shade, with a little brush in front (Tip).
- Cross ridges at a low spot; glass from below the crest (Tip).
```

## What deer see

```diagram mc-skills-colour-see
```

- Two colour cones, no red one: blue pops, orange looks dull (75%).
- They catch motion better than detail (75%).

```more deer vision in detail
- Deer have two cone types and lack the long wavelength red cone (75%, National Deer Association on the University of Georgia work).
- They likely tell blue from red, but not green from red or orange from red (75%). Blue jeans are a bad idea (75%).
- More rods than people: better night vision, poorer daytime colour vision (75%).
- Deer lack the UV (ultraviolet) filter of the human lens and likely see UV better; evidence that UV bright clothing spooks them is weak (60 to 70%).
- Mule deer were not in these studies. Assumption: similar, as both are deer (Tip).
- Orange looks dull to a deer but bright to other hunters. Wear it walking in and out (Tip). BC has no blaze orange rule in the Rule Book (85%, my reading, [Stay unseen](#/s/stay-unseen)).
- Each species' eyes: chapter 3 of its Masterclass, for example [Mule deer senses](#/s/mc-mule-deer-03).
```

## Seen? Freeze

```anim mc-skills-freeze Head down: move. Head up: freeze until it feeds again.
```

- Head down or behind a tree: move. Head up: freeze (Tip).
- Hold the freeze longer than it does (Tip).

```more freezing in detail
- Freeze mid step if you have to. That is why the weight stays back (Tip, [Stalking footwork](#/s/stalking-footwork)).
- Do not blink fast, turn your face, or lift binoculars while it stares (Tip).
- A stare that ends with a head bob or a stamp is a test; stay frozen (Tip).
- Train it: your partner calls "freeze" at random, hold 60 seconds without a wobble (Tip, home drill).
- An animal that has seen you and walks off stiff legged is gone. Let it go and plan again (Tip).
```

## Two hunters, one shape

```diagram mc-skills-hand-signals
```

- One moves, one watches. Then swap (Tip).
- Hand signals, not voices (Tip).

```more moving as two
| Signal | Meaning (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Flat hand up | Stop, freeze |
| Point | Game, that way |
| Palm pushed down | Get low |
| Circling wave toward you | Come to me |
| Thumb up | I see you, all OK |
| Arms crossed over head | Done, unloaded, coming in |

- Signals from [Hunting with a partner](#/s/hunting-partner) (Tip).
- Agree them at the truck, with zones of fire ([Chapter 10](#/s/mc-skills-10)).
- The watcher keeps eyes on the animal and signals when its head comes up (Tip).
```

## What a 40 year hunter does differently

| Beginner | Old hand (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Walks at hiking pace | Stands more than walks |
| Steps then looks | Looks, picks a spot, then steps |
| Crosses ridges on top | Crosses at the low point |
| Moves while it stares | Freezes longer than it does |
| Wears new nylon | Wears quiet wool or fleece |

- All lines are hunter wisdom, labelled Tip.

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-skills-still-vs-stalk
```

- **Rhyme:** "Head down, go to town. Head up, shut up."
- **Number trick:** 2 or 3 steps, 20 seconds of looking (Tip).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, the Grandpa's rule in [Stalking footwork](#/s/stalking-footwork)): "Your feet should ask the ground a question before your weight gives the answer."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Fast in the dead ground, slow in the sight line."
- **Rhyme:** "Blue is a beacon, orange is grey" (75%).
- **Number trick:** 6 S's: shape, shine, shadow, silhouette, spacing, movement (70%).
- **Number trick:** 50 m can take a stalker a long, long time (65%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, the Grandpa's rule in [Stay unseen](#/s/stay-unseen)): "Camo hides you when you are still. Nothing hides you when you move at the wrong time."

> [!mistake]
> - Hiking pace, called still hunting.
> - Standing on the skyline to glass.
> - Moving while the animal stares.

> [!field]
> Still hunt 200 m of timber in 30 minutes or more. Your partner follows 30 m back and holds up a finger for every noise. Beat your score each trip.
```
