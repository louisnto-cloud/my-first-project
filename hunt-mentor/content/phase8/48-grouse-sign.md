> [!why]
> Grouse leave small clues everywhere they live. Read them and you hunt where birds are today, not where they were last year.

```diagram mgr-track-grid
```

- Three long toes forward, one short toe back, about 5 cm (75%).
- One line, one print in front of the next (75 to 80%).

```more tracks in detail
- Forest grouse tracks are about 5 cm long: three forward toes and a short hind toe (75%, Elbroch and Marks, Bird Tracks and Sign).
- In winter ruffed grouse grow comb like toe edges (pectinations), so prints look wider and fringed (85%).
- Ptarmigan feet are feathered; in snow the print can look fuzzy, with toes hard to see (Tip, 65%).
- A walking grouse leaves a line of prints, one ahead of the next, gently zig zag (80%).
- Where tracks end in a spray of wing marks, the bird flew (75%).
- Hare tracks look very different: see the diagram below.
```

## Droppings

```diagram mgr-droppings
```

- Fibrous, curved, pencil thick, white tipped (85%).
- A pile means a roost or a drum log (75 to 85%).

```more what droppings tell you
- Normal droppings: long curved cylinders about pencil thick, fibrous and pale tan, with white uric acid (85%, Hinterland Who's Who). Often 2.5 to 4 cm long (70%, Northern Woodlands).
- Caecal droppings: soft, dark, tar like, from the ceca, passed less often (70%, Elbroch and Marks via Laura Erickson).
- Fresh caecal droppings are green and turn black quickly in the sun; they break down in days to weeks (65%, Wyoming Game and Fish, sage grouse).
- Pile under a tree: night roost (75%). Pile on one spot of a log: drum stand (85%).
- What is in them: needles mean conifer feeding, berry seeds mean a berry patch (Tip).
```

## Dust bowls, feathers and feeding sign

```diagram mgr-dust-bowl
```

- Shallow scrapes in dry dirt, a few feathers (80%).
- Clipped clover and dropped berries on an edge at dawn (Tip).

```more more sign
- Dust bowls sit in dry, loose dirt on road edges, banks and root holes (80%).
- Single small body feathers near a bowl or under a roost tree are normal moult or preening (Tip).
- A burst of feathers with no bird: a hawk or owl kill (Tip). Goshawks and great horned owls take adult ruffed grouse (85%).
- Feeding sign: clover leaves clipped short, kinnikinnick or rose hips missing from a patch, aspen buds snipped in winter (Tip).
- Bits knocked from trees onto snow show birds feeding overhead (75%, Canadian Wildlife Federation).
```

## Snow roosts

```anim mgr-snow-dive Dive in at dusk, burst out at dawn.
```

```gallery

hab-grouse-wing-prints | Wing prints where a grouse took off.
```

- Hole, short tunnel, second hole with wing marks (75 to 80%).
- Wing marks on both sides: the bird has left (75%).

```more reading a roost
- Grouse fly straight into soft snow, so there may be no tracks leading in (75%).
- Inside: a small chamber, often with a pile of droppings from the night (75%).
- Exit hole with wing prints on the snow beside it: the bird burst out (75%).
- An entry hole with no exit: the bird may still be inside. Be ready (Tip, 60%).
- Snow roost sign needs soft snow about 20 cm or deeper (70 to 75%).
```

## The drum log

```diagram mgr-drum-log
```

- Big mossy log, open floor, cover overhead, droppings on one spot (85%).
- A spring drum log means a male lives here year round (85%).

```more using drum logs
- The drum stand is often a big moss covered log at a forest edge (85%). Stumps, boulders and mounds are used too (75%).
- A Minnesota study recorded at least 593 logs used by drumming males (75%).
- The male holds that territory for life (85%). Find his log in April, hunt that patch in October (Tip).
- Do not disturb drumming birds in spring: no season then, and it is the breeding time (99% no season; respect Tip).
```

## How fresh?

| Sign | Fresh | Old |
|---|---|---|
| Track in snow | Sharp edges, toe detail | Rounded, melted |
| Normal dropping | Moist, shiny white tip | Dry, faded |
| Caecal dropping | Green | Black, crusted |
| Roost hole | Crisp, no new snow | Filled, soft |

- Tip: new snow overnight resets the clock. Any track on top is from today.

```more detail
- Track ageing is general tracking practice (Tip, 60 to 70%).
- Caecal droppings green to black quickly (65%).
- Wind and sun round track edges within hours on a warm day (Tip).
- Write the time and weather in your Journal next to each find (Tip).
```

## How to follow

```anim mgr-track-follow Slow down near cover. The bird is close.
```

- Walk beside the line, not on it (Tip).
- Slow down as tracks near cover (Tip).

```more following a grouse track line
- Fresh tracks in new snow mean the bird is close, often within 50 m (Tip).
- Walk beside the tracks so you can read them again if you lose the line (Tip).
- Tracks wander and stop under a conifer: look up (Tip).
- Tracks end in a burst of wing marks: it flew. Look ahead for the next tree it may sit in (Tip).
- Gun ready, finger off the trigger, safety on until you shoulder (Tip; safety habit).
- See also [Tracks](#/s/tracks) and [Snow tracking](#/s/snow-tracking).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mgr-track-grid
```

- Rhyme: "Three toes ahead and one behind, a grouse's line is what you'll find."
- Number trick: "5 cm and 50 m": track length, and how close the bird often is.
- Old hand's rule (tradition): "Fresh track, slow feet."

```more more hooks
- "Pile on a log, drummer in the bog."
- "Hole and wings, the bird has gone; hole alone, the bird sits on."
- "Green goo new, black goo old."
- Hooks are made for this course; slowing down on a fresh track is widely repeated advice.
```
