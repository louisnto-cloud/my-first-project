## Dry fire, the safe way

```diagram tk-dryfire
```

- Snap cap in the chamber cushions the firing pin (95%).
- Dummy rounds are for loading drills only (95%).

```more the manual's words and the routine
- "Long term dry firing may damage the bolt" (95%, Tikka manual page 9).
- "Insert an appropriate snap cap into the chamber to cushion the fall of the firing pin and reduce the chance of firing pin breakage. Snap cap cartridges with spring loaded primers are ideal. DUMMY cartridges with empty primer pockets are good for loading and unloading practice but DO NOT protect the firing pin during dry firing" (95%, page 9).
- Routine (Tip): [[PROVE]] the rifle unloaded; live ammunition in another room; snap cap in; aim at a safe backstop (a basement wall, never a person or a window); then 20 slow presses.
- Lifting the bolt cocks the pin. Each dry press needs a bolt lift and close (95%, page 7).
```

## The two stage trigger

```diagram tk-follow-up
```

- Take up the light first stage, then a firm wall, then press (Tip).
- Factory pull about 3 lb. Leave it (95%).

```more trigger technique
- Sako lists a two stage trigger on the Arctic (95%). The all4shooters test measured a crisp break at 2.3 lb (1400 g) on their rifle (75%).
- Technique (Tip, widely taught): pad of the finger on the blade; take up the slack to the wall; let the sights settle; keep pressing straight back until it breaks; hold the press through the recoil (follow through).
- Watch the front post or crosshair at the moment it breaks. If it jumped, you jerked (Tip).
- Adjusting the trigger is in [chapter 7](#/s/mc-tikka-07): factory range 11 to 18 N, never below 11 N (95%).
```

## Four field positions

```diagram tk-positions
```

- Prone off a pack is steadiest. Standing free is the last choice (Tip).
- Lower and more contact means steadier (Tip).

```more Tikka notes for each position
- **Prone off a pack:** rest the fore end, not the barrel. Your Arctic barrel is free floating; pressure on it can move the shot (Tip).
- **Sitting with sticks:** the most useful position in Fraser Valley cutblocks and timber edges (Tip). See [field positions](#/s/field-positions/0).
- **Kneeling:** elbow in front of the knee cap, not on it (Tip).
- **Standing off a tree:** hand between the tree and the fore end, never the rifle against the bark (Tip).
- The front sling stud can also hold a bipod (95%, Tikka manual page 16). All Tikkas have standard studs under the fore end and the butt (95%).
- Bipod or not: see [chapter 9](#/s/mc-tikka-09).
```

## A week of drills

```diagram tk-drill-week
```

- 15 minutes, 4 days a week (Tip).
- Snap caps and dummy rounds only at home (95%).

```more the drills in full
1. **Bolt cycles:** 20 full strokes from the shoulder, cheek on the stock, eyes on a target. The cure for short stroking (Tip).
2. **Load and unload:** 5 full cycles with dummy rounds, saying each step (chapter 2).
3. **Dry fire:** 20 slow presses with a snap cap, eyes on the post or crosshair (95% snap cap rule; routine Tip).
4. **Position changes:** walk 5 steps, sticks up, sit, aim: 10 times. Time it (Tip).
5. **Safety by feel:** ON, OFF, ON with your hunting gloves. The Arctic's big trigger guard was made for gloves (75%, all4shooters).
- Log every session in the journal (Tip).
```

```checklist tk-drill-card
Proved unloaded, ammunition in another room
20 bolt cycles from the shoulder
5 load and unload cycles with dummies
20 dry presses with a snap cap
10 walk, sit, aim position changes
Safety ON and OFF with gloves
```

## The plate test: your real range

```diagram tk-plate-test
```

- Your limit = the last distance with 5 of 5 on a paper plate (Tip).
- Test each position. Write it on your card (Tip).

```more how to run it
- At the range: a paper plate (about 20 cm) at 50, 100, 150 and 200 m (Tip; see [My max ethical range test](#/s/shooting-skills/0)).
- From each position, 5 shots, cold, no sighters (Tip).
- 5 of 5 = that distance is in your range from that position. 4 of 5 = practise more (Tip).
- The numbers in the picture are an example, not your score.
- Iron sights: expect shorter limits than with a scope (Tip).
```

## Follow up shot

```diagram tk-follow-up
```

- Cheek down, eye on the deer, run the bolt (Tip).
- Safety ON if you do not shoot again (95%).

```more why and how
- After the shot, a deer can stand or walk. A practised second round can end it fast (Tip).
- "If you do not plan to fire a second shot, engage the safety (red dot not visible) keeping the firearm pointed in a safe direction" (95%, Tikka manual page 10).
- Then watch, mark the spot, wait. See [After the shot](#/s/after-the-shot/0) (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram tk-dryfire
```

- **"Snap to practise, dummy to load."**
- **"Low and touching."** Lower and more contact is steadier.
- **"Five of five or don't."** The plate test sets your range.

```more mistakes, rule and the field task
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "Practise until you can't get it wrong, not until you get it right."

> [!mistake]
> - Dry firing with a dummy round and breaking a firing pin.
> - Resting the barrel, not the fore end, on a pack or tree.
> - Practising only from the bench, then shooting a deer off sticks.

> [!field]
> This week: 4 sessions of the drill card. At the next range day, run the plate test from sitting and kneeling.
```
