## 1. Dark and weather come early in fall

```diagram mc-nav-light
```

- Legal light for big game ends 1 hour after sunset; that last hour is mostly dark (99%; 85%).
- Interior October can go from sun to sleet in an afternoon (Tip).

```more dark and weather in full
- Legal hours for deer and grouse: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (99%, [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)).
- Heffley Creek legal light ends (85%, my calculation, BC time): about 19:08 on 15 October, 18:48 on 25 October, 18:37 on 31 October, 18:14 on 15 November.
- BC no longer falls back: clocks stay on daylight time after March 2026 (95%, BC government release via [Weather, daylight and season timing](#/s/daylight-weather-bc)). 
- Civil twilight ends about 33 minutes after sunset in October (85%). After that you need a headlamp in timber.
- Kamloops Airport November average low minus 1.1°C; higher ground is colder (75%).
- Plan: a turnaround time, a headlamp and a second light, a dry layer and toque every trip (Tip, [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety)).
- Weather signs and forecasts: [Reading weather in the field](#/s/field-weather).
```

## 2. Cliffs and steep slopes

```diagram mc-nav-slope-classes
```

- Hunt Map Slope steepness: red is 30 to 45 degrees, purple over 45 (99%).
- Most avalanches start on 30 to 45 degree slopes (95%).

```more steep ground in full
- Slope classes in the Hunt Map: 15 to 25 steady climb, 25 to 30 steep, 30 to 45 very steep and avalanche terrain in snow, over 45 cliffy (99%, app).
- Terrain points are about 24 m apart, so small cliffs and cut banks may not show (99%, app). A clean map is not proof of safe ground (Tip).
- Contours touching or merging mean a cliff (95%, [Chapter 3](#/s/mc-nav-03)).
- BCSARA's number 1 worst thing: wrong footwear, causing slips, trips, falls and broken bones (95%).
- Steep descent with a meat pack is slow and risky (Tip, [Planning a route](#/s/route-planning)). Go around on a contour or through a saddle (Tip).
- Snow on 30 degree slopes: check Avalanche Canada first ([Avalanche awareness](#/s/avalanche-hunters)).
- Never shoot an animal standing above a cliff you cannot recover it from (Tip; retrieval rule: [Rule Book: unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts)).
```

## 3. Water and ice crossings

```diagram fs-ice-thickness
```

- Clear blue ice: 15 cm to walk alone (75%). Ice on moving water cannot be trusted (75%).
- Creek crossing: look for a wider, slower, shallower spot (Tip).

```more water and ice in full
- Canadian Red Cross: at least 15 cm of clear blue ice to walk alone, 20 cm for a group (75%, [Ice and cold water](#/s/ice-cold-water)).
- White ice is about half as strong; grey ice is unsafe (75%).
- Waterfowl keep patches open, so ice near birds can be very thin (70%).
- A downed bird or a dog on thin ice is a common start to hunting accidents (70%). Walking onto unsafe ice is not a reasonable effort (Tip, VERIFY with a conservation officer).
- Creek crossing habits (Tip): scout up and down for a wide, slow, shallow riffle; unbuckle the pack hip belt; face upstream; use a stick as a third leg; unload the firearm; never cross above a log jam or a drop.
- Fell through ice: turn back the way you came, arms on the ice, kick, slide out, roll away (75%, Minnesota Department of Natural Resources).
```

## 4. The 10 ft boat and the kayak

```diagram mc-nav-boat
```

- Cold water: about 1 minute of gasp, 10 of useful movement, 1 hour to unconscious (80%).
- PFD (personal flotation device) on and zipped (Tip; carrying one is the law, 99%).

```more boats and kayaks in full
- The 1 10 1 principle: about 1 minute of cold shock, about 10 minutes of useful movement, about 1 hour before hypothermia makes you unconscious. A name, not exact timing (80%, Giesbrecht, via [Water safety](#/s/water-safety)).
- The first gasp is the killer if your head is under (75%).
- Canadian law requires an approved PFD of the right size on board for each person; an inflatable must be worn in an open boat (99%, [Water safety](#/s/water-safety)).
- About 87% of boaters who drowned were not wearing one (75%, Lifesaving Society).
- A motor boat needs the operator's proof of competency on board (99%).
- While the motor pushes the boat, every gun is unloaded (95%). Ducks and geese: no hunting from a moving boat with a motor or sail (99%). Full rules: [Water safety](#/s/water-safety) and [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods).
- Capsized: float, control breathing, stay with the boat, climb onto the hull (75%).
- Small boat and kayak habits (Tip): load low and centred, no standing to shoot from a kayak, trip plan that names the lake, a dry bag with phone, layers and light.
```

## 5. The ATV

```diagram gr-atv-hills
```

- In BC, ATV deaths average almost 13 a year; about half involve rollovers (60%).
- Helmet every ride on Crown land (98%). Never a loaded firearm on it (99%).

```more ATVs in full
- An average of almost 13 people a year died in BC ATV incidents; about half involved rollovers (60%, CBC report, search preview).
- BC Coroners Service data, 2009 to 2018: most deaths in the Interior (46.5%); alcohol or drugs a factor in about half (65%, news summary via search preview).
- Helmet on every rider and passenger on Crown land (98%, [ATV rules](#/s/atv-rules)).
- On a Forest Service Road: valid driver's licence and at least $200,000 third party liability insurance (98%).
- A loaded firearm in or on a motor vehicle is unlawful; an ATV is a motor vehicle; a loaded magazine attached counts as loaded (98 to 99%, [ATV rules](#/s/atv-rules)).
- Never hunt from the seat or the rack (99%).
- Riding habits (Tip): cross slopes straight up or down, not across; walk the steep bit first; ride with a partner; tell the trip plan where the quad will be.
- Lights on from half an hour after sunset (98%).
```

## 6. Wildlife: bears, cougars, moose

```diagram mc-nav-wildlife-grid
```

- Bear and cougar: never run (75%). Moose: run and put a tree between you (85%).
- Bear spray on the hip or chest, not in the pack (75%).

```more wildlife in full
- Bear: stop, stay calm, never run; spray when it is within about 5 to 10 m (75%, WildSafeBC, [Bears and cougars](#/s/bear-cougar-encounters)).
- Defensive attack with contact: lie on your stomach, hands over the neck. Predatory attack: fight back hard (75%).
- Cougar: never run, look big, back away slowly; if attacked, always fight back (75%, WildSafeBC).
- Moose: running is usually a good idea; they won't chase far; get a tree between you (85%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game, via [Moose Masterclass 4](#/s/mc-moose-04)).
- Warning signs of a moose charge: ears pinned back, hackles raised, head lowered, snorting (75%, Colorado and Manitoba agencies, search preview).
- Gut piles draw bears: move meat at least 200 m away (70%).
- Report dangerous bears or cougars: RAPP (Report All Poachers and Polluters) 1 877 952 7277 (99%, synopsis page 2).
```

## 7. Firearms and zones of fire

```diagram zones-of-fire
```

- Two hunters: each shoots only in about 45 degrees straight ahead (75%).
- Lose sight of each other: muzzles safe, no shooting, call out (75%).

```more firearm risk in full
- ACTS and PROVE every time you handle a firearm (85%, [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety)).
- Zones of fire, walking side by side in sight; the middle and behind are no shot zones (75%, [Hunting with a partner](#/s/hunting-partner)).
- Lost or navigating in the dark: unload before you start the walk out (Tip); hunting after legal light is unlawful (99%).
- Fence crossing, falls and crawls: unload, PROVE, pass it under, check the barrel for snow or mud (75%, [Hunting Skills Masterclass 10](#/s/mc-skills-10)).
- BC has no blaze orange rule; my lean is orange hat and vest when two or more (95% for the law; Tip).
```

## 8. Fatigue, judgement and the turnaround

```diagram mc-nav-turnaround
```

- Tired, cold, hungry people make the FACETS mistakes (Tip, [Chapter 1](#/s/mc-nav-01)).
- Set the turnaround at home and keep it on the hill (Tip).

```more judgement in full
- North Shore Rescue: "Know Your Route and Limits: set a clear turnaround time" (75%).
- Turnaround = when you must be at the truck, minus the walk out, minus a buffer of about 30 minutes (Tip, [Planning a route](#/s/route-planning)).
- Scarcity trap: "last hour of the last day" pushes risk up (80% for the trap, avalanche.org; Tip for the hunting example).
- Eat and drink on a schedule; low blood sugar and cold dull judgement (Tip).
- Long days: fitness lowers risk on the pack out ([Fitness for hunting](#/s/hunt-fitness)).
- A shot late in the day means a dark walk with meat. Plan it before you shoot (Tip, [Night navigation](#/s/night-navigation)).
```

## What a 40 year hunter does differently

| Beginner | Old hand (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Hunts till the last legal minute far from the truck | Is near the truck when light goes |
| Shortcuts down a draw | Takes the long known way |
| Rides the quad loaded | Unloads before the quad, every time |
| PFD on the seat | PFD on and zipped |
| "It won't happen today" | "What if it does?" |

- All lines are hunter wisdom, labelled Tip; the rules behind them are linked above.

```more where these come from
- Near the truck at last light: [Night navigation](#/s/night-navigation) (Tip).
- Long known way: North Shore Rescue on ravines (75%).
- Unloaded on vehicles: synopsis page 11, item 28 (99%).
- PFD worn: Lifesaving Society drowning data (75%).
- "What if": BCSARA top 10, number 6 (95%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-nav-risk-wheel
```

- **Rhyme:** "Sunset plus sixty, dark and risky."
- **Number trick:** 1 10 1: 1 minute gasp, 10 minutes to act, 1 hour (80%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, short form of BCSARA's "not thinking what if"): "Ask what if before it asks you."

```more more hooks
- **Number trick:** 30 to 45: red slopes, avalanche angles (95 to 99%).
- **Number trick:** 15 cm of clear blue ice to walk alone (75%).
- **Number trick:** 45 degrees each: zone of fire (75%).
- **Rhyme:** "Bear and cat: stand. Moose: run and land behind a tree."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, [Water safety](#/s/water-safety) lean): "The PFD only works if you wear it."

> [!mistake]
> - Walking out down a dark draw to save time.
> - A loaded magazine clipped in on the quad.
> - The kayak in November with the PFD under the seat.

> [!field]
> Before each trip, read the eight risks on the wheel and say one sentence for each: what could happen today, and what you will do about it. Put the answers in the trip plan.
```
