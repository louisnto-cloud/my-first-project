## Tracks

```diagram mc-black-bear-tracks
```

- Front about 11 x 10 cm, hind about 18 x 9 cm (65%).
- Hind print looks like a wide human foot (65%).

```gallery
hab-bear-track-front | Front track: wide pad, 5 toes.
hab-bear-track-mud | Hind track: like a flat human foot.
```

```more tracks in detail
- Sizes from outdoor track guides, via the quick lesson (65%): front about 11 cm long by 10 cm wide; hind about 18 cm by 9 cm. Big boars run larger, cubs much smaller (Tip).
- Five toes on every foot; the small inner toe does not always print (85%, Idaho Fish and Game: "always look for all five toes").
- Bears walk with a pigeon toed shuffle; the hind foot often lands on or near the front print (Tip, tracking guides).
- Bears step in the same prints on old trails, wearing a line of dents (90%, BC account).
- Measure in the track floor, not the splayed rim (Tip, [track measuring](#/s/rs-track-measuring)).
```

## Black bear or grizzly: the track test

```diagram mc-black-bear-track-test
```

- Straight edge under the toes cuts them: black bear (80%).
- Toes above the line, claws far ahead: grizzly (80%).

```diagram sg-bear-toeline
```

```more the test in detail
- Idaho Fish and Game (80%): lay a straight edge across the front of the front foot pad. Grizzly: it will not cross the toes. Black bear: the foot is more rounded, so it crosses the toes.
- Middle claw marks: grizzly 5 to 11.4 cm (2 to 4.5 in) ahead of the toe pads; black bear 2.5 to 6.4 cm (1 to 2.5 in) (80%).
- Snow melt can change a track's shape; look for clean prints (85%).
- A grizzly track in your hunting area is a reason to be extra careful with ID (identification) and with your kill site (Tip).
```

## Scat

```diagram mc-black-bear-scat
```

- Scat shows what bears eat this week (Tip).
- Berry scat on a trail: a ripe patch is near (Tip).

```gallery
hab-bear-scat-berries | Berry filled scat, pen for scale.
hab-bear-scat-okanagan | Seeds and skins, Kelowna trail.
```

```more scat in detail
- A thick cylinder or loose pile, full of berries, seeds, grass or hair (65%, quick lesson).
- Spring scat is grassy and fibrous; fall scat is often loose and full of berries (Tip, from the seasonal diet in chapter 7).
- Bear droppings spread berry seeds and help shrubs grow (90%, BC account).
- Pick apart old scat with a stick, never your hands (Tip).
```

## Logs, rocks and anthills

```gallery
hab-bear-claw-aspen | Claw marks on aspen: a bear climbed.
```

- Torn logs, flipped rocks, dug anthills: a feeding bear (85%).
- Bark stripped for the sap layer in spring (90%).

```video
yt-bear-sign | Bear sign: scat, torn logs, tracks, rubbed trees.
```

```more feeding sign in detail
- Alberta's bear guide for hunters: "fresh signs that indicate recent bear activity: tracks, scat, overturned logs or dug up anthills" (85%, via [predator sign](#/s/rs-predator-sign)).
- Cambium feeding: bears strip bark to eat the soft layer underneath, sometimes killing trees (90%, BC account).
- Birds (ravens, magpies, crows, jays) gathering in one spot: maybe a carcass that drew a bear (85%).
```

## Rub trees and claw marks

```video
commons-black-bear-marking | A black bear rubbing and marking trees.
```

- Rub trees: hair stuck in the bark, often by a trail (90%).
- Bites and claw marks at head height: marking (90%).

```gallery
ts-bear-rub-tree | Rub tree: bark worn off at bear height.
ts-bear-rub-jasper | Scat on the trail, rubbed trunks behind.
```

```more marking in detail
- BC account (90%): claw and bite marks where a bear "marked the trunk with scent during mating season or chewed at the bark to get at the cambium"; bears "frequently use rubbing trees, either as a scratching post or to mark their home range".
- Boar behaviour includes rubbing on trees and breaking saplings (65%, Boone and Crockett, Jim Shockey).
- Hair colour on a rub can tell you a bear's coat colour, not its species (Tip, chapter 1).
- A trail camera on a rub tree shows which bears use the area (Tip).
```

## Beds, trails and kill sites

```diagram rs-pr-bear-cache
```

- Bears may cover a kill with debris and bed close by (85%).
- Smell of rot plus birds plus torn ground: back away (85%).

```more beds, trails and kills in detail
- Day beds: shallow scrapes in cover or at the foot of a big tree, close to food (Tip).
- Trails: worn paths where bears step in the same prints, used year after year (90%, BC account).
- Bear caches: ground debris raked over the prey; ground "as if it was roto tilled"; a bear may bed close by and defend it (85%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife, via [predator sign](#/s/rs-predator-sign)).
- Found a cache? Leave the way you came, watch behind you, and report bears near people (75%, [bear safety](#/s/bear-cougar-encounters)).
```

## How fresh is it?

```diagram mc-black-bear-fresh
```

- Wet, shiny, smelly scat: hours (Tip).
- Sharp track edges, wet sap on claw marks: very fresh (Tip).

```more freshness in detail
- Freshness reading is a tracker's judgement, not measured fact (Tip, [track aging](#/s/rs-track-aging)).
- Compare with your own boot print made now, beside the track (Tip).
- Dew, frost and rain on top of a track mean it was made before them (Tip).
- In snow, a fresh track has crisp edges and no snow in the bottom (Tip, [snow tracking](#/s/snow-tracking)).
```

## How to follow a bear

```steps Follow a bear trail
anim:sk-trail-beside | Walk beside the trail, not on it.
anim:sk-grid-search | Lost the sign? Grid search from the last one.
fs-bc-kill-1 | Talk, keep spray and rifle in reach.
```

- Look ahead, not down. Glass every opening (Tip).
- Wind in your face, always (Tip).

```more following in detail
- Mark the last good sign with flagging; work forward in short loops (Tip, [track following](#/s/rs-track-following)).
- A trail toward thick cover in the middle of the day leads to a bed: slow down, glass, think about wind (Tip).
- Following an unwounded bear into thick brush is a safety risk; most hunters glass and wait for it to come out to feed instead (Tip).
- Following a wounded bear: chapter 10.
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-black-bear-track-test
```

- **Rhyme:** "Toes in a curve, a black bear's print; toes in a row, a grizzly's hint."
- **Number trick:** 11 and 18: front and hind track length in cm (65%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, common tracking advice): "Track with your eyes ahead."

```more more hooks
- **Number trick:** 1 to 2.5 inches: black bear claw marks ahead of the toes; 2 to 4.5 for a grizzly (80%).
- **Rhyme:** "Berries in the scat, berries on the bush, bear on the slope."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, Alberta bear guidance): "Ravens in one spot: think carcass, think bear."

> [!mistake]
> - Measuring the rim of a soft track.
> - Calling a big black bear track a grizzly, or the reverse, without the straight edge test.
> - Walking into thick cover after fresh sign in midday.

> [!field]
> Next trip: photograph one bear track with a straight edge across the pad and a ruler beside it. Log it.
```
