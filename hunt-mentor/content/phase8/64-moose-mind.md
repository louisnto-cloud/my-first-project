> [!why]
> A moose does not think like a deer. It is a calm, solitary giant that stares, walks off, comes to a call and sometimes fights back. Know its habits and its tempers.

## Alarm and flight

```photo g-moose-cow
```

- Often stares, then trots off into thick cover (Tip).
- Runs up to about 55 km/h; swims strongly (75%).

```more alarm and flight in detail
- Escape style (Tip, hunter consensus, [escape terrain](#/s/escape-terrain)): a moose often stands and stares, then trots off steadily into the thickest timber or willow, or into water. It rarely bounds like a mule deer.
- Speed: "Moose can run 35 mph and easily swim 10 miles without stopping" (75%, Minnesota Department of Natural Resources). That is about 55 km/h and 16 km.
- Alaska reports moose apparently swimming about 10 km (six miles) across Clarence Strait (85%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game).
- A trotting moose steps over logs and windfall you must climb around (Tip). You will not catch it. Let it settle.
- Moose spotted from an aircraft may not be hunted on that tip (99%, [unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts)).
```

## How it learns pressure

```diagram mc-moose-mistakes
```

- Roads and open cutblocks made moose easier to kill (90%).
- Pressured moose stick to cover by day (Tip).

```more pressure in detail
- BC moose research (90%): the "landscape change hypothesis" says beetle salvage logging and new roads made moose more vulnerable "primarily from hunters and predators". Survival should rise when cutblocks grow back enough to block the view and logging roads become impassable.
- BC account (90%): moose need nearby cover "to hide from predators and hunters, for shade in hot weather, and for shelter during blizzards".
- Hunter consensus (Tip): road hunted moose learn to cross roads at dark and feed in openings only at first and last light; a quiet walk in beats a slow drive.
- No moose study on learning and memory of pressure was found: VERIFY detail. Treat the rest as Tip.
- Hunting from a motor vehicle and herding wildlife with a vehicle or boat are unlawful (99%, [unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts)).
```

## Social life

```diagram mc-moose-social
```

```photo bg-moose-cow-calf-bowron
```

- Seasonal home range: seldom over 5 to 10 km² (90%).
- Groups at good food in winter have no social bond (90%).

```more social life in detail
- BC account (90%): "Moose are essentially solitary animals that move about within familiar summer and winter home ranges."
- "Moose do not defend their home range and do not have year round dominance hierarchies like the more social Elk and Bighorn Sheep."
- In northern shrublands they form groups of up to 8 to 10 in the rut; less so in forest (90%).
- Several moose at a mineral lick or great feeding place form a group "but these groups lack a social basis" (90%).
- Unlike other hoofed animals, cows "may be quite aggressive toward one another" and receptive cows choose their mate (90%).
```

## Cow and calf

```gallery
g-moose-cow-calf | Cow and calf: if you see a calf, the cow is close.
g-moose-calf | Spring calf: red brown and gangly.
```

- The bond lasts about one year (90%).
- A cow defends her calf hard (85%).

```video
commons-moose-calf | A calf walking: the cow is never far away.
```

```more cow and calf in detail
- BC account (90%): "The Moose's only lasting social bond is the bond between mother and calf, and it lasts for only a year." Cows protect calves from predators and lead them to the best habitats.
- Pregnant cows seek seclusion in late May and June and chase away last year's young (90%). That is why lone yearlings wander in spring (Tip).
- Calves stay with their mothers 12 to 18 months (75%, Minnesota Department of Natural Resources).
- "A cow moose defends her newborn calf vigorously" (85%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game).
- Black bears can take many newborn calves; wolves kill moose all year (90%, BC account). BC collared calves died mostly from predation: 65% wolf, 9% bear, 7% cougar (90%, BC research report 2022).
- Hunting point: in Regions 3 and 8 the calf and the cow are both closed to you without an LEH (Limited Entry Hunting) draw (99%, chapter 10).
```

## Dangerous moose

```diagram mc-moose-warning
```

- Cows with calves and rutting bulls can attack (85%).
- In Alaska, moose injure more people than bears each year (85%).

```more danger in detail
- Alaska Department of Fish and Game (85%): "In late spring and summer, cow moose with young calves are very protective and will attack humans who come too close." "During the fall mating season in late September and October, termed the rut, bull moose may be aggressive toward humans."
- Hungry, tired moose in deep snow and moose harassed by people, dogs and traffic also turn aggressive (85%).
- "Moose view dogs as enemies and will sometimes go out of their way to kick at one" (85%). Your dog: leashed for moose anyway (99%, page 12, [legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)).
- Warning signs (85%): "The long hairs on its hump are raised, ears laid back (much like a dog or cat), and it may lick its lips." A moose walking slowly toward you is not being friendly.
- Minnesota (75%): moose "are seldom aggressive", except a cow protecting a calf or a bull in the rut.
```

## If a moose charges

```anim mc-moose-charge Run, and put a tree between you.
```

- Run: moose do not chase far (85%).
- Knocked down? Curl up, cover your head, hold still (85%).

```more charges in detail
- Alaska Department of Fish and Game (85%): "Many charges are 'bluff' charges", but take them seriously.
- "Unlike with bears or even dogs, it is usually a good idea to run from a moose because they won't chase you very far." "You can run around a tree faster than a moose."
- If knocked down: "Curl up in a ball, protect your head with your hands, and hold still." Do not get up until the moose has moved off, or it may attack again (85%).
- Note: this is the opposite of bear advice. Do not mix them up (Tip, [black bear](#/s/black-bear)).
- Calling a bull in the rut brings an angry animal toward you on purpose. Keep a tree close and your partner where you both know he is (Tip, chapter 9).
```

## Young bulls and old bulls

```gallery
g-moose-young-bull | Young bull: small antlers, slim neck.
g-moose-bull-side | Mature bull: deep chest, heavy neck, wide palms.
```

- Older bulls do most of the breeding (90%).
- Young bulls wander more and come to calls more freely (Tip).

```more bulls in detail
- BC account (90%): yearling bulls can breed, "but older bulls are responsible for most of the successful breeding".
- Prime bulls of equal size shove with locked antlers; the fights mainly show cows who is best; bulls do not hold a harem (90%).
- Serious fights are rare, but bulls get punctures, broken ribs and sometimes die of wounds (85%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game).
- Body clues (Tip, hunter consensus): an old bull has a deep chest, heavy neck, sagging belly and wide heavy palms; a young bull looks long legged and slim, "a cow with antlers".
- Hunter consensus (Tip): young bulls, beaten off by older ones, wander and answer calls more readily, which suits a spike fork season.
```

## Predictable habits

```diagram mc-moose-mistakes
```

- Stares, answers calls, feeds at the edges at first and last light (Tip).
- Comes back to the same willow (Tip).

```more habits you can plan around
1. **The stare** (60%, hunter lore): gives you seconds to count and settle.
2. **The call** (65%, Outdoor Canada and BC account): in the rut, cows and bulls respond to a cow moan; a bull may walk right in.
3. **The edges** (75%, BC account and app): feeding where willow, water and cover meet, mostly near dawn and dusk.
4. **The same patch** (Tip): moose feed on the same browse for days; fresh sign means come back.
5. **Roads at dusk** (70%, road safety sources): moose cross and lick roads at dusk and at night.
- None of these is guaranteed. A moose in thick cover by day is very hard to find (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-moose-warning
```

- **Rhyme:** "Ears back, hair high, lips licked: get a tree, be quick."
- **Number trick:** 1 bond, 1 year: cow and calf (90%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Where there's a calf, there's a cow. Where there's a cow in October, there may be a bull."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Bear: stand tall or play dead. Moose: run round a tree instead."
- **Number trick:** 55 and 16: runs about 55 km/h, swims 16 km (75%).
- **Number trick:** 5 to 10: seasonal home range in km² (90%).
- **Old hand's rule** (widely reported, opinion): "Never get between a cow and her calf."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "A moose walking slowly toward you is not your friend."

> [!mistake]
> - Walking closer to a cow and calf "for a photo".
> - Letting a dog off leash near moose.
> - Treating a moose charge like a bear charge.

> [!field]
> On your next scouting walk, pick a "charge tree" each time you stop near willow or water. Make it a habit.
```
