> [!why]
> A clean kill, a found bird and a good meal all start with one close shot at the front half of one named duck.

```diagram mdk-shot-zones
```

- Head, neck and front half (70%).
- Close: 20 to 30 m over decoys (Tip).

```more why the front half
- To kill, pellets must reach the brain, spine, or heart and lungs (75%).
- Heart and lungs sit high and forward under the wing roots; the rear belly is air sacs and gut (75 to 80%). See [Masterclass 2](#/s/mc-ducks-02).
- With the right pellet size, 1 to 2 hits in the vitals are enough for ducks (70%).
- Patterns thin out at the edges; birds on the edge of a pattern get few pellets (70%).
- The commonly quoted loss: about 1 in 5 ducks shot are not recovered in the United States (65%). Close shots cut that.
```

## Pattern, pellets and range

```diagram mdk-pattern-ring
```

- Steel 3 is the best all round duck size in one big test set (70%).
- Teal need a denser pattern than mallards (70%).

```more the lethality table, and your gun
| Ducks over decoys | Range | Steel size | Min pellets in 76 cm | Choke |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Big: mallard, pintail, goldeneye, gadwall | 18 to 41 m (20 to 45 yd) | 6 to 2 | 85 to 90 | Improved cylinder to 32 m, modified beyond |
| Medium: wigeon, scaup, shoveler | 18 to 41 m | 6 to 3 | 115 to 120 | Same |
| Small: teal, bufflehead | 18 to 41 m | 6 to 4 | 135 to 145 | Modified, full beyond 32 m |

- Source: Tom Roster's 2012 nontoxic shot lethality table, built on 15 United States steel against lead shooting tests (70%).
- Your beginner limit stays 35 m (38 yd) even though the table goes farther (Tip).
- Steel needs a choke rated for steel; tight chokes may not be (Tip). See [Shotgun skills](#/s/shotgun-skills/6) and the [[Choke]] and [[Patterning]] terms.
- Pattern your gun on paper at 30 m before the season (Tip).
- Non toxic shot only, even in your pocket (99%). [Rule Book](#/s/rb-migratory-birds/18).
```

## Aim and lead

```anim sk-leads Three ways to lead a duck.
```

- Decoying duck: look at and shoot the bill (65%).
- Crossing duck: swing through and keep the gun moving (Tip).

```more how to hit more ducks (opinion)
- Ducks Unlimited: over decoys, little lead is needed; aim at the bill on the first shot (65%).
- Focusing on the head helps put the shot in front; aiming at the body often shoots behind (65%).
- Most misses are behind the bird, often from stopping the swing (65%).
- Stay on the same bird until it falls (Tip).
- Practise on clays: [Shotgun skills](#/s/shotgun-skills/3).
```

## After the shot

```anim mdk-cripple Bird down, head up: shoot again now.
```

- Head up on the water: shoot again at once (65%).
- Mark it against a landmark, then fetch it (Tip).

```more what happens after the shot
- A clean hit: the bird folds, wings stop, it falls limp (Tip).
- A cripple: wings still beating, head up, it swims or dives. Wounded ducks dive and stay under longer with each dive (65%).
- A bird that "towers" (climbs straight up then falls) has often been hit in the lungs: watch it all the way down (Tip, widely reported).
- No waiting game like a deer. Fetch at once, before it reaches cover (Tip).
- Most cripples head for shore; check the bank cover (65%).
- Dispatching a wounded bird: a quick finishing shot on the water, or by hand once in hand (Tip). Victoria's game agency publishes a humane dispatch method (70%).
- The law: you must have a way to retrieve and make the effort to retrieve every bird you kill or wound (99%). [Rule Book](#/s/rb-migratory-birds/21).
```

## Field care in 4 steps

```steps Look after your ducks in 4 steps
mdk-care-1 | Cool them in a mesh bag, never plastic.
mdk-care-2 | Gloves on; no eating while cleaning.
mdk-care-3 | Pluck or breast; keep one wing on until home.
mdk-care-4 | Cook to the safe line; label and freeze.
```

- Wing or head stays on until preserved (99%).
- Gloves: avian influenza precautions (75%).

```more care, safety and the law
- Let birds cool in a mesh bag in the shade; warm birds spoil fast (Tip).
- Avian influenza: harvest only healthy looking birds; do not handle sick ones and report them; wear gloves; clean birds outdoors or in a ventilated place; no eating, drinking or smoking while cleaning; wash hands and tools (75%, United States agency advice).
- After handling ducks, change or clean clothes and boots before going near poultry (75%).
- Keep a fully feathered wing or head on each unpreserved bird; store so each bird can be counted and named (99%). [Rule Book](#/s/rb-migratory-birds/22).
- Both breasts are the legal [[Edible portions]] you must take home (99%).
- More: [Meat care, bird care](#/s/meat-care/10).
```

## Meat and cooking

```diagram lc-ck-pluck
```

```diagram sb-bird-yield
```

- A mallard gives about 350 to 450 g of meat (Tip).
- Pluck fat fall ducks to keep the flavour (Tip).

```more cooking notes
- Safe line: the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) recommends 74 °C (165 °F) for duck (65%, secondhand).
- Many cooks serve duck breast pink; wild birds are not inspected, so the safe line is the cautious choice (Tip).
- Fish eating divers and mergansers taste strong: soak in salt water, or make sausage or jerky (Tip).
- Gullet full of grain? A grain fed mallard is the best eating (Tip).
- Recipes and the plucking choice: [Cooking](#/s/cooking/4).
```
