- Glass, judge, stalk: the standard BC way (Tip).
- No bait, no electronic calls, no lights (99%).

## Scouting

```diagram mc-black-bear-food-calendar
```

- Find this week's best food, then the bears on it (Tip).
- Spring: first green. Fall: ripe berries, fruit, fish (90%).

```more scouting in detail
- At home: Hunt Map layers (cutblocks 5 to 20 years, burns, south faces) and harvest numbers by MU (chapters 1 and 7).
- On the ground: berry ripeness, fresh scat, torn logs, trails (chapter 8).
- Trail cameras on trails and rub trees show which bears use an area and when (Tip). No bait near them: bait for bears is unlawful (99%).
- Spring: drive or walk logging roads at last light looking for green edges and bears on them (Tip, quick lesson).
- Fall near Heffley Creek: glass a south slope of ripe berries or a creek with hawthorn in the last hour of light (Tip, quick lesson).
```

```more glassing in detail
- A black spot that was not there before is your bear (Tip). Watch it for 30 seconds: rocks and stumps do not move.
- Brown and cinnamon bears hide against fall colours; look for shape and movement (Tip).
- Glass the same slope at the same hour on several days: bears return to good food (Tip).
```

## Judge the bear: 4 steps

```steps Judge a bear in 4 steps
mc-black-bear-judge-1 | Species: hump, face, ears. Not colour.
mc-black-bear-judge-2 | Cubs: watch 5 minutes or more.
mc-black-bear-judge-3 | Sex and size: ears, neck, belly, legs.
mc-black-bear-judge-4 | Shot: broadside, backstop, in range.
```

- "Be patient. Don't shoot the moment you see a bear!" (98%).
- Pick an adult male (98%).

```more judging in detail
- Synopsis notice, page 80 (98%, via the [quick lesson](#/s/black-bear)): young bears are "all legs", look over the shoulder often, pointed head, ears look big and close together; large adult males have stocky legs, a massive body with the belly near the ground, slow deliberate movement, a big round head "like a basketball", small looking ears, a thick neck.
- Outdoor Life (Tyler Freel, 2016) (60%): sows and young boars look lanky, with small paws, thin wrists, pointier noses, paler muzzles and longer, shaggier hair; big boars look blocky, with thick front legs, a low belly, a rolling waddle, ears set on the sides of the head and short, slick hair.
- Boone and Crockett (Jim Shockey) (65%): look at location first (big bears hold the best food at prime time), then behaviour (boars rub, break saplings, chase other bears), then scale, then body. Nobody can judge skull size exactly in the field.
- Size trick (Tip, Outdoor Life): a bear about 90 cm (36 in) at the shoulder is a big bear. Compare to the vegetation and your known distances.
- A lone bear in spring or fall could still be a sow whose cubs are out of sight (Tip). Watch.
```

## Spot and stalk

```anim mc-black-bear-stalk Move only when the head is down.
```

- Wind in your face, route out of sight (Tip).
- Close in, rest, watch for cubs, wait for broadside (Tip).

```more the stalk in detail
- Before you move: mark two landmarks near the bear; the slope looks different up close (Tip, [stalking](#/s/stalking-footwork)).
- Check the wind before every move; thermals swing in the evening (80%, chapter 3).
- Move while it feeds noisily with its head down; freeze when it lifts its head (Tip).
- Stop at a rest inside your range; never stalk closer than you must (Tip).
- Lean (quick lesson): watch every bear at least 5 minutes before you decide anything (Tip).
```

## Still hunting and sitting a food source

```diagram mc-black-bear-habitat
```

- Sit downwind of natural food with fresh sign (Tip).
- Never over bait or a carcass (99%).

```more sitting and still hunting in detail
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Sit the edge of a berry patch, an orchard with permission, or a creek crossing with fresh scat; arrive early, wind right (Tip).
- Item 21, page 11: unlawful "to hunt bears by placing bait or by using a dead animal or using part of it as bait" (99%, [legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)). So no sitting over your own gut pile for a bear (95%, my reading in the Rule Book).
- Item 20: unlawful to feed bears; baiting is only for cougar, coyote and wolf where authorized (99%).
- Natural food (a berry patch, a fruit tree, a salmon stream) is not bait you placed (Tip, my reading of the bait definition; VERIFY with a conservation officer for any farm crop or orchard you did not grow).
- Still hunting: slow steps through timber edges between food and beds, in wet or windy weather (Tip, [still hunting](#/s/still-hunting)).
```

## Calling and dogs

```diagram mc-black-bear-methods
```

- Mouth or hand distress calls: legal, my reading (95%).
- Electronic calls: not for bear (99%).

```more calling and dogs in detail
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Item 41, page 12: no recorded or electronic calls except for wolf, coyote, cougar, lynx, bobcat and Schedule C birds (99%). Bear is not on that list.
- A fawn or rabbit distress call can bring a bear in close and fast; not for a first hunt (Tip, quick lesson).
- Calls bring in grizzlies and cougars too (75%, [bear safety](#/s/bear-cougar-encounters)). Hunt with a partner watching your back (Tip).
- Dogs, page 12: "Unleashed dogs may be used to hunt small game, lynx, bobcat, black bear, or cougar" (99%, [legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods)). Hound hunting is specialist work; a pet dog in bear country raises your risk (70%, WildSafeBC: dogs in over half of injuries in one review).
```

## Hunting with a partner

```diagram mc-black-bear-partner
```

- One glasses, one stalks (Tip).
- Agree signals before the hunt: stop, go, cubs, shoot (Tip).

```more partner work in detail
- The glasser watches for cubs and other bears the shooter cannot see (Tip).
- Agree who shoots before the stalk; one shooter at a time (Tip).
- Radios on low, or hand signals (Tip, [hunting partner](#/s/hunting-partner)).
- After the shot, the partner marks where the bear went from the high point (Tip).
- Partner tracking a wounded bear: rifle ready, side by side, spray in reach (Tip, chapter 10).
```

## Decisions on the day, and ethics

| Situation | Decision (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Can't see hump and face | Wait or pass |
| Cubs, or a small bear near | No shot, ever (99%) |
| Lone young bear | Pass: let it grow |
| Big boar, quartering to | Wait for broadside |
| Bear near houses or farms | Check 100 m rule (99%) |
| Last light, long walk out | Shoot only if you can recover it |

- A hunter who passes is still a hunter (Tip).
- Fair chase and the meat: see [ethics](#/s/ethics-fair-chase).

## What an old hand does differently

| Beginner | Old hand (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Hunts the road | Glasses the far side |
| Shoots the first bear | Watches 5 minutes, judges |
| Checks wind once | Checks it every move |
| Hunts empty slopes | Follows the ripe food |
| Trusts colour | Counts 3 clues |
| Rushes after the shot | Waits, marks, then tracks |

- These are opinions from hunting educators and guides, not measured facts.
- My lean (quick lesson): spot and stalk on fall food in October (Tip).

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-black-bear-id-flow
```

- **Rhyme:** "See it, judge it, wait it out; one clean shot, no doubt."
- **Number trick:** 5 minutes, 3 clues, 1 shot (Tip and 85%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, the synopsis notice): "Be patient. Don't shoot the moment you see a bear."

```more more hooks
- **Number trick:** 36 inches at the shoulder: a big bear (Tip, Outdoor Life).
- **Rhyme:** "No bait, no box, no beam: just glass and a plan."
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion, widely taught): "The wind is the boss."

> [!mistake]
> - Sitting over a gut pile for a bear (unlawful, 99%).
> - Using a phone speaker call for bear (unlawful, 99%).
> - Stalking straight in with the wind at your back.

> [!field]
> This October: glass 3 evenings, judge every bear with the 4 steps, and write "pass" or "shoot" with your reason. Shoot none of them the first evening.
```
