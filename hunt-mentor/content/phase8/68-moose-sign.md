## Tracks

```diagram mc-moose-track
```

- About 13 to 15 cm long: twice a deer track (75%).
- Long pointed toes; dewclaws in mud and snow (70%).

```gallery
hab-moose-tracks-mud | Moose tracks in mud: long pointed toes.
ts-moose-track-katmai | Moose track beside a hiking boot, Alaska.
```

```more tracks in detail
- App track tables: moose about 11 to 18 cm long, 10 to 15 cm wide, a long pointed heart, often with dewclaws (70%, [tracks](#/s/tracks)); about 13 to 15 cm, more than twice a deer track (75%, [habitat tour](#/s/habitat-big-game)).
- Maine Inland Fisheries and Wildlife track card: adult moose 4.5 to 5.5 in (about 11 to 14 cm); adult white tail 2.5 to 3 in (85%, via [measuring tracks](#/s/rs-track-measuring)).
- Elk tracks are smaller and rounder (70%). A big track by a beaver pond is most likely moose (75%).
- Dewclaws show when the moose sinks in: soft mud, snow, or when it runs (70%, Tip).
- Bull or cow from a track alone: not reliable (Tip).
```

## Trails in snow

```gallery
hab-moose-trail-snow | A moose trail: a trough with deep holes.
```

- Moose step high: the trail is a trough with deep holes (Tip).
- Snow ages a track by the crust and crumbs (75%).

```more snow trails in detail
- Moose step over deep snow and logs; the trail is a trough with deep holes, not a neat line of prints (Tip, [habitat tour](#/s/habitat-big-game)).
- Loose crumbs that fall apart when poked: fresh; disturbed snow bonds into a crust within hours (75%, [snow tracking](#/s/snow-tracking)).
- Your November season may have early snow on the plateaus: a gift for reading fresh moose sign (Tip).
```

## Droppings

```diagram mc-moose-pellets
```

- Fall and winter: big oval pellets, like pressed sawdust (65%).
- Summer: soft clumps or patties (65%).

```gallery
ts-moose-pellets | Winter pellets: big ovals, like pressed sawdust.
ts-moose-pellets-snow | A winter pile on a snowy trail.
```

```more droppings in detail
- App sign table: moose pellets about 1.5 to 2 cm across and 2.5 to 4 cm long; winter pellets look like pressed sawdust; summer droppings are loose patties (65%, [reading sign](#/s/sign)).
- Habitat tour: big oval pellets about 2.5 to 3 cm long, in large piles (75%).
- Elk pellets are smaller and often more rounded or dimpled; deer pellets much smaller (65%, [droppings](#/s/rs-droppings)).
- In November expect a mix: pellets as twigs take over the diet (Tip).
- Many piles in one small area: a feeding or bedding spot used for days (Tip).
```

## Browse and bark

```anim rp-moose-browse A moose breaks the stem down to reach the tips.
```

- Twigs bitten off up to 2.5 m; stems snapped down (90%).
- Bark stripped from willow and poplar in winter (90%).

```gallery
hab-moose-willows | Willow tips bitten off at chest height.
hab-aspen-grove-snow | Aspen: look for teeth marks and stripped bark.
```

```more browse in detail
- BC account (90%): moose reach twigs up to 2.5 m, "chew off large diameter twigs, and often break down branches with their jaws to get at tips that are out of reach". They "strip bark from willows and poplars in winter".
- Browse line: moose 1.8 to 2.4 m; elk 1.5 to 1.8 m; deer to about 1.2 m (65%, [reading sign](#/s/sign)). Alaska reports moose browse lines 6 to 8 ft (1.8 to 2.4 m) (85%).
- Moose and deer have no upper front teeth: they tear twigs, leaving a ragged end; hares cut clean at 45 degrees (85%, [reading sign](#/s/sign)).
- Broken, bent willow stems with fresh, pale wood: a moose fed here recently (Tip).
```

## Rubs and rut pits

```photo g-moose-bull-side
```

- Rut pits: pawed holes 45 to 105 cm long, soaked with urine (65%).
- Rubs on saplings; brush thrashed (65%).

```more rut sign in detail
- Bull moose dig rut pits: about 45 to 105 cm long and 8 to 15 cm deep, soaked with urine, strong smell (65%, [reading sign](#/s/sign)).
- BC account (90%): cows "wallow in the urine soaked rutting pits the bulls have made".
- Bulls rub velvet off "against trees or shrubs" in early September (90%), then rub and thrash saplings through the rut (65%).
- In November the pits are old; fresh pits mean a bull still keen, or a late cow (Tip, my reading of the second and third heats).
- A [[Wallow]] in the app glossary is the elk word; moose sign is the rut pit (Tip).
```

## Beds

```photo hab-subalpine-forest
```

- Big flattened ovals, often alone, in willow or timber near water (60%).
- Size: VERIFY (no source found).

```more beds in detail
- App: moose beds are bigger than elk or deer beds and often alone, in willow or timber near water (60%, [reading sign](#/s/sign)).
- Melted snow beds in winter; flattened grass or leaves in fall (Tip, [beds](#/s/rs-beds)).
- Droppings next to a bed are common; moose often defecate on rising (Tip).
- A bed still warm, or with crisp edges in fresh snow: the moose is very close. Stop, glass, check the wind (Tip).
- Do not walk through a bedding area you plan to hunt: leave it quiet (Tip).
```

## How fresh is it?

```diagram mc-moose-fresh
```

- Wet and shiny pellets: hours (Tip).
- Browse ends still pale and wet: today or yesterday (Tip).

```more aging sign in detail
- Freshness signs (Tip, [aging sign](#/s/rs-track-aging)): wet shiny pellets; crisp track edges; loose crumbs in snow; pale wet wood on browsed twigs; sap on broken stems; urine smell at a rut pit.
- Weather changes everything: rain, frost and sun age sign fast or slow. Treat any time estimate as a guide (75%, [aging sign](#/s/rs-track-aging)).
- Old sign is still useful: it shows where moose winter or feed year after year (Tip).
```

## Following a moose

```anim sk-trail-beside Walk beside the trail, not on it.
```

- Walk beside the trail; glass ahead every few steps (Tip).
- Wind in your face; the moose may be bedded close (Tip).

```more following in detail
- Following a track (Tip, [following a track](#/s/rs-track-following)): mark the last good print; walk beside the trail; look 50 to 100 m ahead more than at your feet; stop often.
- Moose often loop back and bed facing their back trail or with the wind behind them (Tip, hunter consensus). Swing wide and downwind of the line if you think it bedded.
- On a blood trail after a shot, wait first (chapter 10).
- Your partner on the track, you ahead and to the side with the rifle ready: works well (Tip, [partner](#/s/hunting-partner)).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-moose-track
```

- **Rhyme:** "Big as your hand, twice a deer; dewclaws down, a moose was here."
- **Number trick:** 13 to 15 cm track; 1.8 to 2.4 m browse line (65 to 75%).
- **Old hand's rule** (tradition): "Fresh sign, sit tight. Old sign, keep walking."

```more more hooks
- **Rhyme:** "Pellets in winter, patties in June; pale twig ends: he left here soon."
- **Number trick:** 45 to 105: rut pit length in cm (65%).
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "The bed is the moose's front porch. Do not walk across it."

> [!mistake]
> - Calling every big track a moose: check size and shape.
> - Walking straight down the moose's trail.
> - Judging bull or cow from a track.

> [!field]
> Take a tape measure and a phone. Measure 3 tracks and 3 pellets at a wetland and photograph them next to the tape. Compare with this chapter.
```
