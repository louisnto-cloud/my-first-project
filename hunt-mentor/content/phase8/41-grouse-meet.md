## Ruffed grouse: rooster or hen?

```diagram mgr-sex-tails
```

- Unbroken tail band and 2 rump dots: male (70 to 85%).
- Band broken in the middle, 1 dot: female (70 to 85%).

```gallery
g-ruffed-grey | Grey phase male displaying. Unbroken black tail band.
ruffed-grouse-hen | Hen in leaves. Small crest, barred brown.
```

```more every mark, with numbers
- Tail: the male's dark band near the tip runs unbroken across all the tail feathers. The hen's band is often washed out or broken in the middle two feathers (85%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game aging and sexing guide). Some males break the rule, so use two clues (70%, Connecticut DEEP).
- Rump dots: pluck a feather from the rump just above the tail. 2 or more pale dots: male. 1 dot: female (70%, Connecticut DEEP hunter log; widely used by hunters).
- Middle tail feather length: about 15 cm (6 in) or more is a male; under 14 cm (5.5 in) is a female; in between, use another clue (70%, Connecticut DEEP).
- Weight: a male is about 500 g; hens are smaller (85%, Hinterland Who's Who). Average about 640 g in one dataset (65%, Animal Diversity Web).
- Colour phases: grey and red (brown). Both sexes come in both (80%). West coast birds run more reddish brown (85%, Hinterland Who's Who).
- Neck ruffs: black feather tufts on the neck sides, bigger on the male, flared in display (80%).
```

## Spruce grouse: the dark one

```gallery
g-spruce-male | Male: black throat and breast, red comb.
spruce-grouse-hen | Hen: heavy black and white barring.
```

- Male black below with a red comb; hen barred grey or rust (80%).
- Male tail black; hen tail mottled (85%).

```more marks, Franklin's form and young
- Male: black throat and breast edged in white, scarlet comb over the eye (80%, Idaho Fish and Game).
- Hen: grey to reddish brown, heavily barred (80%).
- Southern BC birds are the [[Franklin's grouse|Franklin's]] form: the male shows white spots above a dark tail and lacks the rusty tail tip (80%).
- Sex by tail: male tail feathers black, hen's mottled tan (85%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game).
- Age: pluck the innermost flight feather (P1). Quill base under 2.4 mm wide: young of the year; 2.4 mm or more: adult (85%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game).
- Weight 450 to 750 g (80%).
- Nickname "fool hen": it often sits and lets people walk close (80%, Idaho Fish and Game).
```

## Dusky and sooty: the blue grouse

```diagram mgr-blue-split
```

- Biggest forest grouse in BC (80%).
- Coast birds are sooty, Interior birds are dusky (85%).

```gallery
dusky-grouse-male | Dusky male in display. Slate grey, neck sacs, yellow comb.
dusky-grouse-hen | Dusky hen. Mottled, long dark tail.
g-sooty-male | Sooty male: the coast form.
```

```more the split, and the marks
- Until 2006 both were one species, the blue grouse. The American Ornithologists' Union split them that year on DNA, voice and display evidence (85%).
- Sooty grouse: wet coastal forest. Dusky grouse: drier Interior forest (85%). Where exactly they meet in northern BC is less certain (75%).
- The Rule Book uses the old name in brackets: Region 2 lists "SOOTY (Blue)", Regions 3 and 8 list "DUSKY (Blue)" (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-2/10)).
- Male: plain slate grey, yellow to orange comb, neck air sacs that show in display, dark tail with a pale grey tip band (80%). Dusky sacs reddish purple (75%, Idaho Fish and Game).
- Hen: mottled brown and grey, dark tail (80%).
- Sex by tail: male black with a grey tip, often square ended; hen black tail with mottled rump feathers (85%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game, sooty grouse).
- Weight: 880 g to 1.6 kg (80%). About 50 cm (20 in) long (75%, Idaho Fish and Game).
```

## Young of the year or adult?

```diagram mgr-age-wing
```

- Look at the 2 outer flight feathers, P9 and P10 (85%).
- Pointed and frayed: young. Rounded: adult (85%).

```more how the moult makes this work
- Grouse replace their flight feathers once a year in midsummer, from P1 outward toward P10 (85%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game).
- Young birds replace only P1 to P8 in their first summer, so P9 and P10 stay as worn, pointed juvenile feathers (85%).
- If P8 is not fully grown, it is a young bird. If P9 or P10 is still growing in, it is an adult (85%).
- Ptarmigan: young birds show more black on P9 than on P8 (85%).
- By October, young birds look like small hens of their species (Tip, 65%).
- Why care: early in the season you shoot mostly young birds; late in the season more adults (75%, Pennsylvania Game Commission). See [The mind](#/s/mc-grouse-04).
```

## Size, weight and numbers

```diagram mgr-weights
```

- Biggest to smallest: dusky, sharp tailed, ruffed and spruce, ptarmigan (80%).
- BC numbers: only sharp tailed has an estimate (85%); the rest VERIFY.

```more weights behind the chart
- Dusky 880 g to 1.6 kg (80%). Another dataset gives 0.91 to 1.36 kg (65%, Animal Diversity Web).
- Sharp tailed 596 to 1,031 g, 41 to 48 cm long (85%, BC species account).
- Ruffed 450 to 750 g, 40 to 50 cm long (60 to 75%, Pennsylvania Game Commission, Wikipedia). Wingspan 56 to 64 cm (75%).
- Spruce 450 to 750 g (80%).
- Willow ptarmigan 450 to 800 g; white tailed about 350 g, the smallest grouse; rock ptarmigan in between (85%, Hinterland Who's Who).
- Meat per bird: see [Shot to table](#/s/mc-grouse-10).
```

```more numbers and trends in BC
| Bird | BC numbers | Certainty |
|---|---|---|
| Sharp tailed | About 10,000 breeders (2001) | 85% |
| Ruffed, spruce, dusky, sooty | No estimate found | VERIFY |
| Ptarmigan | No estimate found | VERIFY |

- In parts of North America ruffed grouse numbers rise and fall on a roughly 10 year cycle (75%, Southeastern Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies paper). Whether this shows in southern BC: VERIFY.
- Ptarmigan, ruffed and spruce grouse are among the animals with cyclic ups and downs (85%, Hinterland Who's Who).
- Dusky grouse are listed as Least Concern worldwide, about 3 million birds, with only a small decline (65%, Animal Diversity Web citing IUCN).
- Hinterland Who's Who suggests there may be more ruffed grouse now than before European settlement (75%).
- No BC wide grouse harvest number was found (VERIFY).
```

## Sharp tailed grouse

```gallery
sharp-tailed-grouse | Pale, spotted belly, short pointed tail.
sharp-tailed-grouse-snow | In snow near Fort St. John, BC.
```

- Pale, V marks on a white breast, pointed tail (85%).
- Region 3: open only in MUs (Management Units) 3-30 and 3-31 (99%).

```more marks, numbers and trend in BC
- Field marks: white breast with brown V shaped marks, barred head, neck and back, wedge shaped tail with the two middle feathers longer (85%, BC species account). In flight the tail shows white (80%).
- Male in display: pink neck air sacs, middle tail feathers with long white lines (85%).
- Three subspecies live in BC. The Columbian one lives from near Vanderhoof south to Merritt (85%).
- Status: Columbian sharp tailed grouse is on the provincial Blue List (85%, BC species account, 2004, kept for history in 2026).
- About 10,000 breeding birds in BC (2001 estimate): central Interior 4,000 to 8,200, southern Interior 600 to 1,200 (85%).
- Thompson Okanagan grassland leks: numbers fell close to 50% from 1990 to 1999; only 43% of 23 known leks were still active in 1998 (85%).
- Newer numbers: VERIFY (none found).
- Look alikes: a dusky hen at a grassland edge (dark tail, no point) and a hen pheasant (much longer tail) (Tip).
```

## Ptarmigan: the alpine grouse

```gallery
g-ptarmigan-summer | White tailed ptarmigan in summer: mottled.
g-ptarmigan-winter | Willow ptarmigan in winter: white, black tail.
```

- Three kinds; white wings all year, feathered feet (85%).
- White tailed: white tail. Willow, rock: black tail (85%).

```more the three ptarmigan
- White tailed ptarmigan: strictly alpine, about 350 g. The common one in southern BC, breeding at 1,830 to 2,650 m in the Interior (85%, BC Breeding Bird Atlas, Hinterland Who's Who).
- Willow ptarmigan: 450 to 800 g, treeline and shrubby valleys, mostly in the northern half of BC (85%).
- Rock ptarmigan: in between in size, high dry tundra. Males show a black stripe through the eye in winter (85%).
- Three plumages a year, not two; all white in winter (85%). By mid October they are moulting white (85%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game).
- Rules: ptarmigan rows exist in Regions 2 and 3, not in Region 8 (99%, [Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-3/12)).
```

## Look alikes: legal or not

| Bird | Season? |
|---|---|
| Sage grouse | None in BC (90%) |
| Sharp tail outside 3-30, 3-31 (Region 3) | None (99%) |
| Ptarmigan in Region 8 | No row (99%) |
| Hen pheasant | Check region (99%) |

```gallery
g-sage-grouse | Sage grouse: no season anywhere in BC.
g-pheasant-hen | Hen pheasant: long pointed tail.
```

```more the details
- Sage grouse: no season row anywhere in the synopsis, so no open season in BC (90%, my reading, [Rule Book Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3/16)).
- Sharp tailed grouse: Region 3 opens only MUs (Management Units) 3-30 and 3-31, and not the part of 3-30 south of the Scottie Creek Forest Service Road (99%). No row in Regions 2 or 8 (99%).
- Region 8 (Okanagan) has no ptarmigan and no sharp tailed grouse row (99%).
- Pheasant: Region 8 cocks only; Region 2 cocks only in MUs 2-4 and 2-8 (99%). A hen is never legal there.
- Old hand's rule (opinion): "If it flushed from the wrong habitat, look twice."
```

```more more hooks
- "Black breast and red brow, a spruce grouse sits there now."
```
