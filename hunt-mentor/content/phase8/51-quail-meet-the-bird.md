- Three small upland birds share the South Okanagan: quail, chukar and gray partridge (85%).
- Quail is the smallest: 140 to 230 g (75%).

## The male: five marks

```diagram mql-id-marks
```

- Curled black plume, black face rimmed in white (80%).
- Scaled belly with a chestnut patch, white streaks on the sides (80%).

```more male marks in full
- Length 24 to 27 cm, wingspan 32 to 37 cm, weight 140 to 230 g (75%, Audubon).
- The male's plume is six feathers that curl forward like a comma (85%, BC Breeding Bird Atlas).
- Male: black face and throat edged with white, buff forehead, brown cap, blue grey breast, scaled belly with a chestnut centre patch (80%).
- Sides: grey brown with white streaks (80%).
- He walks with a nodding head, often in the open next to cover (75%, Audubon).
```

## The female and the pair

```gallery

g-quail-female | Female: plain grey brown face, small plume, scaled belly.
g-quail-pair | A pair. The female is plainer and smaller in the plume.
```

- Both sexes carry a plume. Hers is smaller and brown (80%).
- She has no black face and no white rim (80%).

```more female marks in full
- Female: grey brown head and back, no black and white face, smaller plume, the same scaled belly (80%, Audubon and Edgewood notes).
- In a flushing covey you rarely see the face. Sex does not matter for the bag in Region 8: the row is "QUAIL", 10 a day (99%, Rule Book).
- Tip: on the ground, count black faces to guess how many cocks are in a covey.
```

## Young of the year

```diagram mql-age-wing
```

- In fall, most of a covey are young of the year (60%).
- Buff tips on the wing coverts mark a young bird (70%).

```more aging in full
- Juvenile California quail keep buff coloured upper primary coverts and pointed outer flight feathers until their first full moult (70%, 1963 Condor aging study, search preview).
- Hunters across North America use the buff covert tips to age quail of every species (60%, Project Upland, search preview).
- Many young in your bag means a good hatch. A New Zealand study used the young share of the bag as an index of breeding success (65%, search preview).
- By October the young look like small, dull females (Tip, 65%, from the quick lesson).
```

## Look alikes: legal or not

```diagram mql-lookalikes
```

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Straight plume? Mountain quail. No open season (99%).
- Brown bird with a long tail? Hen pheasant. Never legal (99%).

```more look alikes in full
- Mountain quail: long straight plume, chestnut throat, broad white bars on red brown sides (80%, Audubon). The Rule Book: "No open season on mountain quail." Region 1, page 21 (99%).
- The Region 8 row says only "QUAIL". Whether it covers mountain quail: VERIFY. Shoot California quail only (Tip, from the [Region 8 Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-8)).
- Chukar and gray partridge: legal in Regions 3 and 8 (99%). Pheasant: cocks only, Regions 2, 3 and 8 (99%).
- Grouse flush from the same draws in September and October. Different rows, different limits (99%, Rule Book).
```

## Chukar: the bigger cousin

```photo g-chukar Chukar: red bill and legs, black necklace, barred flanks.
```

- 538 to 765 g, red bill and legs, black necklace (75%).
- Sexes look alike. Young are duller (75%).

```more chukar marks in full
- Length 34 to 38 cm, wingspan about 51 cm (75%, Audubon).
- Sandy brown back, grey breast, buff belly. A black band runs from the forehead through the eye and down to a necklace around a cream throat (75%).
- Flanks: bold vertical bars of black, white and chestnut. Bill, legs and eye ring coral red (75%).
- Males are a little heavier. Juveniles are mottled grey brown without the sharp head and flank pattern (75%).
- Call: a loud, fast "chuck chuck chukar" from the rocks (75%).
- Native to Asia. BC is the only Canadian province where it took hold (65%, BC Outdoors).
```

## Gray partridge: the Hun

```diagram sb-partridge-id
```

- Orange face, chestnut horseshoe, rusty tail in flight (75%).
- 300 to 500 g. Flushes with a cackle (75%).

```more gray partridge marks in full
- Length 30 to 33 cm, wingspan 50 to 56 cm (75%, Audubon).
- Grey below, orange face and throat, chestnut belly patch, rusty outer tail feathers that flash on the flush (75%).
- The chestnut patch is smaller on the female (75%).
- Call: a hoarse "kee ah"; a rapid cackle when flushed (75%).
- The synopsis spells it "GRAY (Hungarian) PARTRIDGE" (99%). Hunters say [[Huns]].
```

## Size and weight

```diagram mql-size-bars
```

- Quail 140 to 230 g. Hun 300 to 500 g. Chukar 538 to 765 g (75%).
- A quail is about the weight of a cup of water (Tip).

```more why size matters
- On the flush, size and wingbeat help name the bird: a quail is half a Hun and a third of a chukar (75%, from Audubon weights).
- Small bird, small shot: lead 7.5 or 8 are standard for quail (75%, Washington Fish and Wildlife). See chapter 10.
- About 160 g is a typical BC quail (65%, BC Outdoors).
```

## In BC: where they came from

```diagram mql-bc-timeline
```

- Introduced to BC between 1860 and 1912 (85%).
- Two main areas: the Okanagan and Kettle valleys, and southeast Vancouver Island (85%).

```more history, numbers and trend
- California quail were released on the coast and in the southern Interior between 1860 and 1912, from a native range in California and Baja California (85%, BC Breeding Bird Atlas).
- Earliest Okanagan record: 1912 (70%, Lewin 1965, The Condor, search preview). The Okanagan birds may also have come north from releases in northern Washington (60%, iNFOnews).
- The atlas (2008 to 2012) found the Fraser Valley population apparently gone (85%). So no quail near Mission.
- Breeding Bird Survey trend for BC, 1970 to 2012: up about 5.8% a year, low reliability (85% that this is what the atlas reports).
- The increase comes mostly from Interior birds; coastal birds are doing less well (85%).
- BC quail harvest: up to 4,600 birds in the 1970s, under 1,000 a year recently (65%, BC Outdoors 2021).
- Conservation status: not at risk (85%).
- Chukar: released in the south central Interior in the 1950s; best on low slopes of the Thompson from Kamloops to Lytton, also the South Okanagan and Similkameen (65%, BC Outdoors and KnowBC).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mql-lookalikes
```

- **Rhyme:** "Curl is legal, straight is not; ring on the cock, or hold your shot." (Tip)
- **Number trick:** "2, 4, 6": quail about 200 g, Hun about 400 g, chukar about 600 g (75%, rounded).

```more more hooks
- **Face code:** black face = cock quail; orange face = Hun; black necklace = chukar (75 to 80%).
- **Old hand's rule (opinion):** "Name it in the air or let it go." Hunting educators teach positive ID before every shot (Tip).
- **Date hook:** quail came to BC between 1860 and 1912: "sixty to twelve" (85%).
```
