> [!why]
> Geese need three things close together: safe water, open food and a clear view. Find all three and you have found geese.

```diagram mgs-habitat-section
```

- Roost on big water, feed in open fields nearby (80%).
- Towns and parks hold geese you cannot hunt (Tip).

```more what this chapter covers
- Habitat: roost, loafing and feeding ground.

- Elevation through the year.

- Finding them on the Hunt Map: layers and terrain tools.
```

## Roost, loaf, feed

```gallery
hab-kamloops-pond-farm | Pond, hay fields and cottonwoods near Kamloops: water and food close together.
hab-geese-stubble | Geese in corn stubble in snow, the Fraser Valley pattern.
hab-lac-du-bois-potholes | Pothole lakes in open grassland north of Kamloops.
hab-osoyoos-wetland | Calm water out of the wind, Osoyoos Lake.
```

- Nest near water; winter where food is within commuting distance of water (80%).
- Open views: geese avoid feeding next to tall cover (60%).

```more habitat in full
- Lakes, ponds, bays, marshes and fields; always nests near water; winters where feeding areas are within commuting distance of water (80%, Audubon).
- Geese spend as much time on land as on water (85%, quick lesson).
- Many urban and refuge geese are now year round residents (80%, Audubon).
- Loafing spots: river bars, sandbars, mudflats, points and pond edges (Tip, 65%).
- Fields: they avoid decoys and spots near fence lines, brush piles and ditches that could hide a predator (60%).
```

## Foods by season

```diagram mgs-food-seasons
```

- Almost all plants: grass, sedges, seeds, berries, grain (80%).
- Fall and winter: waste grain and green grass (80%).

```gallery
hab-pondweed | Floating pondweed: summer water food.
hab-cattail-marsh | Cattail marsh: cover and nesting edges.
```

```more foods in full
- Diet: grass and sedge stems and shoots, water plants, seeds, berries and cultivated grain; rarely insects or small fish (80%, Audubon).
- Many races feed on waste grain and winter cereal crops (75%, Ducks Unlimited).
- They graze on land, tip up in water, grub for roots and pick seeds (75%).
- Snow geese on the Fraser delta dig roots of American three square bulrush and now rely on farm and refuge fields as well (85%, Demarchi 2006).
- BC farm foods you will see: cut hay and pasture, corn stubble in the Fraser Valley, grain stubble in the Interior (Tip, 65%).
```

## Elevation by month

```diagram mgs-elevation-months
```

- Nest mostly below 500 m (85%).
- When high water freezes, birds drop to valley rivers and the coast (75%).

```more elevation in full
- BC Breeding Bird Atlas: Canada geese nest across the southern two thirds of BC, mostly below 500 m (85%, via the quick lesson).
- High plateau lakes freeze first; birds then crowd onto open valley water and the coast (60%, migration lesson).
- Named wintering water: the South Thompson River from Little Shuswap Lake to Kamloops Lake, Vaseux Lake, and the Fraser delta and valley marshes (80 to 90%, migration lesson).
- Tranquille at the head of Kamloops Lake is spring and fall staging for geese and swans, and it is a No Shooting area (85%). Vaseux Lake is a bird sanctuary with no hunting (99%).
```

## Heffley Creek, Region 3

```photo hab-kamloops-pond-farm Valley bottom ponds and hay near Kamloops: classic goose country.
```

- Look for river bars and hay or grain fields in the valley bottom (Tip, 65%).
- Light pressure: under 30 estimated goose hunters a year in 3-27 and 3-28 (99%, survey).

```more Region 3 notes
- Heffley Creek is MU (Management Unit) 3-27 east of the North Thompson and 3-28 west, both District 3 (85 to 99%, [Rule Book: Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3)).
- Canada geese and cacklers are common on the Thompson River all year (70%, first duck hunt lesson).
- The fields that hold geese are almost all private [[Cultivated land]]: written permission first (99% that consent is required).
- Map C7: no hunting or shooting along the South Thompson band to the Lafarge Bridge, no shooting from there to the Pritchard Bridge (99% rule, 90% map reading). Tranquille (Map C17) is a No Shooting area (85%).
- Region 3 goose harvest is small: 196 geese in 2024, 614 in 2020 (99%, survey estimate). An open niche for a hunter who scouts (Tip).
- Local plan: [Heffley Creek](#/s/local-heffley).
```

## Mission and the Fraser Valley, Region 2

```photo hab-geese-stubble Corn stubble in winter: the Fraser Valley goose field.
```

- Most BC geese are taken in Region 2; MU (Management Unit) 2-4 leads (99%, survey).
- Mission needs the special area licence and insurance (99%).

```more Region 2 notes
- 2024 survey: MU 2-4 (Delta, Boundary Bay) 251 hunters, 1,028 geese; MU 2-8 (Mission) 109 hunters, 201 geese (99%).
- Fraser Valley Special Area: all of MU 2-4 and the parts of 2-8 inside Maple Ridge, Pitt Meadows, Mission and Coquitlam need the Fraser Valley Special Area Hunting Licence and $1,000,000 public liability and property damage insurance (99%, [Region 2 Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-2), Map B10).
- Pitt Addington Marsh (2-8) and Bert Brink Wildlife Management Area (2-4): no motor vehicles all year (99%).
- Burns Bog (2-4): no hunting (99%).
- Bert Brink has open fields partly flooded in winter that draw wigeon and Canada geese (90%, habitat tour lesson).
- Snow geese: the Fraser delta and Westham Island area (80%). MUs 2-4 and 2-5 carry the extra snow goose limit (99%).
- Local plan: [Mission](#/s/local-mission).
```

## South Okanagan, Region 8

```photo hab-osoyoos-wetland Osoyoos Lake wetland: water out of the wind.
```

- Many Okanagan geese are introduced residents that do not migrate (85%).
- Orchards and vineyards are cultivated land: consent first (90%).

```more Region 8 notes
- Okanagan nesting geese are hybrids of races introduced in the 1960s and 70s (85%, Regional District of Okanagan Similkameen).
- District 8 Canada and cackling geese: 20 September to 28 November, 20 December to 5 January, and 21 February to 10 March in a year that is not a leap year, 10 a day (99%).
- Vaseux Lake bird sanctuary: no hunting (99%).
- Region 8 goose harvest: 185 in 2024, 925 in 2023 (99%, survey estimate).
- Local plan: [South Okanagan](#/s/local-okanagan). Rules: [Region 8 Rule Book](#/s/rb-region-8).
```

## Find them on the Hunt Map

```steps Find goose water and fields in 4 steps
mgs-map-1 | Turn on Private land, Wetlands, Duck waters, Wildlife Management Areas.
mgs-map-2 | Slope steepness: flat open ground is goose ground.
mgs-map-3 | What can I see from here: pick a road spot that sees the field.
mgs-map-4 | Weather and wind, Sun and shade: wind at your back, sun behind.
```

- Open the [Hunt Map](#/map), then scout from public roads (Tip).

```more layers and tools explained
- Layers to turn on: "Private land" (permission needed), "Wetlands", "Duck staging and winter waters" (goose roosts too), "Wildlife Management Areas" (WMA) and "City and town limits" (most towns ban shooting) (layer notes in the app, 90%).
- "Motor vehicle closures" before you drive a farm or dike road (99% that closures apply).
- Terrain tools: "Slope steepness" and "Slope direction" (aspect), "Sun and shade", "What can I see from here" (viewshed), "Elevation profile".
- Wind tools: "Weather and wind" and "Scent cone". For geese the wind matters for landing, not scent (Tip).
- Save each roost, flight line and field as a Spot with the date and the count. See [Map scouting](#/s/map-scouting).
```

## Memory hooks

> [!tip] Memory rhymes (made for this app)
> - "Water to sleep, field to eat, a clear view to keep."
> - "Below five hundred they nest. When lakes freeze, the valley's best."
> - "Red on the map: knock first."

- Number trick: **3 of 3**. Safe water, open food, a clear view: miss one and the geese go elsewhere (Tip).

> [!rule] Old hand's rule (tradition, opinion)
> Find the field, not the goose. The goose comes back to the field; you cannot chase it there. From the app's quick lesson and repeated by goose guides.
