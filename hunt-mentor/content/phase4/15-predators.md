> [!why]
> Predator seasons are the longest seasons in Region 3, and the easiest place for a beginner to make a legal or ethical mistake. Know the animal, the paper and the reason before you shoot.

- Region 3 has seasons for cougar, wolf, coyote, bobcat and lynx; wolverine has no season here (98%).
- Wolf has **no closed season** in MU (Management Unit) 3-17 and MUs 3-26 to 3-44, and coyote is open Sept 1 to June 30 with no bag limit (98%).
- Cougar needs a $30 species licence, Compulsory Inspection (CI) and a kitten rule (98%).
- Bobcat and lynx need an $8 licence each, bag 1, and a Compulsory Report in Region 3 (98%).
- You may use unleashed dogs and electronic calls for these animals (98%).
- Fur is the product. Learn to skin before you hunt, or bring the animal to someone who can (Tip).

## Identify: five animals, two pairs of look alikes


- **Cougar:** light brown cat, compact head, long heavy tail about a third of the body with a black tip (85%). Tracks show no claws (85%). Kittens are spotted (85%).

```photo grey-wolf
```

- **Wolf:** 30 to 50 kg in BC, females about 20% smaller; tail carried out behind when running; big feet, broad muzzle (85%).

```photo coyote
```

- **Coyote:** 9 to 23 kg, bushy black tipped tail usually carried down when running, slim pointed muzzle, tall ears, small feet (85%).

```photo canada-lynx
```

- **Lynx:** long ear tufts, huge furry paws, long legs, grey coat with few markings, short tail with a fully black tip (70%).

```photo bobcat
```

- **Bobcat:** smaller, spotted tan coat, black bars on the front legs, short ear tufts, tail black on top only with a white underside (70%).
- A big dog at 200 m looks like a wolf. A hunter shot a neighbour's dog is a story that ends in court (Tip).

> [!law]
> A cougar kitten is any cougar with spots or under one year old. You may not hunt a kitten or any cougar in its company (98%). You may not hunt a lynx or bobcat that is with another lynx or bobcat (98%).

## Licences, weapons and methods

- Species licences: cougar $30.00 (not valid until 2 days after issue), bobcat $8.00, lynx $8.00, wolverine $8.00; **no species licence for wolf or coyote** for a resident (98%).
- Provincial bag limits: cougar 2, wolf 3, bobcat 5, lynx 1, wolverine 1, with regional limits taking priority (98%).
- **Cougar and wolf:** centrefire rifle, air gun .35 calibre or larger, or shotgun of 20 gauge or larger with No. 1 buck or larger; **no rimfire**; bows A, C, D (98%).
- **Bobcat, lynx, wolverine:** rimfire allowed, plus centrefire, air gun, shotgun as above; bows B, C, D (98%).
- **Coyote:** small game; air, rimfire, centrefire and shotgun all allowed (98%).
- Electronic or recorded calls are allowed only for wolf, coyote, cougar, lynx and bobcat (98%).
- Unleashed dogs may be used to hunt small game, lynx, bobcat, black bear and cougar (98%).
- Bait: the feeding ban on dangerous wildlife makes an exception for lawful hunting where baiting is authorized, for cougar, coyote and wolf only (90%). Bears may never be baited (98%).
- Hours: 1 hour after sunset to 1 hour before sunrise is closed (98%). No lights, no night vision, no infrared (98%).

## Compulsory inspection and reporting

- **Cougar:** CI in any MU within 30 days (4 days in Region 6) (98%). Bring the skull and the hide with a testicle or part of the penis, or a portion of the teats or mammary gland, naturally attached (98%). By appointment at the Kamloops office or a listed inspector (95%).
- 2026 change: evidence of sex must be naturally attached for cougar CI (95%).
- **Lynx in Region 3, bobcat in Regions 2 and 3, wolverine anywhere:** a Compulsory Report within 30 days with location, date, days hunted, weapon and class (98%). File it in WILD (Wildlife Information and Licensing Data), at FrontCounter BC, or by mail (98%).
- **Wolf:** Compulsory Report only in Regions 1 and 2, not Region 3 (98%).
- You are not in legal possession of a cougar until CI is done (98%).

## Fur and meat handling

- Wolf, coyote, wolverine, lynx and bobcat are furbearing animals. You must remove the hide, or the edible portions, to your home, a meat cutter, cold storage, taxidermist, tanner or fur trader (98%).
- Edible portions do **not** have to be kept from wolf, lynx, bobcat or wolverine (98%). They **do** from cougar: neck, ribs, four quarters and loins (98%).
- Selling a pelt taken under a hunting licence needs a royalty paid, unless you sell to a licensed fur trader, who handles it (98%). Royalty applies only to the first sale (98%).
- Furbearing animals may only be hunted where an open hunting season is declared (98%).
- Skin a cat or canine cased (like a sock) the same day; freeze or salt if you cannot flesh it (Tip).

> [!warn]
> Cougar meat carries Trichinella larvae often. Cook it thoroughly; freezing and low heat smoking do not reliably kill the larvae (85%). Wear gloves when skinning any predator (Tip).

## Daily and yearly pattern

- Cougars hunt mostly at dawn and dusk, stalk and ambush, and stay near a covered kill for days (85%). Males may travel 50 km in a day (85%).
- Wolves run in packs of 4 to 8, howl to talk across distance; lone wolves stay silent (85%). Pups born April to May (85%).
- Coyotes breed late January to mid March, pups in April to May; by early winter more than half the population is young of the year (85%).
- Lynx follow snowshoe hares; where hares boom, lynx follow a few years later (Tip).
- Winter is fur season: pelts are prime from about November to February, which matches the cat seasons (Tip).

## Tracks and sign

- Cougar track: round, no claws, four toes, about 8 to 10 cm; a covered carcass with leaves and debris raked over it (85%).
- Wolf track: 10 to 13 cm long with claws, in a straight travel line; coyote about 6 to 7 cm (60%).
- Lynx track: huge and furry, toes hard to see, sinks less than a bobcat's (70%).
- Scat on trail junctions and road edges; wolf scat full of hair and bone (Tip).
- If you find a fresh cougar kill, leave at once and report it to the Conservation Officer Service at 1 877 952 7277 (85%).

## Tactics: options

### Option A: calling coyotes (the beginner's entry)
- Sit at dawn with the wind in your face, play a distress call for 20 to 30 minutes, shotgun or rifle ready.
- Pros: legal all winter, no licence beyond the basic, teaches wind and patience (Tip).
- Cons: coyotes come fast and from downwind; many hunters never see one.

### Option B: hounds for cougar or bobcat
- A dog handler cuts a fresh track in snow and the hounds tree the cat.
- Pros: the only consistent cougar method; you can judge the cat before shooting (hunter consensus) (60%).
- Cons: years of dog training; join an experienced houndsman first.

### Option C: incidental wolves
- Carry a wolf tag mindset on deer and moose hunts; glass meadows and frozen lakes at first light.
- Pros: no extra licence (98%); long season.
- Cons: wolves are rarely seen; most "wolves" are dogs or coyotes at distance.

> [!lean]
> Option A for your first winter. Learn the fur trade while you do it: find a licensed fur trader before you pull a trigger.

## Safety with predators

- Cougar: keep calm, never run, look large, back away slowly with the cat in view; if attacked, fight back (85%). Pick up children at once (85%).
- Wolf: do not stare or take photos, scare it off; back out of a kill site without running (85%).
- Coyote: do not run or turn your back; strike at the ribs if it attacks (85%).
- No coyote rabies has been reported in BC (85%), but gloves and a mask for skinning are still wise (Tip).
- A wounded cat in thick cover is the most dangerous moment of the hunt (Tip).

## Ethics for a beginner

- The synopsis asks hunters to "respect wildlife and the sanctity of the harvested animals' lives" and to make every kill clean (98%).
- A predator is not vermin. Shoot it for fur, for meat (cougar), or for a management reason you can explain, and use what you take (Tip).
- No bag limit is not a goal. Leaving three coyotes to rot in a field is legal and still wrong (Tip).
- Government wolf reduction for caribou recovery is a separate program; your Region 3 wolf hunt is not part of it (95%).
- Public opinion on predator hunting is sharp. Keep photos private and respectful (Tip).

## Shot placement (brief)

- Cats and canines are thin skinned; a broadside lung shot behind the shoulder drops them (Tip).
- Shotgun on coyotes: 40 m maximum with No. 1 buck or larger, aim at the chest (Tip).
- Treed cougar: wait for a clear angle through the chest, with a backstop; a wounded cat falls into the dogs (Tip).

## Beginner mistakes

> [!mistake]
> - Shooting a cougar with kittens nearby. Watch the snow for small tracks first.
> - Hunting bobcat or lynx with a .22 and no Compulsory Report plan. The report is due in 30 days.
> - Shooting a "wolf" that is a dog, or a "lynx" that is a bobcat with its own rules and licence.

## Grandpa's rules

> [!rule]
> If you would not skin it, do not shoot it.

> [!rule]
> The wind tells the coyote everything. Set up where he has to come to you to smell you.

> [!field]
> This winter: buy a mouth call, sit for three dawns on a Region 3 hayfield edge with permission, and keep a log of what came and from where.
