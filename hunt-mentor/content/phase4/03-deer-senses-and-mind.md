> [!why]
> A deer's whole life is staying alive. Think like a scared prey animal and you stop spooking them.

```photo g-md-doe-front
```

- Nose first, then ears, then eyes (Tip).
- Wind and **thermals** (air sliding up or down a slope) carry your scent (80%).

```more the key points
- A deer trusts its **nose** first, then its **ears**, then its **eyes** (Tip, ranking based on the facts below).
- Its nose almost never lies. Its eyes are great at motion, poor at detail (85%).
- Wind and **thermals** (air sliding up or down a slope) decide where your scent goes (80%).
- Alarm has stages: alert, stamp, snort, run. You can sometimes stop it at stage 1 (Tip).
- Cougars kill more southern Interior mule deer than any other cause. Deer are built around that fear (85%).
- Hunters change deer too: pressure pushes them into thicker cover and night movement (70%).
```

## Eyes

```diagram sg-deer-vision
```

- No red cone: blaze orange looks dull to a deer (75%).
- Motion and outline give you away, not colour (85%).

```more cones, UV and lights
- Deer have 2 colour cones, peaking near 455 nm (blue) and 537 nm (green). No red cone (85%).
- So blaze orange looks dull, close to grey or brown, to a deer (75%).
- Deer pick up more ultraviolet (UV) light than we do (75%).
- Some laundry detergents add UV brighteners that may glow to a deer (Tip) (65%). Use unscented, no brightener soap.
- Motion and outline give you away, not colour (85%).
- Lights: a moving headlamp or phone screen in the dark is pure motion. Red light, pointed at the ground (Tip).
```

## Ears

```diagram sg-deer-hearing
```

- Zips, Velcro and metal sit right in its best band (85%).
- Walk, stop, walk, like a deer (Tip).

```more hearing details and the walking tip
- Deer hear about 115 Hz (hertz) to 54 kHz (kilohertz). Best around 4 to 8 kHz (85%).
- That band is exactly zippers, Velcro, nylon swish, metal on metal, and snapping twigs (85%).
- Each ear swivels on its own to point at a sound (75%).
- Natural sounds (a squirrel, a deer walking, wind) are filtered out. Odd rhythms and metal are not (Tip).

> [!tip]
> Two legged walking is a steady "crunch crunch crunch". A deer walks, stops, walks. Copy that rhythm.
```

## Nose

```diagram sg-scent-cone
```

- Smell is its strongest sense (85%).
- Cover scents help a little. The wind helps a lot (Tip).

```more scent sources, folklore and the lure rule
- Smell is a deer's strongest sense (85%).
- The often quoted "297 million scent receptors" figure is folklore, not a measured fact (45%).
- Your scent comes from breath, skin, clothes, boots and your truck (Tip).
- Cover scents and scent control help a little. The wind helps a lot (Tip).
- Deer and elk urine lures from outside BC are banned for hunting (page 12 item 51) (95%). The synopsis warns that urine based scents can spread [[CWD]] (Chronic Wasting Disease) (inside back cover) (99%).
```

## Wind and thermals

```anim sg-thermal-day Morning scent rises, evening scent sinks.
```

- Morning: approach from above or the side (80%).
- Evening: hunt from below or the side (80%).

```diagram deer-hillside
```

```more thermal details, my lean and the wind check
- Sun warms the slope in the morning. Warm air rises, so **scent drifts uphill** (80%).
- Slopes cool in the evening. Cool air sinks, so **scent drifts downhill** (80%).
- At dawn before the sun hits, air often still sinks. It flips after sun warms the slope (75%).
- Draws and saddles swirl the air. Your scent can go everywhere (Tip).
- Main wind usually beats thermals when it blows hard (Tip).

> [!lean]
> Morning: approach from above or from the side. Evening: hunt from below or the side, and leave before the cold air drains your scent down to the bedded deer.

> [!field]
> Carry a puffer bottle of unscented powder or a thread on your rifle. Check it every 10 minutes.
```

## When a deer is alarmed

```steps 5 stages of alarm
sg-alarm-1 | Alert: head up, staring. Freeze.
sg-alarm-2 | Head bob: fakes feeding, snaps up.
sg-alarm-3 | Foot stamp: it wants you to move.
sg-alarm-4 | Snort: every deer near is alert.
sg-alarm-5 | Escape: mule deer stot, white tails flag.
```

- Stared at? Do not move a finger. Count to 100 (Tip).

```more each stage in words
1. **Alert:** head up, ears forward, stare. Freeze until it looks away (Tip).
2. **Head bob:** drops the head as if to feed, then snaps it up to catch you moving (hunter lore) (70%).
3. **Foot stamp:** stamps a front hoof. Warns other deer and tries to make you move (70%).
4. **Snort or blow:** a loud whoosh of air. All deer nearby are now on alert (70%).
5. **Escape:**
  - Mule deer **stot**: bounces on all 4 feet, good for steep broken ground (85%).
  - White tail **flags** and gallops to cover (85%).

- Mule deer often stop and look back from about 150 to 300 m (hunter lore) (65%).

> [!tip]
> If a deer stares at you, do not move a finger. Count to 100. Most deer go back to feeding if nothing else happens.
```

## Predator pressure

```diagram sg-bed-spot
```

- Cougars kill the most southern Interior mule deer, coyotes next (85%).
- So deer bed where they can see below and smell above (Tip).

```more predators and how deer respond
- In the southern Interior mule deer project, cougars were the top cause of death, coyotes second (85%).
- Wolves have come back to the Thompson area (85%).
- Against cougars (ambush hunters), deer pick spots with long sightlines (80%).
- Against wolves and dogs (chase hunters), deer use rugged steep slopes (80%).
- Does live in family groups: more eyes and ears (85%).
- What this means for you: deer bed where they can **see below and smell above** (Tip).
```

## Human pressure

```photo g-md-run-away
```

- Pressured deer move at night and use thick cover (70%).
- Walk 500 m past the road. Close the door with your hip (Tip).

```more human pressure and my lean
- Pressured deer move more at night and use thicker cover (70%).
- Opening days and weekends push deer away from roads (Tip).
- Deer learn sounds: a truck door slam at a pullout can clear a slope (Tip).
- Deer return to the same home range year after year, so spooking them is not forever (85%).

> [!lean]
> Walk 500 m or more past the road and the pullouts. Park quietly. Close the door with your hip.
```


| Deer can't see orange | It looks dull. Motion gives you away (75%) |
| 297 million scent receptors | Unmeasured folklore (45%) |
| The rut makes mule deer migrate | Snow and cold do (85%) |
| Urine lures are a must | Banned if from outside BC (99%) |

```more the full folklore table
| Folklore | What we know |
|---|---|
| Deer can't see blaze orange | Blaze orange looks dull to deer. Motion still gives you away (75%) |
| Deer have 297 million scent receptors | Unmeasured folklore. Nose is still their best sense (45%) |
| Bucks always bed on the top third of a slope | Often true, not a rule (lore) (60%) |
| The rut makes mule deer migrate | Snow and cold trigger migration (85%) |
| Deer urine lures are a must | Deer products from outside BC are banned for hunting (page 12 item 51) (99%). Wind beats lures (Tip) |
| Grunt calls work all season | Rattling and calling work best near the rut, early November (70%) |
```

## Mistakes and rules

```diagram sg-alarm-3
```

> [!mistake]
> - Checking the wind once at the truck, then never again.
> - Moving when a deer is staring at you.
> - Wearing loud nylon and a jingling pack.

> [!rule]
> When a deer stamps, you've already lost the eyes. Don't lose the ears too. Freeze.

> [!rule]
> The deer never makes a mistake with its nose. Make yours with the wind in your favour.
