> [!why]
> A spring gobbler is the loudest, most exciting bird in BC and the only one you hunt by talking to it. It is also the one hunt where another hunter can mistake you for the bird.

```photo g-turkey-tom A Merriam's tom: the bird you call in.
```

- **No turkey season in Region 3.** Nearest: Region 8, then Region 4 (95%).
- Spring: bearded birds only. Fall: any turkey (98%).

```more what this session covers
- BC's turkeys are **Merriam's** wild turkeys, introduced and spread from the United States (75%).
- Strongest numbers in the Kootenays (Region 4), then the south Okanagan (Region 8) and parts of Vancouver Island (75%).
- **Region 3 has no turkey season** (95%). Your nearest hunt is Region 8, then Region 4.
- Spring seasons are **bearded birds only**; fall seasons are any turkey (98%).
- No species licence; a turkey is an upland game bird on your basic licence (98%).
- A shotgun or a .22 rimfire is legal; a centrefire rifle is not (98%).
```

## Identify: tom, hen, jake


- Look for the beard first, then the head (Tip).
- Merriam's: white tips on the tail fan (75%).

```more every mark, and the legal meaning of bearded
- **Tom (gobbler):** big, dark, bronze body, bare red, white and blue head, a beard of bristles on the chest, spurs on the legs, a full round tail fan (75%).
- **Hen:** smaller, duller brown, blue grey head, usually no beard (75%). A few hens grow a thin beard and are legal in a bearded season (Tip).
- **Jake:** a year old tom. Short beard about 5 to 10 cm, short spurs, uneven fan with longer centre feathers (hunter lore) (65%).
- Merriam's have **white tips** on the tail fan and rump feathers (75%).

> [!law]
> The spring class is "Bearded". In law, a bearded turkey has a tuft of hair like feathers on the chest that grows larger with age (98%). Any bird with a visible beard qualifies; sex does not matter.

"BEARDED TURKEY - means a turkey with a tuft of hair-like feathers on the chest that grow larger with age." (Synopsis page 3) (98%)
```

## Where in BC

```diagram sb-turkey-history
```

- Kootenays and the south Okanagan. Roosts in big pines on ridges (75%).

> [!lean]
> From Heffley Creek: Region 8 is 2 to 3 hours away and has a short spring season in the MUs (Management Units) listed below. Region 4 is a longer drive but has three times the season.

```more history, numbers and habitat
- First release: James Island near Vancouver Island, 1910 (75%).
- 1960s: birds spread north from Washington, Idaho and Montana into southern BC (75%).
- 1980s: the province moved birds from the Creston Valley to the East Kootenay (75%).
- Now: the valleys of the East and West Kootenay and the southeast Okanagan, with about 4,000 to 5,000 birds in 2004 and more since (search preview) (75%).
- Region 4 hunters took about 620 turkeys a year from 2016 to 2020 (search preview) (70%).
- Habitat: open conifer and deciduous forest edges in foothills; roosts in large pines on ridges (75%).
```

## Bearded bird rules and the rest of the law

```diagram rb-evidence-birds
```

- Beard and one feathered wing stay on until home (98%).
- Shotgun, air gun, .22 or bow. No centrefire (98%).

```diagram sb-upland-weapons
```

```more every rule in full
- Spring is a bearded only season: the beard must stay attached to the carcass, along with one feathered wing, until the bird is home (98%).
- Hours: no hunting from 1 hour after sunset to 1 hour before sunrise (98%).
- Weapons: shotgun, air gun or **.22 rimfire** allowed; centrefire rifle is for grouse or ptarmigan only, not turkey (98%). Bows B, C and D allowed (98%).
- Lead shot is still legal for upland birds including turkey (98%).
- Recorded or electronic calls and live decoys are unlawful for game birds (98%). Mouth, box and slate calls are fine (Tip).
- No species licence for residents; a non resident needs the $50 upland game bird licence (98%).
- Possession limit for upland birds is three times the daily limit (98%). With a season limit of 1 or 2 that rarely matters (Tip).
- Dogs: allowed for game birds, and may be trained on game birds Aug 1 to Apr 30 (98%).
```

## Daily and yearly pattern

```diagram sb-turkey-day
```

- Down from the roost at first light, back up at dusk (75%).

```more spring, fall and snow
- Roosts in tall trees at dusk, flies down at first light, feeds and travels through the morning, loafs midday, feeds again late afternoon (75%).
- Spring: toms gobble on the roost and strut for hens from mid April; breeding and nesting run through May (75%).
- Fall: flocks of hens with young, and separate groups of toms; birds scratch for seeds, acorns, insects and waste grain (Tip).
- Snow pack often lingers until a week or two before the spring opener (75%).
```

## Tactics: options

```diagram sb-turkey-setup
```

- Find the roost the evening before. Set up 100 m away before light (Tip).

> [!lean]
> Option A in Region 8 on opening week. Scout the roost two days before.

```anim sb-turkey-walkin He gobbles, flies down, goes quiet, walks in.
```

```more the three options, pros and cons
### Option A: roost and call at dawn
- Locate a roost the evening before. Set up 100 m away before light with a hen decoy, call softly as the sun comes up.
- Pros: the classic hunt; a gobbler often flies down to your decoy (75%).
- Cons: one mistake in setup and the birds go the other way.

### Option B: run and gun with locator calls
- Walk ridges midday, use an owl or crow call to make a tom "shock gobble", then move close and call.
- Pros: finds birds when roost hunts fail (75%).
- Cons: more walking, more chance of bumping birds.

### Option C: fall flock busting
- Find a flock, scatter it, sit down and call the young birds back.
- Pros: works in October with no gobbling.
- Cons: easy to break the law on bag limits with a flock in front of you.
```

## Shotgun, load and range

```diagram sb-turkey-range
```

- Head and neck only. Max about 40 m (Tip).

```more loads, patterns and the .22
- A 12 gauge with a full or turkey choke, patterned at 30 and 40 m (33 and 44 yd) (Tip).
- Shot sizes 4, 5 or 6 lead, or heavier tungsten loads (Tip). An example from an experienced hunter: 2 ounce loads of number 5 shot patterned to 50 m (75%).
- Maximum range: where 8 or more pellets land in the head and neck of a paper turkey, usually 40 m (Tip).
- Aim at the **head and neck**, never the body; body shots waste the bird and often fail (Tip).
- A .22 rimfire is legal; it is a precise tool for a still bird's head at short range, not a flying shot (Tip).
```

## Safety

```diagram sb-turkey-safety
```

- You sound like a turkey. Dress so no one thinks you are one (Tip).

```more safety in full
- Never wear red, white or blue; those are a tom's head colours (Tip).
- Sit with your back against a tree wider than your shoulders (Tip).
- Never stalk a gobble. Another hunter may be making it (Tip).
- When carrying a dead bird, wrap it in blaze orange (Tip).
```

## Meat

| Part | How (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Breast | Lean: brine it |
| Legs, thighs | Tough: braise them |
| Beard, spurs, fan | Save for the wall |

```more meat in full
- Pluck or skin, keep the wing and beard attached until home (98%).
- Breast is lean; brine it. Legs and thighs are tough; braise them (Tip).
- Save the beard, spurs and fan for your wall (Tip).
```

## Beginner mistakes

| Mistake | Do this instead |
|---|---|
| A second bird in Region 8 | 1 per season there |
| Calling loud and often | Quiet, confident calls |
| Standing when he goes silent | Stay still: he is coming |

```more the mistakes in full
> [!mistake]
> - Shooting the second bird in a flock in Region 8, where the limit is one per season.
> - Calling too much and too loud. Hens call little; toms come to quiet, confident calls.
> - Standing up when the gobbler goes silent. He is probably walking in.
```
