> [!why]
> Ducks come in fast, in poor light, and some have their own bag limits. You must name the bird before you pull the trigger, from the side, from below and in the air.

- Each screen shows one species group: drake (male), hen (female) and, where we found one, a flight shot.
- In fall, many drakes are still in drab [[Eclipse plumage|eclipse plumage]] early in the season and look like hens (80%).
- Learn the wing patch, the [[Speculum|speculum]]: it shows on both sexes and in flight (80%).
- Learn the shape too: [[Puddle duck|puddle ducks]] jump straight up off the water; [[Diver|divers]] run along the surface to take off (80%).
- Photos are illustrations. The written field marks are the source of truth.

## American wigeon

```gallery
wigeon-drake | Drake, Reifel sanctuary, Ladner, BC. White crown, green band behind the eye.
wigeon-hen | Hen. Grey head, rusty sides, small blue grey bill with a black tip.
g-wigeon-flight | Drake dropping in, South Dakota. White shoulder patches.
```

- Drake: white forehead and crown, so hunters call it baldpate (80%).
- Both sexes: short blue grey bill with a black tip (80%). In flight the drake shows a big white patch on the front of the wing (80%).

## Green winged teal

```gallery
green-winged-teal-drake | Drake. Chestnut head, green swoosh, white bar on the side.
green-winged-teal-hen | Hen, California. Very small brown duck, small dark bill.
g-gwt-flight | Flock in flight, Wyoming. Small, fast, bunched.
blue-winged-teal-drake | Look alike: blue winged teal drake in flight. White face crescent, pale blue forewing.
```

- The smallest dabbling duck in BC. Flocks twist and turn in tight bunches (80%).
- Blue winged teal mostly leave BC early in fall (Tip).

## Gadwall

```gallery
gadwall-drake | Drake. Plain grey, black rear end, dark bill.
gadwall-hen | Hen, North Dakota. Orange sided bill, white wing patch.
gadwall-drake-flight | Drake in flight, Oregon. Small square white patch on the back of the wing.
```

- The only puddle duck with a white speculum (80%).
- A gadwall hen looks like a mallard hen. Check the wing: white, not blue (80%).

## Northern shoveler

```gallery
shoveler-drake | Drake, North Dakota. Spoon bill, green head, white chest, rusty sides.
shoveler-hen | Hen. Mottled brown with a long spoon bill.
g-shoveler-flight | Drake in flight. Spoon bill, pale blue forewing, rusty belly.
g-shoveler-flock | Flock taking off, Texas.
```

- The bill is longer than the head and wide at the tip (80%). Flies with the bill pointed slightly down (Tip).

## Wood duck

```gallery
wood-duck-drake | Drake. Green crest, white chin straps, red eye.
wood-duck-hen | Hen. White teardrop around the eye.
g-wood-duck-flight | Drake in flight, Texas. Long square tail, head up.
```

- Lives on wooded ponds, beaver ponds and sloughs with trees (80%).
- In flight: long square tail, head held up, bill pointed down (Tip).

## Ring necked duck and scaup

```gallery
ring-necked-duck-drake | Ring necked drake, New Mexico. Peaked head, white ring on the bill, white spur.
ring-necked-duck-hen | Ring necked hen. White eye ring, pale band on the bill.

lesser-scaup-hen | Scaup hen. Bold white patch at the bill base.
```

- Ring necked drake: black back. Scaup drake: grey back (80%).
- Ring necked ducks like small ponds and marshes; scaup like big lakes and the coast (Tip).

## Bufflehead and goldeneyes

```gallery
bufflehead-drake | Bufflehead drake, California. Tiny, white wedge on the back of the head.
bufflehead-hen | Bufflehead hen. One white oval on the cheek.
common-goldeneye-drake | Common goldeneye drake. Round white spot, yellow eye.
barrows-goldeneye-drake | Barrow's goldeneye drake, Wyoming. White crescent, more black on the sides.
common-goldeneye-hen | Common goldeneye hen. Brown head, yellow eye.

```

- Goldeneye wings whistle in flight (80%).
- Goldeneyes have their own limit inside the duck bag: 2 a day, Barrow's and common together (99%, page 12, 2 October 2026 edition).

## Bufflehead and goldeneye in flight

```gallery
g-bufflehead-flight | Bufflehead drake, California. Big white wing patches, white head wedge.
g-goldeneye-flight | Common goldeneye hen. Brown head, white wing patches.
```

- Both fly fast and low over open water, often in small groups (Tip).
- Black and white small diver with a white head wedge: bufflehead. Bigger, with a round white face spot: goldeneye drake (80%).

## Canvasback and redhead

```gallery
canvasback-drake | Canvasback drake, Maryland. Long sloping black bill, rusty head, white back.
canvasback-hen | Canvasback hen. Pale, same sloping profile.
redhead-drake | Redhead drake. Round head, grey back, blue bill with a black tip.
redhead-hen | Redhead hen, Ontario. Plain brown, round head.
g-redhead-flight | Redheads in flight, South Dakota. Round red heads, black chests.
```

- Profile tells them apart: canvasback forehead runs straight into the bill; redhead has a round head (80%).
- Canvasback has its own limit inside the duck bag: 4 a day (99%, page 12).

## Taking off and in the air

```gallery
divers-taking-off | Diving ducks running across the water to take off, South Dakota.
mallards-flying | Mallards taking off. Every bird shows the blue wing patch.
mallards-flushing | A flock flushing off a wetland, Nebraska. Pick one bird and name it.
```

- Puddle ducks spring straight up. Divers patter across the water first (80%).
- Never shoot into a flock. Pick one bird you have named (Tip).

## Do not shoot

```gallery
g-loon | Common loon. Dagger bill, checkered back.
g-western-grebe | Western grebes. Long white neck, thin yellow bill.
g-horned-grebe | Horned grebe in winter. Small, black cap, white cheeks.
g-trumpeter-swan | Trumpeter swan. Huge, all white, black bill.
g-tundra-swans-flight | Tundra swans in flight. No black wing tips.
```

> [!warn] Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Loons and grebes have no open season. They are protected (90%).
- Swans: no open season in BC (VERIFY in the ECCC, Environment and Climate Change Canada, BC migratory bird summary). An all white bird bigger than a goose, with no black wing tips, is a swan (80%).
- Harlequin duck is legal in BC, but only 2 a day inside the duck bag (99%, page 12).

## Grandpa's rule

> [!rule]
> If you cannot name it, it is not a duck yet. Let it go and wait for the next one.

## Common mistakes

> [!mistake]
> - Shooting at a dark shape at first light before you can see the wing patch.
> - Calling every small white duck a bufflehead. Check the head pattern.
> - Shooting a grebe or a loon that dove and came up near the decoys.

## Do this in the field

> [!field]
> - Sit at a marsh with binoculars for an hour. Name each duck: species, drake or hen.
> - Watch how each one takes off. Jump up, or run along the water?
> - Count how many goldeneyes and canvasbacks you would have in a bag of 8.
