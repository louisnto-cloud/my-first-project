> [!why]
> These four live where people live: orchards, vineyards, farm edges and dry hillsides. The hunting is easy to find and hard to do safely. Permission and a safe shot are the whole game.

```diagram sb-upland-weapons
```

- Shotgun, air gun or bow. No .22, no rifle for these four (99%).
- BC licence only. No species licence, no federal permit (99%).

```more what this session covers
- California quail, chukar, grey (Hungarian) partridge and ring necked pheasant are all upland game birds in BC (99%).
- Residents need only the BC hunting licence. The fee table on page 8 says "No Species licence required" for upland game birds (99%). No federal permit (99%).
- Legal for these four: a shotgun, an air rifle, or bows B, C and D. No rimfire (.22) and no centrefire rifle (99%, page 13 table and notes 2 and 3).
- This goes deeper than [Your first quail trip](#/s/first-quail-trip): all four birds, every region you hunt, and the exact rows from the [[Synopsis]].
```

## California quail

```gallery
g-quail-male-vernon | Male: black face outlined in white, big plume.
g-quail-female | Female: grey brown head, small plume.
g-quail-pair | A pair: male on the left.
hab-quail-vernon | Quail live in brushy farm and town edges.
```

- Smallest here: 140 to 230 g (80%). Curved black topknot.
- Mountain quail: no open season. Straight plume (99%).

```more marks, coveys, food and the look alike
- 140 to 230 g, the smallest bird here (80%). Curved black topknot, scaled belly, white stripes on grey brown sides (80%).
- **Male:** black face and throat outlined in white, bigger plume, chestnut belly patch (80%). **Female:** grey brown head, small plume, no black face (80%).
- **Young** by October look like small, duller females (Tip, 65%).
- Lives in coveys most of the year, often 20 to 60 birds in fall (Tip, 65%). Runs first, then bursts into fast low flight on whirring wings (80%).
- Feeds on seeds, leaves, berries and waste grain, mostly from the ground, with a sentinel on a post (80%).
- Look alike: no other quail is legal here. "No open season on mountain quail." Region 1, page 21 (99%). Mountain quail has a straight plume and broad white bars on red sides (80%).
```

## Chukar

```photo g-chukar Chukar: black necklace, red bill, barred flanks.
```

```anim sb-chukar-run They run up, then fly down. Get above them.
```

- Steep dry rocky slopes, 250 to 500 m (85%).
- Start at springs and creek mouths below cliffs (Tip).

```more marks, habitat, coveys and water
- 540 to 765 g (80%). Sandy grey with a bold black band through the eyes forming a necklace around a cream throat, red bill and legs, black and chestnut bars on the flanks (80%).
- Sexes look alike; the male is a little heavier (80%). Young of the year are duller with a less sharp necklace (Tip, 60%).
- Dry, steep, rocky slopes with bunchgrass, sagebrush and rabbitbrush, mostly 250 to 500 m in BC (85%).
- Fall coveys of 10 to 40 run uphill and flush downhill across the slope (hunter lore, 65%). Loud "chuk chuk chukar" from the cliffs (80%).
- Needs water in dry weather unless green feed is around (80%). Springs and creek mouths below cliffs are the place to start (Tip).
- Look alike: grey partridge is smaller and plainer with an orange face; a hen pheasant is far bigger with a long tail (Tip).
```

## Grey (Hungarian) partridge

```diagram sb-partridge-id
```

- Orange face, chestnut horseshoe, rusty tail (80%).
- Stubble, hedgerows, pasture. Which fields: VERIFY locally.

```more marks, habitat and where in BC
- 300 to 500 g (80%). Grey below, orange face and throat, a dark chestnut horseshoe on the belly, rusty tail feathers show in flight (80%).
- The horseshoe is smaller or missing on the female (80%). Young are browner with a weak face patch (Tip, 60%).
- Cultivated land, stubble, hedgerows, grassy pastures; the covey burrows into snow in winter (80%).
- Coveys of a family group flush together with a whirr and glide, then run (80%). They rarely fly far (Tip).
- The synopsis spells it "GRAY (Hungarian) PARTRIDGE" (99%). Hunters say "Huns".
- Where in BC: there are seasons in Regions 3 and 8, so birds exist in the dry farm country of both (90%). Which fields hold them: VERIFY locally.
```

## Ring necked pheasant

```gallery
g-pheasant-rooster | Cock: green head, red wattles, white ring. Legal.
g-pheasant-hen | Hen: brown, long tail. Never legal.
```

- **Cocks only** in Regions 2, 3 and 8 (99%).
- Cannot see the ring or the colour? No shot (Tip).

```more marks, habitat, food and where in BC
- Cock: 1.1 to 1.4 kg, iridescent green head, red face wattles, white neck ring, copper body, tail over half its 76 to 91 cm length (80%). Loud "caw cawk" crow and a cackle on flushing (80%).
- Hen: mottled brown, pale belly, long pointed tail, smaller (80%). **Only cocks are legal** in Regions 2, 3 and 8 (99%).
- In poor light a cock looks dark. If you cannot see the white ring or the colour, there is no shot (Tip).
- Habitat: farm fields with cover, hedgerows, ditches, marsh edges, cattails, brush (80%). Not very dry places (80%).
- Feeds on grain, seeds, shoots and insects on the ground, mornings and late afternoon, and roosts in thick grass or cattails (80%).
- Where in BC: small, local wild numbers; farm edges of the Fraser Valley, the Thompson and the Okanagan (Tip, 60%). Many birds anywhere are released stock (Tip, 60%).
```

## Daily pattern and sign

```diagram hf-quail-day
```

- Feed at first light and the last 2 hours. Rest midday (75%).
- At dawn, stand still and listen for calls (Tip).

```more sign, snow and hours
- All four feed at first light and again the last 2 hours before dark, loaf in cover midday, and roost together at night (75%).
- Quail and chukar call at dawn. Stand still and listen before you walk (Tip).
- Dust bowls on dirt roads and under fence lines, with small tracks and droppings, mark covey loafing spots (Tip).
- Chukar droppings pile up under roost rocks and on cliff ledges (Tip). Pheasant tracks are big, three toed and walk in long lines along field edges (Tip).
- Snow after a cold night pushes coveys to the sunny side of a slope and to any bare ground (Tip, 60%).
- Hunting hours: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (99%).
```

## Rifles banned for these four birds

```diagram sb-upland-weapons
```

- No .22, no centrefire for these four (95%).
- Lead shot is legal for upland birds (99%). Plug: 3 shells max (95%).

```diagram gr-lead-ok
```

```more the page 13 words, lead, plug and loads
> [!law]
> BC synopsis, page 13, "Upland Game Birds" row: Rimfire "Yes2", Centrefire "Yes3". Note 2: "Grouse, ptarmigan, or turkey only." Note 3: "Grouse or ptarmigan only." (99%)

- Read together: a .22 or any rifle is **not** legal for quail, chukar, partridge or pheasant (95%). The same row allows an air gun, a shotgun and bows B, C and D (97%).
- Handguns are unlawful for hunting (99%). Shotguns for upland birds: any gauge, no gauge limit is printed for this row (90%).
- Lead shot is legal for upland birds: the shot ban "does not apply to upland game birds (grouse, ptarmigan, quail, partridge, pheasants, and turkey)" (99%).
- Exception in Region 2: no lead shot "when discharging a firearm on any dyke or on the water side" of any dyke in Delta, or on foreshore dykes facing Mud Bay in Surrey (99%). Not near Mission, but know it.
- Plug rule: a pump or auto shotgun must have a plug so the magazine holds no more than 2, so 3 in total with the chamber, for all hunting in BC (95%).
- Loads (Tip): quail and partridge, lead 7.5 or 6, or steel 6; chukar and pheasant, lead 5 or 6, or steel 3 or 4. Improved cylinder for quail, modified for pheasant. Pattern it first.
```

## Tactics without a dog

```anim sb-covey-regroup The covey scatters. Mark where each bird lands.
```

```video
yt-upland-basics | Walk the cover slowly; the stop makes birds flush.
```

- No dog: shoot only over short cover, walk straight to the fall (Tip).
- Retrieval is the law (99%).

```diagram sb-upland-pinch
```

```anim hf-covey-flush Pick one bird, high, in your own wedge.
```

> [!lean]
> Option A for quail and partridge, Option C once you are fit and know one chukar slope. Pheasant only with a landowner who wants them gone.

```more the three options, pros and cons, and the retrieval law
### Option A: the straight line walk
- Two hunters 15 to 25 m apart, walking slowly through cover edges, stopping every 20 steps (Tip). The stop makes nervous birds flush.
- Pros: works for quail, partridge and pheasant. Cons: running birds slip ahead; you need to mark every fall.

### Option B: pinch the cover
- One hunter walks a hedgerow or draw toward the other, who stands at the end in the open (Tip). Birds flush toward the blocker.
- Pros: cuts off runners. Cons: the blocker is in the line of fire. Agree a no shot arc before you start (Tip).

### Option C: chukar from above
- Climb above the slope on an old road, then work down and across. Chukar run up and flush down, so being above them turns a 300 m chase into a shot (65%).
- Pros: real chukar odds. Cons: steep, loose rock, long days.

- No dog means every bird must fall where you can find it. Shoot only over short cover and walk straight to the spot, eyes on it (Tip).
- Retrieval is the law: "No person shall kill, cripple or wound game without making all reasonable effort to locate, dispatch, retrieve and include it in their bag limit." (99%)
```

## Safety near orchards, vineyards and homes

```diagram rb-100m-ring
```

- 100 m from any occupied house, barn or school (99%).
- Orchard = cultivated land: ask first (95%).

```diagram hf-low-bird
```

```more the exact words, bylaws and orchard habits
- "It is unlawful to hunt or discharge a firearm within 100 m of a church, school building, school yard, playground, regional district park, dwelling house, or farm or ranch building that is occupied by persons or domestic animals." (99%)
- A packing shed with workers, a barn with horses, a worker cabin: all count (95%). Rangefind every building before the first flush (Tip).
- "A person is not permitted to hunt on cultivated land or on Crown land which is subject to a grazing lease while the land is occupied by livestock, without the consent of the owner, lessee, or occupant of the land." (99%) An orchard is [[Cultivated land]] (95%).
- Municipal bylaws are not in the synopsis (99%). Kelowna, Penticton, Osoyoos and the regional districts have their own firearm rules: VERIFY each one.
- Shot falls 150 to 250 m away. Never shoot toward a road, a house, a crew, a vehicle or a ladder, no matter how far (Tip).
- Low birds in brush are off limits. A quail at knee height has a hidden person behind it more often than you think (Tip).
- Orchards have irrigation pipe, ladders, bins and people all season. Ask where the crew is today, and leave gates as you found them (Tip).
```

## Care and yield

```diagram sb-bird-yield
```

- One feathered wing stays on until home (99%).
- Mesh vest, cool fast in October sun (Tip).

```more care steps and yields
- One feathered wing stays on each bird until it is home (99%). Both breasts must come home (99%).
- Quail: pluck or skin, gut with a thumb. A quail gives about 60 to 80 g of meat, one per person as a starter (Tip, 60%).
- Chukar and partridge: skin and breast, keep the legs. About 150 to 250 g of meat per bird (Tip, 60%).
- Pheasant cock: hang a day in the cold, pluck, roast whole, or breast out and braise the legs. About 450 to 600 g of meat (Tip, 60%).
- Cool fast in October sun. A game vest with a mesh back beats a plastic bag (Tip).
- Count birds by species as you clean them and write the tally in the Journal with the MU (Tip).
```

## Beginner mistakes

| Mistake | Do this instead |
|---|---|
| A quail at Heffley Creek | Region 3 has no quail season |
| A hen pheasant in bad light | See the white ring, or no shot |
| An orchard with no sign | Ask first: cultivated land |
| A .22 for chukar | Shotgun only (95%) |

```more the mistakes in full
> [!mistake]
> - Shooting a quail at Heffley Creek. Region 3 has no quail season.
> - A hen pheasant in bad light. Cocks only, and if you cannot see the ring you cannot shoot.
> - Walking into an orchard without asking because nobody was there. Cultivated land needs consent, posted or not.

- Also: a .22 for "sitting" chukar. Rifles are not legal for these birds (95%).
- Also: shooting at the covey instead of one bird, then shooting again at a low straggler toward your partner (Tip).
```
