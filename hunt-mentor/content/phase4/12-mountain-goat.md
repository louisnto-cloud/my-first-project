> [!why]
> Half the world's mountain goats live in BC, and the one thing a goat hunter must get right is billy or nanny. Nannies carry the herd.

```photo g-goat-radium
```

- Billy or nanny is the whole game. Shoot billies only (98%).
- Region 3: draw only, no general season (95%).
- Licence $40.00, bag 1, every goat inspected (98%).

```more the key points
- BC holds about 39,000 to 65,500 mountain goats, roughly half of all goats in the world (85%).
- Goats are blue listed in BC (S3) because of local declines and disturbance (85%).
- Billies and nannies look alike. The law protects some nannies, and the Ministry asks you to shoot only billies (98%).
- Region 3 has **no general open season**; goat hunting near home is LEH (Limited Entry Hunting) only (95%).
- Every goat goes to Compulsory Inspection (CI) (98%). Species licence: $40.00 (98%). Bag limit: 1 (98%).
- Recovery is the hidden danger: a goat shot on a cliff is a goat lost (Tip).
```

## Billy or nanny, step by step

```steps Billy or nanny in 6 steps
sc-goat-1 | Find the whole group first. Look for hidden kids.
sc-goat-2 | Kids or yearlings in it? Nannies. Do not shoot.
sc-goat-3 | Alone, or 2 or 3 adults, no young? Likely billies.
sc-goat-4 | Check the horn base: thick and evenly curved is a billy.
sc-goat-5 | Check the body and pee posture.
sc-goat-6 | Watch 10 minutes. Not sure? No shot.
```

- Behaviour is the best clue. Horns come second (85%).

## Identify: billy or nanny

```diagram goat-sex
```

- Both sexes: white, black dagger horns up to 30 cm, never shed (85%).
- A **kid** in law: horns under 10 cm (98%).

```more every identification clue
- Both sexes are white with black, dagger like horns up to 30 cm long, never shed (85%).
- **Billies:** 70 to 120 kg on average. Horns thicker at the base, curving evenly along the whole length (85%).
- **Nannies:** 55 to 75 kg. Horns more slender at the base and a bit more curved toward the tip (85%).
- **Behaviour is the best clue:** lone adults and groups of 2 or 3 with no kids or yearlings are usually billies (85%).
- A group with kids (under 1 year) or yearlings is almost certainly nannies, except in the mating season, about early November to early December (85%, BC mountain goat management plan 2010).
- A **kid** in law is a goat with horns under 10 cm (98%).
- Billies shed their winter coat by early July, nannies later; in fall both are white and shaggy (85%).
- Pee posture: billies stretch forward, nannies squat (hunter lore) (60%).

> [!tip]
> Spend a long time in the spotting scope. Compare horn base to eye width and watch how the goat behaves with others before you decide.
```

## Why shooting nannies hurts the herd

```diagram sc-goat-math
```

- One kid a year at most, and half die young (85%).

```more the herd numbers
- Goats have a low reproductive rate and do not readily repopulate ranges after they are removed (85%).
- A nanny usually has one kid; twins are rare; up to 40% of mature nannies may have no kid in a year (85%).
- 40 to 60% of kids die in their first winter (85%).
- Because all adult goats have similar sized horns, male only seasons are impractical, so the Ministry relies on hunters choosing billies (85%).
- The synopsis notice: harvest data since 2012 show fewer females taken overall, but "in some localized areas, there continues to be concerns over the high proportions of female mountain goats in the harvest" (98%).
```

## The nanny rule

```diagram sc-goat-legal
```

- Nanny with a kid, or in a group with kids: unlawful (98%).

```more the law
> [!law]
> It is unlawful to hunt a female mountain goat accompanying a kid, or a female mountain goat in a group with one or more kids (98%). A lone nanny is still legal, but shooting her is a poor choice (Tip).
```

## Day, year, food, cover

```diagram sc-goat-year
```

- Summer: at and above timberline, next to cliffs (85%).
- Feed morning and evening, bed on ledges midday (Tip).

```more habitat, food and the goat year
- Built for cliffs: huge shoulders, short legs, hooves with traction pads and toes that spread (85%).
- Summer and early fall: at and above timberline, lush alpine swales and boulder meadows next to steep cliffs (85%).
- Winter ranges: steep sites that shed snow, with a warm south to west exposure (85%).
- Food: grasses, sedges and low plants in summer; woody browse in winter (85%).
- Rut: early November to early December, peak about 15 to 20 November (85%, BC mountain goat management plan 2010). Kids born late May or early June, about 3 kg (85%).
- Nursery bands average 4 or 5, up to 15 to 20 after kidding; billies alone or in groups of 2 to 4 (85%).
- Daily: feeds morning and evening, beds on ledges with a view in the heat of the day (Tip).
```

## Tracks and sign

```diagram sc-alpine-sign
```

- Look for white hair on rock and brush, and dusty beds (Tip).

```more every sign
- Track: squarer than a deer, two blunt toes that spread wide, about 7 to 8 cm long (60%).
- Long white hair on brush, rock edges and bed sites (Tip).
- Beds: pawed dusty ovals on ledges and ridge points, often reused, with pellets (Tip).
- Dust wallows on dry slopes in summer (Tip).
```

## Tactics: options

```anim sc-alpine-glass Glass from below or across first. Sort billies before you sweat.
```

```anim sc-steep-stalk Then climb out of sight to a spot level with or above the goat.
```

| Option | Pro | Con |
|---|---|---|
| A: glass from the valley, climb | Sort billies first | Long climbs, loose rock |
| B: ridge walk above cliffs | Downhill shots | You skyline yourself |

```more the two options in full
### Option A: glass from the valley, then climb
- Find goats from below with a spotting scope, mark the exact cliff, then climb to a shooting spot on their level or above.
- Pros: you see many goats and can sort billies before you sweat (hunter consensus) (60%).
- Cons: long climbs, loose rock, weather closes in fast.

### Option B: ridge walk above the cliffs
- Walk the ridge top at first light and glass down into basins and cliff bands.
- Pros: shots are downhill and recovery is below you.
- Cons: you skyline yourself; goats look up.
```

> [!lean]
> Option A for a first goat. Watch the goat for 10 minutes or more. Shoot only when it stands where a dead goat will stay put.

## Rifle, load and range

```diagram sc-uphill-hold
```

- Centrefire, big air gun or bow. **No rimfire, no shotgun** (98%).
- Heavy bonded bullet. Hold for the level distance (Tip).

```more weapons, load and dogs
- Legal weapons: centrefire rifle, air gun of .35 calibre or larger, bow A, C or D. **No rimfire, no shotgun** (98%).
- Goats are tough and heavy boned. A 165 to 180 grain bonded bullet in your .308 (Tip).
- Shots are often steep. Hold for the level distance (Tip).
- Dogs must be leashed when hunting goats (98%).
```

## Shot placement (brief)

```diagram sc-goat-aim
```

```diagram sc-cliff-shot
```

- Long hair hides the body: aim lower (Tip).
- Never on a cliff edge. Be ready for a second shot (Tip).

```more where to aim
- Broadside, behind the shoulder, one third up, but a goat's long hair hides the body; aim lower than the hair suggests (Tip).
- Shoot only a goat that is on a bench or slope you can reach, never one on a cliff edge (Tip).
- Be ready for a second shot; goats often walk after a hit (Tip).
```

## Legal specifics

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

```diagram sc-goat-ci
```

```diagram sc-ci-clock
```

- CI (Compulsory Inspection): horns plus evidence of sex (98%).

```more every legal rule
- Species licence: $40.00 resident (98%). Not valid until 2 days after issue (90%).
- Provincial bag limit: 1 (98%).
- Compulsory inspection within 30 days and before December 5 of the year of the kill, whichever is first (98%). By appointment, no drop ins (98%).
- Parts for CI: the horns, plus a testicle or part of the penis for a billy, or a portion of the teats or mammary gland for a nanny (98%).
- Evidence of sex must stay attached during transport (98%).
- Unlawful to hunt a nanny with a kid or in a group with kids (98%).
- Region 3 Kamloops office does CI by appointment for goats and sheep (95%).
- LEH: main draw deadline June 23, 2026; you need the resident hunting licence and the goat species licence as well as the authorization (98%).
- Horns must not be altered until the carcass reaches a meat cutter, home or CI (98%).
```

## Meat and the cape

```diagram sk-cape
```

- Plan 2 to 3 pack loads for a billy. Skin and salt the hide the same day (Tip).

```more meat and cape care
- A billy field dresses heavy; expect a hard pack out of 2 to 3 loads (Tip).
- Meat is good, though old billies in rut can be strong; grind and braise (Tip).
- The hide is thick and the hair long; skin it the same day and salt it if you want a mount (Tip).
- Keep horns, CI data sheet and species licence together for life (95%).
```

## Beginner mistakes

```diagram sc-goat-1
```

> [!mistake]
> - Shooting the first white goat you see. The first one is often a nanny with a kid out of view.
> - Shooting a goat above a cliff. It falls, the horns break, and the meat is gone.
> - Climbing in running shoes. Loose rock and wet grass kill goat hunters.

```more one more
- Also: no spotting scope, no rope, and no partner (Tip).
```
