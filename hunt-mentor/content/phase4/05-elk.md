> [!why]
> Elk are scarce around Heffley Creek and common in the Okanagan. Learn the 6 point rule and the bugle now, so the first bull you meet does not fool you.

- Region 3 (Thompson) has **no general open season** for elk in the 2026 to 2028 synopsis (98%).
- Region 8 (Okanagan) has a **6 point bull** rifle season, 10 September to 20 October, plus a bow only season 1 to 9 September (98%).
- Thompson elk: about 300 to 500 and increasing. Okanagan: about 2,500 to 3,500 (2022 estimates, search preview) (80%).
- **6 point bull:** at least 6 tines on one antler. The brow tine counts (98%).
- New for 2026: a Mandatory Hunter Report replaces compulsory inspection and reporting for elk (95%).
- Rut and bugling: early September to mid October (80%).
- Elk live in herds. Many eyes, ears and noses (Tip).

## Identify: bull, cow, calf

- **Bull:** long antlers sweeping up and back, one main beam with tines pointing forward and up (85%). Tan body, dark brown neck mane, cream rump patch, short tail (80%).
- **Size:** a bull is about 150 cm at the shoulder and 300 to 350 kg; some near 500 kg before the rut (search preview) (80%). Cows 225 to 275 kg (80%).
- **Cow:** no antlers, same colours, thinner neck. Travels in groups with calves (80%).
- **Calf:** born late May to June, spotted at first, spots gone by fall (70%).
- **Elk or moose?** Moose are dark brown to black all over, with a long nose, a bell and palm shaped antlers (Tip).
- **Elk or deer?** Elk are 2 to 3 times a mule deer's size, with the big cream rump and the dark neck (Tip).
- Synopsis: a "bull" is one bearing visible bony antlers, and does not include a calf (page 3) (98%).

## The 6 point rule

```diagram elk-antlers
```

- Synopsis page 3: "ELK - SIX POINTS OR GREATER BULL - means any bull having at least six tines on one antler" (98%).
- The [[Brow tine]] counts. Every [[Tine]] must be at least 2.5 cm and longer than it is wide (page 4) (98%).
- The main beam tip counts as a tine if it meets that test (interpretation) (85%).
- A typical mature bull is a "6 by 6". Young bulls carry spikes, 4s and 5s (Tip).
- Region 8 rows read `6 Point Bulls`. Footnote: "See Definitions section: Elk. The antlers must accompany the species licence" (page 68) (98%).
- Some regions also use a 3 point class, for example Region 7B Peace (page 62) (98%). Not Region 8.

> [!warn]
> Count on one antler, in good light, through binoculars, with the bull standing. If you cannot count 6 with certainty, it is not a 6 point.

## Where elk live: BC, Thompson, Okanagan

- An older BC ministry account: about 45,000 Rocky Mountain elk in the Interior, roughly 25,000 in the Kootenays, 15,000 in the north and 1,000 in the Thompson Okanagan (search preview) (70%).
- 2022 provincial estimates: Region 3 Thompson 300 to 500, increasing; Region 8 Okanagan 2,500 to 3,500, stable to increasing (search preview) (80%).
- Transplants since 1917 moved elk around the province and started new herds (search preview) (75%).
- Thompson elk live in scattered pockets. Hunters mention the Lytton, Princeton and Adams Lake areas (hunter forums) (50%).
- Roosevelt elk live on Vancouver Island and parts of the coast. Not your hunt (75%).
- Near Heffley Creek: rare. Treat any elk sighting as a note for the Journal, not a hunt (Tip).

## Rut and bugling

- The rut runs early September to mid October. Bugling can continue into November (80%).
- A bugle is a rising whistle scream that ends in grunts. Bulls bugle to gather cows and warn rivals (80%).
- A mature bull holds a harem of cows, sometimes 20 or more (65%).
- Bulls are most vocal at dawn, dusk and through the night (70%).
- The Region 8 rifle dates, 10 September to 20 October, sit right on the rut. Calling works then (Tip).
- Cows call too: a short mew or chirp. A cow call pulls bulls as well as a bugle does (hunter consensus) (65%).

## A day in the life: late September

- Elk are crepuscular: two activity peaks, around dawn and dusk (GPS, Global Positioning System, collar study) (80%).
- Dawn: feeding in meadows and openings, then a drift into timber (80%).
- Midday: bedded in cool timber, often on north slopes or benches (Tip).
- Dusk: back out to feed. In the rut, bulls move all night (70%).
- Legal hours: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (page 11) (98%).

## Yearly pattern

- **Late May to June:** calves born after about 250 days of pregnancy, peak in the first week of June (70%).
- **Summer:** high meadows and timber edges; bulls in bachelor groups growing antlers (70%).
- **August:** velvet shed; bachelor groups split up (70%).
- **September to mid October:** rut (80%).
- **Late fall and winter:** herds move down to [[Winter range]] on low open slopes; antlers drop late winter (70%).

## Food, water and cover

- Grazer and browser. Grasses and sedges first, plus forbs and shrub leaves; more browse in winter (75%).
- Burns, cutblocks and meadows next to timber are elk country (Tip).
- Water: elk drink daily and wallow in the rut. Springs, seeps and pond edges (Tip).
- Cover: dark timber, often a kilometre or more from the feeding opening (Tip).

## Tracks and sign

- Track: rounder than a moose or deer print, about 7.5 to 12.5 cm long (65%).
- Pellets: about 2 cm, larger than deer, smaller than moose; clumped on green feed (65%).
- Rubs: shredded saplings and small trees, higher than deer rubs (Tip).
- Wallows: muddy pits that stink of urine, near water, in the rut (65%).
- Trails: deep, wide paths through timber, like a cattle trail (Tip).
- Herd sign: tracks and pellets of many sizes together (Tip).

## Tactics: options

### Option A: call in the rut (Region 8)
- Set up downwind of a bugling bull at dawn. Cow call, bugle sparingly, stay still.
- Pros: the classic September hunt; bulls come to you.
- Cons: easy to over call; you must still count 6 points on a moving bull.

### Option B: glass openings at dawn and dusk
- Sit high over a burn or meadow next to timber. Find the herd, then move in with the wind.
- Pros: time to count points; safer shots.
- Cons: elk feed in the dark and are gone early.

### Option C: sit a wallow or water
- Downwind of a fresh wallow in the afternoon.
- Pros: quiet, a rested shot.
- Cons: one spot; patience.

> [!lean]
> Not an October hunt from Heffley Creek. If you go to Region 8 in September with your brother, Option B first, Option A once you have heard real bulls.

## Rifle, load and range

- Legal methods for elk: centrefire rifle or bow only. No shotgun, no rimfire, no air rifle (page 13) (98%).
- Your .308 Winchester works for elk with a 165 to 180 grain premium bullet, inside about 200 m (219 yd) (hunter consensus) (65%).
- Elk are tough and run far on a poor hit. Wait for broadside (Tip).

> [!tip]
> Same 15 cm plate rule, 9 of 10 from a field rest. Elk make hunters stretch shots. Do not.

## Shot placement (brief)

- Behind the front leg, one third up: both lungs (standard hunter education advice) (75%).
- Quartering away: aim at the far side front leg (Tip).
- No neck, head or running shots (Tip).
- Full lesson: the shot placement session (coming in a later phase).

> [!warn]
> Six tines on one side, broadside, inside your range, solid [[Backstop]]. All four, or no shot.

## Legal specifics

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- **Species licence:** Elk, BC resident $25.00. Includes the HCTF (Habitat Conservation Trust Foundation) surcharge, not GST (Goods and Services Tax) (page 8) (98%).
- **[[Bag limit]]:** 1 (pages 13 and 68) (98%).
- **Region 3 Thompson:** no elk row in the general open season table, page 34. No general open season (98%). [[LEH]] (Limited Entry Hunting) draws, if any: VERIFY in the [LEH synopsis](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/sports-recreation-arts-and-culture/outdoor-recreation/fishing-and-hunting/hunting/limited-entry-hunting/leh-regulations-synopsis.pdf).

| ELK | 8-1 to 8-15, 8-21 to 8-26 | `6 Point Bulls` | Sept 10 to Oct 20 | 1 |

- Footnotes on page 68: "All individuals with an elk species licence must submit a Mandatory Hunter Report by March 31st" and "See Definitions section: Elk. The antlers must accompany the species licence" (98%).
- **Region 2** (Mission base): no elk row in the Lower Mainland table, page 28. No general open season (98%).
- **Antlers:** keep them naturally attached to part of the upper skull, with the licence, while returning from the hunt (page 15) (98%). No tampering (page 11) (98%).
- **[[Evidence of sex]]:** the tail or a hide piece of at least 6 cm² plus a testicle or part of the penis (page 15) (98%).
- **Mandatory Hunter Report:** all elk licence buyers, by 31 March (page 16) (98%). New in 2026: it replaces compulsory inspection and reporting for elk (changes summary) (95%). Missing it blocks next year's licence and LEH application (98%).
- **[[CWD]] (Chronic Wasting Disease):** Region 3 asks for elk heads for testing (page 31) (95%). No elk parts or urine scents from outside BC (page 11) (98%).
- **Hours:** 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (page 11) (98%).

## Meat care and yield

- Bull elk: boneless lean meat about 50% of the skinned carcass, or about 30% of live weight (University of Wyoming study, search preview) (70%).
- Bulls range up to about 320 to 340 kg; cows up to about 225 kg (search preview) (70%). So a mature bull gives roughly 90 to 100 kg boneless, a cow about 65 to 70 kg (assumption, Tip).
- [[Gutless method]], quarter, hang in shade, pack in several loads (Tip).
- Elk hide is thick and holds heat. Skin or quarter quickly in September heat (Tip).
- [[Edible portions]] rule applies: neck, ribs, four quarters, loins (page 11) (98%).
- [[Record of Receipt]] when your brother carries your meat (page 14) (90%).

## Beginner mistakes

> [!mistake]
> - Shooting the first bull that bugles. Count 6 on one side first.
> - Bugling at a herd bull on open ground. He gathers his cows and leaves.
> - Hunting elk in Region 3 on a species licence alone. There is no general season.

- Also: a 300 m shot because "elk are big". They are big animals, not big targets.

## Grandpa's rules

> [!rule]
> Six you can count, or none at all.

> [!rule]
> An elk you hear is an elk you can hunt. An elk you see at a kilometre is already gone.

> [!rule]
> One elk, two men, three trips. Plan the pack before you plan the shot.

> [!field]
> September, if you get to the Okanagan: sit a ridge at dawn and just listen. Learn the bugle before you ever carry a tag.
