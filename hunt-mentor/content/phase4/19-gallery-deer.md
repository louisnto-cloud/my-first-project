> [!why]
> You will see most deer for two seconds, half hidden, from the side or from behind. Learn every angle here so your eyes know the animal before your brain does.

- This session is pictures first. Each screen shows one view: side, front, rear, running, bedded, in cover, far away.
- Read the caption, then look for the one or two marks it names.
- BC has three deer you can hunt: mule deer, white tailed deer and black tailed deer (85%).
- The law treats black tailed deer as mule deer: the synopsis says "Mule (Black tailed) Deer" (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition).
- Photos are illustrations. The written field marks are the source of truth.
- Photos come from many places in western North America. Captions say where.

## Mule deer buck: side and front

```gallery
g-md-buck-side | Side view, California. White rump, thin tail with a black tip, grey coat.
g-md-buck-front | Front view, North Dakota. Ears out wide like a mule. Antlers fork, then fork again.
mule-deer-buck | Big ears, forked antlers, rope tail with a black tip.
g-md-buck-4pt | Side on in snow, Oregon. Practise counting the tips on the near antler.
```

- Ears: huge, about three quarters the length of the head (85%).
- Antlers: each main beam splits into two, and each branch can split again (85%).
- Face: pale, with a dark patch on the forehead in fall (Tip).

## Mule deer from behind

```gallery
g-md-buck-rear | Buck from behind, Colorado. Big white rump patch, thin rope tail with a black tip.
g-md-doe-fawns-rear | Doe and two fawns, Wyoming. Every rump is white, every tail tip is black.
g-md-doe-sage-snow | Doe in snowy sagebrush, Wyoming. Grey winter coat, tail held down.
```

- The rear view is the fastest ID: white rump, small white tail, black tip (85%).
- A mule deer tail hangs down, even when the deer runs (85%).

## Mule deer on the move

```gallery
g-md-bound | Doe bounding across a road, Utah. Legs tucked, a stiff legged bounce.
g-md-run-away | Running away, California. Tail stays down. Look for the black tip.
```

- A spooked mule deer often [[Stot|stots]]: all four feet hit and push off together, like a pogo stick (85%).
- It often stops on a ridge and looks back (Tip).

## Bedded, in cover and in sagebrush

```gallery
g-md-fawn-bedded | Fawn bedded in grass. Only the head and ears show. Leave fawns alone.
g-md-doe-fawn-cover | Doe and fawn in brush, Wyoming. In cover you see ears and a face first.
g-md-buck-cover | Buck in brush, Oregon. Pick out the antler tines against the twigs.
g-md-buck-sage | Buck in sagebrush, Idaho. The grey coat matches the sage. Find the white rump.
```

- In cover, look for parts, not a whole deer: an ear, the white throat, a leg, an antler tip (Tip).
- Sagebrush, bunchgrass and open pine are classic mule deer country in the BC Interior (85%).

## Mule deer at distance

```gallery
g-md-distance | Several mule deer in sagebrush, eastern Oregon. Grey shapes with white rumps.
g-md-buck-open | Buck crossing open flats, Yellowstone. Antlers above the ears, white rump.
g-md-radium | Three mule deer near Radium, BC. Open pine and grass winter range.
```

- Past 300 m (328 yd), use binoculars, then a spotting scope. Never use the rifle scope to look at deer (Tip).
- Colour fools you at distance. Shape, rump and ear size are steadier clues (Tip).

## Mule deer doe and fawn

```gallery
g-md-doe-front | Doe, front view, Wyoming. No antlers, huge ears, dark forehead.
```

- A doe has no antlers. A [[Button buck]] (male fawn) has only bumps under the hair (85%).

## White tailed buck

```gallery
g-wt-buck-side | Side view, New Jersey. Brown tail with white edges; tines rise off one main beam.
g-wt-buck-front | Front view. White nose band and throat patch; beams sweep forward.
g-wt-buck-flag | Running in snow, West Virginia. Tail up, white underneath: the flag.
```

- White tail antlers: one main beam with single tines growing up from it. They do not fork like a mule deer's (85%).
- Ears are smaller than a mule deer's (85%).

## White tailed doe, running

```gallery
g-wt-doe | Doe, side view, in snow. Long, wide brown tail.
g-wt-doe-flag | Doe trotting with the white [[Flag|flag]] up.
g-wt-run | White tails bounding away, Montana. Long, low gallop, tails up.
```

- White tails gallop with the tail raised (85%). Mule deer bounce with the tail down (85%).

## Mule versus white tailed

```gallery
g-md-buck-rear | Mule deer rear: white rump, thin tail, black tip.
g-wt-buck-flag | White tail rear: wide tail, white underside, raised when alarmed.
g-md-buck-front | Mule deer antlers: forks on forks.
g-wt-buck-front | White tail antlers: tines off one beam.
```

| Clue | Mule deer | White tailed deer |
|---|---|---|
| Tail | Thin, white, black tip (85%) | Wide, brown on top, white below (85%) |
| Ears | Very large (85%) | Medium (85%) |
| Antlers | Fork, then fork again (85%) | Tines off one main beam (85%) |
| Running | Stots, tail down (85%) | Gallops, flag up (85%) |

## Black tailed deer: the coast deer

```gallery
g-bt-buck | Buck in a river, Washington. Smaller, redder deer; forked antlers.
g-bt-doe | Doe, Washington. The whole top of the tail is dark.
g-bt-victoria | Young buck, Victoria, BC. Wide tail held down, dark on top.
g-bt-bedded | Young buck bedded by a highway, Ucluelet, Vancouver Island.
g-bt-buck-velvet | Buck in velvet, Mount Rainier. Antlers fork like a mule deer's.
```

- Black tailed deer live on the coast and in the Fraser Valley, the deer you would meet around Mission (85%).
- Tail: wider than a mule deer's and dark or black down the whole top (Tip).
- Antlers fork like a mule deer's, so the 4 point rules apply to them too (99%, page 4).

## Is it legal? Count the points

```gallery
g-md-buck-front | Many tines a side. Count one antler and leave out the brow tine.
g-md-buck-small | Young buck, Yellowstone. Fewer than 4 tines a side. Not a 4 point buck.
g-md-forkhorn | Fork horn in velvet, Yosemite. 2 points a side. Not a 4 point buck.
g-md-young-bucks | Small antlers on all three. Not 4 point bucks.
```

> [!warn] Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] "MULE (BLACK-TAILED) DEER - FOUR POINTS OR GREATER BUCK - means any buck having at least four tines, excluding the brow tine, on one antler."

- Plain English: count one antler. Four or more true tines, not counting the [[Brow tine|brow tine]], and he is a [[4 point buck]] (99%, page 4, 2 October 2026 edition).
- A [[Tine]] must be at least 2.5 cm long and longer than it is wide (99%, page 4).
- A spike or a fork horn (2 points) or a 3 point is not a 4 point buck (99%, page 4).
- Some seasons are for 4 point bucks only. The class column of the season table for your MU (Management Unit) says which (90%, my reading).
- Cannot count the points for sure? Do not shoot (Tip).

## Grandpa's rule

> [!rule]
> Look at the back end first, then the head. The tail tells you the species, the antlers tell you if he is legal.

## Common mistakes

> [!mistake]
> - Calling a deer from its colour. Grey, brown and red coats all happen in every species.
> - Counting the brow tine on a mule deer. It is excluded from the 4 point count.
> - Shooting at a deer in cover when you only saw a leg or an ear. Wait for the whole animal.

## Do this in the field

> [!field]
> - Every deer you see this week, say out loud: species, sex, and which view you had.
> - For every buck, count the points on the near antler through binoculars before you touch the rifle.
> - Note which mark you saw first. Most days it will be the rump or the ears.
