> [!why]
> One Canada goose is a week of dinners. They are big, loud and easy to see, which makes them the best bird for learning to scout, hide and call.

- Same federal rules as ducks: [[MGBHP]] (Migratory Game Bird Hunting Permit), [[Non toxic shot]], 3 shells, no moving motor boat, retrieval (99%). Read [Ducks](#/s/ducks) first.
- Four groups count in BC: Canada and cackling geese together, snow and Ross's geese together, white fronted geese, and brant (99%).
- Each group has its own dates and limits in each district (99%).
- Assumption: no dog, 12 gauge with steel, two hunters, a 10 ft boat.

## Canada goose and cackling goose

- Both: black head and neck, white chinstrap up each cheek, grey brown body, white under the tail, black bill and feet (85%). Sexes identical (85%).
- **Canada goose:** 1.35 to 5.45 kg depending on race (80%). Resident birds in BC are mixed race introductions, often big (85%). Deep "ka lunk" honk (80%).
- **Cackling goose:** an Arctic nester that looks like a small Canada goose with a short neck, a stubby bill and a high yelping call (85%). Most weigh around 1 to 2 kg (75%).
- Telling them apart in the air is hard and sometimes not possible (85%). It does not matter for your bag: the law counts "Canada Geese and Cackling Geese, combined" (99%).
- Young of the year look like adults by fall (80%). No sex or age rules apply (99%).
- Resident geese sit on golf courses, parks and river bars all year (85%). Migrants arrive with the first hard frosts of October (Tip, 65%).

## Snow goose, Ross's goose, white fronted goose, brant

- **Snow goose:** white with black wing tips, pink bill with a dark "grin patch", pink feet (85%). 2.95 to 3.4 kg (80%). A blue phase is dark with a white head (85%). Young are dusky grey (80%).
- **Ross's goose:** a miniature snow goose with a short bill and no grin patch (85%). Counted with snow geese (99%).
- **White fronted goose:** brown grey with a white patch at the base of a pink orange bill, orange legs, black bars on the belly, so "specklebelly" (85%). 2.8 kg (80%). High laughing "kow kow kow" in V flocks (80%).
- **Brant:** small, black head and neck with a thin white necklace, no white cheek, dark belly (85%). A sea goose of the coast (85%).
- Brant: no open season in Districts 3 and 8 (99%). In District 2 only MU 2-4, 1 to 10 March, 3 a day (99%). Not your bird this year.
- Swans are white and far bigger than any goose. Trumpeter and tundra swans are protected, no open season (90%).

## Where and when in BC

- Canada geese nest across the southern two thirds of BC, mostly below 500 m, and numbers have more than doubled since 1970 (85%).
- Most of the resident population lives around the towns and valleys of southern BC (85%). The Thompson rivers and the Fraser Valley both carry geese all year (75%).
- Snow geese stage from September through November and arrive on wintering areas October to December (75%). In BC the big flocks are on the Fraser delta, MUs 2-4 and 2-5, where the limit is higher (99%).
- White fronted geese pass through on the Pacific and Central flyways (80%). Expect only passing flocks in the Interior (Tip, 60%).
- Cackling geese leave the Arctic in late August or September and reach wintering areas in October or November (75%).
- Mid to late October near Kamloops and the Okanagan: resident Canada geese plus migrant flocks on the move (Tip, 65%).

## A goose's day

- Geese spend as much time on land as on water (85%). They feed in fields a few hours early morning and late afternoon, then rest on a lake or big river (80%).
- They eat grass, waste grain, and in fall berries and crops. Twelve hours a day of feeding is normal (85%).
- In the morning they usually return to the field they fed in the afternoon before (65%).
- Fall flocks are families travelling together, from a few families to thousands (85%).
- In bitter cold they may sit on the roost all morning and feed in the warm afternoon (65%).
- Sign: green and white droppings about the size of a cigarette, flattened grass, feathers, tracks like big webbed arrows in mud (Tip).

## Tactics: field or water

### Option A: a cut field over decoys
- Scout the evening before. Watch where the flock lands, wait until it leaves, then mark the exact spot (65%).
- Set 1 to 3 dozen shell or silhouette decoys, about 80% feeders and 20% heads up, landing hole downwind of the feeders (65%).
- Hide where you can hide, even 50 to 100 m off the spot (65%). Lie in stubble, a ditch or a layout blind.
- Pros: close shots at slow landing birds, dry. Cons: permission needed on [[Cultivated land]] (99%); decoys cost money.

### Option B: water, from shore
- Set floaters near a bar or point geese loaf on. Hide on the bank (Tip).
- Pros: your duck gear works. Cons: geese often land out of range; a boat is needed to fetch.

### Option C: pass shooting a flight line
- Stand under the line between roost and field, in cover (65%). Pros: no decoys. Cons: high birds, long shots, cripples. Only with birds under 30 m (Tip).

> [!lean]
> Option A with permission and a dozen decoys, or Option B on a river bar you can reach with the boat. Scouting beats gear every time.

## Calling

- Four basic sounds: the honk, the cluck (short excited honk), the murmur (feeding) and the moan (65%).
- Loud and continuous to turn far geese; soft "ground talk" once they turn toward you (65%).
- The biggest mistake is calling too much (65%). A beginner can win with a mouth call used little, or no call at all and good decoys (Tip).
- Electronic calls and recordings are banned for geese in BC (97%). Flags and hand waved wings are allowed (Tip).
- One hunter calls the shot. Everyone else stays hidden and still until "take them" (Tip).

## Gun, shot size, range

- Your 12 gauge is enough. 3 inch steel loads are the common choice (Tip).
- Steel BB is the all round pellet for large Canada geese; BBB and T for longer shots (70%). Steel 1 or 2 for small cackling geese and snow geese over decoys (Tip).
- Pattern test: at least 60 pellets in a 76 cm (30 in) circle at your longest range (70%). Modified choke is the usual starting point with BB steel (Tip).
- Head and neck shots at close decoying geese kill clean (65%). Aim at the white cheek, not the whole bird (Tip).
- Max range for you this season: 35 m (38 yd). A goose looks close at 50 m because it is big (Tip).
- Finishing a crippled goose on the ground: steel 2 or 4 at about 15 m to the head (65%). Carry a few in a different pocket (Tip).

## The rules that catch goose hunters

- Mission needs the Fraser Valley Special Area licence, $10, plus $1,000,000 liability insurance, in all of the district of Mission (99%). Also the federal permit (99%).
- No hunting within 400 m of bait (99%). A farmer's spilled grain from normal harvest is not bait (99%). A pile someone dumped is (99%).
- No hunting from a motor vehicle or a moving motor boat (99%). No loaded gun in the truck or on the quad while you scout (99%).
- No shooting within the [[Road allowance]] or across a road, and never within 100 m of an occupied house or barn (99%).
- Keep a fully feathered wing or head on each goose until it is preserved (99%). Both breasts must come home (99%).
- Possession is three times the daily limit at any time, home included (99%).
- Hours: half an hour before sunrise to half an hour after sunset (99%).

## Care and yield

- A goose holds heat. Open the body cavity or breast it out within an hour or two in mild weather (Tip).
- A mature Canada goose of about 3.6 kg gives roughly 1 kg of boned breast meat; individual breasts run about 280 to 450 g each (60%, hunter reports in search previews).
- Legs and thighs are worth keeping. Slow cook them (Tip).
- Pluck only if you plan to roast whole. Otherwise skin, breast, and keep one wing on until the freezer (Tip; wing rule 99%).
- Older resident geese are tough. Young birds have soft pliable bills and smaller feet (Tip, 60%).

## Beginner mistakes

> [!mistake]
> - Shooting at the first flock at 60 m because geese look close. Wait for feet down inside 35 m.
> - Hunting a field without written permission because "the farmer will not mind".
> - Calling non stop while the flock is already coming. Go quiet and still.

- Also: forgetting the District 3 closure from 21 to 30 September when planning an early trip (99%).
- Also: shooting a flock in the air as a group. Pick the lead bird, then the next (Tip).

## Grandpa's rules

> [!rule]
> Find the field, not the goose. The goose comes back to the field. You cannot chase it there.

> [!rule]
> Hide first, decoys second, call last. A silent hunter in a good hide beats a loud one in the open.

> [!field]
> This week: one evening drive past cut grain fields and river bars near your base at the last hour of light. Note every flock, the field, the time, and the wind. That is your morning spot.
