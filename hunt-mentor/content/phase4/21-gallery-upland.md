> [!why]
> Upland birds flush at your feet and are gone in a second. If you know the shape, the tail and the habitat before you go, you name the bird as it lifts.

- Each screen shows one bird or one group, male and female where we found both.
- Habitat is half the ID: aspen and birch for ruffed grouse, dark conifers for spruce grouse, open ridges for dusky grouse, rock and cheatgrass for chukar (80%).
- Some look alikes have no season at all. Those are on the last photo screen.
- Photos are illustrations. The written field marks are the source of truth.

## Ruffed grouse

```gallery

g-ruffed-snow | In snow, Oregon. Crest, barred body, long tail with a dark band.

```

- Grey and red (brown) colour phases. The tail band is the same in both (80%).
- Wide dark band near the end of a long fan shaped tail (80%).
- Explodes off the ground with a roar of wings (80%).

## Dusky and sooty grouse (blue grouse)

```gallery
dusky-grouse-male | Dusky male displaying in sagebrush. Slate grey, red air sacs, yellow orange comb.

g-sooty-male | Sooty male displaying, Washington. The coast form.

```

- The biggest forest grouse in BC (80%).
- Sooty grouse is the coast form; the synopsis lists it in Region 2 as "SOOTY (Blue)" (99%).
- Male: plain slate grey with a pale grey band at the tail tip (80%).

## Spruce grouse

```gallery
g-spruce-male | Male displaying, Ontario. Black throat and breast, red comb.
spruce-grouse-hen | Hen. Heavy black and white barring on brown.
```

- Dark conifer forest, often on the ground near spruce and lodgepole pine (80%).
- Very tame; it often just sits and watches you (80%).
- In southern BC the male shows white spots above a dark tail: the [[Franklin's grouse|Franklin's]] form (80%).

## Sharp tailed grouse and ptarmigan

```gallery
sharp-tailed-grouse | Sharp tailed grouse in summer grass. Pale, spotted, short pointed tail.
sharp-tailed-grouse-snow | Sharp tailed grouse in snow near Fort St. John, BC.
g-ptarmigan-summer | White tailed ptarmigan in summer, Mount Rainier. Mottled, on alpine rock.
g-ptarmigan-winter | Willow ptarmigan in winter, Alaska. White, with black tail feathers.
```

- Sharp tailed grouse: pale, with a short pointed tail that shows white in flight (80%).
- White tailed ptarmigan: white tail in every season; willow and rock ptarmigan have black tails (80%).
- Both are legal only in listed MUs (Management Units) and places (99%).

## California quail

```gallery
g-quail-male-vernon | Male, Vernon, BC. Black face edged in white, forward curling plume.
g-quail-female | Female with a chick. Grey brown face, small plume.
g-quail-pair | Male (right) and female, Nevada.
hab-quail-vernon | Quail country near Vernon: brushy draws and farm edges.
```

- Both sexes carry the teardrop plume; the male's is bigger and black (80%).
- They live in a [[Covey|covey]] and run before they fly (80%).
- Legal in Region 8, not in Region 3 (99%, Region 3 page 34 has no quail row).

## Chukar and pheasant

```gallery
g-chukar | Chukar. Red bill and legs, black necklace, barred flanks.
g-pheasant-rooster | Pheasant rooster, South Dakota. Green head, red face, white ring, long tail.
```

- Chukar: steep rocky slopes and cheatgrass; runs uphill, flies downhill (80%).
- Pheasant: farm edges, ditches and cattails. Check your region's rules on hens (VERIFY for your region).

## Wild turkey

```gallery
g-turkey-tom | Tom strutting, Zion National Park, Utah. Fanned tail, bare head, chest beard.
g-turkey-hen | Hen, Virginia. Smaller, browner, feathered blue grey head.
g-turkey-flock | Flock, California. Check each bird for a beard.
```

- Spring seasons are for bearded birds only (98%, turkey session).
- A bearded turkey has a tuft of hair like feathers on the chest (98%, page 3). A few hens grow beards (80%).

## Snowshoe hare: summer and winter

```gallery
snowshoe-hare-summer | Summer coat, Banff. Grey brown, dark ear tips.
snowshoe-hare-winter | Winter coat. White, with huge furred hind feet.
```

- Turns white in fall and brown in spring, by day length, not snow (80%).
- In a snowless fall a white hare stands out on brown ground (Tip).

## Do not shoot: no season look alikes

```gallery
g-sage-grouse | Greater sage grouse male displaying. Spiky tail, white chest.
```

> [!warn] Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Sage grouse: no season row anywhere in the synopsis, so no open season in BC (90%, my reading). It is also listed as gone from BC (Tip).
- Mountain quail: "No open season on mountain quail" (99%, Region 1, page 21). It has a straight plume, not a curled one (80%).
- Sharp tailed grouse outside the listed MUs: no season (99%). See the Grouse session.

## Grandpa's rule

> [!rule]
> Name the bird by its tail and its trees. If it flushed from the wrong habitat, look twice.

## Common mistakes

> [!mistake]
> - Shooting a sharp tailed grouse because it "looked like a grouse" in an MU with no season.
> - Calling a dusky hen a ruffed grouse. Look for the black tail band.
> - Shooting a hen pheasant where only roosters are legal.

## Do this in the field

> [!field]
> - Every bird you flush, call out the tail: fan with a band, plain dark, pointed, or white.
> - Note the trees and ground where it rose. Match it to the habitat on this screen.
> - For turkeys in spring, find the beard through binoculars before anything else.
