> [!why]
> Grouse are the beginner's bird: a long season, no species licence, and they sit still. The traps are the roads you find them on and the three species you must tell apart.

```photo g-ruffed-tree Ruffed grouse: the beginner's bird.
```

- 3 forest grouse near Heffley Creek: ruffed, spruce, dusky (85%).
- BC licence only. Hours: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (99%).

```more what this session covers
- BC has three forest grouse you will meet near Heffley Creek: ruffed, spruce (the Franklin's form) and dusky, the old "blue grouse" (85%).
- Sharp tailed grouse and ptarmigan are legal only in listed [[MU]]s (Management Units) and places (99%).
- Residents need only the BC hunting licence. The fee table on page 8 says "No Species licence required" for upland game birds (99%). No federal permit (99%).
- Hunting hours are 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset, the big game hours, not the duck hours (99%).
```

## Tell the three forest grouse apart

```diagram grouse-id
```

- Dusky: biggest, plain grey. Spruce: dark, tame. Ruffed: crest, banded fan (80%).

```diagram sb-grouse-tails
```

```more every mark and weight
- **Ruffed grouse**, 480 to 740 g: small crest, fan tail with a wide dark band near the tip, barred flanks, grey or reddish colour phases (80%). Black neck ruffs show only in display (80%). Explodes off the ground with a roar (80%).
- **Spruce grouse**, 450 to 750 g: dark. Male has a black throat and breast edged in white, a red comb over the eye, and in the Franklin's form found in southern BC, white spots above a dark tail (80%). Hen is mottled brown or grey with a shorter, darker tail than a ruffed grouse (75%). Absurdly tame (80%).
- **Dusky grouse**, 880 g to 1.6 kg: biggest. Male plain grey, darker than other grouse, with a yellow to orange comb and a broad pale grey tip on a dark tail (80%). Hen mottled brown and grey (80%). Deep hoots in spring, mostly silent in fall (75%).
- Sooty grouse is the coast version of the blue grouse. Region 2 lists it as "SOOTY (Blue)" (99%); the Interior lists dusky (99%).
- Young of the year by October look like hens of their species, slightly smaller (Tip, 65%).
```

## Sharp tailed grouse and ptarmigan

```gallery
g-ptarmigan-summer | Ptarmigan in summer: mottled, above timberline.
g-ptarmigan-winter | Ptarmigan in winter: white.
```

- Sharp tailed: only MUs (Management Units) 3-30 and 3-31 in Region 3 (99%).
- Ptarmigan: above timberline (80%).

```more sharp tailed grouse and the three ptarmigan
- **Sharp tailed grouse**, 800 g to 1.3 kg: pale, spotted below not barred, short pointed tail with white outer feathers in flight (80%). Grassland with aspen and shrub groves (80%).
- In BC the Columbian subspecies lives in the Southern and Central Interior grasslands and numbers about 8,500 to 14,000 birds (85%). Many old populations are gone (85%).
- Open season in Region 3 only in MUs 3-30 and 3-31, with a closed portion of 3-30 (99%). Look alikes: a hen dusky grouse in the same grassland edge. The pointed tail and spotted belly settle it (Tip).
- **White tailed ptarmigan**, 350 to 500 g: the smallest grouse, lives above timberline, white tail, white in winter (80%). The common ptarmigan of southern BC, breeding at 1,830 to 2,650 m in the Interior (85%).
- **Willow ptarmigan**, 450 to 750 g, and **rock ptarmigan**: black tails, northern and high mountain birds (80%). Willow ptarmigan is mostly in the northern half of BC (85%).
- Ptarmigan near Sun Peaks means a steep climb above the trees in October. Snow comes early up there (Tip).
```

## Where in BC, and near your bases

```diagram sb-grouse-elev
```

- Ruffed low, in aspen. Spruce and dusky higher, in conifers (85%).
- Heffley Creek is in MU (Management Unit) 3-27 (90%). Check the map.

```more where each grouse lives, by region
- Ruffed grouse: widespread across BC, most abundant between 750 and 1,250 m, in aspen and poplar stands near water (85%).
- Spruce grouse: east of the Coast Mountains, in conifer forest with a spruce or lodgepole pine component, up to 2,500 m (85%).
- Dusky grouse: widespread in the southern Interior, breeding in Douglas fir and spruce fir zones, often 1,500 to 2,250 m but also 500 to 1,250 m (85%).
- Region 3 near Heffley Creek: the Douglas fir and aspen benches above the valley hold ruffed grouse; the higher pine and spruce toward Heffley Lake and Sun Peaks hold spruce and dusky grouse (Tip, 65%).
- Region 8 Okanagan: all three forest grouse, no sharp tailed or ptarmigan season (97%).
- Region 2 Mission: sooty grouse, ruffed and spruce in the mountains north of the valley; ptarmigan in listed MUs only (99%).
- Heffley Creek, Heffley Lake and Sun Peaks sit in MU 3-27 on the 2026 Region 3 MU map (90%). Confirm on the official map before you rely on it.
```

## Habitat and the grouse day

```diagram sg-grouse-move
```

```video
yt-ruffed-grouse-tips | Find young forest, edges and food. That is where grouse are.
```

- Feed at dawn and late day on road edges and cut blocks (75%).
- Best weeks: late September and October (65%).

```anim sb-grouse-flush Walk, stop, wait. The stop flushes the bird.
```

```more food, home range, moves and the daily pattern
- Ruffed grouse: early forest of aspen, poplar, birch and alder, with buds and catkins as winter food (85%). In fall: berries, clover, leaves and seeds (85%).
- Adults live their whole life within a few hectares and do not migrate; young birds disperse in fall (80%). Males drum on a log; the log is piled with droppings (85%).
- Spruce grouse: mostly on the ground late spring to early autumn, then up in the trees from late autumn through spring eating pine and spruce needles (80%).
- Dusky grouse: open edges, even sagebrush flats, in summer. In autumn they walk **uphill** into dense conifers for winter, up to 50 km but usually much less (75%).
- Daily pattern: feed early morning and late afternoon; dust and gravel on roadsides; sit in cover midday; roost in trees or on the ground at night (75%).
- The best fall weeks: late September and October, when young birds feed on berries and clover along road edges and cut blocks (hunter lore, 65%).
```

## Is a .22 legal for grouse?

```diagram sb-upland-weapons
```

- Yes: a .22 is legal for grouse and ptarmigan (95%).
- Head shot on a sitting bird, inside 25 m (Tip).

```diagram hf-backstop
```

> [!warn]
> Know your [[Backstop]]. A .22 bullet carries over 1 km. A grouse on a road edge often has the road, a cut bank, or nothing behind it.

```more the page 13 table, bans and loads
> [!law]
> BC synopsis, page 13, Legal Hunting Methods table, row "Upland Game Birds": Air rifle "Yes", Rimfire "Yes2", Centrefire "Yes3", Shotguns "Yes", Archery "Bow B, C, D" (97%). Note 2: "Grouse, ptarmigan, or turkey only." Note 3: "Grouse or ptarmigan only." (99%)

- So a .22 LR (Long Rifle) rimfire is legal for grouse and ptarmigan, and for turkey, but **not** for quail, chukar, partridge or pheasant (95%).
- A centrefire rifle is legal only for grouse or ptarmigan (95%). Any rifle for ducks: "No" in the same table (99%).
- Handguns are unlawful for hunting (99%). Full metal jacket bullets are unlawful for hunting with a rifle (99%).
- Single projectile ban zones apply to a .22 too: 400 m of the road allowance of listed highways, for example Hwy 5, the Coquihalla, between Hope and the junction of Hwys 1 and 5 at Kamloops (99%). Shot only areas too (99%).
- Pattern of use (Tip): a .22 LR head shot at a sitting grouse inside 25 m saves all the meat. A body shot with a .22 ruins the breast and may lose the bird. Shotgun for anything flying.
- Shotgun load: 12 gauge, lead 6 or 7.5 for grouse is legal; the federal lead ban does not apply to upland birds (99%). Steel 4 or 6 works if you only own steel (Tip). Improved cylinder [[Choke]] (Tip).
```

## Roads and grouse: the real rules

```diagram rb-road-allowance
```

- Out of the truck, 15 m off the road, then load (99% law, Tip habit).

```anim rb-road-walk Walk out of the road allowance before you load.
```

```more the exact page 10 and 11 words, FSRs and Sun Peaks
> [!law]
> BC synopsis, page 10: "It is unlawful to discharge a firearm across or to discharge a firearm or hunt within the road allowances of all numbered highways and any two lane or greater public road in B.C. that is maintained by the Ministry of Transportation and Infrastructure (or their contractors), the federal government or another province or territory." (99%)

- The [[Road allowance]], same page: "(a) 15 m on either side of the middle of a road with less than three lanes, or (b) 15 m from the edge of the paved surface of a highway with three lanes or more, or (c) to the boundary of private or cultivated land, whichever comes first." (99%)
- Page 11, item 22: it is unlawful "To hunt wildlife from a motor vehicle." (99%, 2 October 2026 edition). Item 23: no hunting game from a boat unless the boat is not being propelled by a motor (99%).
- Page 11, item 28: unlawful to have a firearm "containing live ammunition in its breech or in its magazine attached to the firearm, in or on a railway car, motor vehicle, sleigh, aircraft, bicycle or other conveyance." (99%)
- An [[FSR]] (Forest Service Road) is kept by the Ministry of Forests, not Transportation, so my reading is that the road allowance ban above does not name it (70%, interpretation, VERIFY with a conservation officer). The vehicle rules still apply on every road (99%).
- Safe habit on any road: stop, engine off, out of the truck, walk 15 m off the road and off the pavement, then load (Tip). Never shoot along a road or at a bird on the road surface (Tip).
- Motor vehicle closed areas and hunting closures in Region 3 are mapped at gov.bc.ca/thompsonaccess (99%). Above 1,700 m in Region 3, vehicles only on existing roads and trails (99%).
- Sun Peaks: no shooting or hunting within 400 m of any existing ski lift, run, facility or development of the resort, MU 3-27, Map C11 (99%).
```

## Tactics

| Option | How | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| A: walk the edges | Slow, stop often, dawn and dusk | Your first trips |
| B: still hunt roosts | Look up for a dark blob midday | .22 head shots |
| C: ride and walk | Spot from the truck, walk off, load | Only if every road rule is kept |

```video
yt-grouse-anyone | Grouse hunting needs no special gear. Just walk.
```

> [!lean]
> Option A with the shotgun for the first trips. Add the .22 once you can tell the three species on the ground every time.

```more the three options, pros and cons
### Option A: walk the edges
- Walk old roads, cut block edges and aspen draws slowly at dawn and the last 2 hours of light. Stop often; a stopped hunter makes a grouse flush (Tip).
- Pros: simple, good exercise, all three species. Cons: birds flush at your feet, snap shots.

### Option B: still hunt the roosts midday
- Spruce grouse sit in pines and spruce midday. Look up for a dark blob on a branch. Dusky grouse sit in big Douglas fir (Tip).
- Pros: calm birds, .22 head shots. Cons: slow, needs a good eye.

### Option C: ride and walk
- Drive an FSR at dawn with the gun unloaded and cased, spot a bird on the shoulder, stop, get out, walk off the road, load, then hunt (Tip). Legal only if every rule on the last screen is kept (99%).
- Pros: covers ground. Cons: the easiest way to get a ticket.
```

## Care and yield

```diagram rb-evidence-birds
```

- One feathered wing stays on until home (99%).
- Note the species: Region 3 limits are per species (99%).

```diagram sb-bird-yield
```

```more care steps and yield
- Keep one feathered wing on each grouse until home. BC synopsis, page 15: anyone who possesses or transports a game bird other than a migratory game bird "must leave attached to the carcass one feathered wing" (99%).
- [[Edible portions]] are both breasts and you must take them home (99%).
- Gut right away in warm weather. The skin peels off with the thumbs; the breast lifts off the keel (Tip).
- A ruffed grouse of about 500 g gives roughly 200 to 280 g of boned meat (60%, search preview). A dusky grouse gives twice that (Tip).
- Grouse breast is white, dry meat. Do not overcook. Spruce grouse in late fall can taste of pine needles; soak or brine it (Tip).
- Note the species in the Journal. The aggregate limit is by species in Region 3 (99%).
```

```more the mistakes in full
> [!mistake]
> - Shooting from the open truck window at a grouse on the shoulder. Two offences in one shot.
> - Calling every dark grouse a "blue" and losing count of the 5 per species in Region 3.
> - A .22 shot at a bird on a skyline branch with nothing behind it but sky and a house below.

- Also: hunting the Sun Peaks ski runs or within 400 m of a lift (99% closed).
- Also: taking a sharp tailed grouse outside MUs 3-30 and 3-31 because it "looked like a grouse" (99% no season).
```
