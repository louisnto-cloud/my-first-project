> [!why]
> Alaska is the dream and the paperwork. As a Canadian you are a "nonresident alien" there: a guide for every big game animal, a $630 licence, and a US firearm permit before you leave home.

Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

## Can a BC resident hunt here

- Yes, as a **nonresident alien**: not a US citizen and not living in the US (85%).
- Big game: "A nonresident alien must be personally accompanied by an Alaska licensed guide to hunt ANY big game animal" (85%, quote shown without hyphen).
- That list: black bear, brown or grizzly bear, bison, caribou, Dall sheep, deer, elk, moose, mountain goat, muskox, wolf and wolverine (85%).
- The guide must be within 100 yards (91 m) of you when you attempt to take game (85%).
- Small game and waterfowl: no guide needed (80%).
- The "second degree of kindred" exemption, where a US nonresident hunts sheep, goat or brown bear with a close Alaska resident relative, does **not** apply to nonresident aliens (85%).
- Your brother is a nonresident alien too. Two guides, or one guide booked for two hunters: ask the outfitter.

## Without a guide, or with one

| Animal | BC resident may hunt | What it takes |
|---|---|---|
| Grouse, ptarmigan, hares | Yes, no guide (80%) | Nonresident alien hunting licence (85%) |
| Ducks and geese | Yes, no guide (80%) | Nonresident alien licence, Alaska waterfowl stamp, Federal Duck Stamp (85%) |
| Black bear, deer, caribou, moose, wolf | Guide required (85%) | Licence, locking tag, harvest ticket or permit (85%) |
| Brown or grizzly bear, Dall sheep, mountain goat | Guide required (85%) | Licence, locking tag, often a draw permit (85%) |
| Bison, muskox, elk | Guide required (85%) | Draw or registration permits (80%) |

- Harvest tickets and locking tags must be in hand before the hunt. The locking tag goes on the animal (80%).

## How to apply

1. Buy the nonresident alien hunting licence online at the Alaska Department of Fish and Game store. Do this first: ATF needs a copy (85%).
2. File ATF Form 6NIA (see firearms screen). Allow 4 to 6 weeks (85%).
3. Book a registered Alaska guide for any big game. The guide usually handles tags and permits with you (80%).
4. Buy locking tags before the hunt. Carry harvest tickets (80%).
5. Read the current Alaska Hunting Regulations for your GMU (Game Management Unit) (85%).

## Best first trip for you

> [!lean]
> Not big game, not yet. The honest first Alaska trip for two Canadians is a September road trip through Yukon to the Tok and Anchorage country for ptarmigan, grouse and ducks on the nonresident alien licence, with the paperwork done right. ### Leaving Canada
- CBSA (Canada Border Services Agency): you do not have to stop at a CBSA office when leaving with a non restricted firearm (85%).
- Recommended: CBSA Form BSF407, Identification of Articles for Temporary Exportation, listing the serial number, so you can show the rifle was not bought abroad (85%). Formerly Form Y38 (85%).
- RCMP (Royal Canadian Mounted Police): an export permit from Global Affairs Canada "may be required" for firearms, parts or ammunition taken out temporarily for hunting (80%). Call Global Affairs, 343 203 4331, and ask: VERIFY whether it applies to a hunter's own rifle.

### Entering the US
- ATF (Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives) Form 6NIA: "any nonimmigrant alien temporarily bringing firearm(s) and/or ammunition into the United States for legitimate hunting or lawful sporting purposes must first obtain an approved ATF Form 6NIA" (85%).
- The application needs a copy of a valid US state hunting licence. Your Alaska licence satisfies this (85%).
- Processing about 4 to 6 weeks. Approval valid one year (85%).
- At the border, CBP (Customs and Border Protection): present the approved 6NIA plus the hunting licence (85%).
- The form covers the Tikka .308, the 12 gauge and the ammunition you list. One form per hunter: your brother files his own (80%).
- Form fee: VERIFY, believed none.

### Coming home
- CBSA: residents may re import non restricted firearms by presenting a valid [[PAL]] (Possession and Acquisition Licence) for that class (85%).
- Declare the firearms. Have the BSF407 card ready (85%).
- Leftover US ammunition coming back: declare it. Limits: VERIFY on the CBSA page.

> [!warn]
> This is the complicated part. Start the 6NIA in June for a September trip. No approved form, no rifle across the line. The guide can rent you one, but ptarmigan with a borrowed gun is not the trip you planned.

## Bringing meat, hides and antlers home

### Alaska's export rules
- All brown or grizzly and black bears must be **sealed** by Fish and Game before they leave Alaska (85%).
- Sheep rams from listed units must be sealed, and horns may not leave the state before sealing (85%).
- Sealing is done within 30 days of the kill, or sooner if the permit says so (85%).
- A CITES (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species) permit is required to ship hides, skulls, meat or other parts of bears, wolves or lynx out of the US. Issued by USFWS (US Fish and Wildlife Service) offices in Anchorage and Fairbanks (85%).
- USFWS Form 3-177, Declaration of Importation or Exportation of Fish and Wildlife, may be required for meat, hides, skulls or trophies leaving the US (85%).

### Canada's import rules
- CFIA (Canadian Food Inspection Agency): a hunter harvested game carcass from the US may enter if the hunter's licence accompanies it and you bring it yourself, not by courier. No CFIA quantity limit (80%, search preview). canada.ca was not reachable: VERIFY on the CFIA page linked below.
- Wild game birds you shot in the US are permitted with the hunting permit (80%). Bird flu rules change fast: VERIFY before the trip.
- CBSA declares everything. Keep the Alaska licence and tags with the meat (80%).

### BC's import rule (CWD)
- [[CWD]] (Chronic Wasting Disease) has not been found in Alaska, but BC's rule does not care where the animal came from (80%).
- From the BC Synopsis 2026 to 2028, page 12, It's Unlawful item 50 (99%):
- "To possess any part of a Cervidae (caribou, deer, elk, moose), that was killed outside of British Columbia, other than (1) the edible meat of the four quarters, loins, neck, and ribs, or (2) the hide, antlers, and skull plate if, before being brought into British Columbia, the part is cleaned in a manner that removes all tissue." (99%)
- So an Alaska caribou or Sitka deer comes home boned out, antlers on a skull plate scraped clean. Item 51 also bans using any out of province cervid part for hunting in BC (99%).
- You also drive through Yukon, which bans cervid carcasses and parts except cleaned skull caps with antlers, cleaned teeth, meat detached from head and backbone, finished mounts and tanned hides (85%).

## Blaze orange rules

- "Alaska does not require wearing hunter orange clothing, but investigators consistently have found that it reduces hunting accidents." (85%, Alaska hunter education page)
- Some youth hunts require blaze orange vests or jackets (85%).
- My lean: orange hat at minimum. You will be hunting the same ptarmigan slopes as locals in September.

## Before you go

```checklist trip-alaska-prep
Buy nonresident alien hunting licence online, print it
File ATF Form 6NIA for each hunter, 4 to 6 weeks ahead
Ask Global Affairs Canada whether an export permit applies
CBSA Form BSF407 card for each firearm
Alaska waterfowl stamp and Federal Duck Stamp if hunting ducks
Guide booked in writing for any big game
Current Alaska Hunting Regulations for your GMU downloaded
Passports, PAL cards, licences and 6NIA in one folder
Plan the meat: bone out, clean skull plate, keep tags with the meat
```

> [!rule]
> Paperwork is part of the hunt. A trip that starts with a refused rifle at the border ends there.

> [!mistake]
> - Thinking "nonresident" rules apply to you. Canadians are nonresident aliens and need a guide for all big game.
> - Applying for the 6NIA before buying the Alaska licence. ATF wants the licence copy with the form.
> - Packing a caribou head for the taxidermist. Yukon and BC both say no.

> [!field]
> Ptarmigan hold in willow above treeline in September. Walk the brush with the 12 gauge, modified choke, 6 shot, and watch for bears on every berry slope.
