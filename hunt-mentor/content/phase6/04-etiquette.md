> [!why]
> Most of what keeps hunting open in BC is not written in the Wildlife Act. It is how we treat gates, ranchers, hikers and each other.

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law. Most of this session is manners. Where a line is also law, it says so with a page.

- The synopsis has its own ethics code on page 77. It opens: "It is extremely important for hunters and trappers to realize that they use a public resource!" (99%).
- "Respect your fellow hunters. Work with one another to promote good relationships, safety, knowledge and a fair distribution of opportunity and harvest" (99%).
- "Lead by example, you are an ambassador for the public and media image of hunting." (99%)
- Everything else here is my opinion, learned the hard way or borrowed from better hunters. Tip.

## Other hunters

- A truck at a pullout means someone is on that ridge. Drive on. Tip.
- Someone sitting a meadow at first light owns it for the morning. Hunt a different drainage. Tip.
- Walking in on another hunter's stalk is the worst thing you can do short of a bad shot. Back out quietly. Tip.
- It is unlawful "to interfere with or obstruct a person licensed or permitted to hunt, guide or trap while that person is lawfully so engaged" (99%, page 12, item 45).
- Meet on a trail: unload, muzzle down, say where you are headed and ask where they are. BC Parks asks for exactly this (95%).
- Someone has a deer down? Offer a hand with the pack out. You will want the same one day. Tip.

## Gates, fences and roads

- "Cattle grazing takes place on both public and private land. To keep cattle in the correct pasture, it is important that hunters leave gates and fences as they are found." (99%, Region 3 notice, page 33)
- "Please respect gates, fences, and equipment as they are there for a reason. Leave everything the way you found it." (99%, page 77)
- Open gate stays open, shut gate stays shut. Chained gate means do not enter. Tip.
- [[FSR]] (Forest Service Road) courtesy: loaded logging trucks have the right of way, drive on the right, slow down past camps and pullouts, call your kilometres on the radio channel if the road is posted. Tip.
- ATVs on an FSR: the driver needs a valid driver's licence and $200,000 third party liability insurance (99%, page 9). The [[ORV]] (Off Road Vehicle) must be registered and the plate shown (99%).
- Mud bogging and new trails are an offence under the Forest and Range Practices Act, with fines starting at $575 (99%, page 9).

## Camps and cabins

- "Respect the environment by packing out everything you pack in! Nobody wants to arrive at a campsite and clean up the mess left by the previous occupants." (99%, page 77)
- Litter is an offence (99%, page 11, item 9).
- Recreation sites: no shooting on or into the developed portion, which runs 100 m from any structure, day use or camping area, and no dumping game residue there (99%, page 9).
- Trappers' and guides' cabins: "They are private and are not to be entered, damaged, or disturbed" (99%, page 9).
- Keep your camp small, your fire legal and your meat pole down when you leave (95%, BC Parks guide). Tip.
- Quiet hours: no generators or engines before shooting light. Everyone came for the same dawn. Tip.

## Landowners and locals

- "Respect private property. Always seek permission from landowners and thank them for the opportunities given. Unless explicitly stated, permission granted once does not entitle you to access the property on subsequent occasions." (99%, page 77)
- Stop at the ranch house even when the land is Crown next door. Tell them where you will park. Tip.
- Buy fuel, coffee and a sandwich in the small town. Locals notice who spends money and who only spends their patience. Tip.
- Session 6 covers how to ask for permission and keep it.

## Photos and social media

- "Have respect for the sensitivities of others. Do not display your kill or firearms where it may offend other people. What may impress your friends may not have the same effect on your neighbours." (99%, page 77)
- Clean the animal up for the photo: tongue in, blood wiped, muzzle pointed away. Tip.
- No GPS (Global Positioning System) coordinates, no gate, no road sign, no landowner's name in the picture or the caption. A spot shared online is a spot lost. Tip.
- Turn off location tagging on the phone camera before the trip. Tip.
- Do not post a kill from someone else's land without asking them. Tip.
- A tailgate full of grouse in the grocery store parking lot is not a flex. Tip.

## Sharing meat

- Giving meat away is fine. Selling it is not. It is unlawful to "traffic in live wildlife, wildlife meat or offer to do so, except as authorized by permit" (99%, page 11, item 7).
- Meat that travels without the hunter needs a [[Record of Receipt]] (99%, page 15).
- The record shows the date and place of receipt, the name and address of the person who killed the animal, the name and address of the person receiving it, and the [[FWID]] (Fish and Wildlife ID) or permit number of the hunter (99%, page 15). The list continues with the licence details (90%).
- Game birds other than ducks and geese keep one feathered wing attached while transported (99%, page 15).
- Share the good cuts, not just the trim. Label every package with the date and the cut. Tip.
- Tell the person how to cook it. Most wild meat gets ruined on the stove, not in the field. Tip.

## Non hunters

- Hikers, bikers and mushroom pickers own the bush as much as you do. Say good morning first. Tip.
- Gut piles: keep them away from trails, campsites and recreation sites, or tell park staff where the kill is (95%, BC Parks guide, page 9 of the synopsis).
- Firearm in the open at a trailhead: cased or slung, muzzle down, action open. Tip.
- A loaded firearm in or on a vehicle is an offence, and so is shooting from one (99%, page 11, items 22 and 27). Non hunters report exactly this.
- Never argue hunting at a trailhead. Be polite, be brief, be gone. Tip.
- The RAPP (Report All Poachers and Polluters) line, 1 877 952 7277, works for us too. Report the ones who make us all look bad (99%, page 9).

```checklist s6-etiquette
Gates left exactly as I found them

Record of Receipt written for any meat that travels without me
Hikers met: unloaded, muzzle down, greeted first
```

> [!rule]
> Hunt like the rancher is watching, because he usually is.

> [!mistake]
> - Driving past the ranch house to the Crown gate without stopping. Next year the gate is locked.
> - Posting a photo with a road sign in the background. Ten trucks next weekend.
> - Walking the ridge another hunter is already on because "it is Crown land." Legal, and still wrong.

> [!field]
> When you meet another hunter, say three things: where you are going, where you have been, and good luck. Then go somewhere else.
