> [!why]
> Heffley Creek sits inside a ring of 10 MUs you can reach in 60 to 90 minutes. Know the lines, the closures and the roads, and the whole Thompson opens up.

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Base: Heffley Creek, north of Kamloops on Hwy 5. You sleep there and hunt the whole Region 3.
- Region 3 is the Thompson region. Its rules live on pages 32 to 35 of the synopsis (99%).
- Quotes below are verbatim except hyphens, which this app's style removes.
- Assumption: Heffley Creek is on the east bank of the North Thompson, so it sits in [[MU]] (Management Unit) 3-27 (90%, read from Map C7 and the Region 3 MU map).
- Assumption: parts of Heffley Creek may be inside the City of Kamloops boundary. The city bans all firearm discharge inside its limits (see No Shooting screen). VERIFY the city boundary line on the City of Kamloops map before any shot near the base.

## MU boundaries in words

- 3-27: east of the North Thompson River, north of the South Thompson River. It holds Heffley Creek, Sun Peaks, Paul Lake and Louis Creek. Its north line toward 3-38 is not drawn clearly on the small map: VERIFY (85%).
- 3-28: west of the North Thompson River, from the Kamloops city edge north toward Barriere and Bonaparte Lake. The Deadman River side is its west edge toward 3-29 and 3-30 (85%).
- 3-29: the Tranquille and Deadman country north of Kamloops Lake, Lac du Bois Park on its east side (85%).
- 3-26: east of 3-27 and 3-20, from the Pritchard and Chase area along the Shuswap south shore to Salmon Arm and Sicamous (85%).
- 3-20: south of the South Thompson, east of Kamloops, bounded by the Salmon River country toward Vernon (85%).
- 3-19: south of Kamloops Lake and the Thompson, west of 3-20, down to the Nicola Lake side (85%).
- 3-38: north of 3-27 and 3-28, around Little Fort, east to Adams Lake country (80%).
- 3-37: around the south end of Adams Lake and the Adams River. 3-36: north of it toward Seymour Arm (80%).
- 3-39, 3-40, 3-41: the three units that meet at Clearwater (85%).

## Deer seasons, October to December

| MU group | Mule deer | White tailed deer |
|---|---|---|
| 3-12 to 3-20, 3-26 to 3-44 | Bucks Oct 1 to Oct 31. 4 point bucks Nov 1 to Dec 10 | Bucks Sept 10 to Dec 10. Antlerless Oct 10 to Oct 31 |
| 3-46 (Wells Gray north) | Bucks Oct 1 to Oct 31. 4 point bucks Nov 1 to Dec 10 | Bucks Sept 20 to Dec 10. Antlerless Oct 10 to Oct 31 |

- Mule deer rows, page 34: "3-12 to 3-20, 3-26 to 3-44, 3-46 Bucks Oct 1 - Oct 31" and "3-12 to 3-20, 3-26 to 3-44, 3-46 4 Point Bucks Nov 1 - Dec 10" (99%).
- White tailed deer rows, page 34: "3-12 to 3-20, 3-26 to 3-44 Bucks Sept 10 - Dec 10" and "3-12 to 3-20, 3-26 to 3-44, 3-46 Antlerless Oct 10 - Oct 31" (99%).
- New this edition: "Bow Only 3-12 to 3-20, 3-26 to 3-44, 3-46 Either Sex Dec 11 - Dec 20" for white tails (99%).
- So in late October any mule deer buck is legal in every MU on your list. From 1 November a mule deer buck needs 4 points on one side (95%).
- Footnote, page 34: "See Definitions Section: Mule deer. The antlers must accompany the species licence." (99%)
- Antlerless white tails: 10 to 31 October only. After that, bucks only unless you are under 18 (95%).
- No general season for antlerless mule deer. Page 33: "Antlerless mule (black tailed) deer are under [[LEH]] only." LEH is Limited Entry Hunting (99%).

## Deer bag limits and reporting

- Page 33: "The aggregate bag limit for deer is 3. The bag limit for mule (black tailed) deer is 1. Antlerless mule (black tailed) deer are under LEH only. The bag limit for white tailed deer is 2, either sex." (99%)
- Page 14: only 1 mule deer buck per licence year from Regions 3, 4, 5, 6, 7A, 7B and 8 combined (98%).
- Page 32: "Submit deer, elk, and moose heads for [[CWD]] testing." CWD is Chronic Wasting Disease. Testing is voluntary in Region 3; it is mandatory only in listed Kootenay MUs (95%).
- Page 32: "Submit deer, elk, and moose heads from MUs 3-12, 3-19, and 3-20 for Bovine Tuberculosis testing." (99%)
- Page 33, It's Unlawful: "To intentionally bait or feed ungulates within 200m of a dwelling, school yard, or playground in region 3." (99%)
- Hours, page 12: no hunting game except migratory birds "from one hour after sunset to one hour before sunrise" (99%).

## Late October deer: where to look

- Mule deer come down to [[Winter range]] in October and November, pushed by snow and cold, not the rut (85%).
- Winter range here is the low, sunny ground: south and west facing slopes, old Douglas fir, grassland benches (85%). The Dewdrop range below 760 m north of Kamloops is a studied winter range (80%).
- Late October, no snow yet: bucks are often still mid slope, 900 to 1,500 m, on open fir and burn edges. First snow moves them down (75%).
- White tails: river bottoms, cottonwoods and hayfields along the North Thompson and the Salmon River side of 3-20 (75%).
- Landscape types to glass, from a seat with a tripod:
  - A grassland bench with a timber edge above it, viewed from across a draw. Deer feed on the bench at first light (Tip).
  - A south facing burn or cutblock with 5 to 15 year regrowth. Browse plus sight lines (Tip).
  - Broken rock and fir benches above a lake or river, viewed from the far shore (Tip).
  - A hayfield edge at dusk for white tails, from a fence corner 200 m back (Tip).
- Wind: valley air sinks downstream and downslope at night and in the evening, rises in late morning (80%). Glass from above in the morning, from below or across in the evening (Tip).
- Aspect rule: cold morning, glass the sunny east and south faces first. Warm afternoon, check north and east shade edges where they bed (Tip).
- MU 3-28 and 3-29 are foot or truck only for hunting in the fall. Park on an open road and walk in. Fewer ATVs means calmer deer (Tip).
