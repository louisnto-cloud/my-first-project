> [!why]
> The shot is 1 second. The next 3 hours decide if you find the deer and if the meat is good.

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Fast order: **wait, mark, track, approach, notch, evidence, dress, cool, transport, report.**
- Tagging and evidence rules are law. Tracking and dressing steps are skills (Tip).
- Your brother hunts too. Each of you notches only your own licence, for your own kill (99%).

## 1. Wait and mark

- Stay still. Watch where it runs. Listen for a crash. Tip.
- Rifle reloaded and on safe. Watch for 5 minutes in case it stands. Tip.
- Drop a phone map pin where you shot from and where the deer stood. Tip.
- Note a landmark at the last place you saw it (a tree, a rock). Tip.
- Wait about 30 minutes before you follow. Longer if you think the hit was far back. Tip, common hunter practice, not law.

## 2. Track

- Go to where it stood. Look for blood, hair, scuffed ground. Tip.
- Bright pink, bubbly blood often means a lung hit. Dark blood or gut matter: back out and wait hours. Tip.
- Mark each blood spot with tape or a phone pin. Tip.
- Lost the trail? Go back to the last blood. Search in slow circles. Get your brother to help. Tip.
- Trail goes into fenced or posted private land: stop. The Trespass Act has no exception for tracking wounded game. Get the owner's permission first, in writing if you can (99%).
- Trail goes into a No Shooting Area or a park closed to hunting: no shooting there (99%). Call a conservation officer. Tip. See Phase 2 Session 5.
- You must make all reasonable effort to find, finish and retrieve a wounded animal, and count it in your bag limit (99%).

## 3. Approach and notch

- Come from behind the back, rifle ready. Touch the eye with a stick. No blink means dead. Tip.
- **Notch (cancel) the species licence immediately after the kill and before you handle the animal** (99%).
- Cancel it the way the instructions printed on that licence say (99%). The synopsis does not list the steps (95%).
- A cancelled licence records the date, the region number and, where it applies, the antler configuration (95%).
- Read the instructions on your own licence before the hunt. Tip.
- Keep the notched licence with you. Carry all licences for the year (99%).
- Take photos: the deer, the notched licence, the spot. Tip.

## 4. Evidence of sex

- Evidence of sex stays **naturally attached** to the carcass, or to one part of it, until it is home and butchered there, at a meat cutter or cold storage plant, or inspected (99%).
- Buck: the unskinned tail **and** a testicle or part of the penis (99%).
- Doe: the unskinned tail **and** part of the udder and teats (99%).
- Antlers on the head are not on the deer list. Keep the tail and sex parts even on a buck (95%).
- Antler restricted season (like 4 point): keep the antlers naturally attached to part of the upper skull, together with the species licence, while you return from hunting (99%).
- Do **not** cut these off during field dressing. Plan your cuts around them. Tip.

## 5. Field dress (gutted method)


- Gloves on. Do not cut the stomach or guts (Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife food safety page, 75%).
- If gut contents touch meat, wipe them off right away with a clean cloth (75%).
- Do it fast. Remove the guts as quickly as you can (75%).
- Leave the evidence of sex attached. Check before each cut. Tip.

## 6. Gutless method (overview)

- Skin one side down the back. Take the hind quarter, front quarter, back strap. Tip.
- Reach in behind the last rib for the tenderloin. Tip.
- Flip and repeat. Then neck meat and rib meat. Tip.
- You must still take neck, ribs, all four quarters and loins (99%). These are the legal edible portions.
- Boned meat: leave the unskinned tail and the sex parts naturally attached to one piece. The rest can be cut and bagged (99%).
- Keep that piece with the rest of the meat, labelled. Official advice (95%).
- Good for a deer far from the truck. Guts and bones can stay at the site, but not where people are likely to be: a carcass is a wildlife attractant and can draw large predators (95%).

## 7. Cool the meat

- Cool it below 4°C (40°F) as fast as you can (75%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife).
- Best: in a cooler or cold room the same day (75%).
- Prop the chest open. Air must move. Shade, off the ground. Tip.
- **No plastic or tarp** on a warm carcass. It holds the heat (75%).
- Light game bags keep flies off (75%).
- Warm October afternoon in the valley: get it to a cooler fast. Tip.

## 8. Transport legally

- Carry your notched species licence with the meat (99%).
- Anyone moving your deer **without you** needs a **Record of Receipt** (99%).
- Record of Receipt: date and place, your name and address, who gets it, your [[FWID]] (Fish and Wildlife ID), species licence number, species and sex (99%).
- Evidence of sex stays attached in transport (99%).
- Rifle unloaded in the truck or on the [[ATV]] (all terrain vehicle): chamber and attached magazine empty (99%).
- Region 3 is not in the [[CWD]] (Chronic Wasting Disease) Management Zone, so its parts rules do not apply here (99%).

## 9. Report

- Deer are not on the compulsory inspection or compulsory reporting lists (99%).
- Those lists cover goat, sheep, caribou, cougar, lynx, bobcat, wolverine and a few more. Reports are due within 30 days with location, date, days hunted, weapon and more (99%).
- Mandatory hunter reports cover elk, moose and caribou licences, not deer (99%).
- Region 3 asks for deer heads for [[CWD]] (Chronic Wasting Disease) testing. Outside the Kootenay zone it is your choice (95%).
- Write it in your journal tonight. Tip.

> [!rule]
> Notch first, knife second. And never let a warm deer sit in a closed truck.

> [!mistake]
> - Following too soon and pushing a hit deer out of the country.
> - Cutting off the evidence of sex while gutting.
> - Wrapping a warm carcass in a tarp for the drive home.

> [!field]
> Pack: knife, spare blade, gloves, game bags, 10 m of rope, flagging tape, pen, Record of Receipt notes.
