> [!why]
> A new scope is not aimed until you shoot it. A shotgun is not understood until you see its pattern.

## The plan

```diagram gr-sight-plan
```

- **3 shot groups** from a solid rest. Adjust from the group centre (70%).
- Same load you hunt with. Different bullets hit different spots (75%).

```more full plan and what to bring
- Rifle: [[Bore sight]], then 25 m to get on paper, then 100 m (109 yd) for the real [[Zero]] (Tip).
- Shoot **3 shot groups** from a solid rest. Adjust from the centre of the group, not one shot (70%).
- Use the **same load** you will hunt with. Different bullets hit different spots (75%).
- Shotgun: [[Patterning]] at 37 m (40 yd) on a 76 cm (30 in) circle (70%).
- Bring: ammo, targets, stapler, rest or bags, ear and eye protection, tools for the turrets (Tip).
- Let the barrel cool between groups. A hot barrel can shift shots (Tip).
```

## Step 1: bore sight

```steps Bore sight in 3 steps
gr-bore-1 | Prove it unloaded. Bolt out. Solid rest, target at 25 m.
gr-bore-2 | Look through the bore. Centre the target.
anim:gr-bore-align | Rifle still. Turn turrets onto the same spot.
```

```more the full steps
1. Prove the rifle unloaded. Bolt out ([[ACTS]]).
2. Set the rifle solid in a rest or sandbags, target at 25 m.
3. Look **through the bore** from the back. Move the rifle until the target is centred in the bore (70%).
4. Without moving the rifle, look through the scope.
5. Turn the turrets until the crosshair sits on the same spot (70%).
- A shop laser bore sighter does the same job. It still is not a zero (Tip).
```

## Step 2: 25 m, then 100 m

```steps Zero in 4 steps
gr-zero-25 | 25 m: centre the group left and right.
gr-click-grid | 100 m: measure from the group centre to the bull.
anim:gr-group-move | Turn the clicks. Shoot again to confirm.
gr-zero-field | Then shoot from sitting with sticks.
```

```more the full steps
- At 25 m: fire 1 shot, check, then a 3 shot group. Get it centred left and right (Tip).
- Move to 100 m (109 yd). Fire a 3 shot group at the bullseye.
- Find the centre of the group. Measure how far it is from the bullseye, in cm.
- Turn the turrets the right number of clicks (next screen).
- Fire another 3 shot group to confirm. Repeat until centred.
- Then shoot from sitting and kneeling with sticks. That is how you will shoot a deer (Tip).
```

## Click math (1/4 MOA scope)

```diagram gr-click-grid
```

- Clicks = cm off ÷ 0.73, at 100 m (85%). Check the turret cap says 1/4 MOA (minute of angle) (70%).

```more MOA, yards, 25 m and MRAD
- MOA is a tiny angle: about **2.9 cm at 100 m** (85%).
- Most hunting scopes move **1/4 MOA per click** (70%). Check the turret cap.
- So 1 click moves the shot about **0.73 cm at 100 m** (85%).
  - At 100 yd (91 m): about 0.66 cm per click (85%).
  - At 25 m: about 0.18 cm per click. You need 4 times the clicks (85%).
- Example: group is 6 cm low and 3 cm left at 100 m.
  - Up: 6 ÷ 0.73 = about **8 clicks** "UP".
  - Right: 3 ÷ 0.73 = about **4 clicks** "R".
- Turn the knob the way the arrow says to move the **bullet** (UP, R) (70%).
- Scope in MRAD (milliradian)? Then 0.1 MRAD per click is 1 cm at 100 m (85%).
```

## Which zero?

```diagram trajectory-308
```

- Zero at **100 m** this season. Then see the real drop at 200 m (Tip).

```more 100 m, 200 m or MPBR
| Zero | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| 100 m | Easy to set and check at any range. Dead on at typical deer distance | Bullet drops more past 200 m |
| 200 m | Flatter to about 250 m. About 4.6 cm high at 100 yd (65%) | Harder to check. Need a 200 m range |
| MPBR (maximum point blank range) | Hold centre, hit a 15 cm (6 in) vital zone out to the longest distance possible (70%) | Needs a ballistic calculator. Bullet is high at middle ranges |

- MPBR picks the zero so the bullet never rises or falls more than half the vital zone (70%).
- Your 20 inch barrel shoots slower than factory test barrels. Expect more drop than the box says (Tip).
```

> [!lean]
> **Zero at 100 m** this season. Shoot one group at 200 m to see the real drop.
> Your limit: the distance where you hit a paper plate from sitting (Tip).

## Patterning the shotgun

```steps Pattern in 3 steps
gr-pat-1 | Paper, aim dot, pace off 37 m (40 yd).
pattern-target | Circle the densest part. Count holes inside.
gr-pattern-math | Holes ÷ pellets in the shell = your percent.
```

```more the full steps
- Hang paper about 1 m square. Mark an aim dot in the middle (Tip).
- Pace or measure **37 m (40 yd)**. Shoot from a rest at the dot (70%).
- One shell per sheet. Shoot 3 sheets with your duck load and choke (Tip).
- Draw a **76 cm (30 in)** circle around the **densest part** of the pattern (70%).
- Count the pellet holes inside the circle.
- Percent = holes in circle ÷ pellets in the shell × 100 (70%).
```

## Reading the pattern

```diagram gr-pattern-math
```

- Look for **gaps** a duck could slip through. Gaps matter more than percent (Tip).

```more pellet counts and benchmarks
- #2 steel has about 120 to 124 pellets per ounce. A 1 1/8 oz shell holds about 136 to 140 (65%).
- Example: 82 holes in the circle ÷ 138 pellets = about **59%**. That acts like Modified (70%).
- Benchmarks: Cylinder 40%, IC (Improved Cylinder) 50%, M (Modified) 60%, Full 70% (70%).
- Is the pattern centred on the dot? If not, the gun shoots high, low or to a side. Know it (Tip).
- Look for **holes** big enough for a duck to slip through. Gaps matter more than the percent (Tip).
- Try IC and M with 2 loads. Keep the combo with the even, centred pattern (Tip).
```

> [!rule]
> Three shots tell the truth. One shot tells a story. Never move the scope for a single hole.

> [!mistake]
> - Zeroing with cheap ammo, hunting with another bullet.
> - Adjusting after every shot.
> - Never patterning the shotgun.

> [!field]
> Write zero, load and 200 m drop on tape inside the scope cap. Check with 1 shot before each trip.
