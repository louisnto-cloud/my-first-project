## Magnification and objective

```diagram gr-zoom
```

- "3 to 9x40": zoom 3 to 9 times, 40 mm front lens (85%).
- More zoom is not better. It shrinks your view (Tip).

```more low power, high power, lens size
- "3 to 9x40" means zoom 3 times to 9 times, with a 40 mm front lens (85%).
- Low power (2.5 to 3x) gives a wide view for close deer in timber (65%).
- 9 to 10x is plenty for a deer at 300 m (65%).
- Objective 40 to 44 mm is the sweet spot. Bigger sits higher and weighs more (65%).
- More zoom is not better. It shrinks your view and adds weight (Tip).
```

## Focal plane and reticle

```anim gr-focal-plane Watch the crosshair as the scope zooms.
```

```diagram gr-reticle
```

- Beginner pick: **SFP** (second focal plane) with a **duplex** crosshair (70%).

```more SFP, FFP, BDC explained
- SFP (second focal plane): crosshair stays the same size when you zoom (70%).
  - Simple. Best for beginners. Hash marks are only true at one power (70%).
- FFP (first focal plane): crosshair grows with zoom. Marks are true at every power (70%).
  - Better for long range dialing. Thin crosshair at low power in dim light (65%).
- Reticle: **duplex** (thick outer posts, thin centre) is simplest (70%).
- BDC (bullet drop compensator) hash marks help only after you confirm drops on paper (70%).
- The distances on the BDC drawing are an example only. Every scope and load differs.
```

## Turrets, eye relief, parallax

```diagram gr-turret
```

- 1 click = 1/4 MOA (minute of angle), about 0.73 cm at 100 m (70 to 85%).
- Eye relief 9 cm (3.5 in) or more. A .308 kicks (65%).

```more turrets, eye relief, parallax
- **Turrets:** the knobs that move the crosshair. Capped turrets cannot get bumped in the bush (65%).
  - Most hunting scopes move 1/4 MOA per click (70%). Session 5 has the math.
- **Eye relief:** distance from your eye to the rear lens for a full picture (85%).
  - Look for about 9 cm (3.5 in) or more. A .308 kicks, and short eye relief cuts your brow (65%).
- **Parallax:** the crosshair seems to shift if your eye is off centre (80%).
  - Fixed at 100 yd (91 m) is fine for hunting out to about 300 m (65%).
```

## Your Tikka T3x Arctic

```diagram gr-tikka-clearance
```

- Bring the rifle to the store. **Dry fit** scope and rings before you pay (Tip).

```more rail, sight and ring notes
- 20 inch stainless barrel, 1 in 11 twist, removable rail (80%).
- Rail is short, about 12 to 13 cm (4.75 to 5 in), to make room for the rear sight (70%).
- Rear sight is a rotary drum peep sight, 100 to 600 m (75%).
- Owners report **high rings** needed to clear the drum with a 40 to 44 mm scope (60%).
- A cantilever mount clears the drum but puts the scope high (60%).
- Some owners remove the irons. Removing them yourself: VERIFY with a gunsmith (60%).
- Rail type (Picatinny or Weaver slots): VERIFY at the store.
- Dry fit: check clearance over the drum, and that the rings land on the scope tube, not on the turret or bells (Tip).
```

## Mounting steps

```steps Mount the scope in 6 steps
gr-mount-1 | Prove it unloaded. Bolt out.
gr-mount-2 | Clean the rail. Push ring bases forward in the slots.
gr-mount-3 | Lay the scope in the bottom rings. Caps loose.
gr-mount-4 | Slide for a full picture, then 1 cm forward.
gr-mount-5 | Level the crosshair to the rifle.
gr-mount-6 | Tighten in a cross. Torque to the maker's number.
```

```more the full steps and torque table
1. Prove the rifle unloaded. Bolt out ([[ACTS]]).
2. Clean and degrease rail, ring bases and screws (Tip).
3. Fit ring bases to the rail. Push them forward in the slots, toward the muzzle (Tip).
4. Lay the scope in the bottom ring halves. Caps on loosely.
5. Set eye relief: rifle at your shoulder, scope on highest power, slide it until the full picture appears. Then 1 cm more forward (Tip).
6. Level the crosshair to the rifle, not to the floor (Tip).
7. Tighten cap screws a little at a time, in a cross pattern. Keep the gaps even on both sides (Tip).
8. Torque to the ring maker's number with a torque wrench.

| Rings | Maker torque | Certainty |
|---|---|---|
| Vortex Pro | Caps 18 in lb, cross bolt 65 in lb | (70%) |
| Burris Signature Zee | Caps 20 in lb, cross bolt 30 in lb | (65%) |
| Leupold (standard rings) | Ring screws up to 28 in lb. Varies by model | (60%) |

- Use the number on **your** ring maker's sheet. Too tight can crush a scope tube. Too loose and the scope moves after a few shots.
```

> [!lean]
> **Leupold VX Freedom 3 to 9x40** in high 1 inch rings. About $500 to $725 with rings.
> Want brighter glass for about $350 more? Leupold VX 3HD 3.5 to 10x40.

> [!rule]
> A scope you can't trust is worse than iron sights. Mount it right, mark the screws, check them.

> [!mistake]
> - Rings bought before checking drum clearance.
> - Scope too far back. The .308 bites your eyebrow.
> - Ring screws cranked with no torque wrench.

> [!field]
> Paint pen line across each ring screw head. A broken line means a screw moved.
