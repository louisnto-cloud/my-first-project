> [!why]
> Late October near Heffley Creek is a real chance at a deer. A safe, simple plan you can repeat beats a long shot at a far ridge.

- Two hunters: you and your brother. He has CORE (Conservation and Outdoor Recreation Education), a PAL (Possession and Acquisition Licence) and an FWID (Fish and Wildlife ID).
- He needs his own BC hunting licence and deer [[Species licence|species licences]] before he hunts (80%).
- Assumption: your Tikka .308 is zeroed (Session 5). His rifle: VERIFY what he will carry.

## Mule deer or white tail?

| | Mule deer | White tailed deer |
|---|---|---|
| Antlers | Fork, then fork again | One main beam, single tines up |
| Tail | Thin, rope like, black tip | Wide, brown on top, flags up white |
| Ears | Very big | Smaller |
| Runs | Bounces on all 4 feet | Gallops to cover |

- These field marks are reliable on clear looks (85%).
- Mule deer often stop and look back at 150 to 300 m. Hunter lore (65%).
- They are **separate licences and separate rules**. Know which one you are looking at before the safety comes off (80%).

## Rules you must check first

- Your MU (Management Unit) for the property and the Crown land behind it: VERIFY on the official map.
- Mule deer season dates and antler rules (for example a 4 point rule) in your MU: VERIFY.
- White tail season and any antlerless days in your MU: VERIFY.
- Region 3 deer bag limit: VERIFY. A search preview said 3 deer combined, 1 of them mule deer (60%).
- Legal hours: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (80%).

> [!warn]
> No MU, no season dates, no hunt. Read the Region 3 synopsis table for your exact MU before opening day.

## Where deer are in late October

- Mule deer are the main deer in the southern Interior (85%).
- Mule deer use steeper, higher, open broken ground. White tails use valley bottoms, creeks and farm fields (85%).
- Local reading: mule deer on grassland and open Douglas fir benches above the valley. White tails in North Thompson hayfields and cottonwood bottoms (75%).
- Many mule deer move toward winter range in October to November. Snow and cold push them, not the rut (85%).
- Late October is early pre rut. Bucks start sparring. Bachelor groups break up (70%).
- Deer move most around dawn and dusk (85%).
- Day beds: timber edges, benches, draws, out of the wind. Sunny south and west slopes on cold days (80%).

## Glassing

```diagram glassing-grid
```

- [[Glassing]] finds more deer than walking. Sit, rest the binoculars, go slow (Tip).
- Split the hillside into squares. Scan near first, then far, in a snake pattern (Tip).
- 1 to 2 minutes per square. Then do it again. Deer appear (Tip).
- Look for parts, not whole deer: an ear flick, a white rump, a leg line, a horizontal back (Tip).
- Best times: first light and the last 2 hours of light (80%).
- Binoculars find. The rifle scope is never used to look at people or unknown shapes (Tip).

## Wind and thermals

- Smell is a deer's best sense. Keep the wind in your face or across you (85%).
- Morning: air warms and flows **uphill** later in the morning. Evening: air cools and flows **downhill** (80%).
- So: hunt from above in the evening only if the wind lets you. Come from below in late morning (Tip).
- Check wind with a puff bottle or a bit of milkweed fluff every 15 minutes (Tip).
- Wind wrong? Leave that spot for today. Do not push it (Tip).

## Scenario A: the property plus Crown behind it

- Your brother's house is on the 20 acres. Neighbours are 100 to 200 m away.
- **No shooting within 100 m (109 yd) of an occupied house, farm or ranch building** (75%). That includes his house.
- So the usable area on the 20 acres may be small or zero. The Crown land behind is the real option.
- Map it first: draw a 100 m circle around every house and barn. Mark safe shooting lanes and earth [[Backstop|backstops]] (Tip).
- Mark the property line and the Crown edge. Walk it in daylight with a GPS (Global Positioning System) app (Tip).
- Assign sides: you shoot left of the line, he shoots right. No shots toward any house, road or each other (Tip).
- Pros: close to home, short pack out, easy to repeat dawn and dusk.
- Cons: small area, people and pets nearby, deer may be few.
- Odds: fair if deer cross the Crown edge. Opinion.

## Scenario B: ATV on Forest Service Roads

- [[FSR]] (Forest Service Road) networks reach benches and cutblocks fast. Use them to get to glassing spots, then walk.
- **No loaded firearm in or on a motor vehicle, and that includes an [[ATV]] (All Terrain Vehicle)** (80%).
- Loaded means a round in the chamber **or** in an attached magazine (80%).
- **No shooting from a motor vehicle**, ATV included (80%).
- No hunting or shooting on or across numbered highways and 2 lane public roads kept by the province, out to 15 m from the road centre (75%).
- FSRs are not usually treated as those highways. Still never shoot along or across any road (60%, VERIFY).
- ATV on Crown land: needs registration and a plate. Helmet required (75%). Insurance on FSRs: VERIFY.
- Motor vehicle closures in Region 3: VERIFY on the synopsis maps.
- Pros: covers ground, easy meat haul, beats walking in the dark.
- Cons: road hunters push deer back from roads. Noise. Easy to break rules by accident.
- Odds: good for finding deer, fair for a shot. Opinion.

## Scenario C: walk in 5 to 10 km

- Quiet country away from roads. Fewer hunters (Tip).
- Meat math for a mule deer (US agency figures):
  - Live weight about 68 to 113 kg (150 to 250 lb) for a buck (75%).
  - Field dressed about 54 to 86 kg (120 to 190 lb) (75%).
  - A forkhorn or doe can give only about 23 kg (50 lb) of boned meat (75%).
- Bone in quarters for a mature buck: about 41 to 48 kg (90 to 105 lb) total. Hunter reports (60%).
- Two of you, about 20 to 25 kg each per trip: 1 trip boned out, 2 trips bone in (Tip).
- Loaded and off trail you may do 2 km an hour. 8 km out is about 4 hours per trip (Tip).
- **You must bring out the edible meat: neck, ribs, 4 quarters and loins** (75%).
- Pros: best chance at a calm, unpressured deer. Great skill builder.
- Cons: a late shot means packing in the dark. Meat must cool fast.
- Odds: good chance to see deer, hard first kill. Opinion.

> [!lean]
> Start with A and B together. Map the 100 m buffers and hunt the Crown edge behind the property at dawn. Use the ATV mid day to glass new benches. Save C for after your first deer, when you know the pack out and the cutting.

## A hunt day

- **Night before:** check legal light, wind, MU, closures. Pack and charge everything.
- **Sunrise minus 90 min:** leave the house. Rifles unloaded and cased in the vehicle.
- **Sunrise minus 60 min (legal light):** sit at the first glassing spot. Load only when sitting and ready.
- **First 3 hours:** glass. Do not walk much.
- **Mid morning:** walk slowly into the wind, still hunting or checking sign. Eat, drink.
- **Midday:** move spots, scout benches, check trail cameras if you use them.
- **3 hours before sunset:** back on a glassing spot that faces where deer feed.
- **Sunset plus 60 min:** legal light ends. Unload. Walk out with headlamps.
- Shot taken late? Mark it, wait, track carefully. See Field Mode "After the shot".

> [!rule]
> Look at the deer, then look past it. If you cannot see what the bullet will hit after the deer, you do not have a shot.

> [!mistake]
> - Shooting toward a house you forgot was there. Map the 100 m buffers first.
> - Keeping a round in the chamber on the ATV "for a quick shot".
> - Walking fast all morning instead of sitting and glassing.

> [!field]
> This week: walk the property line and the Crown edge with your brother. Drop a GPS pin at every house and barn. Draw 100 m circles on the map tonight.
