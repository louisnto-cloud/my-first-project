> [!why]
> A pack that fits puts the weight on your hips, not your shoulders. A bad lift from the ground can end a season. Both are skills you can learn in an evening.

## Key points

```diagram fl-pk-zones
```

- Most of the weight rides on your hip bones (70%).
- Heavy close to your spine, light low, quick items on top (65%).
- Lift with your legs, load close, never twist (85%).

```more the key points in full
- Torso length, not your height, decides pack size (70%, REI).
- Hip belt centred on the top of your hip bones (the iliac crest) (70%, REI, via search summary).
- "You'll carry most of your pack weight on your hips" (70%, REI sizing guide, search preview).
- Heaviest items in the middle of the pack, close to your spine (65%, several outdoor guides agree).
- A loaded backpacking pack should weigh no more than about 20% of body weight, a day pack about 10% (70%, REI pack fit specialist).
- Meat loads: 20 to 30 kg per trip is sustainable for a fit adult on rough ground (Tip, from [Packing out](#/s/packing-out/0)).
- Safe lifting: firm footing, good grip, bend knees, tighten your core, keep the load close, lift with the legs, back upright, turn with your feet (85%, BC Forest Safety Council crew talk citing WorkSafeBC).
```

## Which pack for which hunt

| Hunt | Pack | Why (Tip) |
|---|---|---|
| Ducks from the boat or blind | Small bag or none | Gear stays in the boat |
| Quail in the Okanagan | Vest or small day pack | Light, fast, water |
| Deer, truck nearby | Day pack | Essentials, game bags, rope |
| Deer, far from roads | Frame pack | Meat out in quarters |
| Moose (later) | Frame pack with load shelf | Many heavy trips |

- Start with one good day pack. Add a frame pack when you hunt far from roads (Tip).

```more choosing in more detail
- **Day pack:** carries the 10 essentials, a layer, water, game bags and rope (75% for the essentials list, AdventureSmart; see [Day pack and car kit](#/s/pack-kit/0)).
- **Frame pack:** a stiff frame and a padded hip belt let the hips take the load. Built for heavy, awkward loads like meat (Tip).
- **Load shelf:** some frame packs let meat ride between the frame and the bag, against your back. Meat stays out of the bag and close to your spine (Tip).
- Try packs on in a store with weight in them. Every brand fits differently (70%, REI: torso ranges vary between brands).
- Price tiers: VERIFY in store. Prices change; check the day you buy.
```

## Measure your torso

```steps Torso length in 3 steps
fl-pk-torso-1 | Find the bump at the base of your neck.
fl-pk-torso-2 | Hands on hip bones, thumbs to the spine.
fl-pk-torso-3 | Measure bump to thumb line. Match the chart.
```

- You need a friend and a soft tape (Tip).
- Torso length picks the size, not your height (70%).

```more measuring in full
1. Tilt your head forward. Find the bony bump where your neck meets your shoulders (the C7 vertebra, "C" for cervical, the neck bones). That is the top of your torso length (70%, REI, via search summary).
2. Put your hands on top of your hip bones (the iliac crest), thumbs pointing to your spine. A line between your thumbs is the bottom (70%).
3. A friend measures along your spine from the bump to the thumb line, while you stand up straight (70%).
4. Hip size: wrap the tape around the top of your hip bones. That is a little higher than your belt line (70%, REI).
- Between sizes? Try both on with weight in them (70%, REI).
```

## Fit it in 5 steps

```steps Adjust a loaded pack
fl-pk-adjust-1 | Load it, loosen every strap.
fl-pk-adjust-2 | Hip belt on the hip bones. Snug.
fl-pk-adjust-3 | Shoulder straps: hug, no gap.
fl-pk-adjust-4 | Load lifters: pull the load close.
fl-pk-adjust-5 | Sternum strap: clip it.
```

- Always hips first, sternum last (Tip).
- Re adjust when the load changes, like after meat goes in (Tip).

```more fit checks
- Gap between the shoulder straps and the top of your shoulders: torso setting too long (65%, outdoor fit guide).
- All the weight on your shoulders: torso setting too short or hip belt too low (65%).
- The middle of the hip belt should sit right on top of your hip bones (65%).
- Load lifters pull the top of the load toward you. Snug, not cranked (Tip).
- Sternum strap at a comfortable height across your chest so you breathe freely (Tip).
- On a long climb, loosen the shoulder straps a little and let the hips work. On a side hill, snug everything (Tip).
```

## Where the weight goes

```diagram fl-pk-zones
```

- Light and bulky at the bottom (65%).
- Heavy in the core, against your spine (65%).
- Quick things on top and in the lid (65%).

```more why this order
- Heavy items close to your back and centred keep the load from pulling you backward (65%, Andrew Skurka and other guides).
- Heavy items too low make a pack feel saggy (65%).
- Off trail, some guides put heavy items a little lower for balance. On steep hunting ground, centred and close is a safe default (Tip).
- Things you need fast (rain shell, headlamp, first aid, map) go on top or in the lid (65%).
```

```steps Pack it in 5 steps

fl-pk-order-3 | Fill the gaps with soft things.
fl-pk-order-4 | Top and lid: grab in seconds.
fl-pk-order-5 | Life savers: same pocket every hunt.
```

- Same spot every time: you find it in the dark (Tip).

```more where hunting gear goes
| Item | Where (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Tourniquet, first aid | Same outside pocket every hunt |
| Licences, tags, photo ID | Zip bag in a fixed pocket |
| Knife, game bags, rope | Top or a side pocket |
| Ammo for this hunt only | A separate closed pocket |
| Rangefinder, wind checker | Hip belt pocket |
| Water | Core, close to your back |
| Puffy jacket | Bottom |
| Rain shell | Top |

- Firearm ammo: load only where you may lawfully shoot (99%, federal rule, see [BC rules you must know](#/s/bc-rules/0)).
```

## How heavy is too heavy?

```diagram fl-pk-weight
```

- Day pack: about 10% of body weight (70%).
- Overnight pack: about 20% (70%).
- Meat: 20 to 30 kg a trip, fit adult, rough ground (Tip).

```more weight in more detail
- REI's pack fit specialist: a loaded backpacking pack should not weigh more than about 20% of body weight; a day pack about 10% (70%, REI, search preview).
- REI also says these percentages do not fit every case (70%).
- Example, 80 kg (176 lb) hunter (Assumption): day pack about 8 kg, overnight about 16 kg.
- Meat: 20 to 30 kg per trip is sustainable for a fit adult on rough ground; 40 kg is a one trip hero load (Tip, [Packing out](#/s/packing-out/0)).
- "A load that blocks your view or pulls you off balance is too heavy" (70%, BC Outdoors Magazine, quoted in Packing out).
- A bone in deer hind quarter is heavy and awkward. Bone it out or make two trips (Tip).
```

## Carrying meat

```anim sk-pack-load Heavy piece high and close, then strap it tight.
```

```diagram fl-pk-meat-high
```

- Meat high and close to your back, strapped tight (Tip).
- Meat in breathable game bags, not plastic (Tip).

```more meat in the pack, in full
- Strap the meat high and tight against the frame, so it does not swing (Tip).
- Bone in quarters sit best on a load shelf; boned meat fits inside the pack in game bags (Tip).
- Leave the meat in the shade at the kill site while you pack the first load (Tip, see [Meat care](#/s/meat-care/0)).
- Going back for the next load: wind in your face and make noise, bears claim meat (70%, BC Outdoors Magazine, quoted in Packing out).
- Edible portions must come out: for deer, neck, ribs, all four quarters and the loins (99%, see [Rule Book: after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill/0)).
- Evidence of sex must stay attached as the rules say. Pack it so it stays attached (99%, see [Tagging](#/s/tagging-legal/0)).
```

## A rifle and a pack

```diagram gr-carry-1
```

- Sling the rifle, muzzle up, or carry it in your hands, never pointed at a partner (Tip).
- Unloaded when you climb, cross fences, or are near the truck (Tip and rules).

```more rifle and pack, in full
- Many packs have a rifle carry. Practise getting the rifle out fast and safely (Tip).
- A heavy pack throws your balance. Unload before steep climbs, fences and logs (Tip, see [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety/0)).
- In or on a vehicle, including an ATV (All Terrain Vehicle), the firearm must be unloaded: no live round in the chamber or in an attached magazine (99%, synopsis page 11, item 28).
- Carrying out a deer through the bush: tie blaze orange to it so no one mistakes it for a live animal (Tip, common hunter education advice).
```

## Lift a heavy load safely

```steps Lift in 5 steps (BC Forest Safety Council)
fl-lift-1 | Size it up. Too heavy? Split it or get help.
fl-lift-2 | Firm footing, good grip.
fl-lift-3 | Bend knees, tighten your core.
fl-lift-4 | Load close, lift with your legs.
fl-lift-5 | Turn with your feet. No twist.
```

- Do not lift a bulky load alone: ask for help (85%).

```more the lifting guide in full
- Before you lift, ask: how heavy is it, can I grip it, is it bulky, how do I feel today, can I lift this alone (85%, BC Forest Safety Council, "Lifting, handling or carrying objects").
- Lifting techniques: get a firm footing and good grip; bend your knees; tighten your stomach muscles and engage your core; keep the load close; lift with legs while keeping your back upright (85%).
- Tips: warm up first; turn with your legs, do not twist at your waist; do not lift bulky loads alone (85%).
- WorkSafeBC reported 20,501 lifting and MSI (musculoskeletal injury) claims from 2016 to 2020 (85%, same source). Backs get hurt at work and in the bush.
- Hunting version (Tip): lift a deer into the truck with two people, or use a ramp or a winch.
```

## Getting up with a heavy pack

```anim fl-pk-getup Sit, buckle, roll to a knee, push up, stand.
```

- Heavy meat load? Do not swing it up from the ground (Tip).
- Sit in, buckle up, roll to a knee, push up (Tip).

```more other ways up
- Put the pack on a log, rock or tailgate first, then back into it (Tip).
- A partner can lift the pack while you slide into the straps (Tip).
- Use a pole or a tree to steady yourself as you stand (Tip).
- Practise this at home with a training load (see [Fitness for hunting](#/s/hunt-fitness/0)).
```

## Walking with a load

```diagram fl-pk-walk
```

- Short steps, poles, rest when your legs shake (Tip).

```more walking in full
- Rest step on steep climbs: pause for a moment on the straight back leg each step so bones, not muscles, hold you (Tip, mountaineering technique).
- Downhill with meat: slow and short. Most falls happen going down (Tip).
- Side hills: face uphill on the steepest bits and side step (Tip).
- Take breaks before you are wrecked, with the pack resting on a log so it is easy to get back into (Tip).
```

## Keep it dry

```diagram fl-pk-dry
```

- Liner inside, cover outside, dry bags for the important things (Tip).

```more dry in full
- Fraser Valley fall: about 145 mm of rain in October and 225 mm in November at Abbotsford (95%, Environment Canada normals 1991 to 2020). Your pack will get wet.
- A pack liner (a big strong bag inside the pack) keeps the insulation dry (Tip).
- Licences and phone in a small dry bag (Tip).
- Meat goes in breathable game bags. Plastic traps heat and spoils meat (Tip, see [Meat care](#/s/meat-care/0)).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fl-pk-mistakes
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Hips carry, shoulders steady, legs lift. Your back just comes along for the ride.

> [!mistake]
> - Hip belt too low, so the shoulders carry everything.
> - Heavy gear at the bottom or far from your back.
> - Twisting a heavy pack up off the ground.

```more the mistakes in full
- Hip belt riding on the belly or too low: the hip bones are the shelf. Fix the belt first (70%, REI).
- Heavy items at the bottom or the outside: the pack sags and pulls you backward (65%).
- Twisting while lifting: turn with your feet (85%, BC Forest Safety Council).
```


- Fit order: **Hips, Shoulders, Lifters, Sternum** ("Hungry Sheep Like Salt").
- Packing: **light low, heavy close, quick on top**.
- Lifting: **"Close, knees, no twist."**

```more more hooks
- **10 and 20:** day pack 10% of you, overnight pack 20% (70%, REI).
```
