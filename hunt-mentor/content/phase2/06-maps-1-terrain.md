> [!why]
> Deer bed on benches, cross at saddles and feed on edges. A topo map shows all three from your kitchen table.

```diagram hf-topo-3d
```

- Each contour line is one flat slice of the hill (98%).
- Close lines: steep. Far apart: gentle (98%).

```more topo map basics
- A topo (topographic) map shows the shape of the ground with contour lines (95%).
- Canada's standard sheets are the NTS (National Topographic System) at 1:50,000 and 1:250,000 (98%).
- Every phone map app draws the same contours. Learn them once, use them everywhere.
```

## Scale: what 1:50,000 means

```diagram hf-scale-bar
```

- 1 cm on the map is 500 m on the ground (98%).
- A thumb, about 2 cm, is 1 km: 20 to 30 minutes of walking (Tip).

```more scale details
- 1:50,000 means 1 unit on the map is 50,000 units on the ground. 1 cm on the map is 500 m on the ground (98%).
- 1:250,000: 1 cm is 2.5 km (98%). Good for driving, useless for picking a bench.
- One 1:50,000 sheet covers about 1,000 square km (95%).
- Rule of thumb at 1:50,000: a thumb width, about 2 cm, is 1 km (Tip).
- Walking speed in hills is about 2 to 3 km per hour, so a thumb is 20 to 30 minutes (Tip).
- North is at the top of an NTS (National Topographic System) map (98%).
```

## Contour lines

```diagram hf-contour-spacing
```

- The thick line carries the height. The top of the number faces uphill (98%).
- The height step between lines is printed in the map margin (90%).

```more contour line details
- A contour line joins points of equal elevation (98%).
- Lines close together: steep. Lines far apart: gentle (98%).
- The contour interval is the height step between lines. It is printed in the map margin and varies by sheet (90%). Newer sheets use 20 m (70%). Check the margin.
- Every fifth line is thicker, the index contour, and carries a number (90%).
- The numbers read uphill: the top of the number points up the slope (98%).
- Colours on NTS (National Topographic System) sheets: brown contours, blue water, green wooded, white open ground (98%).
- Water runs in draws. Arrows and falls symbols show which way it flows (95%).
```

## Shapes that hold deer

```steps Five shapes to find on any topo
hf-shape-ridge | Ridge: the Vs point downhill.
hf-shape-draw | Draw: the Vs point uphill. Water runs here.
hf-shape-saddle | Saddle: the hourglass between two peaks. Deer cross here.
hf-shape-bench | Bench: a wide gap between tight lines. Deer bed here.
hf-shape-cliff | Cliff: lines touch. Plan around it.
```

```more every shape, in words
- **Ridge:** a long high spine. Contours make U or V shapes that point downhill (90%).
- **Draw:** a small valley running down a slope. Contours make V shapes that point uphill, toward the high ground (90%). Draws carry water, cool air and deer.
- **Saddle:** the low point on a ridge between two highs. Two sets of closed loops with an hourglass between them (90%). Deer cross ridges at saddles (65%). Hunter observation.
- **Bench:** a flat step on a slope. A wide gap between lines with tight lines above and below (90%). Deer bed on benches with a view (65%). Hunter observation.
- **Spur:** a short ridge running off the main ridge. Contours point downhill, like a tongue (90%).
- **Cliff:** lines touch or merge. Do not plan a route through them (90%).
- **Knob or peak:** small closed loops. A glassing seat if it looks across a bench (Tip).
```

## Reading steepness

```diagram hf-slope-profile
```

- Count lines per cm before a dawn climb (Tip).
- Follow one contour line to stay flat and quiet (Tip).

```more steepness table and maths
| Lines per cm, 20 m interval | Rise over 500 m | Slope | Walking |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 m | 4 percent | Easy stroll |
| 3 | 60 m | 12 percent | Steady climb |
| 5 | 100 m | 20 percent | Hard, hands free |
| 10 | 200 m | 40 percent | Hands on, pick another line |

- Percent slope is rise divided by run (98%). 100 m up over 500 m along is 20 percent.
- 20 percent feels steep with a pack and a rifle. 40 percent is scrambling (Tip).
- Count lines between the truck and your bench before you commit to a dawn climb (Tip).
- A route that follows one contour line is flat. Side hilling on a contour is the quiet way around a slope (Tip).
```

## Aspect: which way the slope faces

```diagram hf-aspect
```

- South and west faces: October sun and feed (85%).
- North faces: shade and bedding timber (Tip).

```more aspect details
- Aspect is the compass direction a slope faces (95%). Find it by picturing water running down across the lines.
- South and west faces catch October sun: feed, warmth, early snow melt (85%). Interior mule deer [[Winter range]] sits on south and west aspects in old Douglas fir (85%).
- North faces stay shaded and cool: bedding timber, late snow (Tip).
- The best October benches face south or southwest with timber above them (65%). Hunter observation.
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram hf-ridge-vs-draw
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Read the lines before you read the leaves. The map tells you where to look. Your boots confirm it.

> [!mistake]
> - Mixing up ridge and draw. Water runs in draws, never on ridges.
> - Trusting green. Cutblocks and burns change it.
> - A straight line across ten lines in a centimetre.

```more the mistakes in full
- Mixing up ridge and draw. The V points uphill for a draw and downhill for a ridge. Water runs in draws, never on ridges.
- Trusting the colour green. Green means trees on the day the map was made. Cutblocks and burns change it.
- Planning a straight line that crosses ten lines in a centimetre.
```

> [!field]
> On a known ridge, find the saddle, the nearest draw and a bench below you. Say which way the Vs point. Repeat until boring.
