> [!why]
> Your feet are the loudest thing you own. The last 100 m of a stalk is won or lost one step at a time, and quiet feet are a skill you can train on the living room floor.

```steps The fox walk in 4 moves
fn-ft-fox-1 | Weight on the back foot. Lift the front.
fn-ft-fox-2 | Outside edge of the ball down, lightly.
fn-ft-fox-3 | Roll in, then lower the heel.
fn-ft-fox-4 | Quiet? Shift your weight. Crack? Lift and place again.
```

- Weight stays back until the new foot is quiet (65%).

```more what this lesson adds
- Pace, the heel to toe step, cover and what deer hear: [Moving quietly](#/s/still-hunting/0). Do that first.
- This lesson adds the fox walk, when to use which step, balance, rhythm, using wind noise, crawls, ground types, and a drill plan to practise at home before the season.
- The fox walk: set the outside of the ball of the foot down lightly, roll onto the whole foot with partial weight, then shift full weight; it lets you feel the ground and slowly compress leaves and sticks (65%, Princeton outdoor action nature observation guide, search preview).
- Camp Kawartha's stalking guide: carry your weight on the back leg, test with the front toes for crunchy leaves or sticks, roll along the outside of your foot onto the heel once the ground is clear (65%, search preview).
```

## Key points

| Skill | One line |
|---|---|
| Weight back | You can freeze mid step |
| Feel first | The foot finds the stick before your weight does |
| Two steps | Heel first for pace, edge first for the stalk |
| Short strides | Balance and less noise |
| Broken rhythm | No steady crunch, crunch |
| Wind cover | Move in gusts |
| Crawls | Kneel, hands and knees, belly |
| Drills | Ten minutes a day before the season |

```more the key points in full
1. Keep your weight on the back foot until the front foot is set and quiet (65%, stalking guides).
2. Feel through the boot: thin, flexible soles feel sticks better than stiff mountain boots (Tip).
3. Heel first works at normal still hunting pace on quiet ground (65%, see Moving quietly); outside edge first is quieter but very slow (60%, hunter forum, search preview).
4. "A good stalker will take a long time to silently move 50 metres." (65%, Camp Kawartha)
5. A steady crunch, crunch rhythm sounds like a person; a deer's steps come in uneven bursts (Tip).
6. Deer hear about 0.25 to 30 kHz with best hearing at 4 to 8 kHz, above much of what we hear well (75%, see Moving quietly).
7. Every drill here is hunter or tracker practice, labelled Tip.
```

## Heel first or edge first?

```diagram fn-ft-compare
```

- Heel first: quiet trails, still hunting pace (Tip).
- Edge first: crunchy ground and the last 100 m of a stalk (Tip).

```more choosing a step
- Heel first, rolling to the ball and testing before full weight, is the step in Moving quietly (65%).
- Outside edge of the ball first, rolling in, then the heel, lets the foot find sticks before the heel crushes them (65%, Princeton and Camp Kawartha).
- Edge first is "quieter but really slow travelling" (60%, BC hunting forum, search preview).
- Mix them: heel first to cover ground between glassing stops, edge first once you are inside 200 m (Tip).
```

## Can you freeze mid step?

```diagram fn-ft-freeze
```

- Weight back means you can stop on one leg when a head comes up (Tip).
- Train it with the balance drill below.

```more balance in full
- Short strides keep your centre over the back foot (Tip).
- Knees soft, not locked (Tip).
- Hands quiet. Rifle on the sling or held across the body so it does not swing into brush (Tip). Safety first: muzzle control at all times, see [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety/0).
```

## Break the rhythm

```diagram fn-ft-rhythm
```

- Two steps, a long look, two steps. Never a steady beat (Tip).

```more rhythm in full
- People walk at a steady beat. Deer step, stop, feed, step again (Tip, hunter observation).
- Two or three steps, then stand and look for 20 seconds or more (Tip, see Moving quietly).
- If you snap a stick, stop. Wait a few minutes. A deer may accept one crack from another animal, not a steady series (Tip).
```

## Move in the gusts

```anim fn-ft-gust Step when the wind blows. Stand when it is calm.
```

- Wind noise in the trees covers footsteps (Tip).
- Calm air: slow to almost nothing (Tip).

```more wind and noise in full
- Strong wind: you cannot hear well either, and scent spreads unpredictably (Tip, see Weather, moon and what moves game).
- Creek noise, rain on leaves and gusts all cover your steps (Tip).
- Wind direction still rules: see [Wind mastery](#/s/wind-mastery/0).
```

## Ground types

| Ground | Do this (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Dry leaves | Edge first, slowest, or go around |
| Crusty snow | Wait for midday soft or wind; tiny steps |
| Soft new snow | Quiet: still hunt it |
| Gravel and shale | Step on larger stable rocks |
| Wet moss, grass | Quietest: use it |
| Logs and blowdown | Step over, never on; bark cracks |
| Game trails | Packed and quiet, but deer watch them |

```more ground notes
- After rain or a thaw, the whole forest goes quiet: a still hunting day (Tip).
- Pine cones and dry twigs hide under needles. Feel first (Tip).
- Snow tracking: [Snow tracking](#/s/snow-tracking/0).
```

## Crawls for the last metres

```steps Three ways to get low
fn-ft-crawl-1 | Kneel and duck: low brush, short moves.
fn-ft-crawl-2 | Hands and knees: rifle on your back or in one hand.
fn-ft-crawl-3 | Belly crawl: the last few metres over a crest.
```

- Knee pads or thick pants save your knees on rock (Tip).

```more crawls in full
- Crawl only when you must. It is slow and tiring (Tip).
- Keep the muzzle out of the dirt and pointed safely. Check the barrel for snow and mud after any crawl (Tip; see Firearm safety).
- Move the rifle first, then your body, so it never swings (Tip).
- Peek around a rock or tree, not over it: [Moving quietly](#/s/still-hunting/0).
```

## A stalk, start to finish

```steps Spot and stalk in 5 steps
sa-stalk-1 | Glass from high ground at first light.
sa-stalk-2 | Pick 2 landmarks near the deer.
sa-stalk-3 | Check the wind before every move.
sa-stalk-4 | Close in out of sight, behind cover.
sa-stalk-5 | Get a rest. Wait for broadside.
```

- Fast where it cannot see you, slow where it can (Tip).

```more the stalk in full
- Plan the approach from where you glassed: landmarks, dead ground and the wind (Tip). See dead ground in [Topo deep dive](#/s/topo-deep-dive/0) and What can I see in [The Hunt Map like a pro](#/s/hunt-map-pro/0).
- Stalking is "a chess game", alternating fast and slow movement (65%, Princeton guide).
- Behind a ridge or in dead ground: walk normally. In view: fox walk, freeze on head up (Tip).
- Stay unseen: [Stay unseen](#/s/stay-unseen/0).
```

## Home drills

```steps Five drills, ten minutes a day
fn-ft-drill-1 | Balance: one foot, 30 seconds, eyes up.
fn-ft-drill-2 | Cereal box: cross crushed cereal in socks, no crunch.
fn-ft-drill-3 | Leaf strip: 10 m of dry leaves, partner listens.
fn-ft-drill-4 | Freeze game: partner calls freeze, hold 60 s.
fn-ft-drill-5 | Timed stalk: 100 m of timber in 30 minutes.
```

- Start three weeks before the season (Tip).

```more drills in full
- **Balance:** 30 seconds each leg, then with eyes on a far point, then in hunting boots (Tip).
- **Cereal box:** spread crushed cereal on a sheet. Cross it in socks, then boots, with no crunch (Tip).
- **Leaf strip:** your brother sits with eyes closed at the end of a 10 m strip of dry leaves and points at every noise (Tip).
- **Freeze game:** while you fox walk, your partner calls freeze at random. Hold 60 seconds without a wobble (Tip).
- **Timed stalk:** still hunt 100 m of timber in 30 minutes or more. Moving quietly sets 200 m in 30 minutes for a first drill; this one is slower (Tip).
- **Rifle drill:** kneel, sit and rise again with an unloaded rifle, no clink, muzzle safe (Tip).
```

## Three week plan

| Week | Daily, 10 to 15 min (Tip) |
|---|---|
| 1 | Balance and cereal box, socks then boots |
| 2 | Leaf strip and freeze game with your brother |
| 3 | Timed stalk in real timber, crawl 20 m, rifle drill |
| Season | 5 minutes of fox walk at the truck before each hunt |

```more plan notes
- Wear the boots and clothes you will hunt in by week 2 (Tip). Noisy kit: see Moving quietly.
- Log each drill in your journal: time, noises heard, score (Tip). See [Hunt journal guide](#/s/hunt-journal-guide/0).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fn-ft-freeze
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Your feet should ask the ground a question before your weight gives the answer.

> [!mistake]
> - Putting weight down before the foot is quiet.
> - Walking in a steady beat.
> - Stepping on logs instead of over them.

```more the mistakes in full
- Putting weight down before the foot has found the stick. The crack comes from your weight, not your foot.
- A steady crunch, crunch beat. It sounds like a person.
- Stepping on a log. Dry bark cracks like a rifle shot in a quiet wood.
```

> [!field]
> On the first scouting trip, fox walk the last 100 m to every glassing seat. Your brother stays 30 m behind and holds up a finger for every noise he hears. Beat your score each time.

```checklist fn-footwork
Balance drill done three weeks before the season
Cereal box crossed in boots, no crunch
Leaf strip crossed with no point from my partner
Held a freeze for 60 seconds
Timed stalk of 100 m in 30 minutes or more
Crawled 20 m with an unloaded rifle, muzzle safe
```

## Memory hooks

| Hook | Means |
|---|---|
| Feel, roll, then weight | The fox walk |
| Heel to roam, edge to close | Which step when |
| Two steps, look long | Rhythm |
| Gust, go. Calm, slow | Using wind noise |
| Over logs, never on | Blowdown rule |
| Fifty metres, long time | Stalking speed |

```more more hooks
- **Weight back, stop on a dime:** freeze mid step.
- **Fast in the dead ground, slow in the sight line.**
- **Crack means stop:** pause after any noise.
```
