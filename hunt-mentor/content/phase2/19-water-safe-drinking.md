> [!why]
> A long day climbing for deer drains you, and the clear creek looks perfect. One mouthful can mean weeks of "beaver fever" after the hunt.

```diagram fs-wat-clear
```

- Treat every lake, creek and spring. Clear is not clean (75%).

```more what this session covers
- Finding water in dry Interior hills, picking the best source, the germs, and the 4 ways to treat it: boil, filter, chemicals, UV light. Cloudy water, water no method fixes, and toilet distance.
- Water is also for wounds: [First aid](#/s/field-first-aid/0) says flush cuts with drinkable water.
- Assumption: you carry water from the truck most days. This is for long days, nights out, and backup.
```

## Key points

```diagram fs-wat-methods
```

- Boiling kills everything that makes you sick from germs (75%).

```more the 8 key points in full
1. Do not drink untreated water from lakes, streams, rivers, ponds or springs (80%, HealthLink BC File 10).
2. Giardia ("beaver fever") symptoms usually start 1 to 3 weeks after infection and can last 2 to 6 weeks (80%).
3. Boil at least 1 minute; above 2,000 m, at least 2 minutes (80%, HealthLink BC File 49b).
4. A filter for Giardia needs a pore size of 1 micron or smaller, certified (80%). Jug filters like Brita do not remove it (80%).
5. Chlorine bleach: 2 drops per litre, wait 30 minutes; cloudy or cold water: double, wait 1 to 2 hours (80%).
6. Chlorine is generally not effective against Giardia (80%, HealthLink BC File 10). Filter first, then disinfect (75%, CDC).
7. Cloudy water: settle and strain before treating (80%).
8. Boiling and disinfecting do not make water with chemicals or toxins safe (75%, CDC).
```

## Beaver fever: why bother

```diagram fs-wat-clear
```

- Symptoms start 1 to 3 weeks later, often after you are home (80%).

```more Giardia in full
- Giardia is a parasite of the gut in humans and animals. The illness is giardiasis, often called "beaver fever" (80%, HealthLink BC File 10, updated 10 July 2026).
- You get it mainly from contaminated water, including swallowing lake or stream water (80%).
- Symptoms: diarrhea, cramps, gas, bloating, pale greasy stools, nausea, vomiting, weight loss, tiredness. Some people have none (80%).
- Symptoms usually appear 1 to 3 weeks after infection and can last 2 to 6 weeks (80%).
- Cryptosporidium is another parasite in surface water; HealthLink names both Giardia and Cryptosporidium as reasons boiling is best (80%).
- Sick after a trip? Tell your doctor you drank surface water (Tip).
```

## Where water hides in dry country

```diagram fs-wat-find
```

- Look for green: a line of brush in a draw often marks water (Tip).

```more finding water in full
- All Tip (hunter and outdoor practice):
  - A line of green shrubs or a different colour of grass down a draw.
  - Cottonwood, willow and red osier dogwood grow along water.
  - Seeps and springs below rock outcrops and at the toe of slopes.
  - Game trails heading downhill in the evening often go to water.
  - Snow and ice in November: melt them in a pot. Add a little water to the pot first so it does not scorch.
- Food, water and cover for game: [Food, water and cover](#/s/food-water-cover/0).
- Your map shows creeks and lakes, but small creeks dry up by October in the grassland (Tip). Check on the map scouting day: [Map scouting](#/s/map-scouting/0).
```

## Pick the best source

```diagram fs-wat-rank
```

- Upstream of cattle and beaver dams when you can (Tip).

```more ranking sources
- All Tip: there is no official ranking. Any source can carry germs.
- A spring right where it comes out of the ground is usually cleanest.
- Fast clear creeks beat still water.
- Lakes: away from the shore and from inlets.
- Ponds, sloughs and cattle troughs: last choice. Interior range land has cattle; manure washes into water.
- Beaver ponds: the nickname "beaver fever" exists for a reason, though people and other animals spread Giardia too (Tip).
- Then treat it anyway (80%).
```

## What kills what

```diagram fs-wat-methods
```

- Boil or filter for Giardia. Chemicals alone are weak on it (75%).

```more the full table
- From the US Centers for Disease Control (CDC) backcountry water page, read directly (75%):
  - **Boiling:** protozoa (Giardia, Cryptosporidium) yes; bacteria yes; viruses yes.
  - **Filter 1 micron or smaller, NSF (National Sanitation Foundation) Standard 53 or 58 cyst reduction:** protozoa yes; bacteria not all; viruses no.
  - **Filter 0.3 micron or smaller:** protozoa yes; bacteria yes; viruses no.
  - **Chlorine bleach or iodine:** protozoa not well; bacteria yes; viruses yes.
  - **Chlorine dioxide tablets:** Giardia yes; Cryptosporidium somewhat; bacteria and viruses yes.
  - **Portable UV (ultraviolet) light:** all three, but only in small amounts of clear water.
- Filter first, then disinfect, is the safest combination (75%, CDC).
```

## Boil it

```steps Boil in 3 steps
fs-wat-boil-1 | Cloudy? Settle and strain through cloth.
fs-wat-boil-2 | Rolling boil for at least 1 minute.
fs-wat-boil-3 | Above 2,000 m: at least 2 minutes.
```

- The surest method. Costs fuel and time (80%).

```more boiling in full
- HealthLink BC File 49b, updated 25 July 2025 (80%): boil at least 1 minute; above 2,000 m (6,500 ft), at least 2 minutes. Boiling is the most effective method, especially for Giardia and Cryptosporidium.
- The US CDC says 3 minutes above 6,500 ft (75%). The two differ. Boiling longer does no harm: my lean, 3 minutes up high.
- Most of your hunting ground near Kamloops is below 2,000 m (Tip: check the map).
- Let it cool covered (Tip).
```

## Filter it

```steps Filter in 3 steps
fs-wat-filter-1 | Certified, 1 micron or smaller.
fs-wat-filter-2 | Keep dirty and clean sides apart.
fs-wat-filter-3 | Keep it from freezing.
```

- A filter alone does not stop viruses (75%).

```more filters in full
- HealthLink BC: use an NSF certified filter with a pore size of 1 micron or less, or a certified UV or distillation unit (80%, File 10).
- Jug style filters like Brita do not remove Giardia (80%).
- Viruses: filters alone do not stop them (75%, CDC). In BC backcountry, protozoa and bacteria are the usual worry; add a chemical step if the water is near camps or people (Tip).
- Many hollow fibre filters can be damaged if they freeze. Read your filter's instructions and keep it warm in November (Tip, VERIFY with the maker).
- Back flush or clean the filter as the maker says (Tip).
```

## Chemical treatment

```diagram fs-wat-bleach
```

- Good backup in the pocket kit. Weak on Giardia (80%).

```more chemicals in full
- **Bleach** (HealthLink BC File 49b) (80%): 2 drops (0.1 mL) of unscented household bleach (about 5.25% chlorine) per litre. Cover, wait 30 minutes. No slight chlorine smell? Add 2 more drops and wait 15 more minutes. Cloudy water or water colder than 10 °C: double the bleach and wait 1 to 2 hours.
- **Iodine** (80%): 6 drops of 2% tincture of iodine per litre, wait 30 minutes (several hours if cold); cloudy water 10 drops. Short term use only. Not for children or pregnant women.
- **Chlorine is generally not effective against Giardia** (80%, HealthLink BC File 10). That is why boiling or a filter comes first.
- **Chlorine dioxide tablets:** Giardia yes, Cryptosporidium somewhat (75%, CDC). Follow the label contact time.
- In October, creek water is often below 10 °C: double dose and long wait (Tip, based on File 49b).
```

## Cloudy water

```anim fs-wat-settle Settle, pour off the clear water, then treat.
```

- Mud shields germs from chemicals and UV (80%).

```more cloudy water in full
- Cloudiness reduces how well disinfectant works. Clarify first: strain through a clean cloth or coffee filter, let the rest settle, pour the clear water into a clean container, then disinfect (80%, HealthLink BC File 49b).
- UV light needs clear water; particles shield germs (75%, CDC).
- A bandana over the pot works as a strainer (Tip).
```

## Water no method fixes

```diagram fs-wat-no-fix
```

- Find other water (75%).

```more toxins and chemicals in full
- Boiling and disinfecting cannot make water with harmful chemicals, toxins or radioactive material safe (75%, CDC).
- Algae blooms (blue green algae) can make toxins. Scummy, paint like or bright green water: do not drink it (Tip; the toxin point is from the CDC chemical and toxin line).
- After a wildfire, ash and runoff can change water quality (70%, HealthLink BC topic "Wildfire: its effects on drinking water quality", title only). See [Wildfire](#/s/wildfire-smoke-heat/0).
- Fuel sheen near roads, ATV crossings and boat launches: avoid (Tip).
```

## Keep water clean: 30 m

```diagram fs-wat-30m
```

- Toilet at least 30 m from any water source (80%).

```more hygiene in full
- HealthLink BC: when camping, do not urinate or have a bowel movement within 30 m of a water source (80%, File 10).
- Dig a small cat hole for solid waste (Tip).
- Wash hands, or use sanitizer, before eating and after field dressing (Tip). Hand sanitizer does not replace washing off blood (Tip).
- Gut piles away from water too (Tip). Rules on leaving parts: [Rule Book: after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill/0).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fs-wat-rank
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): The creek always looks clean. Boil it anyway, and thank yourself in three weeks.

> [!mistake]
> - One "quick sip" from a clear creek.
> - Bleach in cold, cloudy water with a 5 minute wait.
> - A frozen filter trusted the next morning.

```more the mistakes in full
- Drinking untreated because it looks clear (80%: clear water can carry germs).
- Short contact time with chemicals in cold or cloudy water (80%).
- Relying on chlorine alone for Giardia (80%).
- Dipping the clean bottle in the creek after filtering (Tip).
- Leaving the filter in the truck overnight at minus 5 °C (Tip).
```
