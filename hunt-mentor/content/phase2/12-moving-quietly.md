> [!why]
> Most beginners walk through the bush at a hiking pace. Deer hear and see them long before. Slow down and the woods change.

## Still hunting vs stalking

```diagram hf-still-vs-stalk
```

- **Still hunting:** slow walking through cover to find a deer (70%).
- **Stalking:** closing in on a deer you have already seen (70%).

```more still hunting vs stalking, in full
- **Still hunting:** walking very slowly through deer cover, hoping to see a deer before it sees you (70%).
- **Stalking:** you have already seen the deer. You close the distance for a shot (70%).
- Still hunting suits thick timber and wet, quiet days.
- Stalking suits open country after [[Glassing]].
- Both depend on wind. Read the wind session first.
```

## Pace

```anim hf-step-cadence 2 or 3 steps, then a long look.
```

- Move 2 or 3 steps, then stand and look 20 seconds or more (Tip).
- A few hundred metres an hour is normal (Tip).

```more pace in full
- Move 2 or 3 steps, then stand and look for 20 seconds or more. Some hunters stop for minutes (Tip, hunter method).
- Look much more than you walk (65%).
- Expect to cover very little ground: a few hundred metres an hour is normal (Tip).
- Look near as well as far. A bedded deer may be very close (65%).
- Before each step, pick where your foot goes next.
- Heel down softly, roll to the ball, test before putting weight on it (65%).
```

## Using cover

```anim hf-stalk-cover Head down: move. Head up: freeze.
```

- Move from cover to cover. Pause at each.
- Stay in shade, off the skyline.

```more using cover, in full
- Move when the deer's head is down feeding or behind a tree (Tip).
- Stay in shade. Avoid the skyline on ridges.
- Keep a tree or bush between you and where deer may be, then look around it, not over it (Tip).
- Move from one piece of cover to the next. Pause at each.
- Freeze when a deer looks up. Wait until it relaxes (Tip).
```

## Noise: what deer hear best

```diagram hf-hearing
```

- Deer hear higher pitch than people (75%).
- A steady crunch, crunch sounds like a person (Tip).

```more hearing studies and noise, in full
- White tailed deer hear about 0.25 to 30 kilohertz in one study, best at 4 to 8 kilohertz (75%).
- People hear up to about 20 kilohertz. Deer hear higher pitch than we do (75%).
- A second study found deer can hear even higher, past 50 kilohertz (65%).
- Sharp, high sounds are the giveaway: zippers, Velcro, nylon scrape, metal on metal (Tip).
- A snapped stick can sound like a deer. A steady crunch, crunch rhythm sounds like a person (Tip).
- Mule deer hearing studies: none found. Assumption: similar.
```

## Clothing and kit noise

```diagram hf-quiet-kit
```

- Jump up and down at the truck. Fix anything that rattles.

```more clothing and kit, in full
- Wool and brushed fleece are quieter in brush than hard nylon shells (Tip).
- Tape or wrap metal parts that clink: sling swivels, buckles, knife sheath (Tip).
- Swap Velcro pockets for buttons or magnets, or leave them open.
- Before you leave the truck, jump up and down. Fix anything that rattles.
- Keep the gun's action and safety quiet. Practise moving the safety slowly at home.
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram hf-near-far
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): If you think you are going slow enough, halve it.

> [!mistake]
> - Hiking pace, called still hunting.
> - Looking only far and walking past a bedded deer at 20 m (22 yd).
> - A swishing nylon jacket and a Velcro rip near a feeding deer.

> [!field]
> Still hunt 200 m (219 yd) of timber. Aim for 30 minutes or more. Your brother walks behind and names every noise.
