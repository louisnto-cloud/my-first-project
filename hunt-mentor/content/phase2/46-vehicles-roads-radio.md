> [!why]
> Most of your hunting ground is reached by gravel resource roads built for logging trucks. Knowing how those roads work keeps you out of a head on crash and out of trouble with the law.

## Key points

```diagram fl-fsr-rules
```

- Lights on, keep right, yield to industrial traffic (85%).
- No posted limit? Never over 80 km/h (85%).
- Firearm unloaded in or on any vehicle (99%).

```more the key points in full
- Resource roads are gravel, often one lane, with tight curves, steep grades and soft shoulders (85%, BC Forest Safety Council Resource Road User Safety Guide, 2017).
- They are built for industrial vehicles. Yield to them (85%).
- If no limit is posted, the maximum speed on a resource road in BC is 80 km/h (85%, same guide).
- Drive with head and tail lights on; drive on the right; do not overtake industrial vehicles unless the driver pulls over and signals (85%).
- Where posted, mobile radio users "must use the posted channels and call protocols" (95%, BC government resource road radio page).
- Most resource roads are "radio assist": radio use is not mandatory and not everyone has one (95%, BC government).
- A loaded firearm in or on any vehicle is unlawful (99%, synopsis page 11, item 28).
- An ATV (All Terrain Vehicle) on an FSR needs a valid driver's licence and at least $200,000 third party liability insurance (99%, synopsis page 9).
```

## Resource roads are not highways

| What you meet | What to do (85%, BC Forest Safety Council) |
|---|---|
| Loaded logging truck | Yield. Pull right, stop if needed |
| Dust ahead | It means traffic. Slow down |
| Single lane bridge | Yield to oncoming traffic |
| Washouts, potholes, water bars | Slow. Damage may not be marked |
| No cell signal | Plan for it before you go |

- Avoid driving resource roads in the dark (85%).

```more more from the safety guide
- Roads may not be drivable, may be overgrown or not ploughed, even though they show on a map or GPS (Global Positioning System) (85%, BC Forest Safety Council guide).
- Expect off road vehicles around any corner (85%).
- Some industrial vehicles are very long or wide, with no pilot car (85%).
- Follow industrial vehicles at a safe distance; slow and pull over if they do (85%).
- Allow extra travel time; do not try to keep up (85%).
- Check road status and closures before you go: see [Maps 3: land status and access](#/s/maps-land-status/0) and the local plans in [Local plan: Heffley Creek](#/s/local-heffley/0).
```

## Read the entry sign

```diagram fl-fsr-sign
```

- Entry signs show the road name to call, the RR (resource road) channel and how to call (95%).
- Write the road name and channel in your trip plan (Tip).

```more signs in full
- Signs at road entry points identify the road name to be called, the RR channel and the call frequency, and kilometre signs show where you must call (95%, BC government resource road radio presentation, 2013).
- "The radio channel signage in the field will govern over the maps at all times" (95%, BC government resource road radio page).
- Channel planning maps exist but change and are planning tools only (95%).
- Not every road has adopted the standard channels yet (95%).
- This app gives no channel numbers on purpose. Read the sign, every road, every trip.
```

## Up and down

```anim fl-fsr-updown Numbers rising: up. Numbers falling: down.
```

- Heading toward higher kilometre signs: call "up" (65%).
- Heading toward lower ones: call "down" (65%).
- Usually up = away from town (65%).

```more up and down in full
- BC standardised "up" and "down" and the calling order from 2009 (95%, BC government presentation, 2013).
- Common explanation: call "up" when kilometre signs increase, "down" when they decrease (65%, BC Forest Safety Council material quoted by a secondary site).
- As kilometre numbers go up you are usually travelling further from town (65%).
- "Down" or loaded traffic usually has the right of way (65%, same secondary source). Tip: whatever the rule, the loaded truck cannot stop fast. You can.
- Posted local instructions win over anything here (95%).
```

## Make a radio call

```steps The call in 4 parts
fl-fsr-call-1 | Road name, as on the sign.
fl-fsr-call-2 | The kilometre you are at.
fl-fsr-call-3 | Up or down.
fl-fsr-call-4 | Anything else the sign asks.
```

- Call at the kilometre signs where calling is posted (95%).
- Listen first. Do not talk over a truck (Tip).

```more the call in full
- Standard call content in BC: road name, location, direction travelled (up or down), vehicle type, and calling interval (95%, BC government radio pilot projects page).
- Common order: road name, kilometre, up or down (65%, BC Forest Safety Council via secondary source).
- Example shape (made up name, Tip): "Sample Road, 12, up, pickup."
- Pull over at a pullout and call "clear" if you have a radio, so trucks know you are out of the way (85%, BC Forest Safety Council guide: use the radio to advise traffic that you are clear).
- Calling interval: follow the sign. Some roads mark "must call" kilometres (95%).
```

## Which radio, which licence?

```diagram fl-fsr-radios
```

- Road radios on RR channels need a licence (95%).
- FRS (Family Radio Service) handhelds need no licence (85%).
- An FRS handheld will not reach the trucks (Tip).

```more radios and licences in full
- "A mobile radio license is required to operate on commercial land mobile radios" such as those used on resource roads (95%, BC government radio page).
- The standard channels are available to licence holders for programming at local commercial radio shops (95%).
- ISED (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada) lists FRS (Family Radio Service) portable radios and equipment used under the general mobile radio service among equipment that may not need a licence (85%, ISED radio licensing page, read directly).
- FRS radios use different frequencies from RR channels, so they cannot talk to logging trucks (Tip, logic: different bands).
- Option A: no road radio, drive defensively, follow a radio truck (85%, safety guide). Option B: licensed mobile radio, programmed by a shop (95%). My lean: Option A for your first season (Tip). VERIFY how busy the haul is on your roads: the maintenance company is named on the entry sign (85%).
```

## No radio? Wait and follow

```steps No radio on an active haul road
fl-fsr-noradio-1 | Wait at the start of the road.
fl-fsr-noradio-2 | Follow a radio truck. Pull over when it does.
fl-fsr-noradio-3 | Nobody came? Go with extreme caution.
```

- Lights on, right side, slow on every curve (85%).

```more the advice in full
- "If you don't have a two way radio, wait at the beginning of active haul roads for a vehicle that does; follow behind that vehicle, pulling over if it does. If you wait for some time and no vehicle comes, proceed with extreme caution" (85%, BC Forest Safety Council guide, 2017).
- Weekend and evening haul is less common on many roads but never guaranteed (Tip).
- Dust ahead means traffic (85%).
```

## Where to stop

```diagram fl-fsr-stop
```

- Use a pullout, on a straight, visible both ways (85%).
- Never on a curve or a hill crest (85%).

```more stopping in full
- If you must stop on an active road, find a pullout; if there is none, find a straight section with good visibility both ways, wide enough for others to pass (85%, BC Forest Safety Council).
- Avoid stopping in a curve or on the crest of a hill (85%).
- A pullout on the left is OK only if there is no other place and it is safe (85%).
- Glassing from the road: pull fully into a pullout first, engine off, and step away from the truck (Tip).
```

```steps FSR habits (from Maps 3)
hf-fsr-1 | Note the posted radio channel. Headlights on.
hf-fsr-2 | Pull right for loaded trucks.
hf-fsr-3 | Never park on a corner.
hf-fsr-4 | Check road status before you go.
```

## Park so others can get by

```diagram fl-fsr-park
```

- Off the road, nose out, never blocking a gate (Tip).

```more parking in full
- Leave gates as you found them (99%, synopsis ethics code page 77: "Leave everything the way you found it").
- Nose out: if weather or a fire changes, you leave fast (Tip).
- Leave a note on the dash with your return time, or not, if theft is a concern in that area. Your trip plan already has it (Tip).
- Never park in a turnaround or a pullout that loaded trucks need (Tip).
```

## Firearms and vehicles: the rules

> [!warn] Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

```anim rb-loaded-truck Magazine out, chamber empty, then the truck or ATV (All Terrain Vehicle).
```

- No live round in the chamber or an attached magazine, in or on any vehicle (99%).
- No hunting from a motor vehicle (99%).

> [!law] Item 28, page 11
> "To discharge, carry or have in possession a firearm containing live ammunition in its breech or in its magazine attached to the firearm, in or on a railway car, motor vehicle, sleigh, aircraft, bicycle or other conveyance."

```more the vehicle rules in full
> [!law] Item 22, page 11
> "To hunt wildlife from a motor vehicle."

> [!law] Item 25, page 11
> "To use a power boat, aircraft, or motor vehicle or other mechanical device to herd or harass wildlife."

- Plain English: unload before the firearm goes in or on the truck or ATV; never hunt from it; never use it to push or chase animals (99%, synopsis page 11, 2 October 2026 edition).
- Road allowance: no hunting on, or shooting across, the road allowance of numbered highways and 2 lane or wider public roads maintained by the province (99%). An FSR is not named in that rule (95%). Tip: never shoot from or across any road. Details: [BC rules you must know](#/s/bc-rules/0) and [Rule Book: where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt/0).
- Safe habit: walk at least 15 m off the road, then load (Tip).
- Federal rules: transport and storage of firearms are in [BC rules you must know](#/s/bc-rules/0).
```

## ATVs on Forest Service Roads

> [!warn] Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

```diagram fl-fsr-atv
```

- On an FSR, ATV drivers need a driver's licence and $200,000 insurance (99%).
- Registration, plate, helmet: Off Road Vehicle Act (99%).

```more the ATV text in full
- "While travelling on a Forest Service Road, operators of ATVs are required to hold a valid driver's licence and carry a minimum of $200,000 third party liability insurance" (99%, synopsis page 9).
- Off Road Vehicle Act: mandatory registration, the plate or sticker clearly displayed on the front, back or left side; helmets for ride astride ORVs (Off Road Vehicles); seatbelts if installed; under 16 supervised by an adult at least 19 (99%, synopsis page 9).
- Environmental damage from off road use can be fined $575, and serious cases up to $100,000 and a year in jail (99%, synopsis page 9, Forest and Range Practices Act).
- Motor vehicle closures by area: [ATV rules in BC](#/s/atv-rules/0) and your Region's Rule Book.
```

## Getting there: the highway

```diagram fl-fsr-highway
```

- Winter tires on many BC highways from 1 October (70%).
- Check DriveBC before you leave (Tip).

```more highway in full
- Winter tire or chain requirements start 1 October on many BC highways and end 31 March, or 30 April on mountain passes and snowy areas (70%, CTV News and Global News reports of ministry rules).
- The Coquihalla (Highway 5) is among the routes listed (70%, news report).
- M+S (mud and snow) and mountain snowflake tires both meet the requirement; tread at least 3.5 mm (80%, BC government release 2018 for tread; 70% news for tire marks).
- Chains: mandatory chain ups apply to commercial vehicles on posted routes (80%, BC government release 2018). Tip: carry chains you have practised fitting.
- Check [DriveBC](https://www.drivebc.ca) for closures and conditions the night before and the morning of (Tip). Exact current rules: VERIFY on the BC government winter driving page.
```

## Stuck or broken down

```diagram fl-fsr-stuck
```

- Stay with your vehicle. It is shelter and easier to find (85%).
- Your trip plan brings help when you are late (75%).

```more stuck in full
- "If you are lost or stranded, stay with your vehicle; it will provide shelter until help arrives. Often, it is easier for rescuers to find a vehicle than an individual in the woods" (85%, BC Forest Safety Council guide).
- Complete a trip plan and give it to a dependable person, with an action plan if you are not back on time (85%, same guide).
- A satellite messenger can send an SOS (emergency call for help) where phones have no signal (70%, see [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0)).
- Phone battery: keep it warm and use airplane mode (see [GPS, phone and battery](#/s/gps-phone-battery/0)).
```

## Radios between you and your partner

- Two way radios between hunting partners: no ban found in the app's Rule Book (80%, my reading of the synopsis items on pages 11 and 12).
- The aircraft and drone rules are the closest: no hunting with wildlife locations from an aircraft or drone (99%).

```diagram fl-fsr-radios
```

```more the related rules and tips
> [!law] Item 34, page 11
> "To hunt using information on the location of wildlife that is obtained by means of an aircraft."

> [!law] Item 36, page 11
> "To operate or posses a drone while on a hunting or trapping expedition, or to use information that a drone obtains during, or less than 6 hours before the start of, a hunting or trapping expedition."

- My reading: radios and phones between partners are not named as banned (80%). VERIFY with a conservation officer if you plan to rely on them for a deer drive.
- Tip: volume low, earpiece in, agree check in times. Noise spooks game.
- Partner plans and signals: [Hunting with a partner](#/s/hunting-partner/0).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fl-fsr-mistakes
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): On a logging road, the loaded truck is always right, even when it is wrong. Give it the road.

> [!mistake]
> - Driving an active haul road with no radio and not waiting.
> - Stopping on a corner to glass.
> - A loaded rifle in the truck.

```more the mistakes in full
- No radio and no waiting: the safety guide says wait and follow a radio truck, or go with extreme caution (85%).
- Stopping on a corner or crest: use a pullout on a straight (85%).
- A full attached magazine in the truck: that is a loaded firearm in law (99%, item 28).
```

> [!field]
> Next scouting drive: at the road entry sign, photograph it, write the road name and channel in your journal, and say the up call out loud at each kilometre sign, even without a radio. Practise until it is automatic.
