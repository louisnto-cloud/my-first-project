> [!why]
> Two hunters see more, carry more and get help faster. Two hunters with no plan can also shoot toward each other or lose each other at dark. The plan is the difference.

```diagram fl-pt-formation
```

- Side by side, in sight, each in your own zone of fire (75%).
- Agree the plan at the truck, every time (Tip).

```more what this lesson covers
- The plan you make before you leave the truck (Tip).
- Zones of fire when walking together (75%, hunter education; full detail in [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety/0)).
- Hand and whistle signals, radios and phones (80% for the distress signal, rest Tip).
- Splitting up and meeting again (Tip).
- A simple push, safely (Tip).
- Who tags the animal: the rules (99%).
- Lost partner: the order of things to do (Tip and 75%).
- Built for you and your brother: both with CORE (Conservation and Outdoor Recreation Education) and a PAL (Possession and Acquisition Licence), each with your own licences (owner confirmed, from [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety/0)).
```

## Key points

```diagram fl-pt-plan
```

- Six things agreed at the truck: where, back by, meeting point, who shoots, signals, if late (Tip).
- Three whistle blasts means help (80%).
- The shooter cancels their own licence. Never your partner's (99%).

```more the key points in full
- Walk in a line, side by side, always in sight. The middle and behind are no shot zones (75%, hunter education, via [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety/0)).
- Each hunter's zone of fire is about 45 degrees straight ahead (75%).
- Distress: whistle blasts, mirror flashes or other signals in threes (80%, BC Search and Rescue Association).
- BC has no blaze orange law. Wear it anyway with a partner (95% for the law, Tip for the advice).
- Whoever kills big game must cancel their own species licence immediately, before handling it (99%, synopsis page 11, item 1).
- Using another person's licence, or lending yours, is unlawful (99%, item 2).
- Radios between partners: not banned in the app's Rule Book, my reading (80%). See [Vehicles, Forest Service Roads and radio use](#/s/fsr-vehicles-radio/0).
```

## The plan at the truck

```diagram fl-pt-plan
```

- Say it out loud, both of you. Then mark it on both phones (Tip).

```checklist fl-pt-truck-plan
Where each of us goes, marked on both phones
Clock time we are back at the truck
Meeting point (a landmark) and meeting time
Who shoots at what, and who covers which side
Hand and whistle signals agreed
How long we wait if someone is late, then plan B
Trip plan left with someone at home, with our return time
Both phones charged, offline map loaded
Whistle on each of us, tourniquet in the same pocket
```

```more why each line
- "Later" is not a time. A clock time lets the other person act (Tip).
- Marking spots on both phones means either of you can find the other (Tip; see [GPS, phone and battery](#/s/gps-phone-battery/0)).
- A trip plan with someone at home is the third safety net (75%, AdventureSmart, see [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0)).
- First aid with a tourniquet: [First aid in the field](#/s/field-first-aid/0).
```

## Zones of fire, together

```diagram zones-of-fire
```

```anim gr-zone-swing Deer runs into the gap: hold fire.
```

- About 45 degrees straight ahead for each hunter (75%).
- A deer runs into the gap: no shot (75%).

```more zones in full
- A zone of fire is the only slice of ground where you may shoot (75%).
- Hunter education teaches about 45 degrees straight ahead for each hunter (75%).
- Never swing the muzzle outside it, even tracking a running deer (75%).
- Every shot also needs a safe [[Backstop]]. Sky is never one.
- Lose sight of each other: muzzles up or down, no shooting, call out (75%).
- Detail and videos: [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety/0).
```

## Walking side by side

```diagram fl-pt-formation
```

- One gets ahead or drops back: both stop and fix the line (75%).
- Orange on both of you, so you see each other in timber (Tip).

```more walking in full
- Walk in a line, side by side, always in sight (75%).
- In thick timber, close the gap so you keep sight (Tip).
- Crossing a fence or a creek: both unload, one crosses, hands over, the other crosses (Tip, see [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety/0)).
- BC has no blaze orange rule (95%, synopsis, Wildlife Act and Hunting Regulation checked by the firearm safety lesson). My lean: orange hat and vest when you are two or more (Tip).
```

## Silent hand signals

```diagram fl-pt-hands
```

- Agree them at home, practise them on a walk (Tip).
- There is no official standard. Your code is what you agree (Tip).

```more signals in full
| Signal | Meaning (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Flat hand up | Stop, freeze |
| Point | Game, that way |
| Circling wave toward you | Come to me |
| Palm pushed down | Get low |
| Thumb up | I see you, all OK |
| Arms crossed over head | Done, unloaded, coming in |

- Check you can see each other's hands at your usual spacing (Tip).
- In low light, hand signals fail. Agree a soft whistle or radio click for dawn and dusk (Tip).
```

## Whistle code

```diagram fl-pt-whistle
```

- Three blasts, repeated, means help (80%).
- Every other code is your own (Tip).

```more whistles in full
- Signal distress in threes: whistle blasts, mirror flashes, horn blasts, signal fires or rock piles (80%, BC Search and Rescue Association).
- Pattern: three blasts, a pause to listen, repeat (65%, BC survival guide summaries).
- Searchers often use two long blasts so they are not mistaken for distress (60%, searcher forum post). Tip: listen for any whistle reply.
- A whistle carries much farther than a shout and saves your voice (Tip).
- Whistle is in the 10 essentials as a signalling device (75%, AdventureSmart).
```

## Radios and phones

```diagram fl-fsr-radios
```

- FRS (Family Radio Service) handhelds need no licence (85%).
- Volume low, earpiece in, agree check in times (Tip).

```more radios in full
- ISED (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada) lists FRS portable radios among equipment that may not need a licence (85%, ISED radio licensing page).
- Pick your channel from your radios' own manual and write it in the trip plan (Tip). This app gives no channel numbers.
- No radio ban for partners found in the app's Rule Book (80%, my reading). Rules on aircraft and drone information: [Vehicles, Forest Service Roads and radio use](#/s/fsr-vehicles-radio/0).
- Texting is silent. Agree a short code, like "1" for "I am at my spot" (Tip).
- Phones die in cold. Battery care: [GPS, phone and battery](#/s/gps-phone-battery/0).
```

## Splitting up

```anim fl-pt-split Different routes, one meeting place, one time.
```

- Each knows the other's route on the map (Tip).
- Never split up without a meeting point and a clock time (Tip).

```more splitting up in full
- Split when it helps: covering two glassing points, or two sides of a draw (Tip).
- Do not split into the same patch of timber from two sides. You end up facing each other (Tip).
- Mark both routes on both phones before you go (Tip).
- Shooting near your partner's route: know where the route runs and never shoot toward it (Tip).
```

## Meeting again

```steps Meet in 4 steps
fl-pt-meet-1 | A landmark you both know.
fl-pt-meet-2 | A clock time, not "later".
fl-pt-meet-3 | A wait window, then whistle and call.
fl-pt-meet-4 | Plan B: the truck.
```

- Legal light ends 1 hour after sunset for big game (99%). Meet before dark (Tip).

```more meeting in full
- Meeting time before dark: walking out in the dark on rough ground is where people fall (Tip).
- Big game hunting ends 1 hour after sunset (99%, synopsis page 11, item 38). Near Heffley Creek on 31 October, sunset is about 5:40 pm (90%, see [Weather, daylight and season timing](#/s/daylight-weather-bc/0)).
- Wait window: 30 minutes is a sensible default (Tip).
- If one of you has an animal down at dark: that changes the plan. Text or radio, and agree who comes to help (Tip).
```

## A simple push

```diagram fl-pt-push
```

- One sits at a crossing, one walks slowly through the cover (Tip).
- The stander shoots only away from the pusher's line (Tip).

```more pushes in full
- The stander picks a spot at a funnel or saddle with a safe zone of fire that never points at the cover the pusher walks (Tip; see [Trails and funnels](#/s/funnels/0)).
- The pusher walks slowly with the wind at their back toward the stander, so scent pushes deer ahead (Tip, see [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals/0)).
- The pusher may also get a shot. Agree before: the pusher shoots only to the sides, never toward the stander (Tip).
- Both in orange. Both know the exact spot of the other (Tip).
- It is unlawful to use a motor vehicle or other mechanical device to herd or harass wildlife (99%, synopsis page 11, item 25). Pushes are on foot only.
```

## The shot and the tag

> [!warn] Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

```diagram fl-pt-tag
```

- The hunter who kills it cancels their own species licence right away (99%).
- Never use or lend a licence (99%).

```more the rules in full
> [!law] Item 1, page 11
> "To be in possession of a big game animal without a properly cancelled species licence or otherwise authorized by licence, permit, or an exemption provided by regulation. Any person who kills any big game species must immediately after the kill and before handling the big game killed, cancel the appropriate species licence in accordance with the instructions on that licence."

> [!law] Item 2, page 11
> "To use another person's licence or to loan or transfer any licence under any circumstance."

- Plain English: whoever killed it tags it with their own licence, at once (99%). Your brother cannot put his tag on a deer you shot (99%).
- Both shot at it? Agree before the hunt how you will decide (Tip). If you cannot tell, the honest answer is the one you agreed in advance.
- Tagging steps: [Tagging and the legal paper trail](#/s/tagging-legal/0).
```

## After the shot

```steps Two hunters, one animal down
fl-pt-after-1 | Shooter calls it. Both watch.
fl-pt-after-2 | Both unload. Mark shot spot and last seen.
fl-pt-after-3 | One tracks, one watches ahead.
fl-pt-after-4 | Walk up together, shooter first.
```

- Wait times and trailing: [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing/0).

```more after the shot in full
- Both unload, then walk up together. The shooter leads (Tip, from [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety/0)).
- Trailing as a team: one on the last blood, one ahead looking for the animal or more sign (Tip).
- You must make all reasonable effort to find, finish and retrieve wounded game and count it in your bag (99%, synopsis page 12, Retrieval).
- After: [After the shot](#/s/after-the-shot/0).
```

## Partner missing

```diagram fl-pt-lost
```

- Go to the meeting point first, then whistle and call (Tip).
- At the agreed time, call 911 or send an SOS (Tip).

```more lost partner in full
- Do not wander off searching alone in the dark. Now two people are lost (Tip).
- The lost person should STOP: Stop, Think, Observe, Plan, and stay put (75%, see [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0)).
- Stay put helps searchers: they head to the last known place (65%, BC survival guidance).
- 911 does not work in all areas of BC. A satellite messenger SOS works where phones do not (85% and 70%, see [Vehicles, Forest Service Roads and radio use](#/s/fsr-vehicles-radio/0)).
- Injury: [First aid in the field](#/s/field-first-aid/0).
```

## Agree it before, not after

```diagram fl-pt-share
```

- Money and meat: decide at home, not at the carcass (Tip).

```more sharing in full
- Costs: fuel, food, processing, shared gear (Tip). See [Planning a season](#/s/season-planning/0) for a budget.
- Meat: an even split is common. Write it down if it helps (Tip).
- You may give meat to another person; they then take it out and handle it under the rules (99%, see [BC rules you must know](#/s/bc-rules/0)).
- Who calls "too late, too windy, home time"? Agree the rule: either one can call it, and no arguing in the field (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fl-pt-mistakes
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Know where your partner is before you know where the deer is.

> [!mistake]
> - Splitting up with no time and no place.
> - Swinging past your zone on a running deer.
> - Both shooting at one deer with no plan.

```more the mistakes in full
- "See you later" is not a plan. Name the landmark and the clock time (Tip).
- Swinging past your arc: a running deer is never worth it (75%).
- Both shooting: agree who shoots before the hunt, and only the person who killed it tags it (99% for the tag, Tip for the plan).
```

> [!field]
> Next walk with your brother: do the six item plan at the truck out loud, walk 200 m side by side unloaded, point out each other's zone, practise all six hand signals, then split up for 20 minutes and meet at a landmark on a clock time.
