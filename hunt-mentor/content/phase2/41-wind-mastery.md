> [!why]
> Wind is the one thing a deer trusts more than its eyes. Learn how terrain bends it and how the sun flips it hour by hour, and you can predict where your scent goes before you take a step.

```anim fn-wd-day One slope, one day: down at night, up in the sun, calm, then down again.
```

- Slope winds flip almost right at sunrise and sunset (75%).
- Valley winds lag by hours (75%).

```more what this lesson adds
- The basics (keep the deer upwind, morning up, evening down, puffer and milkweed) are in [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals/0). Do that first.
- This lesson adds the timing by slope and by hour, the speeds, what ridges, saddles, draws, timber and junctions do to the wind, how to read wind strength on the land, and a five step read before every move.
- Main source: the USDA Forest Service Fire Weather handbook (Schroeder and Buck, 1970), written for firefighters who must predict exactly this (75%, via Forest Encyclopedia).
- Mountain science review: the switch from night downslope to day upslope happens "almost immediately after sunrise", and the reverse after sunset; valley winds switch "several hours" later (75%, boundary layer research review, search preview).
```

## Key points

| Fact | Hunting meaning (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Upslope starts soon after sun hits a slope | East faces flip first |
| Up valley wind starts mid to late morning | Valley floor wind lags |
| Downslope starts as shade arrives | Evening sits low |
| Calm between switches | Swirl time, scent pools |
| Timber: shallow downslope under the canopy | Scent can creep downhill in the shade at 10 am |
| Eddies at bends and side draw junctions | Avoid sitting at a junction |
| Lee side of a ridge can blow back uphill | Never sit just over a crest from the wind |
| Head height wind is 50 to 70% of forecast | Forecast overstates your wind |

```more the key points in full
1. "Upslope winds begin as a gentle upflow soon after the sun strikes the slope." East facing slopes start first (75%, Fire Weather handbook).
2. Up valley flow waits until the whole valley air warms, usually "middle or late forenoon", peaks early afternoon and runs into the evening (75%).
3. On slopes going into shade: the upslope wind dies, a brief calm, then gentle downslope flow begins (75%).
4. Down valley flow lasts through the night and fades after sunrise (75%).
5. On dense forested slopes, upslope flow may rise above the treetops while a shallow downslope flow persists under the canopy (75%).
6. Eddies "may form at canyon bends and at tributary junctions"; along upper ridges wind speed and direction may change quickly (75%).
7. On the lee side of a peak a turbulent reversal or eddies are possible (70%, NWCG, search preview).
8. Beaufort speeds are at 10 m; at 2 m wind may be only 50 to 70% of them (90%, Royal Meteorological Society).
```

## Which slope flips first

```diagram fn-wd-aspect
```

- East faces first. South strongest near midday. West mid afternoon (75%).
- North faces stay weakest, and sink first in the evening (75%).

```more timing by aspect in full
- South slopes peak "soon after midday", west slopes by about mid afternoon (75%, Fire Weather handbook).
- South and southwest slopes heat most and have the strongest upslope winds, several times stronger than on north slopes (75%).
- Bare and grassy slopes heat faster than brush or tree covered slopes, so upslope winds are weaker under vegetation (75%).
- Where slopes of different aspects drain into one basin, the shaded slopes start downslope first, and late afternoon up valley winds can turn up to 180 degrees after sunset (75%).
- Find aspect fast with the Hunt Map's Slope direction tool, and the sun's position with Sun and shade (99%). See [The Hunt Map like a pro](#/s/hunt-map-pro/0).
```

## Thermals by the hour

```diagram fn-wd-hours
```

- Clear calm day: slopes up from soon after sun to shade (75%).
- Switch times are swirl times. Be set up before them (Tip).

```more hour by hour, mid October near Heffley Creek (Tip)
| Time | Slopes | Valley floor | You |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before sunrise, 6:30 | Down | Down valley | Walk in from above or the side |
| Sun hits east faces, about 8 | Switching, swirl | Down valley | Sit, do not move in |
| 9 to noon | Up on sunny faces | Switching to up valley | Hunt from above sunny faces |
| Noon to 3 | Up, strongest on south | Up valley | Still hunt into the wind |
| Shade arrives, 4 to 5 | Calm, then down | Up valley still | Sit below or beside |
| After sunset, 18:08 | Down | Switching to down valley | Leave low, wind in your face |

- Clock times are estimates for a clear day near Heffley Creek (Tip). Sunrise about 7:25, sunset about 18:08 on 15 October (85%, my calculation). Cloud, strong wind or a north face change everything.
- Strong forecast wind beats thermals; on calm days thermals rule (Tip, see Wind and thermals).
```

## How strong are thermals?

| Wind | Speed |
|---|---|
| Upslope | about 5 to 13 km/h (3 to 8 mph) (75%) |
| Downslope at night | about 3 to 8 km/h (2 to 5 mph) (75%) |
| Up valley, big valleys | about 16 to 24 km/h (10 to 15 mph) (75%) |

```diagram fn-wd-beaufort
```

- Thermals are breezes: you feel them on your face (95%).

```more speeds in full
- Upslope winds generally range from 3 to 8 mph and depend on the length and steepness of the slope and the exposure (75%, NWCG Estimating Winds for Fire Behavior, search preview).
- Downslope winds are very shallow and slower, generally 2 to 5 mph (75%).
- Up valley winds in larger valleys typically 10 to 15 mph (75%, Fire Weather handbook).
- Downslope afternoon winds in some places are three times as strong as the morning upslope (75%). Local patterns vary.
- Beaufort scale on land (95%, Royal Meteorological Society): force 0 calm, smoke rises straight; force 1, 1 to 5 km/h, smoke drifts; force 2, 6 to 11, felt on face, leaves rustle; force 3, 12 to 19, leaves and small twigs in constant motion; force 4, 20 to 28, dust and small branches move; force 5, 29 to 38, small trees sway.
```

## Forecast wind is not your wind

```diagram fn-wd-height
```

- Forecasts are at 10 m. At head height it may be 50 to 70% (90%).
- In timber, less again (Tip).

```more forecast versus ground in full
- Beaufort figures are measured at 10 m and "do not reflect the wind speeds that you would feel on the ground"; at 2 m wind may be only 50 to 70% of them (90%, Royal Meteorological Society).
- The Hunt Map scent cone uses forecast wind 10 m above the ground (99%, app). Treat it as a starting guess.
- The forecast is a model for a grid square. Draws and timber can differ (99%, app text: "Wind in draws and timber can differ").
```

## Ridges and the lee eddy

```anim fn-wd-lee Fast over the top, swirling and backward just over the crest.
```

- Just over a crest from the wind, air can tumble back uphill (70%).
- Do not sit just behind the crest (Tip).

```more ridges in full
- Over mountains, terrain usually overshadows the general wind; the flow is channelled, and over sharp crests eddies are produced (75%, Fire Weather handbook).
- Some acceleration of the general wind is likely over the tops and flanks of isolated peaks (70%, NWCG).
- On the lee side, a turbulent reversal or eddies are possible (70%).
- Along upper ridges "wind speed and direction may change quickly" (75%).
- Tip: glass from the windward side or well down the lee side, not in the tumble just behind the crest.
```

## Saddles squeeze the wind

```diagram fn-wd-saddle
```

- Wind speeds up through low gaps (Tip).
- Your scent pours through the saddle with it.

```more saddles and draws in full
- Air flowing over a ridge is channelled through low gaps and along valleys (75%, Fire Weather handbook: "The windflow is channeled").
- Saddles are where animals cross ridges (65%, Maps 1). If you sit a saddle, sit off to the downwind side, not in the gap (Tip).
- Draws act like gutters for cool evening air (75%; Wind and thermals).
```

## Timber: two winds at once

```diagram fn-wd-timber
```

- Sun on the canopy: air rises above the trees.
- Shade on the floor: a thin cool layer still sinks (75%).

```more timber in full
- "On dense forested slopes, upslope flow may rise above the treetops while a shallow downslope flow persists at the surface under the canopy shade." (75%, Fire Weather handbook)
- "A dense understory blocks nighttime downslope flow, which is diverted or confined to openings such as streams and roads." (75%)
- Hunter meaning (Tip): at 10 am in thick north timber your scent may still creep downhill along the floor, and at night cold air runs down creeks and roads like water.
- Puffer at your feet as well as at chest height (Tip).
```

## Eddies at bends and junctions

```diagram fn-wd-junction
```

- Eddies form at canyon bends and side draw junctions (75%).
- A junction is a swirl trap. Sit above or away from it (Tip).

```more eddies in full
- Strong up valley and up canyon winds can be turbulent because the air is unstable and the terrain rough; eddies may form at canyon bends and tributary junctions (75%, Fire Weather handbook).
- Bowls swirl too. See [Topo deep dive](#/s/topo-deep-dive/0).
- Cold air pools where draws meet the valley floor at night (75%). See [Reading weather in the field](#/s/field-weather/0).
```

## Read the wind in 5 steps

```steps Before every move
fn-wd-read-1 | Forecast: direction, speed, gusts.
fn-wd-read-2 | Terrain: ridge, saddle, draw, timber, bowl.
fn-wd-read-3 | Clock: which slopes are sunny now.
fn-wd-read-4 | Check with the puffer at every new feature.
fn-wd-read-5 | Steady: commit. Swirling: plan B.
```

- Five questions, ten seconds each (Tip).

```more the five steps in full
1. Forecast wind: Hunt Map, Weather and wind: direction, speed, gusts (99%).
2. Terrain: what will the ridge, saddle, draw or timber do to it? (this lesson)
3. Clock: is your slope in sun or shade? Has it switched yet? (this lesson)
4. Real check: puffer bottle or milkweed, every few minutes and at every new feature (Wind and thermals).
5. Decide: steady and right, commit. Swirling or wrong, go to plan B. Never force a bad wind (Tip).
- Map tool: Scent cone from my location shows where forecast wind takes your scent; the app reminds you the real cone is wider in timber and around ridges (99%).
```

## Where to sit: wind and time

| Situation | Sit (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Dawn, deer feeding below, calm | Above, or across, after the switch |
| Dawn, before sun hits | Beside, not straight above |
| Midday, sunny slope | Above, wind rising past you |
| Evening, shade arrives | Below or beside |
| Steady strong wind | Downwind side of the trail, ignore thermals |
| Swirl, bowl or junction | Do not sit. Move on |

```more seats in full
- All rows are hunter experience, labelled Tip, built on the timing facts above (75%).
- Before the morning switch, slope air is still sinking. A seat directly above deer at first light can work only once the upslope starts (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram hf-swirl
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): The wind you checked five minutes ago is gossip. The puff in your hand is news.

> [!mistake]
> - Trusting the forecast arrow in a draw.
> - Sitting just behind a ridge crest.
> - Moving in during the morning switch.

```more the mistakes in full
- Trusting the forecast arrow in a draw or thick timber. Terrain beats the forecast.
- Sitting just over a crest from the wind, in the lee tumble that blows back uphill.
- Walking in during the morning switch, when the slope air is calm and swirling.
```

> [!field]
> On one clear day, puff the wind at the same spot on an east face and a west face every hour from 7 to 11 am. Write the time each one turns uphill. That is your local thermal clock.

```checklist fn-wind-day
Checked forecast direction, speed and gusts
Named the terrain feature for every planned seat
Noted when the sun hits my slope
Puffed the wind at every new feature
Had a plan B seat for the other wind
Logged when the morning switch happened
```
