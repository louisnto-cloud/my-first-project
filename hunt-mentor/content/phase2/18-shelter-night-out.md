> [!why]
> A blood trail at dusk, a twisted knee, fog on the ridge: one day you will sleep out. Shelter is what makes that a cold night, not a rescue.


- Bed under you first, roof over you second (Tip).

```more what this session covers

- Why you lose heat: [Cold injuries](#/s/cold-injuries/0). Fire: [Fire in wet weather](#/s/fire-wet-weather/0). Being found: [Lost](#/s/lost-survival-kit/0).
- Assumption: October nights near 0 °C in the Kamloops hills, wet nights near 5 °C in the Fraser Valley.
```

## Key points

```diagram fs-sh-site
```

- Start building at least an hour before dark (Tip).

```more the 8 key points in full
1. Insulation and a barrier from the ground and weather are the core of preventing and treating hypothermia (75%, Wilderness Medical Society via American Family Physician).
2. The ground pulls heat by conduction. A thick bed matters more than a roof on a dry night (75%, physics; Tip on the order).
3. Wind and wet speed heat loss a lot (70%).
4. Cold air sinks into valley bottoms at night (75%, see [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals/0)).
5. A tarp or bivy is faster and safer than building from the bush when tired or hurt (65%).
6. Foil blankets reflect radiant heat but do little against conduction from the ground (65%).
7. A shelter hidden in thick timber can hide you from searchers. Put a signal in the open (65%).
8. Two people sharing one bed and cover stay warmer (Tip).
```

## Pick the spot

```diagram fs-sh-site
```

- Bench part way up, near trees and fuel. Not the valley floor (Tip).
- Look up: no dead trees or hanging branches (Tip).

```more choosing a site in full
- Choose the driest spot you can, out of the wind, away from low points where cold air and water collect (65%, shelter guides via search).
- Cold air sinks downhill at night and pools in draws and valley floors (75%, thermals, [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals/0)).
- Ridge tops are windy (Tip).
- Look up for dead standing trees and broken branches that can fall in wind (Tip).
- Not in a gully, a creek bottom or an avalanche path. See [Avalanche awareness](#/s/avalanche-hunters/0) (Tip).
- Near firewood and water, and near an opening where you can be seen (Tip).
```

## Bed first

```anim fs-sh-conduction Heat drains into bare ground. A thick bed stops it.
```

- Thicker than you think. It squashes flat under you (Tip).

```more the bed in full
- Insulate the ground contact area: it is one of the key places to protect (75%, Wilderness Medical Society via American Family Physician).
- Bed materials (Tip): closed cell foam pad, your pack, spruce or fir boughs, dry grass, dry leaves, layered thick.
- Build it so it is still a hand or more thick when you lie on it (Tip).
- Sit on your pack while building to stay off the ground (Tip).
- Cutting boughs: take a few from many trees, not one tree stripped. Check land rules where you are (Tip).
```

## Tarp shelter

```steps Tarp shelter in 4 steps
fs-sh-tarp-1 | Tie a tight cord between two trees.
fs-sh-tarp-2 | Drape the tarp over the cord.
fs-sh-tarp-3 | Pin the windward edge low.
fs-sh-tarp-4 | Build the bed under it before dark.
```

- A 2 m by 3 m tarp and 10 m of cord cover two people (Tip).

```more tarp in full
- Tarps and bivy bags are among the most effective shelter tools for their weight, and often safer than building when tired, hurt or short of light (65%, shelter guides via search).
- Open side away from the wind. Windward side pinned to the ground with rocks, logs or stakes (Tip).
- Steep pitch sheds rain and snow (Tip).
- Carry a light tarp in the day pack in October (Tip). An emergency bivy bag works alone if you have no tarp.
- Lean to version: tie only the top edge to the cord and stake the bottom edge on the windward side. More open, good with a fire in front (Tip).
```

## Foil blanket or bivy bag?

| | Foil blanket | Bivy bag |
|---|---|---|
| Weight | Very light | Light |
| Stops wind | If wrapped well | Yes |
| Stops ground cold | No | A little |
| Tears | Easily | Less |
| My lean | Backup | Carry this |

```more what the sources say
- Foil blankets reflect radiant heat, but the "reflects 90%" claim on packets is marketing (65%).
- The thin film does almost nothing against conduction into the ground (65%, medical equipment supplier and gear reviews).
- They need an air gap to work against radiation; touching skin, they act like a thin plastic sheet (60%).
- They do not breathe: moisture gets trapped (60%).
- A bag shape (bivy) wraps you fully and stops wind; you still need a pad or bed under it (Tip).
- My lean: carry an emergency bivy bag in the pack and a foil blanket in the pocket kit.
```

## Debris hut: no gear

```steps Debris hut in 4 steps
fs-sh-debris-1 | Pole from a stump to the ground.
fs-sh-debris-2 | Lean close ribs along both sides.
fs-sh-debris-3 | Pile leaves and boughs thick.
fs-sh-debris-4 | Fill the inside, crawl in, plug the door.
```

- Takes hours and lots of material. Start early (65%).

```more debris hut in full
- Debris shelters can give emergency insulation when time is short, but they are uncomfortable and need large amounts of dry material (65%, shelter guides via search).
- Make it just big enough for you: a small space warms faster (Tip).
- Thickness: "as thick as your arm is long" is a common bushcraft rule (Tip, not tested here).
- In the dry Interior you may not find enough leaves. Boughs and needles work (Tip).
- My lean: a tarp in the pack beats 3 hours of building.
```

## Fire and shelter together

```diagram fs-sh-reflector
```

- Small fire, safe gap, and only when fire is legal (80%).

```more fire and shelter in full
- A wall of logs or rocks behind the fire bounces heat toward you (Tip).
- Keep the fire small and the tarp high above and away (Tip). See [Fire](#/s/fire-wet-weather/0) for the size limit and ban checks (80%).
- Feed it through the night, or let it die and rely on the bed. A big fire next to a sleeping hunter is how sleeping bags and tarps burn (Tip).
- Carbon monoxide: never burn a stove or fire inside a closed shelter (Tip).
```

## The night routine

```diagram fs-sh-night
```

- Food is fuel. Eat before you sleep (Tip).

```more the routine in full
- All Tip unless marked:
  - Eat something with fat before sleeping. Shivering burns fuel.
  - Pee before you settle. Holding it costs heat.
  - Dry socks on. Wet socks inside your shirt to dry from body heat.
  - Toque and buff on all night. Hood up.
  - Boots in a bag or under you, so they do not freeze stiff.
  - Cold in the night: clench big muscles, eat, sit up and move, add a layer.
- Keep the headlamp and whistle in a pocket you can find in the dark (Tip).
- Keep the phone warm for the morning check in. See [Lost](#/s/lost-survival-kit/0).
```

## Two hunters: share heat

```diagram fs-sh-buddy
```

- One bed, one cover, back to back (Tip).

```more sharing heat in full
- Two people huddling share body heat. In cold water, huddling is a taught technique (75%, see [Water safety](#/s/water-safety/0)). On land, the same idea works under one cover (Tip).
- Put the colder or hurt person in the middle of all the insulation (Tip).
- Swap who gets up to feed the fire (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fs-sh-ground
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): When the light turns gold and you are still far from the truck, start building. The dark will not wait for you.

> [!mistake]
> - Building after dark.
> - A roof but no bed.
> - Camping in the valley bottom.

```more the mistakes in full
- Starting the shelter after dark. Everything takes 3 times longer by headlamp (Tip).
- A great roof and bare ground under you. The ground wins (75%).
- The valley floor: coldest air, and often wet (75%).
- Under a dead tree (Tip).
- A shelter hidden so well no one can see you. Put orange in the open (65%).
```

## Do this in the field

```diagram fs-sh-tarp-3
```

> [!field]
> Before the season, pitch your tarp in the backyard or a legal camp spot in under 10 minutes, then sleep one cold night under it with only your day pack kit.

```checklist fs-sh-ready
Tarp or emergency bivy in the day pack
10 m of cord
Closed cell foam sit pad
Puffy, toque, spare dry socks
Foil blanket in the pocket kit
Practised the tarp in under 10 minutes
```

| Hook | Means |
|---|---|
| Bed first, roof second | Ground steals the most heat |
| Gold light, start building | Begin an hour before dark |
| Bench, not bottom | Cold air pools low |
| Look up | No dead trees overhead |
| Low to the wind | Pin the windward edge |
| Eat, pee, socks, toque | Night routine |
