> [!why]
> Tired legs make bad decisions: a rushed stalk, a shaky shot, a fall on the way down. Fitness is a safety skill.

```diagram fl-fit-demands
```

- Hills, a pack, long days, and meat out at the end.
- This lesson: a safe 12 week plan you can start today (Tip).

```more what this lesson covers
- What a hunt day asks of your body: climbing, carrying, long hours, moving meat, cold and wet, and steep downhill (Tip, from the packing and meat lessons in this app).
- October days near Heffley Creek run from about 11 h 43 min of daylight on 1 October to about 9 h 51 min on 31 October (90%, NOAA solar equations, see [Weather, daylight and season timing](#/s/daylight-weather-bc/0)).
- A hunt day can fill all of that light, plus the walk in and out in the dark (Tip).
- A whole gutted deer is about 50 kg to drag; boneless meat from one deer is about 25 kg (Tip, from [Packing out](#/s/packing-out/0)).
- The plan here is a Hunt Mentor plan built on the national movement guidelines and on what mountain hunting coaches teach. It is opinion, labelled Tip, not a medical prescription.
- Related: [Packs and carrying loads](#/s/packs-carrying/0), [Food and water for long hunt days](#/s/hunt-food-water/0).
```

## Key points

```diagram fl-fit-engines
```

- Train three engines: heart, legs, carry (Tip).
- Base first, pack later. Every fourth week easier (Tip).
- Moderate pace: you can talk but not sing (70%).

```more the key points in full
- Canadian adults: at least 150 minutes a week of moderate to vigorous aerobic activity, and muscle strengthening of the major muscle groups at least twice a week (95%, Canadian 24 Hour Movement Guidelines, CSEP (Canadian Society for Exercise Physiology), read directly).
- The same guidelines: 7 to 9 hours of good quality sleep with consistent bed and wake times (95%).
- Talk test: moderate means you can talk but not sing; vigorous means only a few words before a breath (70%, CDC (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention) talk test via news report).
- Mountain hunting coaches build the aerobic base first and save weighted pack carries for the last 8 weeks of a long plan (65%, Uphill Athlete coach statements on their forum).
- They also use every fourth week as a lighter recovery week (65%, TrainingPeaks plan summary by Uphill Athlete coaches).
- The famous "10% a week" rule has weak research support. Small steady steps still make sense (65%, secondary summaries of running studies). Tip: when in doubt, add less.
- Strength twice a week protects knees and back on steep ground and under a pack (Tip).
- Break in boots and socks during training, not on the hunt (Tip).
```

## Honest timing: your hunts are days away

```diagram fl-fit-timeline
```

- Today is 11 October. Your first hunts are mid to late October.
- 12 weeks from today ends about 3 January.
- So: start now, gain safely, and peak for the late season and next year (Tip).

```more what to do this week and next
- You cannot build 12 weeks of fitness in 10 days. Trying to cram it is how people get hurt (Tip).
- This week (Tip):
  - Walk 30 to 45 minutes most days, with some hills or stairs.
  - Wear your hunting boots and socks on every walk.
  - Do one short strength session (screen "Strength moves").
  - Carry your real day pack on two of the walks, not heavier.
  - Sleep 7 to 9 hours (95%, CSEP).
- On the hunt: pace yourself, rest before steep climbs, and pass on any shot you reach out of breath (Tip).
- Deer buck seasons in Region 3 close on 10 December (99%, from [The BC hunter's year](#/s/bc-calendar/0)). Weeks 1 to 8 of this plan land before that date.
- Next season: start the 12 weeks in early July and finish before the September and October openers (Tip).
```

## Safety check first

```diagram fl-fit-check
```

- Health condition, injury, pregnancy, or chest symptoms? Ask first.
- Use the Get Active Questionnaire or a health professional (95%).

```more who should check first
- The Canadian movement guidelines say adults who are pregnant or who have a disability or medical condition should consider the Get Active Questionnaire, condition specific advice, or a health professional (95%, CSEP, read directly).
- Chest pain, dizzy spells or fainting with effort are reasons to stop and get checked before training (Tip, standard first aid caution).
- Sore is normal. Sharp pain, joint pain that gets worse, or pain that changes how you walk is not. Stop and get it looked at (Tip).
- Training alone on trails: leave a [[Trip plan]] like you would on a hunt (75%, AdventureSmart, see [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0)).
```

## How hard should it feel?

```diagram fl-fit-talk
```

- Most base training: moderate. Talk, but not sing (70%).
- Steep climbs push you into vigorous. That is fine in short bursts (Tip).

```more the talk test in full
- Moderate intensity: you can talk but not sing. Your voice may sound strained (70%, CDC talk test, reported by CNN).
- Vigorous intensity: you cannot say more than a few words without pausing for a breath (70%).
- Uphill hiking is listed as a vigorous example (70%).
- Why moderate for most sessions: you can do more of it, recover faster, and it builds the base that lets you climb all day (65%, mountain hunting coaches; Tip).
- Field use: if you cannot talk on a climb, slow down. You will arrive with steadier hands (Tip).
```

## The 12 week plan at a glance

```diagram fl-fit-12wk
```

- Minutes climb from 150 to 260 a week (Tip).
- Pack weight starts in week 5 and grows in small steps (Tip).
- Weeks 4, 8 and 12 are easier on purpose (Tip).

```more the whole plan as a table
| Week | Cardio minutes | Strength days | Pack | Key session |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 150 | 2 | none | 3 walks with hills, 45 to 60 min |
| 2 | 165 | 2 | none | one walk of 75 min |
| 3 | 180 | 2 | day pack, light | one hike of 90 min on hills |
| 4 (easy) | 130 | 1 | none | short walks only |
| 5 | 180 | 2 | 5 kg | stairs or hill repeats, 30 min |
| 6 | 200 | 2 | 6 kg | hike of 2 h |
| 7 | 220 | 2 | 8 kg | hill repeats with pack, 40 min |
| 8 (easy) | 160 | 1 | 5 kg | one easy hike |
| 9 | 220 | 2 | 10 kg | hike of 2.5 h on hills |
| 10 | 240 | 2 | 12 kg | hike of 3 h |
| 11 | 260 | 2 | 14 kg | long day: 4 h, steep |
| 12 (test) | 150 | 1 | 16 kg | test hike, then rest |

- All of this table is a Hunt Mentor plan (Tip), built on the 150 minutes and 2 strength days in the national guidelines (95%, CSEP).
- Pack loads assume an 80 kg (176 lb) person. Assumption. Scale to yours: the week 12 load is about 20% of body weight, which matches a common backpacking guideline (70%, REI (Recreational Equipment Inc.) pack fit specialist).
- Missed a week? Repeat it. Do not skip ahead (Tip).
```

## Weeks 1 to 4: build the base

```diagram fl-fit-phases
```

- Walk and hike at talk pace. No pack yet (Tip).
- Two short strength sessions a week (95% that guidelines say twice a week).
- Week 4 is lighter. Let the body catch up (Tip).

```more weeks 1 to 4 day by day
| Day | Week 1 to 3 (Tip) | Week 4, easy (Tip) |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Strength 25 min | Strength 20 min |
| Tue | Walk 45 min, some hills | Walk 30 min |
| Wed | Rest or easy walk | Rest |
| Thu | Strength 25 min, then walk 20 min | Walk 30 min |
| Fri | Rest | Rest |
| Sat | Long walk or hike: 60, 75, 90 min | Hike 60 min |
| Sun | Easy walk 30 to 45 min | Easy walk 30 min |

- Talk pace on everything (70% that this is moderate).
- Boots and hunting socks on every walk (Tip).
- Mission and the Fraser Valley are wet in fall: about 145 mm of rain in October at Abbotsford (95%, Environment Canada normals 1991 to 2020). Train in your rain gear and learn what keeps you dry (Tip).
```

## Weeks 5 to 8: hills and strength

```steps Hill repeats, step by step
fl-fit-move-1 | Step ups build the climbing muscles.
fl-fit-move-2 | Split squats for each leg alone.
fl-fit-move-6 | Calf raises for steep side hills.
```

- Add hills every week. Stairs count (Tip).
- The pack starts: 5 kg, then 6, then 8 (Tip).
- Week 8 is easier: 5 kg, shorter days (Tip).

```more weeks 5 to 8 in full
- Hill repeats: walk up a steep hill or stairs for 2 to 4 minutes, walk down easy, repeat 5 to 8 times (Tip).
- Pack weight: use water jugs or sandbags. Water is easy to dump on the way down to save your knees (Tip).
- Strength stays twice a week, but now with more weight or more reps (Tip).
- One longer hike each week: 2 h by week 6 (Tip).
- Coaches warn that heavy loads too early can undercut the aerobic base. That is why the pack starts light (65%, Uphill Athlete).
```

## Weeks 9 to 12: pack and long days

```anim fl-pack-progress Small steps up, an easy week, then up again.
```

- Long hikes with your real hunting pack, 2.5 to 4 h (Tip).
- Week 11 is the big day. Week 12: test, then rest (Tip).

```more weeks 9 to 12 in full
- Week 9: 10 kg, hike 2.5 h on hills (Tip).
- Week 10: 12 kg, hike 3 h (Tip).
- Week 11: 14 kg, a 4 h day with your full hunting kit, rifle sling weight included as a dummy weight, never a loaded firearm on a training hike in town (Tip).
- Week 12: short sessions early in the week, then the test hike with 16 kg, then two easy days (Tip).
- Example loads assume an 80 kg body. Assumption. Your week 12 load: about 20% of your weight (70%, REI guideline for loaded backpacking packs).
- Practice getting the pack on and off from the ground. That is in [Packs and carrying loads](#/s/packs-carrying/0).
```

## Strength moves: the six

```steps Six moves, twice a week
fl-fit-move-1 | Step up: whole foot on the box, drive up.
fl-fit-move-2 | Split squat: back knee down, stand tall.
fl-fit-move-3 | Goblet squat: jug at chest, sit back.
fl-fit-move-4 | Hip hinge: flat back, hips back.
fl-fit-move-5 | Plank or heavy carry: straight line.
fl-fit-move-6 | Calf raise: up, pause, slow down.
```

- 2 or 3 rounds, 8 to 12 slow reps a move (Tip).
- Stop 2 reps before you could not do another (Tip).

```more how to do the six
| Move | Sets and reps (Tip) | Why it helps (Tip) | Form cue (Tip) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Step up | 3 x 8 each leg | Climbing | Whole foot on the box, push through the heel |
| Split squat | 3 x 8 each leg | Balance and knees | Back knee toward the floor, front knee over the foot |
| Goblet squat | 3 x 10 | Legs and getting up | Hold a full 4 L jug at your chest |
| Hip hinge | 3 x 10 | Lifting a pack or a quarter | Flat back, hips back, stand by driving hips forward |
| Plank or carry | 3 x 20 to 45 s, or 3 x 40 m | Trunk that steadies the pack | Straight line, breathe |
| Calf raise | 3 x 12 | Steep side hills | Pause at the top, lower slowly |

- No gym needed: a sturdy step, water jugs and your pack do it (Tip).
- Lifting technique for real loads (bend knees, tighten core, keep the load close, lift with the legs) comes from the BC Forest Safety Council lifting guide (85%). See [Packs and carrying loads](#/s/packs-carrying/0).
```

## Downhill is where knees hurt

```diagram fl-fit-downhill
```

- Short steps, soft knees, poles (Tip).
- Train it: walk down your hills too, not only up (Tip).

```more downhill in full
- Going down loads the thigh muscles as they lengthen. That is what makes legs sore the next day (Tip).
- Short steps keep your feet under you on loose shale and wet grass (Tip).
- Trekking poles take load off the knees and catch slips (Tip).
- Heavy meat loads downhill: slow, short, and stop to rest before your legs shake (Tip). See [Packing out](#/s/packing-out/0).
```

## Feet: boots, socks, blisters

```steps Look after your feet
fl-feet-1 | Break in boots on short walks first.
fl-feet-2 | Train in the socks you hunt in.
fl-feet-3 | Hot spot? Stop now.
fl-feet-4 | Cover it, dry socks, carry on.
```

- A blister can end a hunt. Prevent it (Tip).

```more feet in full
- Wear new boots around the house, then on short walks, then on long ones (Tip).
- Wool or synthetic socks. Cotton holds sweat and rubs (Tip, see [What to wear](#/s/what-to-wear/0)).
- Carry tape or blister pads in the first aid kit (Tip).
- Stop at the first hot spot. Five minutes now saves the hunt (Tip).
- Dry socks every day of a camp hunt. Dry the wet pair inside your jacket or sleeping bag (Tip).
```

## How hard is hunting?

```diagram fl-fit-met
```

- Hiking with hunting gear: 9.5 METs (Metabolic Equivalents) (95%).
- Dragging a carcass: 9.8 METs (95%).

```more what METs mean and your calories
- A MET (Metabolic Equivalent of Task) compares an activity with sitting still, which is 1 MET (90%, Compendium of Physical Activities).
- Compendium values, read directly: hunting from a stand with limited walking 2.5; hunting, general 5.0; hunting deer, elk, large game 6.0; hunting, hiking with hunting gear 9.5; hunting large game, dragging a carcass 9.8; hunting pheasants or grouse 6.0 (95%, 2024 Adult Compendium, codes 04085, 04100, 04080, 04125, 04081, 04110).
- Rough calories: METs x body weight in kg x hours (85%, standard way MET tables are used).
- Example, 80 kg (Assumption): 6 hours hiking with gear at 9.5 METs = about 4,560 kcal for those 6 hours (Tip, estimate).
- Food plan: [Food and water for long hunt days](#/s/hunt-food-water/0).
```

## Recovery is training too

```diagram fl-fit-recover
```

- Sleep 7 to 9 hours (95%).
- Easy week every fourth week (Tip).

```more recovery in full
- 7 to 9 hours of good quality sleep, consistent bed and wake times (95%, CSEP).
- Fitness grows during rest, not during the workout (Tip).
- Eat a real meal with protein after long sessions (Tip).
- Sick or injured: drop back a week, do not push through (Tip).
- Hunt week: no hard training in the 3 days before (Tip).
```

## Three tests before the hunt

```diagram fl-fit-tests
```

- Pass all three and you are ready for a normal hunt day (Tip).

```checklist fl-fit-test-list
Test hike: 2 h on hills with my real hunting pack, no long stops
Get up from sitting on the ground with the pack on, without help
Climb a steep 5 minutes, then hold binoculars steady within 1 minute
Walked downhill 30 minutes on rough ground, no knee pain
Boots and socks used on 5 or more training walks
Sleep 7 hours or more most nights this week
```

```more why these tests
- The test hike checks the engine and the pack fit (Tip).
- Getting up from the ground with a pack is the lift you will do 20 times a day (Tip).
- A steady hold after a climb is what fair, clean shots need. The synopsis ethics code says do not shoot unless you have a clear shot of a vital part (99%, synopsis page 77).
- If a test fails: repeat weeks 9 and 10 and try again (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fl-fit-mistakes
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): The mountain does not care how strong you were in June. Train for the hill you will climb, with the pack you will carry.

> [!mistake]
> - Too much, too soon: big jumps in load or distance.
> - Only flat ground. Hunting is hills.
> - New boots on opening day.

```more the mistakes in full
- Too much, too soon. Big jumps hurt more than they help. Add small steps and take the easy week (Tip).
- Only flat ground. Train on hills, stairs or step ups every week (Tip).
- New boots on day one. Break them in over weeks (Tip).
- Bonus mistake: training alone with no trip plan on remote trails (Tip, see [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0)).
```

> [!field]
> On your next scouting trip, carry your full hunting pack for 2 hours on the real hills. Note where you got tired and how long the climbs took. Plan your hunt day around those times.

## Memory hooks

```diagram fl-fit-hooks
```

- **HILLS**: Heart, Incline, Legs, Load a little, Sleep.
- "Talk, not sing" = the right pace.
- "Every fourth week, let the body speak" (easy week).

```more more hooks
- **3, 2, 1 rule (Tip):** 3 walks or hikes, 2 strength sessions, 1 rest day, every week.
- **"Base first, pack last"**: no heavy pack until the base is built (65%, coach practice).
- **20% of you**: the biggest training pack is about 20% of body weight (70%, REI guideline).
- **"Short steps down"**: knees thank you.
- **The number 150**: minutes of moderate to vigorous activity a week (95%, CSEP).
```
