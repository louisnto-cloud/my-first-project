> [!why]
> The Hunt Map is your onX style map inside this app: land colours, MU lines, slope, sun, what you can see, wind, waypoints and tracks, all working offline. Ten minutes of practice at home saves an hour of fumbling on the hill.

```diagram fn-hm-screen
```

- Open it: [Hunt Map](#/map). Everything below matches the buttons there.

```more what this lesson covers
- Every button name in this lesson is copied from the app itself (99%, read in the app code on 2026-10-11).
- Short forms: MU (Management Unit), LEH (Limited Entry Hunting), WMA (Wildlife Management Area), GPS (Global Positioning System), GPX (GPS Exchange Format), KML (Keyhole Markup Language).
- The map starts at Heffley Creek, the owner's base (99%, app default view).
- Legal layers are a study aid. "Study aid only. The official regulations are the law." Rules: [Rule Book: where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt/0).
```

## Key points

```diagram fn-hm-bar
```

- **Hunt Map Layers** (low left): land, MUs, closures, habitat.
- **Tools**: waypoints, measure, terrain, wind.
- **Offline Maps**: save your area at home.

```more the key points in full
1. Top: menu and elevation profile buttons, HUNT MAP, search. Under them: scale bar and the elevation at the map centre (99%).
2. Right side: Weather and wind button. Low right: the basemap and 3D pill, and the locate button (99%).
3. Low left: Hunt Map Layers. Bottom bar: Offline Maps, My Content, Tools, Insights, Go & Track (99%).
4. Press and hold the map anywhere for This spot: Drop waypoint, Range rings, Measure from here, Scent cone, Insights (99%).
5. The cross in the middle is the map centre. Many tools work "at map centre" (99%).
6. Everything you make stays on the phone. My Content exports GPX, KML or GeoJSON (99%).
```

```steps Turn a layer on

fn-hm-lay-3 | Drag Opacity so the map shows through.
```

- Groups: Land status, Hunting, Habitat and migration, Access, Spots.
- **What do these colours mean?** opens the full guide.

```more layers in full
- Each row: a colour swatch, the layer name, an i button, and an on or off switch. Switched on, it shows Opacity, the data date and a certainty badge, plus a legend (99%).
- Month chips (All, Jan to Dec) show seasonal layers for the month you pick (99%).
- Spots group has filter chips: Access (Drive, ATV, Walk, Backcountry, Camp) and Species (99%). Spots are candidates scored from open data: scout every one (99%, app text).
- Land chip: while a land layer is on, a small Land key sits above the bottom bar; tap Key for names (99%).
- Tap any coloured area on the map for a card with its details (99%).
- Layer data comes from the BC Data Catalogue, Open Government Licence BC (99%).
```

## Land colours

```diagram fn-hm-land
```

- Orange: private. Yellow: reserve. Red stripes: no hunting.
- Purple dashed: vehicle closure. Crown land: no colour.

```more land colours in full
| Colour | Layer | Why a hunter cares (app guide) |
|---|---|---|
| Orange, strong edge | Private land (ParcelMap BC) | Owner's permission to hunt or cross |
| Green, dark green edge | Parks and protected areas | Many ban hunting and shooting |
| Yellow, gold edge | First Nations reserves | Treated like private land: ask the band office |
| Teal edge | WMA | Hunting often allowed, some limits |
| Red dashed edge | City and town limits | Shooting bylaws, ask the town |
| Red stripes, red edge | No hunting or shooting | Do not hunt or shoot inside |
| Purple dashed | Motor vehicle closures | Driving or using a vehicle to hunt may be illegal inside |
| Thin black lines, numbers | MU boundaries | Seasons and limits are set by MU |
| Dark brown dashed | LEH zones | Some seasons need a draw |
| Blue dashed lines | Routes in closures | Listed road status and dates |
| Brown lines | Forest Service roads | Can be gated or washed out |

- All rows: 99%, read in the app's layer guide. Crown land has no layer of its own in the current data; the map shows through (99%, layer list). No colour does not prove Crown: parcel data can miss land. Check before you hunt (Tip).
- Owner names are not open data in BC (99%, app).
- Rules: [Rule Book: where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt/0), [Region 3 Thompson](#/s/rb-region-3/0), [Maps 3: land status](#/s/maps-land-status/0).
```

## Slope steepness

```diagram fn-hm-slope
```

- Yellow, orange, red, purple. No colour under 15°.
- Red is 30 to 45°: avalanche terrain in snow (95%).

```more slope in full
- Classes: 15 to 25° steady climb; 25 to 30° steep; 30 to 45° very steep, avalanche terrain in snow; over 45° cliffy, scrambling (99%, app).
- Most avalanches start on 30 to 45 degree slopes (95%, Avalanche Canada). The app says: not an avalanche forecast, check Avalanche Canada before snowy mountains (99%).
- Benches and flats (no colour) on a coloured hillside are where animals often bed and feed (Tip, app).
- Steep ground means slow packing out and a hard drag (Tip, app). See [Planning a route](#/s/route-planning/0).
```

## Slope direction (aspect)

```diagram fn-hm-aspect
```

- South and west faces: most sun, snow melts first (Tip).
- North faces: shade, thicker timber, late snow (Tip).

```more aspect in full
- Colours: north blue, northeast teal, east green, southeast lime, south yellow, southwest orange, west red, northwest purple (99%, app).
- Ground under 8 degrees gets no colour (99%).
- East faces get first morning sun, west faces the last evening sun (Tip, app).
- October in the Interior: south and west faces hold feed and warmth (85%, Maps 1).
```

## What can I see from here

```diagram fn-hm-viewshed
```

- Pick a spot: Tap the map, My location or Map centre.
- Pink: ground you can likely see. Trees not counted (99%).

```more viewshed in full
- Eye height slider from 1 to 15 m. Standing eyes are about 1.7 m; a tree stand about 4 to 6 m (99%, app).
- How far to check: 1, 2 or 3 km (99%).
- It looks for something about 1 m tall, a deer's back, on bare ground (99%, app).
- The result shows what percent of the circle you can likely see (99%).
- Use it to pick glassing seats that see a lot of open ground, and to plan a stalk that stays out of sight (Tip, app). See dead ground in [Topo deep dive](#/s/topo-deep-dive/0).
```

## Sun and shade

```diagram fn-hm-sun
```

- Slide Time today, or tap Sunrise, Now, Sunset.
- Tip: cold mornings, deer bed where the first sun hits.

```more sun and shade in full
- Switch: Light the map by the real sun. Bright slopes face the sun, dark ones are in shade (99%, app).
- The label tells you where the sun is, for example "sun in the SE, 12° up" (99%).
- Sunrise and sunset at the map centre, Pacific time (99%).
- Long shadows thrown by a ridge onto the next valley are not drawn (99%, app).
- Tips from the app: glass sunny faces early and shady timber in the heat of the day.
- Legal hunting light is not the same as sunrise: see Insights or [Field Mode legal light](#/field/light).
```

## Wind: scent cone and weather

```diagram fn-hm-cone
```

- Scent cone: 30° wide, 400 m long, downwind of you (Tip, opinion).
- Weather: wind now, gusts, pressure trend, next 3 days.

```more wind tools in full
- Tools, Wind: Scent cone from map centre, Scent cone from my location, Weather (99%).
- The cone uses the forecast wind 10 m above the ground. Wind swirls in timber and around ridges, so the real cone is wider (99%, app text).
- Calm air: "scent pools around you" (99%, app).
- Weather and wind: temperature, sky, wind and gusts, a pressure trend line, and the next 3 days. "A forecast from a weather model, not a measurement." (99%) Forecast from Open Meteo, a free weather service.
- The forecast is saved, so offline you see the last one with its time (99%).
- Wind reading on the ground: [Wind mastery](#/s/wind-mastery/0) and [Reading weather in the field](#/s/field-weather/0).
```

## Waypoints

```steps Drop and label a waypoint
fn-hm-wpt-1 | Press and hold the map. Or Tools, Waypoint.
fn-hm-wpt-2 | Pick an icon: Parking, Glassing point, Bed, Blood.
fn-hm-wpt-3 | Name it, add a note or photo. Done.
```

- Mark the truck before you walk. Mark every bed and rub you find (Tip).

```more waypoints in full
- Two ways: Tools, Waypoint, At map centre or At my location; or press and hold on the map, then Drop waypoint (99%).
- Icons: Parking, Camp, Stand, Glassing point, Bed, Rub, Scrape, Tracks, Scat, Water, Gate, Trail camera, Kill, Blood, Other (99%).
- Each waypoint has coordinates with a copy button, elevation, a photo, name, note, colour and folder. Changes save by themselves (99%).
- Buttons on a waypoint: Range rings here, Scent cone here, Insights here, Move to map centre, Share or export, Delete (99%).
- Tip: name with date and what: "Oct 18 rub, big".
```

## Measure, lines, areas, rings

```diagram fn-hm-rings
```

- Measure: tap points, see distance and the bearing of the last leg.
- Range rings: 100, 200, 300 m around a spot.

```more drawing tools in full
- Tools, Draw and measure: Line or route (distance and climb), Area (hectares, perimeter), Measure (quick ruler), Range rings (100, 200, 300 m), Elevation profile (99%).
- Tap the map to add points, or move the map and tap Add centre point. Undo, Save (99%).
- Measure shows the last leg's distance and bearing in degrees, for example "toward NE (48°)" (99%). Check your compass work with it.
- Range rings are 100, 200 and 300 m, 109, 219 and 328 yd (99%). Tap the Range rings chip to clear them.
- Elevation profile: Draw a line, or pick a saved line or track (99%). See [Planning a route](#/s/route-planning/0).
```


## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fn-hm-bar
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): A map you have not practised on is a map you cannot read in the rain. Play with it at the kitchen table.

> [!mistake]
> - Trusting a candidate spot without checking land and closures.
> - Not saving the area offline.
> - Reading the viewshed as truth in timber.

```more the mistakes in full
- Trusting a candidate spot without checking land status, closures and the Rule Book.
- Driving out of signal before saving Offline Maps.
- Reading What can I see as truth in timber. Trees are not counted.
```

> [!field]
> At home: save My base area, turn on Private land, MU lines and Slope steepness, find one bench under 15° near a south face, drop a Glassing point waypoint, run What can I see from it, and check Insights there.

```checklist fn-huntmap-drill
Saved My base area offline and tested it in airplane mode
Turned on land, MU and closure layers and opened the colour guide
Found a bench with Slope steepness and Slope direction
Ran What can I see from a glassing point
Used Sun and shade at sunrise
Dropped and named three waypoints with icons
Measured a line and read its bearing
Recorded and saved a short track
```
