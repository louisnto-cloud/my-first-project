> [!why]
> Your drop table is only as good as your distance. A 50 m mistake at 300 m puts the bullet under the heart.

```diagram ff-jd-error
```

- Guess 250 m, deer at 300 m: about 20 cm low (75%).
- Laser first. Reticle second. Eye third.

```more the cost of a wrong guess
- Drops with a 100 m zero, 150 gr (grain) .308: 28 cm at 250 m, 48 cm at 300 m (75%, [ballistics deep dive](#/s/ballistics-deep/0)).
- Hold for 250 m on a deer at 300 m: about 20 cm low, the full height of the vital zone (75%).
- Inside 150 m, a 25 m mistake costs about 2 to 4 cm. That is why close shots forgive bad guesses (75%).
- Hunters estimate badly without practice, so a laser rangefinder is the primary tool and the reticle is the backup (70%, Vortex: reticle ranging "provides a good back up should your laser rangefinder fail").
```

## Three tools compared

| Tool | Good to | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Laser rangefinder | Hundreds of m on deer (65%) | Fast, exact, some give flat distance | Battery, brush, rain and fog |
| Scope reticle | About 400 m with practice (Tip) | Always with you | Needs known size and steady hold |
| Eye methods | To about 400 m (80%) | Free, silent | Easy to fool |

- Learn all three. Batteries die on the coldest day (Tip).

```more why the order matters
- Canadian Army fieldcraft lists other ways too: maps, pacing, the mil formula, range cards and rangefinders (80%).
- In the field: laser, then confirm with the reticle or the map if the reading looks odd (Tip).
- Your phone mapping app can measure distance between two points, a good check before or after (Tip). See [maps 2: compass, GPS and apps](#/s/maps-navigation/0).
```

## How a laser rangefinder works

```diagram ff-jd-laser
```

- It times a light pulse to the target and back (70%).
- Deer range is about half the box number (65%).

```more rangefinder specs explained
- The big number on the box is usually the reflective range, on a bright, flat target (65%).
- Example: Vortex Ranger 1800: reflective 9 to 1,800 yd, deer 9 to 900 yd, accuracy plus or minus 3 yd at 1,000 yd, angle reading to plus or minus 60 degrees, 6x22 (65%, retailer listing).
- Its HCD (horizontal component distance) mode shows the flat distance for your hold (65%).
- Rain, snow, fog and low light shorten range (Tip).
- Angle: see [ballistics deep dive](#/s/ballistics-deep/0) for why flat distance matters.
```

## Range it right, step by step

```steps Use a laser rangefinder in 4 steps
ff-jd-lrf-1 | Brace it like a rifle.
ff-jd-lrf-2 | Box on the shoulder, not the edge.
ff-jd-lrf-3 | Three readings. Ignore the one that jumps.
ff-jd-lrf-4 | Read the angle or flat distance.
```

- Range landmarks before the deer comes (Tip).

```more the steps in full
1. Brace the unit on a tree, pack or knee, two hands. A shaking box hits the brush beside the deer (Tip).
2. Put the aiming box on the deer's shoulder, mid body. Edges let the beam slip past onto the hill behind (Tip).
3. Range at least three times. Two that agree within a few metres are the answer (Tip).
4. Read the angle or the flat distance mode, and use that for your hold (65%).
- Before any deer shows, range three landmarks: a rock, a bush, a fence post. Write them down. When a deer stands near one, you already know (Tip).
- Quiet: rangefinders are silent, but your arm movement is not. Range landmarks early, move little when the deer is there (Tip).
```

## The brush trap

```anim ff-jd-brush A branch in the beam gives a short reading.
```

- A reading much shorter than you expect: branch or grass (Tip).
- Much longer: the beam missed and hit the hill (Tip).

```more how to beat it
- Many rangefinders have a "last target" or "distant target" mode that ignores near objects like brush. Check your manual (60%, VERIFY by model).
- Move a step left or right to find a clear gap (Tip).
- Range a solid object right beside the deer, like a tree at the same distance (Tip).
```

## Reticle ranging

```diagram ff-jd-formula
```

- Metres = size in metres × 1,000 ÷ mils (85%).
- Yards = size in inches × 95.5 ÷ MOA (minute of angle) (85%).

```more the formulas in full
- Vortex mil formula: "Known Width or Height of Target (Meters) × 1000 ÷ Target Width or Height (Measured on Reticle in MRADS) = Range (Meters)" (85%).
- Vortex MOA formula: size in inches × 95.5 ÷ MOA = range in yards. Using 100 instead of 95.5 overestimates by about 5% (85%).
- A steady hold matters: brace on a rest, bipod or sling when measuring (85%, Vortex).
- Second focal plane scopes: the marks are only true at one power, usually the highest. Check your manual (70%, [scope session](#/s/scope-guide/0)).
- Duplex reticles: the gap between the thick post tips covers a set number of MOA at a set power, which varies by model. VERIFY yours in the scope manual.
```

## Deer size for the formula

| Chest covers | Range for a 46 cm chest |
|---|---|
| 3 mils | 153 m |
| 2.5 mils | 184 m |
| 2 mils | 230 m |
| 1.5 mils | 307 m |

- The 46 cm (18 in) back to brisket figure is hunter lore. Tip only (55%).

```more why the deer size is the weak link
- Hunters often use 18 in (46 cm) from the top of the back to the bottom of the brisket for deer sized game (55%, forum consensus, not a measured source). VERIFY on deer you see harvested.
- A 2 in (5 cm) error in size at 500 yd gives about a 50 yd error (55%, forum).
- Real chest depth varies with species, sex, age and how the deer stands (Tip).
- Use it as a check on the laser, never alone for a long shot (opinion).
- Table: Hunt Mentor arithmetic, 0.46 × 1,000 ÷ mils (95% math, 55% for the size).
```

## Eye method 1: unit of measure

```diagram ff-jd-unit
```

- Picture 100 m, count how many fit (80%).
- Needs all the ground visible. Poor past 400 m (80%).

```more the method in full
- "A known accurate distance is visualized (e.g., a football field or the distance between two telephone poles), and that unit is applied repeatedly between the observer and the target until the range is determined" (80%, Canadian Army fieldcraft).
- You must see the ground between you and the target. "This method is not accurate beyond 400 metres" (80%).
- Build your unit: a Canadian football field is 110 yd (101 m) goal line to goal line (60%, VERIFY), close enough to 100 m. Any measured 100 m works better: walk one at the range (Tip).
```

## Eye method 2: appearance

```diagram ff-jd-appearance
```

- Learn what a deer looks like at 100, 200, 300 m (80%).
- These pictures are tips. Build your own with a laser (Tip).

```more the method in full
- The appearance method compares how an object looks at 100 m and farther. As distance grows, it gets smaller, the outline blurs and details fade (80%, Canadian Army fieldcraft).
- The Army's guide is for a person: at 200 m all parts of the body are distinct, at 300 m the face blurs, at 400 m the face is hard to see, at 500 m the body tapers, at 600 m the head is a dot (80%).
- For deer, the panel above is a Hunt Mentor tip, not a source. Range every deer you see this fall and say the detail you see out loud (Tip).
```

## Tricks the eye plays

```diagram ff-jd-illusions
```

- Bright light, snow, water, clear air: looks nearer (80%).
- Fog, dusk, part showing, lying down: looks farther (80%).

```more the full lists
Objects seem nearer (80%, Canadian Army fieldcraft):
- in bright light or with the sun behind you
- when the colour contrasts sharply with the background
- looking over water, snow or a uniform surface
- in the clear air of high altitudes
- with dead ground (a hidden dip) between you and it
- when bigger than things around it

Objects seem farther (80%):
- looking over a depression you can fully see
- in poor light, fog, or with the sun in your eyes
- when only a small part shows
- looking down a street or tree lined road
- when it blends in with the background
- when smaller than things around it
- when you are lying down

- Field meaning: a deer across a snowy cutblock on a bright morning is farther than it looks. A deer in a shady draw at dusk may be closer (Tip).
```

## Eye method 3: halving and bracketing

```diagram ff-jd-halving
```

- Halving: judge to halfway, then double (80%).
- Bracketing: "more than X, less than Y", call the middle (80%).

```more aids in full
- Halving: "A point is chosen halfway to the target and the distance is estimated to the point and doubled" (80%, Canadian Army fieldcraft).
- Bracketing: add the near and far limits and halve the sum (80%).
- Key ranges: know the range to one landmark, judge others from it (80%).
- Unit average: you and your brother both guess, take the average (80%).
```

## Pacing and maps

```diagram ff-jd-pace
```

- Know your pace count for 100 m (80%).
- Map measure before the hunt (Tip).

```more pacing and maps in full
- Pacing and maps are on the Army's list of other methods (80%).
- Pace count: walk a measured 100 m three times, counting each left foot, and average (Tip). Uphill, brush and snow shorten your pace (Tip).
- After a shot, pace to the spot where the deer stood. It tells you the true range and trains your eye (Tip).
- Map: measure from your glassing point to the far edge of a cutblock before the hunt. Then you know the "edge is 280 m" before any deer steps out (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Ranging brush in front of the deer | Box on the body, three readings |
| Trusting a snowy, bright guess | Bright and snowy look nearer |
| Reticle ranging at the wrong power | Use the power the manual names |

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Range it twice. Shoot once.

```more the mistakes in full
- Ranging the branch in front of the deer and holding for 60 m on a 200 m animal.
- Guessing on snow or across water, where everything looks closer.
- Reading mils on a second focal plane scope at the wrong power.
```

> [!field]
> Practice game on every scouting walk: guess a distance out loud, then range it. Log it. Within a month your guesses close to 10% (Tip).

```checklist ff-jd-practice
Guess 10 objects, then laser them
Range 3 landmarks at every sit
Pace one known 100 m on new ground
Check the reticle formula on a fence post
Log guesses and errors in the journal
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram ff-jd-hooks
```

- **"Brace, box, three."** Rest it, box on the body, three readings.
- **"Bright and white, near in sight."** Sun and snow trick you closer.
- **"Thousand over mils."** Size in metres × 1,000 ÷ mils.

```more more memory tricks
- **"Half, then double."** Halving.
- **"Four hundred, eyes are done."** Eye methods fade past 400 m.
- **"Short means brush, long means hill."** A wild laser reading.
```
