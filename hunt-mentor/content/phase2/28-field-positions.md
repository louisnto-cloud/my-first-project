> [!why]
> Deer never stand in front of a bench. The steadier you get in grass, sage and timber, the farther you can ethically shoot.

## Key points

```diagram ff-ps-bone
```

- Bone support, not muscle (Tip).
- Find your natural point of aim, then shoot (80%).
- Pad between rifle and rest. Never the bare barrel on rock (85%).

```more all key points
- Support the rifle with stacked bones and the ground, then relax the muscles (Tip).
- Natural point of aim (NPOA): "the point where the barrel naturally orients when the shooter's muscles are relaxed and support is achieved" (80%, US Army TC 3-22.9 via American Rifleman).
- Hunter education: the safest and most accurate field shots come from a stable rest like a log or rock, but "Don't rest the barrel directly on a hard surface, or the rifle will fire higher than normal." Pad it with a hat or jacket (85%).
- Get into position in under 5 seconds with practice (Tip).
- Practise from the clothes and pack you hunt in (Tip).
- Drills beat bench groups for hunting skill (opinion).
```

## Natural point of aim, step by step

```steps Find your natural point of aim in 4 steps
ff-ps-npoa-1 | Settle in with the crosshair on target.
ff-ps-npoa-2 | Breathe, let every muscle go.
ff-ps-npoa-3 | Look: where did the crosshair settle?
ff-ps-npoa-4 | Move your body around a pivot, not the rifle.
```

- If you hold the crosshair on with muscle, the shot drifts (80%).
- Groups that string left and right can mean a bad NPOA (70%).

```more the method in full
- American Rifleman (Matt Robertson, January 2026): pick a fixed pivot, like a foot or an elbow supported on something, and move the rest of your body around that point to adjust your NPOA (80%).
- Test: crosshair on target, relax slightly, a small "shimmy" of the body. "If they settle back on the target, you're good to go!" (80%)
- Keep your eyes open during the test. Instructor John Simpson advises against closing them (75%, American Rifleman).
- Prone: shift your hips left or right to swing the rifle. Sitting: shuffle your seat. Sticks: move your feet (Tip).
- A group that drifts left to right can point to a bad NPOA (60%, search preview of NPA articles).
```

## Prone off a pack

```diagram ff-ps-pack
```

- Pack under the forend, not under the barrel (85%).
- Rear hand fine tunes the height (Tip).

```more prone with a pack in full
- Lie in line with the rifle, slightly angled, both elbows down (85%, hunter education on prone).
- Pack under the forend. Push it into the pack slightly for a solid feel (Tip).
- The rear hand makes a fist, or squeezes a sock of sand, under the butt to raise or lower the aim (Tip).
- Grass and sage often block a prone view at deer height. Check the bullet path, not only the scope view: the barrel sits about 4 cm below the scope (Tip).
- Hard rests shoot high, soft rests low. Learn yours on paper (70%, from the [shooting skills](#/s/shooting-skills/0) session).
```

## Bipod

```diagram ff-ps-bipod
```

- Lean gently forward to load the legs (Tip).
- Same pressure every shot (Tip).

```more bipod in full
- A rifle mounted bipod works prone and sitting, or braced against a tree (70%, shooting skills session).
- Tikka's front sling swivel stud can take a bipod (95%, Tikka manual).
- Loading: push your shoulder gently forward so the legs press into the ground. The rifle stops hopping (Tip).
- Bipod on hard rock shoots differently from soft dirt. Practise both (Tip).
- Bipods add weight on the hill. Many hunters carry sticks and use the pack as a prone rest instead (opinion).
```

## Three ways to sit

```diagram ff-ps-sit-types
```

- Crossed ankle: lowest and steadiest (Tip).
- Open leg: fits slopes (Tip).
- Sticks: over grass (70%).

```more sitting in full
- Sitting is the next steadiest after prone (85%, hunter education).
- Elbows go inside the knees, flat of the arm against the soft knee, not the point of the elbow on the kneecap (Tip, shooting skills session).
- **Crossed ankle:** legs crossed at the ankles, body leaning forward. Low, very steady, slow to get into (Tip).
- **Open leg:** feet apart, knees up. Quick, works on uneven ground (Tip).
- **With sticks:** tripod or bipod sticks in front, elbows on knees. Clears grass and sage (70%).
- Bring the position to the ground: on a slope, face downhill and sitting becomes almost a bench (Tip).
```

## Kneeling and standing with support

```diagram sk-sticks
```

- Kneeling clears low brush, one arm braced (85%).
- Standing on a tripod makes a hard shot workable (70%).

```more kneeling and standing in full
- Kneeling: sit on the heel of your rear foot, support elbow on the raised knee. Less steady than sitting (85%).
- Standing with three leg sticks: two legs toward you, one toward the deer, top about armpit height, feet nearly square (70%, Richard Mann, Field and Stream).
- Rest the back of your hand on the sticks, hold the forend as normal (70%).
- Offhand: only when the deer is close and there is no time to sit (Tip).
```

## Trees and rocks

```diagram ff-ps-tree
```

- Hand or glove between rifle and bark (85%).
- Hard surface under the barrel: shoots high (85%).

```more trees and rocks in full
- Hunter education: "Don't rest the barrel directly on a hard surface, or the rifle will fire higher than normal." Pad it (85%).
- Against a tree: press the side of your support hand against the trunk, rifle in that hand (Tip).
- On a rock or log: hat, glove or pack between the forend and the rock (85%).
- Sit beside a tree, not in front of it. The trunk breaks your outline and gives a side rest (Tip).
```

## Slopes: uphill and downhill

```diagram ff-ps-slope
```

- Downhill: sit facing down, heels dug in (Tip).
- Uphill: prone or kneel with the pack (Tip).

```more slope positions in full
- Heffley Creek and Okanagan side hills give lots of angled shots (Assumption, based on the owner's areas being hilly).
- Downhill sitting: heels dug in, elbows inside knees. The slope raises your knees to the right height (Tip).
- Steep uphill: prone becomes hard as the muzzle points up. Kneel, or sit with sticks (Tip).
- Recoil on a steep slope can push you downhill. Brace your feet (Tip).
- Hold correction for angles: [ballistics deep dive](#/s/ballistics-deep/0).
```

## The hasty sling

```diagram ff-ps-sling
```

- Sling around the support arm pulls the rifle tight (Tip).
- Practise it dry before you trust it (Tip).

```more sling in full
- This is a common rifleman's technique, shown here as a tip: Hunt Mentor has no hunter education source for it this session (55%). VERIFY with a range officer or instructor.
- Hasty sling: support arm goes between sling and rifle, sling wraps the upper arm, hand forward on the forend. The sling tension pulls the butt into the shoulder (Tip).
- Works sitting and kneeling. Changes your zero slightly for some rifles: test on paper (Tip).
- Carrying with the sling: [safe carries](#/s/firearm-safety/0).
```

## From walking to steady

```anim ff-ps-get-down Stop, say it, sit, settle, safety off.
```

- Say the position out loud. Then do it (Tip).
- Safety off only when the crosshair is settled on the deer (95%).

```more speed drill in full
1. Stop. Find the deer again with your eyes or binoculars.
2. Say it: "Sitting. Sticks." It stops the panic offhand shot (Tip).
3. Sticks out, sit down, elbows in.
4. Rifle in, crosshair on, quick NPOA check.
5. Safety off when you are ready to shoot, finger to the trigger last (95%, Tikka manual: keep the safety on until ready to fire).
- Dry practice target: under 5 seconds, smooth, no fumbling (Tip).
- With your brother: agree who shoots and who watches before any stalk. See [two armed hunters](#/s/firearm-safety/0).
```

## Field drills

```diagram ff-ps-drill-target
```

- Three drills, 15 rounds, one range day (Tip).
- First shot of the day is the one that counts (Tip).

```more the drills in full
| Drill | How | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| Cold plate | First shot of the day, from sticks or sitting, at your hunting distance | Hit the 20 cm plate |
| Position ladder | 3 shots prone, 3 sitting, 3 sticks at 100 m | All in 10 cm per position |
| Timed get down | From standing, rifle slung, to sticks: 1 shot, 20 seconds | 20 cm plate |
| Walk and shoot | Walk 30 m, breathe hard, get down, 2 shots | 20 cm plate at 100 m |
| Uphill and downhill | Find any range bay with a slope, 3 shots each | Note where they land |

- All drills: Hunt Mentor opinion, built on hunter education positions (opinion).
- Live fire only at a range or where shooting is lawful and safe (95%).
- Ammunition: about 15 rounds a session (Tip). Use the [.22 as a cheap trainer](#/s/rimfire-22/0) for most of the reps.
- Max range test: see [shooting skills](#/s/shooting-skills/0).
```

```checklist ff-ps-drills
Cold plate shot from sticks
Position ladder at 100 m
Timed get down under 20 seconds
Walk and shoot, breathing hard
One angled shot if the range allows
Write results in the journal
```

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Muscling the crosshair on | Find NPOA, move the body |
| Barrel on a rock | Pad under the forend |
| Offhand when sitting was possible | Say the position out loud |

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): If you can get lower, get lower. If you can get closer, get closer.

```more the mistakes in full
- Holding the crosshair on target with tense muscles. The shot goes where your body wants, not where you pointed.
- Laying the barrel on a rock or a tree branch, so the shot flies high.
- Firing offhand when sitting was one second away.
```

> [!field]
> Each evening this week: 10 dry get downs to sticks with a snap cap, timer on. Under 5 seconds and the crosshair still settled.
