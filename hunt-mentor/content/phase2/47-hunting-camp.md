> [!why]
> A good camp means real sleep, dry gear and no bear visits. A bad one means a cold night, a tired hunter and a bear that learned where people keep food.

## Key points

```diagram fl-camp-layout
```

- Tent upwind, kitchen and food downwind (Tip).
- Food in a vehicle, locker, or hung 4 m up and 3 m out (75 to 85%).
- Campfire: 0.5 m by 0.5 m max, 8 L water or a hand tool (95%).

```more the key points in full
- WildSafeBC: in undeveloped areas, set tent, cooking area and food storage about 100 m apart in a triangle (85%, WildSafeBC Camping in Bear Country brochure, 2018).
- Where possible, hang food at least 4 m off the ground and 3 m from the nearest tree (75%, same brochure, text partly damaged in the PDF).
- With no lockers, keep food in the trunk of a vehicle or a hard sided RV (85%).
- "Never take food with you into your tent, not even a small snack" (85%).
- "Cook in clothes other than those you will sleep in" (85%).
- Carcasses are attractants in law. Leaving one where people are likely to be and where dangerous wildlife can reach it can be fined up to $50,000 (99%, synopsis page 15).
- A Category 1 campfire is no larger than 0.5 m high by 0.5 m wide, with at least 8 L of water or a hand tool on hand and a fuel break around it (95%, BC Wildfire Service).
- Never run a fuel burning heater or stove in a tent (80%, Technical Safety BC and Vancouver Coastal Health).
```

## Pick the site

```diagram fl-camp-site
```

- Look up for dead trees before you pitch (Tip).
- Not on a trail, not by a creek animals travel (85%).

```more site choice in full
- Inspect the area for bear sign: scat, claw marks, fresh digging, tracks (85%, WildSafeBC).
- Camp away from waterways or other features that may be travel routes for wildlife (85%, WildSafeBC).
- Bears have long memories: a site that gave a bear food before may get a visit again (85%, WildSafeBC).
- Dead standing trees and hanging limbs fall in wind and snow. Never pitch under them (Tip).
- Cold air sinks into hollows and valley bottoms at night (75%, same physics as [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals/0)). A bench a little above the bottom is warmer (Tip).
- Flat, high and dry: check where rain water would run (Tip).
- Respect private land and closed areas: [Maps 3: land status](#/s/maps-land-status/0).
```

## The bear triangle

```anim fl-camp-scent Smell drifts downwind. Keep the tent upwind.
```

- Tent upwind of the kitchen and food (Tip).
- Kitchen and food about 100 m from the tent (85%).

```more the triangle in full
- Tent, cooking area and food storage about 100 m apart, in a triangle (85%, WildSafeBC).
- Sleeping area upwind of the kitchen and food storage, at least 100 m apart (65%, Princeton Outdoor Action, citing Gary Brown, Safe Travel in Bear Country).
- Wind shifts. Pick the most common night wind for the spot; valleys often flow downhill at night (75%, see [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals/0)).
- At a truck camp, a 100 m triangle may not fit. Then: food locked in the truck, cook downwind, clean up right after (Tip and 85% for the truck).
```

## Where the food goes

```diagram fl-camp-storage
```

- Lockers first, then the vehicle, then a hang (85%).
- Never in the tent. Not a snack, not toothpaste (85%).

```more storage in full
- Use bear resistant storage where it is provided (85%, WildSafeBC).
- None provided: trunk of a vehicle or a hard sided RV (85%).
- Backcountry: hang food at least 4 m up and 3 m from the tree (75%).
- Other guides give 3 to 5 m up, 2 to 3 m from the trunk and 1 m below the branch (60%, Girl Guides of Canada BC camping sheet). Use the bigger numbers when you can (Tip).
- Bear resistant containers: an option where trees are poor (Tip). Price tiers: VERIFY in store.
- Attractants are more than food: soaps, toothpaste, dish rags, pots, grey water, garbage, fuel containers, pet food (85%, WildSafeBC list).
- Keep camp free of attractants whenever you leave it, even for a short walk (85%).
```

## Hang food in 4 steps

```steps Hang a food bag
fl-hang-1 | Pick a high branch, far from the trunk.
fl-hang-2 | Rock in a bag, throw the line over.
fl-hang-3 | Tie on the food bag, haul it up.
fl-hang-4 | Tie off on a second tree.
```

- Practise at home before you need it in the dark (Tip).

```more hanging tips
- Throw from the side, never stand under the rock (Tip).
- Use strong cord; 15 m of rope is already in your pack list (Tip, [Day pack and car kit](#/s/pack-kit/0)).
- Hang before dark. Finding a branch at night is hard (Tip).
- Black bears climb. Distance from the trunk matters as much as height (Tip).
```

## Meat in camp

```diagram fl-camp-meat
```

- Hang meat in game bags, in shade, well away from tents (Tip).
- Carcasses and parts are attractants in law (99%).

> [!warn] Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

```more meat and the law in full
- "Carcasses or part of a carcass of an animal or fish are included in the Wildlife Act definition of an attractant" (99%, synopsis page 15, Disposal of Carcasses or Wildlife Parts).
- It is prohibited to leave an attractant where people are or are likely to be, in a way that could attract dangerous wildlife and be accessible to it, except for hunting under the Act. First conviction: fine up to $50,000 and/or up to 6 months in jail (99%, synopsis page 15).
- It is unlawful to intentionally feed or attempt to feed dangerous wildlife (cougar, coyote, wolf and bear), except where baiting is authorized for cougar, coyote and wolf (99%, synopsis page 11, item 20).
- Gut piles: leave them away from camp, trails and roads (Tip, plus the attractant rule above).
- Meat care temperatures and cooling: [Meat care](#/s/meat-care/0).
- Where you can see the meat from camp, you notice a bear early. Approach meat with noise and the wind in your face (70%, see [Packing out](#/s/packing-out/0)).
```

## Clothes, garbage and grey water

| Do | Why |
|---|---|
| Cook in other clothes, store them with the food | Smell on clothes (85%) |
| Pack out all garbage | Synopsis ethics and item 9 (99%) |
| Grey water in a designated spot or far from camp | Attractant (85%) |
| Wash up right after eating | Smell gone sooner (Tip) |

- "Respect the environment by packing out everything you pack in" (99%).

```more the rules and lists in full
- "Cook in clothes other than those you will sleep in" (85%, WildSafeBC).
- Dispose of grey water in the designated area (85%, WildSafeBC campground guidance). Backcountry: scatter it well away from camp and water (Tip).
- It is unlawful "to dump, discard or dispose of litter" (99%, synopsis page 11, item 9).
- Synopsis ethics code: "Respect the environment by packing out everything you pack in!" (99%, page 77).
- Toilet: well away from water, camp and trails, and bury or pack out waste (Tip; Leave No Trace distances: VERIFY, their site was not readable here).
```

## Campfire rules

> [!warn] Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

```diagram fl-campfire
```

- Check for fire bans before every trip (95%).
- 0.5 m by 0.5 m, 8 L water or a hand tool, fuel break, never alone (95%).

```more the campfire rules in full
- Category 1 campfire: "No larger than 0.5 metres high by 0.5 metres wide" (95%, BC Wildfire Service open burning page).
- Have "at least one person watching the fire" with "at least one firefighting hand tool or 8 litres of water" (95%).
- "Establish and maintain a fuel break around the category 1 campfire" (95%).
- "Ensure the fire is fully extinguished before leaving the area" (95%). Tip: drown, stir, drown, then feel it cold.
- "Never leave your campfire unattended for any amount of time" (95%, listed as a safer burning tip).
- During a Category 1 prohibition, only CSA (Canadian Standards Association) or ULC (Underwriters Laboratories of Canada) approved stoves using briquettes, liquid or gas fuel, with flames no taller than 15 cm, and these may also be banned in extreme conditions (95%).
- Illegal open fire on Crown land: report to RAPP (Report All Poachers and Polluters) 1 877 952 7277 (95%).
- Bans change fast in a dry fall. Check BC Wildfire Service the day you leave (Tip, see [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0)).
- Lighting a fire in rain and snow: [Fire in wet weather](#/s/fire-wet-weather/0).
```

## Sleeping bag ratings

```diagram fl-sleep-ratings
```

- Comfort: sleep relaxed. Limit: curled up for 8 hours (70%).
- Extreme is survival only. Never buy for it (70%).

```more the ratings in full
- Bags tested to ISO 23537 (formerly EN 13537) can carry up to three numbers (70%, Australian Hiker and Rab guides).
- Comfort: a standard woman sleeps comfortably, relaxed. Men are usually comfortable a little lower (70%).
- Limit (lower limit): a standard man sleeps 8 hours curled up without waking (70%).
- Extreme: survival only, strong cold and a risk of hypothermia (70%).
- Tests assume a base layer, a toque and an insulated pad (70%).
- Kamloops in November: average low about minus 1 °C, and colder nights happen; the coldest November reading from 1991 to 2020 was minus 24.9 °C (95%, Environment Canada normals, Kamloops A). Hunting camps are usually higher and colder than the airport (Tip).
- My lean: a bag with a Comfort rating near minus 7 °C for October and November camps, plus layers for colder nights (Tip, opinion).
```

## Pads: the ground steals heat

```diagram fl-sleep-rvalue
```

- R value is how well a pad blocks the cold ground (75%).
- Stack a foam pad under an air pad: the R values add (75%).

```more pads in full
- The ASTM F3340 test standard (ASTM, the American Society for Testing and Materials) lets you add the R values of stacked pads (75%, NEMO Equipment).
- Example: a foam pad of R 2 under an air pad of R 4 gives about R 6 (Tip, example numbers).
- For winter, the pad maker Cascade Designs suggests R 4 to 6 (65%, secondary summary).
- A foam pad never goes flat. An air pad can pop. Carry both in cold weather (Tip).
- A cot needs a pad too: cold air under the cot chills you (Tip).
```

## Sleep warm: 5 tricks

```steps Five ways to a warm night
fl-sleep-1 | Eat a warm meal before bed.
fl-sleep-2 | Pee before you zip up.
fl-sleep-3 | Dry base layers, dry socks, toque.
fl-sleep-4 | Vent the tent a little.
fl-sleep-5 | Hot water bottle in a sock.
```

- Wet clothes steal heat. Change into dry ones at night (Tip).

```more the tricks in full
- Food is fuel. A meal with fat and protein before bed helps (Tip, see [Food and water](#/s/hunt-food-water/0)).
- A full bladder makes you colder and wakes you (Tip).
- Sweaty clothes steal heat; sleep in a dry base layer and a toque (Tip, see [What to wear](#/s/what-to-wear/0)).
- Breath carries moisture. A cracked vent cuts frost inside the tent (Tip).
- Hot water bottle: lid tight, in a sock, never boiling into a soft bottle (Tip).
- Signs of hypothermia and what to do: [Hypothermia, frostbite and cold weather](#/s/cold-injuries/0).
```

## Carbon monoxide: no heaters in the tent

```diagram fl-camp-co
```

> [!warn]
> Never run a stove, heater, lantern or generator inside a tent or a closed truck cab. Opening the flap is not enough (80%).

```more carbon monoxide in full
- CO (carbon monoxide) comes from fuel burning appliances: portable stoves and heaters, barbecues, firepits, lanterns, generators (80%, Technical Safety BC and Vancouver Coastal Health).
- They should never go in enclosed spaces such as tents and RVs (80%, same).
- Opening tent flaps or doors is not enough to prevent dangerous build up (80%, US CDC (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention)).
- Symptoms: headache, dizziness, breathlessness, nausea, tiredness, confusion; easy to mistake for flu (80%).
- Suspect it: turn appliances off, get everyone outside, call 911 (80%).
- Wall tents with stove jacks are a different set up with a proper flue. If you ever use one, learn it from someone who does; VERIFY the maker's instructions (Tip).
```

## Firearms in camp

- Unloaded and cased in camp and at night (Tip).
- Never loaded in or on a vehicle (99%).
- Ammunition stored apart from the firearms when you can (Tip).

```diagram gr-agree
```

```more firearms in camp in full
- Camp is where people relax. That is when accidents happen: unload before you walk into camp (Tip, see [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety/0)).
- A loaded firearm in or on any vehicle is unlawful (99%, synopsis page 11, item 28).
- Synopsis ethics code: "Do not consume drugs or alcohol while hunting or trapping" (99%, page 77).
- Grandpa's rule from the app: the whisky stays in camp until the guns are cased (Tip, from [Grandpa's 50 rules](#/s/grandpas-rules/0)).
```

## Camp checklist

```checklist fl-camp-list
Fire ban checked the day I left
Site: no dead trees overhead, not on a trail, back from the creek
Tent upwind, kitchen and food downwind
Food and smelly items locked in the truck or hung 4 m up, 3 m out
Cooking clothes stored with the food
8 L of water or a shovel by the fire
Fire out cold before bed and before leaving
No heater or stove inside the tent
Sleeping bag rated for the nights, foam pad plus air pad
Dry base layer, dry socks and a toque for sleep
Meat hung in game bags, in shade, away from tents
Firearms unloaded and cased
Garbage packed, nothing left behind
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fl-camp-mistakes
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): A clean camp is a quiet camp. Feed the bear once and it comes back for the next hunter.

> [!mistake]
> - Food, snacks or toothpaste in the tent.
> - Cooking where you sleep.
> - A heater or stove inside the tent.

```more the mistakes in full
- A snack in the tent: a bear may smell it and investigate (85%, WildSafeBC).
- Cooking beside the tent: cook about 100 m away, in other clothes (85%).
- Heater in the tent: carbon monoxide builds up even with the flap open (80%).
- Bonus: leaving the fire to "die down" while you go glass the evening. Never unattended (95%).
```

> Next scouting trip, pitch your camp for one night as if it were the hunt: triangle, hang, fire rules, sleep system. Write in the journal what was cold, wet or awkward, and fix it at home.
