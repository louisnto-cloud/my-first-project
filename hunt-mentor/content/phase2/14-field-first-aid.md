> [!why]
> A rifle, a knife and steep ground: your partner is the ambulance for the first hour. What you do in the first 3 minutes decides the rest.

```diagram fs-fa-priorities
```

- Big bleeding kills in minutes. Everything else can wait a little (Tip).
- This session: bleeding, wounds, sprains, breaks, shock and the trauma kit.

```more what this session covers, and what it is not
- Order of care, the bleeding control steps (press, pack, tourniquet), cleaning cuts, shock, sprains, splints, and the kit you carry.
- The short version lives in [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0) and [Pack and car kit](#/s/pack-kit/0). This session is the deep dive.
- Cold injuries are next: [Hypothermia, frostbite and cold weather](#/s/cold-injuries/0).
- This is a study aid, not a first aid course. Take a hands on course with tourniquet practice before the season (Tip). A kit you have never used is just weight.
- Assumption: you hunt with your brother, often out of cell range, an hour or more from an ambulance. That is why the partner is the first responder.
```

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. Not a substitute for a certified first aid course.

## Key points

```diagram fs-fa-three-injuries
```

- Scene safe, call, then stop the bleeding (75%).
- Press, pack, tourniquet: in that order for most wounds (75%).

```more the 8 key points in full
1. Make the scene safe first: unload every gun, look for the hazard (a bear on a carcass, a loose rock) (Tip).
2. Call early: 911 on any phone, or SOS on a satellite device. 911 can connect through any carrier's tower in range (75%, BC Search and Rescue Association).
3. Life threatening bleeding comes before everything else except your own safety (75%, Canadian Red Cross bleeding sheet).
4. Firm direct pressure stops most bleeding (75%).
5. Deep wounds in the groin, armpit or neck, where a tourniquet cannot go, get packed with gauze (70%, Stop the Bleed and military training cards).
6. A limb bleed that pressure cannot stop gets a [[Tourniquet]] (75%).
7. Flush cuts with at least 1 litre of drinkable water (75%, Wilderness Medical Society).
8. Keep every injured person warm: shock and cold make each other worse (75%).
```

## Check, Call, Care

```steps The first minute
fs-fa-ccc-1 | Check: unload guns, find the danger.
fs-fa-ccc-2 | Call: 911 or SOS, location first.
fs-fa-ccc-3 | Care: stop big bleeding first.
fs-fa-ccc-4 | Keep warm and write the times.
```

- Your own safety first. A second casualty helps no one (Tip).

```more Check, Call, Care in full
- **Check** (Tip): stop. Unload your gun and the injured person's gun. Look for what caused this: a slope, a bear near a carcass, a loose rock, ice. Then go.
- **Call** (75%): 911 on any phone. BC Search and Rescue Association: a phone can reach 911 through any tower in range, even another carrier's. Turn on location services so you can read out your coordinates.
- No signal: satellite SOS (iPhone 14 or later in Canada, or a satellite messenger). See [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0) (85%).
- Say your **location first**, then what happened, how many hurt, and what you have done. If the call drops, they still know where to send help (Tip).
- **Care:** life threatening bleeding first, then breathing, then everything else (75%).
- **Write the times:** injury, tourniquet on, pills given, changes. Medics need them (75%, Canadian Red Cross skills sheet: document tourniquet times).
- Phone in an inside pocket: cold kills phone batteries fast (70%, see [Lost](#/s/lost-survival-kit/0)).
```

## Is the bleeding life threatening?

```diagram fs-fa-bleed-signs
```

- Any one sign: act now. Do not wait to see if it slows (Tip).

```more the signs in full
- Life threatening bleeding signs taught in bleeding control courses (70%, Stop the Bleed course material via university and fire department pages):
  - Blood **spurting** or pulsing out.
  - Blood **flowing** that will not slow with pressure.
  - Blood **pooling** on the ground.
  - Clothing or bandages **soaking** through.
  - A partly or fully **missing** arm or leg.
  - The person is **confused**, pale or weak: they have already lost a lot.
- An adult has about 5 litres of blood (70%). Losing a big share of it fast is what kills (Tip: no exact number is needed in the field. If it looks like a lot, it is a lot).
- A rifle wound to the thigh or upper arm can hit a big artery. These are the wounds a tourniquet was made for (70%).
```

## Press and pack

```steps Stop bleeding with your hands
fs-fa-press-1 | Expose: cut clothing, find the source.
fs-fa-press-2 | Press hard with both hands, arms locked.
fs-fa-press-3 | Deep wound in groin or armpit? Pack it.
fs-fa-press-4 | Press on top 3 minutes. Do not peek.
```

- Gloves on if you have them. No gloves? Do not wait (Tip).

```more pressure and packing in full
- **Expose** the wound. Cut or pull clothing away so you press on the real source, not on a jacket (70%).
- **Press:** gauze or any clean cloth on the wound, both hands, arms straight, body weight over it (75%, Canadian Red Cross skills sheet step 1: direct pressure).
- Still bleeding through? Add a pressure bandage over the first layer. Do not lift the first layer (75%, Canadian Red Cross step 2).
- **Pack** (70%): for a deep wound where a tourniquet cannot go (groin, armpit, side of the neck), push gauze into the wound with one finger, following it to the bottom, until the hole is full.
- Training cards say fill the cavity so gauze stands 2.5 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) above the skin, then press on top for **at least 3 minutes** (65%, military training cards via search).
- Do **not** pack wounds of the chest or belly (65%).
- Haemostatic gauze (gauze with a clotting agent) can work faster. Plain gauze or a clean cloth still works (65%).
- After 3 minutes: wrap tight with a pressure bandage. If blood soaks through plain gauze, add more gauze and press again (65%).
```

## The tourniquet, step by step

```steps Tourniquet in 5 steps
fs-fa-tq-1 | Slide it on above the wound, heart side.
fs-fa-tq-2 | Pull the strap tight. No slack.
fs-fa-tq-3 | Twist the rod until bleeding stops.
fs-fa-tq-4 | Lock the rod in its clip.
fs-fa-tq-5 | Write TQ and the time. Leave it on.
```

```anim fs-fa-windlass Twist until the bleeding stops, then lock it.
```

- It will hurt. That means it is working (70%).

```more the tourniquet steps in full
- When: life threatening bleeding from an arm or leg that direct pressure cannot control (75%, Canadian Red Cross and American Red Cross).
- Where: on the limb, between the wound and the heart, **not on a joint**. If the wound is close to a joint, go above the joint (70%, Stop the Bleed guidance from fire department pages).
- How far above: see the next screen. The sources differ.
- Pull the strap as tight as you can **before** twisting. A loose strap means you run out of turns (70%).
- Twist the windlass rod until the bleeding stops, then lock it in the clip so it cannot unwind (70%, American Red Cross search preview).
- Still bleeding with the first one as tight as it goes? Put a **second** tourniquet above the first, closer to the heart (70%, American Red Cross search preview).
- Write the time it went on. Medical staff need it (75%).
- Do not loosen or remove it yourself. Only medical staff do that (70%, American Red Cross search preview).
- Note: an older Canadian Red Cross skills sheet (2012) says to slowly release after 10 minutes if bleeding has dropped a lot. Newer American Red Cross and Stop the Bleed material says leave it on. VERIFY with your course instructor.
- Buy a real one: a CAT (Combat Application Tourniquet) or SOFTT (Special Operations Forces Tactical Tourniquet) style windlass tourniquet. Fakes exist (60%, see [Pack and car kit](#/s/pack-kit/0)).
```

## How far above the wound?

```diagram fs-fa-tq-distance
```

- Two numbers in our sources: 5 to 8 cm, and 5 to 10 cm. VERIFY.
- Both agree: above the wound, heart side, never on a joint (75%).

```more the two numbers and where they come from
- **5 to 8 cm (2 to 3 in):** American Red Cross (search preview) and several Stop the Bleed guides (70%). Also used in [Pack and car kit](#/s/pack-kit/0).
- **5 to 10 cm (2 to 4 in):** Canadian Red Cross workplace skills sheet, Controlling External Bleeding, 2012 (75%, read directly). Also used in [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0).
- This is an open question for the owner. Ask your first aid instructor which number they teach and use that one. VERIFY.
- Some military guidance teaches "high and tight" (as high on the limb as it goes) when you cannot see the wound well. One vendor blog says so; not confirmed in an official source here. VERIFY.
```

## Tourniquet: do and do not

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Strap tight before twisting | Leave slack |
| Above the wound, off joints | Put it on the knee or elbow |
| Second one above if needed | Loosen it to check |
| Write the time | Hide it under a jacket |
| Buy a known brand | Buy a cheap copy |

```more why each line matters
- Slack in the strap uses up the windlass turns before the artery closes (70%).
- A joint has bone in the way; the band cannot squeeze the artery (65%).
- A second tourniquet is the standard fix when one is not enough (70%, American Red Cross search preview).
- Loosening it lets blood flow out again and can wash out a clot (Tip).
- A tourniquet under a jacket gets missed at the hospital handover. Leave it visible, write TQ and the time (Tip).
- Counterfeit tourniquets can snap. Buy from a known medical seller (60%).
```

## Shock

```diagram fs-fa-shock
```

- Lay them flat, keep them warm, nothing to eat or drink (70%).

```more shock in full
- Shock here means the body cannot get enough blood to the organs, often after bleeding (70%).
- Signs: pale, cold, clammy skin; fast weak pulse; thirst; anxiety; confusion (70%).
- Care (70%):
  - Stop the bleeding first. That is the real treatment.
  - Lay the person flat on a pad: a pack, foam, spruce boughs. The cold ground steals heat.
  - Cover them: emergency blanket, jacket, toque.
  - Nothing to eat or drink while they may need surgery, and if they are not fully awake (Tip).
  - Talk to them. Keep them still and calm.
- Shock and hypothermia feed each other. A hurt hunter lying on frozen October ground gets cold fast (Tip). See [cold injuries](#/s/cold-injuries/0).
```

## Cuts while field dressing

```diagram fs-fa-knife-safe
```

- Most knife injuries hit fingers and thumbs (70%).
- Cut away from you. Wipe the handle (Tip).

```more knife cut prevention in full
- In a United States study of emergency department knife injuries (1990 to 2008), fingers and thumbs were hurt in about 66% of cases, and 94% were cuts (70%, PubMed abstract via search). Not hunters only.
- A bloody, wet handle lets the hand slide onto the edge (60%, patent filing describing the problem).
- Habits (Tip):
  - Cut away from your body and away from your partner's hands.
  - Keep the hand that holds the hide behind the blade.
  - Use a sharp knife. A dull one needs force and slips.
  - Wipe the handle and your glove often.
  - Work in daylight. Headlamp gutting doubles the risk.
  - Wear thin nitrile gloves under a cut resistant glove on the holding hand if you own one.
- See [Field dressing](#/s/field-dressing/0) for the cuts themselves.
```

## Clean a cut

```steps Clean a cut in 3 steps
fs-fa-clean-1 | Stop the bleeding first.
fs-fa-clean-2 | Flush with at least 1 litre of water.
fs-fa-clean-3 | Cover it. Check daily for infection.
```

- Drinkable water works. No soap or iodine in the wound needed (75%).

```more the Wilderness Medical Society advice
- Wilderness Medical Society wound guideline (2014): irrigate with **at least 1 litre**, strong recommendation (75%, abstract via search).
- **Drinkable (potable) water** is the preferred fluid in the wilderness (75%).
- High pressure beats low pressure for removing dirt and bacteria (75%). Make pressure: squeeze a soft bottle hard, or poke a pin hole in a clean zip bag and squeeze.
- No additives in the flush water needed, except rabies prone bites (75%).
- Water from a creek? Treat it first. See [Water](#/s/water-safe-drinking/0).
- Cover with a clean dressing. Change it daily (Tip).
- Infection signs: spreading redness, heat, swelling, pus, a red streak up the limb, fever. Get to a doctor (70%).
```

## When a cut needs a doctor

| Sign | Why |
|---|---|
| Gaping, will not close | Needs stitches (70%) |
| Deep in hand or joint | Tendon risk (70%) |
| Dirty, a bite, or a puncture | Infection risk (70%) |
| Will not stop bleeding | Bigger vessel (75%) |
| Numb past the cut | Nerve risk (65%) |

```more more detail
- Tip: a cut on the hand while gutting is easy to shrug off. A deep hand cut can cut a tendon; if a finger will not bend or straighten normally, end the hunt and get it seen.
- Tetanus: ask your doctor when your last booster was before the season (Tip).
- Animal fluids in a cut: wash well. Wear gloves while field dressing (Tip, see [Field dressing](#/s/field-dressing/0)).
```

## Sprains

```steps Rolled ankle in 3 steps
fs-fa-sprain-1 | Sit. Look for a bad bend.
fs-fa-sprain-2 | Cool it in a cloth, then wrap firm.
fs-fa-sprain-3 | Foot up, then try 4 steps.
```

- Can walk? Wrap, poles, partner carries gear (Tip).

```more sprains in full
- RICE: Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation. Widely taught; the evidence for it alone is thin (65%, Wikipedia review of sprain research).
- Newer sports medicine says **PEACE** for the first days: Protection, Elevation, Avoid anti inflammatories, Compression, Education. Then **LOVE**: Load, Optimism, Vascularisation, Exercise (65%, British Journal of Sports Medicine, Dubois and Esculier, 2020).
- Ice timing varies by source: British Red Cross says no more than 10 minutes; UK hospital sheets say 10 to 20 minutes with breaks (65%). Always a cloth between ice and skin (70%).
- In the field: cold creek water in a bag, or snow, wrapped in a buff.
- A firm wrap (elastic bandage) gives support to walk out (65%).
- The "4 steps" test comes from the Ottawa ankle rules, a doctor's tool for deciding on an X ray image: unable to take 4 steps, or bone tenderness at the ankle knobs, means get it imaged (70%, MDCalc). Here it is only a rough walk out guide (Tip).
```

## Walk out or wait?

```diagram fs-fa-walkout
```

- Never split up in the dark to "go get help" without a plan (Tip).

```more the decision in full
- Can walk 4 steps on it: wrap it, use trekking poles or a stick, empty the injured hunter's pack into the partner's, go slow on the easiest route, not the shortest (Tip).
- Cannot: stay put, splint, build warmth and shelter, call 911 or SOS. Search and rescue can carry a person; you two cannot carry each other far (Tip).
- Bone through the skin, a limb that is cold, blue or numb below the injury, or a suspected broken thigh: call for help now (70%). A broken thigh can bleed a lot inside (65%).
- Leaving to get help: only if there is no signal, the hurt person has shelter, warmth, water and a light, and you can mark your route back (Tip). Write the GPS (Global Positioning System) point before you leave.
```

## Suspected broken bone

```steps Splint in 3 steps
fs-fa-splint-1 | Check warmth and feeling below it.
fs-fa-splint-2 | Do not straighten. Pad and splint as found.
fs-fa-splint-3 | Tie off the break. Check again.
```

- Splint: trekking poles, sticks, a rolled foam pad (Tip).

```more splinting in full
- Splint it in the position you found it. Do not try to straighten it (70%, military first aid lessons via search).
- Cover the joint above and the joint below the break (70%).
- Pad between the splint and the skin (70%).
- Tie above and below the break, never on it (70%).
- Check circulation below the splint before and after: warm, pink, can feel touch, can wiggle fingers or toes. Cold, pale, numb or tingling: loosen the ties (70%).
- Take off rings and watches early. Swelling makes them a tourniquet (70%).
- Neck or back injury after a fall: do not move them unless you must (danger, cold). Call for help (70%).
```

## The trauma kit

```diagram fs-fa-kit
```

- Kit on your body. Same pocket every hunt (Tip).

| Tier | What | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | Tourniquet, gauze, gloves, tape | VERIFY |
| Mid | Plus pressure bandage, shears | VERIFY |
| Premium | Plus haemostatic gauze, splint | VERIFY |

```more the kit list and why each item
- **Tourniquet** (windlass type, known brand): the one item that saves a life in minutes (70%). CAT Gen 7: $52 to $59.99 CAD, price checked 2026-10-01 (60%, from [Pack and car kit](#/s/pack-kit/0)).
- **Pressure bandage** (an emergency trauma dressing with a built in pad): wraps and presses in one move (Tip).
- **Packing gauze**, plain or haemostatic (65%).

- **Trauma shears**: cut clothing to expose the wound (Tip).

- **Emergency blanket** and **lighter**: from [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0).
- Extras for a long day: elastic wrap for sprains, blister care, pain pills you know you tolerate, any personal medicine (Tip).
- Prices for kits: VERIFY at a Canadian outdoor or medical store before buying. Not checked here.
```

## Where the tourniquet rides

```diagram fs-fa-carry
```

- Both of you carry one. Show your partner where yours is (Tip).

```more why on the body
- You may be the one bleeding. Your partner must find your tourniquet in seconds, without digging through your pack (Tip).
- Either hand must reach it: if your right arm is hit, your left hand applies it (Tip).
- Cold hands: practise in gloves (Tip).
- Store it ready: strap pre routed, rod folded in, not sealed in plastic that needs scissors (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fs-fa-priorities
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Blood first, then breath, then warmth. And carry the tourniquet where your brother can find it.

> [!mistake]
> - Tourniquet in the truck "for emergencies".
> - Loosening a tourniquet to check.
> - Gutting by headlamp with a wet, bloody handle.

```more the mistakes in full
- The kit stays at the truck because the day hunt is "short". The bleeding happens 3 km in (Tip).
- Loosening a tourniquet to see if the bleeding stopped. Leave it to medical staff (70%).
- Gutting tired, in the dark, with a dull knife and a slippery handle (Tip).
- Splitting up after an injury with no plan and no light (Tip).
- Owning a tourniquet but never practising with it (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

| Hook | Means |
|---|---|
| Check, Call, Care | The first minute, in order |
| Press, Pack, Tourniquet | Bleeding control ladder |
| Heart side, never a joint | Where the tourniquet goes |
| Twist till it stops, lock, clock | Tourniquet finish |
| One litre for one cut | Flush volume |

| Joint above, joint below | Splint length |

```more how to use the hooks
- Say "Check, Call, Care" out loud the moment something goes wrong. It stops the panic loop (Tip).
- "Press, Pack, Tourniquet": climb the ladder only as far as you need (Tip).
- "Twist till it stops, lock, clock": the 3 last moves, and "clock" means write the time (Tip).
```
