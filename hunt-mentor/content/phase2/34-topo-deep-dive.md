## Key points

```diagram fn-topo-slices
```

| Pattern | Means |
|---|---|
| Lines close | Steep |
| Lines wide | Gentle or flat |
| V pointing uphill | Draw or creek |
| U pointing downhill | Ridge or spur |
| Hourglass | Saddle |
| Wide gap in tight lines | Bench |
| Lines touching | Cliff |
| Ticked loop | Hole |

```more the key points in full
1. Contours join points of equal height. They never cross; they only merge at a vertical cliff (95%, USGS Topographic Map Symbols).
2. Close lines are steep, wide lines are gentle, no lines is flat (95%, USGS).
3. Every fifth line is a thicker index contour with its height printed on it (90%; the step can vary by map series).
4. The contour interval, the height step between lines, is printed in the map margin (95%). The Hunt Map draws a line every 20 m and a thick index line every 100 m (99%, read in the app's map code).
5. Closed loops are tops. Loops with small ticks on the inside are holes, called depressions (90%, USGS US Topo specification).
6. Contours bend into a V where they cross a creek. The V points upstream, uphill (90%).
7. Shape matters more than any one line: a bench, a saddle, a convex roll (Tip).
8. Small features can hide between two lines. A 20 m interval can miss a 15 m cliff (95%, simple maths).
9. Every pattern has a hunting use: beds, crossings, glassing seats, stalking cover (Tip, hunter observation).
```

## The five contour rules

```steps Five rules that never change
fn-topo-rule-1 | Lines never cross. They only touch at a cliff.
fn-topo-rule-2 | Close lines are steep. Wide lines are gentle.
fn-topo-rule-3 | Every fifth line is thick and carries a height.
fn-topo-rule-4 | Closed loops are tops. Ticked loops are holes.
fn-topo-rule-5 | The V points upstream. Water flows out the open end.
```

- Learn these five and every map makes sense.

```more the five rules in full
- **Never cross:** each contour is one height, so two can only meet where the ground is vertical, at a cliff (95%, USGS: "contours never cross").
- **Close is steep:** "Contours that are very close together represent steep slopes. Widely spaced contours or an absence of contours means that the ground slope is relatively level." (95%, USGS Topographic Map Symbols)
- **Index lines:** wider lines with the height printed in several places (95%, USGS). On most maps every fifth line (90%). Some series use every fourth (85%, USGS 250K specification).
- **Closed loops:** a ring with no ticks is a knob or summit. A ring with short ticks on the inside is a closed depression; the ticks point down into the low spot (90%, USGS US Topo specification).
- **The V rule:** where a contour crosses a creek it bends upstream, making a V that points uphill (90%, standard map reading; also in Maps 1).
- Colours: brown contours, blue water (95%, USGS; same on Canadian NTS, National Topographic System, sheets, see Maps 1).
```

## Knob or hole?

```diagram fn-topo-depression
```

- Plain rings: a knob, high ground.
- Ticked rings: a hole. Often wet, a pond or a sinkhole (Tip).

```photo hab-lac-du-bois-potholes Pothole lakes in grassland hollows, north of Kamloops.
```

```more knobs and holes for hunters
- A small knob in a flat bench is a natural glassing seat: you sit a few metres higher than the cover around you (Tip).
- Small depressions in grassland and parkland often hold water after rain or snowmelt. Kettle ponds and potholes draw deer and ducks (Tip). Lac du Bois grassland north of Kamloops has pothole lakes in hollows like these (see the photo on this screen and [Habitat tour: ducks](#/s/habitat-ducks/0)) (Tip).
- A depression can be too shallow to show at a 20 m interval. Satellite view and the Slope steepness tool fill the gap (Tip).
```

## Draws versus ridges

```diagram fn-topo-rule-5
```

- Draw: Vs point **up**, into the hill. Water runs here.
- Ridge or spur: Us point **down**, out of the hill.

```more draws, gullies, ridges and spurs
- **Draw (also gully or reentrant):** a small valley on a slope. Sharp Vs pointing uphill (90%).
- **Ridge:** a long high spine. Rounded U or V shapes pointing downhill (90%).
- **Spur or finger ridge:** a short ridge sticking off a bigger one, like a finger. Us pointing downhill (90%).
- A spur and a draw always sit side by side, like fingers and the gaps between them (Tip).
- Hunter use: deer and elk often travel the edge of a draw or along a spur, and cool evening air sinks down draws (Tip; thermals 75%, see [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals/0)).
- Picture water dropped on the map. It always runs down the middle of the V, through the open end (Tip).
```

## Convex or concave?

```diagram fn-topo-convex
```

- Convex: spacing goes wide to tight. The slope rolls away below you.
- Concave: tight to wide. The slope flattens into a bowl you can see.

```more convex and concave in full
- **Convex slope:** gentle on top, steepening below. On the map: wide spacing near the top, tight near the bottom (90%).
- **Concave slope:** steep on top, flattening below. Tight spacing near the top, wide near the bottom (90%).
- **Uniform slope:** even spacing all the way (90%).
- Why it matters: from the top of a convex slope you cannot see the bottom. From the top of a concave slope you see the whole face (95%, line of sight geometry).
- Hunter use: a convex roll is perfect stalking cover. You can walk down to the edge unseen, then peek over (Tip).
```

## Dead ground: where deer hide

```diagram fn-topo-deadground
```

- Dead ground is any ground you cannot see from where you stand.
- Rolls, folds and draws hide whole herds.

```more dead ground in full
- Dead ground is a military and stalking term: ground hidden from view by the shape of the land (75%, common usage).
- Below every convex roll there is dead ground. A deer feeding there cannot see you either (95%, geometry). Use it to close distance.
- Before a stalk, find a fold that hides you from the animal all the way in (Tip).
- The Hunt Map's What can I see tool shades hidden ground for you. See [The Hunt Map like a pro](#/s/hunt-map-pro/0).
- Bare ground only: trees and brush hide even more (99%, the app says so).
```

## Bowls, basins and the head of a draw

```diagram fn-topo-bowl
```

- A horseshoe of lines open downhill: a bowl or basin.
- Tip: wind swirls in bowls. Approach from the side.

```more bowls in full
- A bowl is a rounded hollow with steep sides on three sides and an open bottom (90%).
- High bowls hold snow, shade and often thick timber on the north side (Tip).
- Air drains down and out of a bowl at night and swirls in it on light wind days (70%, slope wind behaviour; see [Wind mastery](#/s/wind-mastery/0)).
- The **head of a draw** is where a draw starts below a ridge. Animals often cross there because it is the easy way around the steep draw below (Tip, hunter observation).
```

## Pinch points

```diagram fn-topo-pinch
```

- Where cliffs, water or steep ground squeeze travel into a narrow gap (Tip).
- Look for tight lines beside a lake, river or second cliff.

```more pinch points in full
- A pinch point (also funnel) is any spot where terrain or water squeezes animal travel into a narrow strip (Tip, hunter term).
- On the map: two barriers close together. Cliff bands (lines touching), a lake or river, a steep draw, or a fence line from satellite view (Tip).
- Pair it with the wind before you sit it (Tip). See [Trails, funnels and pinch points](#/s/funnels/0).
```

## Which way is uphill?

```diagram fn-topo-updown
```

- Number tops face uphill (98%).
- Water runs in the bottom of the V.

```more three clues in full
1. Index numbers are printed so the top of the number faces uphill (98%, NRCan Topographic maps tips, see Maps 1).
2. Creeks and lakes are low. Water never runs along a ridge (98%).
3. Closed rings with no ticks are tops (90%).
- In the Hunt Map: the elevation readout under the scale bar shows the height at the map centre. Pan up a line and watch it change (99%, app feature).
```

## The interval hides small stuff

```diagram fn-topo-interval
```

- A 15 m cliff can fit between two 20 m lines.
- Check steep ground with the Slope tool and satellite view (Tip).

```more what hides between lines
- Any feature smaller than the contour interval can vanish: rock bands, cut banks, small knobs, small depressions (95%, maths).
- The Hunt Map draws 20 m lines (99%, app code). Its terrain data is about 24 m between points, so small cliffs, cut banks and rock bands do not show in the slope colours either (99%, the app says so).
- Belt and braces: topo plus satellite plus your eyes (Tip).
```

## Draw a side view (profile)

```steps Draw a profile in 4 steps
fn-prof-1 | Draw a straight line from A to B.
fn-prof-2 | Mark every place it crosses a contour.
fn-prof-3 | Drop each mark to its height on a graph.
fn-prof-4 | Join the dots. That is the side view.
```

- The Hunt Map's Elevation profile does this for you (99%).

```more profiles in full
- A profile shows the ups and downs along a line: how hard a climb is, and what you can see from where (95%).
- Paper method: edge of a sheet of paper on the line, tick each crossing, write the height, transfer to a graph (90%, standard map reading).
- App method: Tools, Elevation profile, Draw a line. The chart shows climb and drop along it (99%, app feature).
- Use it before every dawn climb: where is the steep bit, where is the bench to rest and glass? (Tip)
```

## Steepness in degrees

| 20 m lines on the ground | Slope | Degrees | Feels like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 200 m apart | 10% | 6° | Easy walk |
| 100 m apart | 20% | 11° | Steady climb |
| 50 m apart | 40% | 22° | Hard, slow |
| 35 m apart | 57% | 30° | Avalanche angle in snow |
| 20 m apart | 100% | 45° | Scramble |

- At 1:50,000, 1 mm on paper is 50 m on the ground (98%).

```more the steepness maths
- Slope percent = rise divided by run times 100. Degrees = the angle whose tangent is rise over run (98%, maths).
- 20 m of rise over 50 m of ground is 40 percent, about 22 degrees (98%).
- Most avalanches start on slopes of 30 to 45 degrees (95%, Avalanche Canada glossary, slope angle).
- The Hunt Map's Slope steepness tool colours this for you: yellow 15 to 25°, orange 25 to 30°, red 30 to 45°, purple over 45° (99%, app code).
- More on steepness and walking speed: [Planning a route](#/s/route-planning/0).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram hf-ridge-vs-draw
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): The map shows the bones. Your eyes add the meat. Read both before you take a step.

> [!mistake]
> - Reading one line, not the pattern.
> - Trusting the map to show every small cliff.
> - Planning a stalk without checking for dead ground.

```more the mistakes in full
- Reading one line, not the pattern. A single bend means little; a stack of Vs means a draw.
- Trusting the map to show every cliff. A cliff shorter than the interval can hide between two lines.
- Planning a stalk across open ground when a fold 100 m over would have hidden you all the way in.
```

## Memory hooks

| Hook | Means |
|---|---|
| Close lines climb, wide lines walk | Spacing is steepness |
| The V points to the source | Vs point upstream |
| Ticks in, ground in | Ticked loop is a hole |
| Fat line, every fifth | Index contour |
| Big Deer Seldom Ride Cliffs | Bench, Draw, Saddle, Ridge, Cliff |
| Convex hides, concave shows | Dead ground |

```more more hooks
- **20 m and 50 m:** at 1:50,000, if 20 m lines are 1 mm apart, the slope is 40 percent. One millimetre, forty percent.
- **35 is 30:** 20 m lines 35 m apart on the ground make a 30 degree slope, the avalanche line.
- **Hourglass, hold the pass:** a saddle is the hourglass; animals cross there.
```
