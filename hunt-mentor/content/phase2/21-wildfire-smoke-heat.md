## Key points

```diagram fs-wf-terrain
```

- Fire runs uphill fast. Escape down, sideways or into ground that already burned (65%).

```more the 8 key points in full
1. Area restriction orders under section 11 of the Wildfire Act close Crown land around fires to everyone without written permission, including hunters (75%, BC Wildfire Service notices via search).
2. Restrictions can stay after evacuation orders are lifted (75%).
3. Violation tickets of $1,150 have been issued for entering restricted areas (75%).
4. Fire races uphill; slope and wind speed it up (70%, University of British Columbia Okanagan study coverage).
5. Plan two ways out (65%).
6. Report a wildfire: 1 800 663 5555, or *5555 on a cell (80%).
7. AQHI 10+: everyone should avoid strenuous activity outdoors (80%, BC Centre for Disease Control fact sheet).
8. Heat stroke: call 911, cool the whole body fast (80%, HealthLink BC).
```

## Area restriction orders

```diagram fs-wf-area
```

- Inside a restricted area, no hunting, even if evacuation orders were lifted (75%).

```more what the orders say
- Orders are made under section 11 of the Wildfire Act. Unless an exemption applies, a person must not remain in or enter the restricted area without prior written authorization from an official (75%, order wording via search).
- Exemptions listed in one order include travelling to or from your home, using a highway, and acting in an official capacity (75%).
- A 2009 order said the restriction applied to all purposes, including recreation and hunting. More recent BC Wildfire Service statements said the same for specific fires (75%).
- A Kamloops area official noted hunters thought downgraded evacuation orders meant they could hunt there. They could not (70%, news coverage).
- A recent fire centre notice: failure to comply may bring a violation ticket of $1,150 (75%).
- Orders get extended and their boundaries change. Read the current order and map before going (75%).
```

## How fire moves

```anim fs-wf-uphill Rising heat pre heats the fuel above. Fire races uphill.
```

```diagram fs-wf-terrain
```

- Afternoon heat and wind make fires most active (Tip, VERIFY).

```more fire behaviour in full
- Fires race uphill (70%, University of British Columbia Okanagan study coverage). Slope effects vary with fuel, wind and terrain (70%).
- Wind and slope are two critical variables for how fast fire spreads (70%, Forest Science comparison).
- The BC Wildfire Service rates fire behaviour on a scale of 1 to 6; higher ranks mean faster spread and fire in the crowns (75%).
- Chimneys (narrow draws), saddles and steep upslopes funnel heat and wind (Tip: wildland firefighting teaching, not read directly here).
- Afternoons are often the most active part of the day (Tip, VERIFY).
- Ash or sparks falling around you suggests the fire is probably within about 1.6 km (1 mile) (60%, hiking safety source).
```

## Escape

```steps Escape a wildfire in 4 steps
fs-wf-escape-1 | Know two ways out before you need them.
fs-wf-escape-2 | Read the smoke: it leans where fire goes.
fs-wf-escape-3 | Go down and across, toward burned ground or water.
fs-wf-escape-4 | Cornered: bare ground, face down, cover your mouth.
```

- Avoid getting trapped at all costs: survival odds are low (65%).

```more escape in full
- Experts say hikers should avoid being trapped by a wildfire at all costs; odds of survival when trapped are low (65%, hiking safety sources).
- Leave early. Stay upwind. Watch for smoke and ash (65%).
- Plan at least two routes out wherever you are; routes on or near burned areas are an option because changing winds can reverse a fire (60%).
- Move downslope and upwind when you can; fire spreads fast uphill (60%).
- Cornered: find a spot with nothing to burn, clear what you can, lie face down in a low spot with feet toward the flames until they pass. Cover your airway with cloth. Do not soak your clothes unless there is a lot of water: it can turn to scalding steam (60%).
- Air 1.5 m above the ground is far hotter and smokier: stay low (60%).
- Vehicle: a road and the truck are often your best way out. Park facing out in fire season (Tip).
- Call 911 if threatened (Tip).
```

## Do not start one

```diagram fs-wf-sparks
```

- Never park a hot truck or [[ATV]] (all terrain vehicle) on dry grass (Tip).

```more prevention in full
- Hot exhaust on dry grass can start a fire (Tip, from [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0)).
- Cigarette butts: put them out and carry them out (Tip).
- Campfires: check bans, keep 8 litres of water or a tool, drown, stir, feel (80%). See [Fire](#/s/fire-wet-weather/0).
- Dragging chains and metal on rock can throw sparks (Tip).
- Report a wildfire: 1 800 663 5555 toll free, or *5555 on a cell (80%). Note where it is, size, colour of smoke, wind (Tip).
```

## Hunting a burn


```diagram fs-wf-burn
```

- New growth draws game. Look up and watch your feet (Tip).

```more burned ground in full
- Burns grow fresh feed that deer, elk and bears use (Tip, see [Food, water and cover](#/s/food-water-cover/0)).
- Roots can smoulder deep in the ground and reignite without warning (60%, hiking safety source).
- Fire weakened trees can fall without wind; avoid camping or sitting under them (Tip).
- Ash pits: burned out stump holes can hide hot coals (Tip).
- Bare slopes can slide and flood after rain (Tip).
- Check that the burn is not inside an area restriction order (75%).
```

## Smoke and the AQHI

```diagram fs-wf-aqhi
```

- At 10+, the air is extremely poor. Everyone should take action (80%).

```more the AQHI in full
- The AQHI runs 1 to 10+ (80%, BC Centre for Disease Control fact sheet, read directly).
- In BC the AQHI is calculated two ways each hour, and the higher value is reported: the usual 3 pollutant formula, and fine particles (PM2.5, particles smaller than 2.5 micrometres) alone (80%).
- PM2.5 is the biggest health risk in wildfire smoke (80%).
- Messages (80%):
  - Low (1 to 3): ideal for outdoor activity.
  - Moderate (4 to 6): no change needed unless you have symptoms; people at higher risk consider easing off.
  - High (7 to 10): consider reducing or rescheduling strenuous outdoor activity if you have symptoms; higher risk people should reduce or reschedule.
  - Very high (10+): reduce or reschedule strenuous activity, especially with symptoms; higher risk people avoid it.
- No cleaner air? Consider a well fitted N95 respirator (80%).
- Being too hot is more risky than breathing smoke for most people (80%, the same fact sheet). Do not overheat in a closed truck to avoid smoke.
- Adults breathe up to 10 times more air when exercising, so slowing down cuts the dose (65%, news quoting BC Centre for Disease Control).
```

## Smoke in the field

| Problem | Do this |
|---|---|
| Hard climb in heavy smoke | Slow down or reschedule |
| Cough, chest tight | Stop, rest, leave the smoke |
| Asthma or heart trouble | Stay home on high days |
| Visibility gone | Hold fire; check backstop |
| Smoke getting thicker | Check the fire map now |

```more why each row
- Strenuous activity raises the smoke dose (80%).
- People with lung or heart disease, pregnant women, infants, children and older adults are at higher risk (80%).
- Smoke can hide people and buildings behind game. Only shoot what you can fully see, with a safe backstop (Tip, see [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety/0)).
- Thickening smoke can mean a fire is moving toward you. Check before you go deeper (Tip).
```

## Heat illness

```diagram fs-wf-heat-signs
```

- Confused and hot: heat stroke. Call 911 (80%).

```more heat in full
- Heat stroke happens when the body cannot control its temperature and it keeps rising, often to 40 °C or higher (80%, HealthLink BC).
- Warning signs of fast progressing heat stroke: unconsciousness more than a few seconds, seizures, trouble breathing, temperature above 40 °C, confusion or aggression, fast heartbeat, heavy sweating or sweating that has stopped, red hot dry skin, severe vomiting and diarrhea (80%).
- Heat exhaustion: move to shade, remove extra clothes, cool wet cloths on head and neck, fluids. Stop cooling if they start to shiver (70%, BC Forest Safety Council).
- Early season (September) hunts in the Okanagan can be hot (Tip). Carry more water than you think. See [Water](#/s/water-safe-drinking/0).
- Heat spoils meat fast. Cool it quickly: [Meat care](#/s/meat-care/0).
```

## Heat stroke: what to do

```steps Heat stroke in 4 steps
fs-wf-heat-1 | Call 911 or SOS, then shade.
fs-wf-heat-2 | Remove extra clothing, soak with cold water.
fs-wf-heat-3 | Fan hard. Cold packs on neck, armpits, groin.
fs-wf-heat-4 | Sips only if fully awake. No fever pills.
```

- Cool first, ask questions later (Tip).

```more the steps in full
- HealthLink BC heat stroke first aid (80%):
  - Call 911 or other emergency services.
  - Move the person to a cool place out of direct sun.
  - Remove unnecessary clothing; lay them on their side to expose skin to air.
  - Cool the whole body by sponging or spraying cold water, and fan them.
  - Ice packs on the neck, groin, back and armpits.
  - Do not give aspirin or acetaminophen for the high temperature.
  - Awake and able to swallow: fluids, about 1 to 2 litres over 1 to 2 hours, sitting up. Not fully awake: nothing by mouth.
- In the field: a creek, wet shirts, a game bag soaked in water and fanned (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fs-wf-check-2
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): In fire season, know where the fire is, which way the wind blows, and which road gets you out.

> [!mistake]
> - Hunting inside an area restriction because "the evacuation was lifted".
> - Parking on tall dry grass.
> - Climbing hard in heavy smoke.

```more the mistakes in full
- Entering a restricted area to hunt. It is closed for safety and tickets have been issued (75%).
- Hot exhaust on dry grass (Tip).
- Strenuous effort at high AQHI (80%).
- Running uphill ahead of a fire (65%).
- Leaving meat in the sun on a hot afternoon (Tip).
```
