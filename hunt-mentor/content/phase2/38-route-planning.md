> Most bad days in the hills start with "it looked close on the map". Put numbers on the walk and set a turnaround time, and you stop being surprised by the dark.

| Step | Tool |
|---|---|

```more the key points in full
1. Measure the real route, not a straight line (Tip).
2. Climb costs more than distance: 600 m up is worth 5 km of flat walking in Naismith's rule (75%).
3. Naismith is a minimum. One source suggests adding about 50 percent in practice; it ignores rest, weight, bad weather and rough footing (60%, search preview of a navigation FAQ).
4. Legal hunting light ends 1 hour after sunset for big game and upland birds (99%, Rule Book). Hunting, including tracking with a firearm, stops then.
5. A turnaround time is a clock time you decide at home and keep (Tip).
6. Break the route into legs with checkpoints you will recognise (Tip, from orienteering).
7. Leave a trip plan: route, start and return times, where the truck is, and when to call for help (75%, AdventureSmart guidance via search and rescue groups).
```

## Speed changes with slope

```diagram fn-rt-tobler
```

- Fastest on a gentle downhill. Flat is about 5 km an hour (75%).
- Steep up or down is slow (75%).

```more Tobler's curve in full
- Tobler's hiking function: speed in km an hour = 6 times e to the power of minus 3.5 times the absolute value of (slope plus 0.05), with slope as rise over run (75%, Wikipedia and research papers).
- Peak 6 km an hour on a 5 percent downhill; 5 km an hour on the flat (75%).
- Off path, Tobler multiplies by 0.6: about 3 km an hour on the flat (75%).
- Researchers say the sharp peak is not realistic, but the shape is right: steep is slow both ways (70%).
- Langmuir adds a descent rule: on slopes over about 12 degrees, add 10 minutes per 300 m of descent (70%).
- Steep descents with a meat pack are slow and risky (Tip). See [Packing out](#/s/packing-out/0).
```

## Worked example

```diagram fn-rt-example
```

- 3 km off trail, 400 m up: about 1 h 25 min (75%).
- With a pack in the dark, allow 2 h (Tip).

```more the example maths
- Distance: 3 km at 4 km an hour off trail = 45 minutes (Aitken, 75%).
- Climb: 400 m at 600 m an hour = 40 minutes (Naismith, 75%).
- Total: about 1 hour 25 minutes, a minimum.
- Add about 50 percent for a heavy pack, brush, dark or rests: about 2 hours (60% plus Tip).
- Walking out is mostly downhill, so faster in theory. With meat or in the dark it is often slower (Tip).
- Still hunting is far slower: a few hundred metres an hour (Tip, see [Moving quietly](#/s/still-hunting/0)).
```

## Over the top or around?

```diagram fn-rt-around
```

- A longer route on one contour can be faster and quieter than over the top (Tip).

```more route choice in full
- Each 100 m of climb costs about 10 minutes in Naismith's rule (75%). Going over a 300 m hill and back down can cost 30 minutes plus a slow descent.
- Sidehill on a contour or cross at a saddle (Tip).
- Use handrails: creeks, roads, ridges, cutblock edges (85%, orienteering).
- Avoid red slopes in Slope steepness, cliffs (touching contours) and thick blowdown (Tip).
- Approach with the wind in your face or across you. See [Wind mastery](#/s/wind-mastery/0).
- Stay off skylines on ridges. See [Stay unseen](#/s/stay-unseen/0).
```

## Late October light

```diagram fn-rt-daylight
```

- Mid October: sunset about 18:08, legal light ends 19:08 (85%).
- Clocks go back on 1 November 2026 (90%).

```more light in full
- Times for Heffley Creek, 50.86 N 120.27 W, worked out with the standard sunrise equations (85%; within a couple of minutes, check the app's legal light table on the day).

| Date | Sunrise | Sunset | Legal light ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 Oct 2026 | 7:25 | 18:08 | 19:08 |
| 25 Oct 2026 | 7:41 | 17:48 | 18:48 |
| 31 Oct 2026 | 7:52 | 17:37 | 18:37 |
| 15 Nov 2026, standard time | 7:17 | 16:14 | 17:14 |

- Legal hours, deer and grouse: from 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset (99%, synopsis page 11). Ducks and geese: half an hour before sunrise to half an hour after sunset (99%). See [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods/0).
- Civil twilight (enough light to walk without a lamp in the open) ends about 33 minutes after sunset here in October (85%, my calculation). So the last legal hour is mostly dark. Walking out after legal light means walking in the dark.
- Daylight saving time ends Sunday 1 November 2026 (90%, first Sunday in November rule).
- Mission and the Okanagan are a few minutes different (Tip). Use the app's Field Mode: [legal light](#/field/light).
```

## Turnaround time

```diagram fn-rt-turnaround
```

- Turnaround = when you must be at the truck, minus the walk, minus a buffer.
- Decide it at home. Write it on your hand (Tip).

```more turnaround in full
1. Pick when you want to be at the truck. For a beginner: before full dark (Tip).
2. Subtract the walk out time from your plan.
3. Subtract a buffer: 30 minutes for a shot, a drag, a wrong turn (Tip).
4. That clock time is your turnaround. Set a phone alarm (Tip).
- Shot late in the day? The plan changes: meat care and tracking rules come first. See [After the shot](#/s/after-the-shot/0) and [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing/0).
- Mountain climbers use the same idea: a fixed turnaround time protects you from summit fever (Tip).
```

## Legs and checkpoints

```diagram fn-rt-legs
```

- Truck, creek fork, saddle, knob, bench: each one you can recognise.
- Behind by 30 minutes at a checkpoint? Rethink (Tip).

```more legs in full
- Orienteers plan each leg: control, attack point, route, exit (75%, Little Book of Orienteering Techniques, the CARE system).
- Choose checkpoints that are hard to miss: a creek fork, a road bend, a saddle, the top of a draw (Tip).
- Write the planned time at each checkpoint on your map. Compare as you walk (Tip).
- Drop a waypoint at each one in the Hunt Map. On the way out, they lead you back (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fn-rt-turnaround
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): The mountain does not care what time you meant to be back. Pick your turnaround at the kitchen table and keep it on the hill.

> [!mistake]
> - Measuring a straight line and ignoring the climb.
> - No turnaround time, so "just one more ridge".
> - Using sunset as the walk out time and forgetting the last legal hour is dark.

```more the mistakes in full
- Measuring a straight line on the map and ignoring 400 m of climb.
- No turnaround time. "Just one more ridge" becomes a two hour walk in the dark.
- Planning to walk out at legal light's end. In late October that is about an hour after sunset, already dark.
```

> [!field]
> Plan one route on the Hunt Map. Write your predicted time at each checkpoint. Walk it with your brother and write the real times beside them. Now you know your own multiplier.

```checklist fn-route-plan
Route drawn and saved in the Hunt Map
Distance and climb written down
Time worked out with Naismith plus extra
Sunset and legal light end written down
Turnaround time set, alarm on the phone
Checkpoints picked and waypoints dropped
Trip plan left with someone
```

| Hook | Means |
