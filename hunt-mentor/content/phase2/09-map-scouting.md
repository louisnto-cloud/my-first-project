## Step 1 to 3: MU, seasons, land

```diagram hf-season-bars
```

- Pick the MU (Management Unit). Heffley Creek: MU 3-27 (85%).
- Read your rows on page 34. Write the class beside each date.
- Keep only [[Crown land]]. Check in season changes online (99%).

```more steps 1 to 3 in full, with every season row
1. **Pick the MU.** Heffley Creek sits in MU 3-27 (85%, read from the Region 3 map; see Maps 3).
2. **Read your rows on synopsis page 34.** Find the MU in the "Management Unit" column for each species you hold a licence for.
   - Mule deer bucks, 1 to 31 October, in 3-12 to 3-20, 3-26 to 3-44 and 3-46 (99%, page 34).
   - Mule deer 4 point bucks, 1 November to 10 December, same units (99%, page 34).
   - White tailed deer bucks, 10 September to 10 December, in 3-12 to 3-20 and 3-26 to 3-44 (99%, page 34).
   - White tailed deer antlerless, 10 to 31 October, in 3-12 to 3-20, 3-26 to 3-44 and 3-46 (99%, page 34).
   - Write the class (bucks, 4 point bucks, antlerless) next to each date. The class is the rule, not just the date.
3. **Find the [[Crown land]].** Turn on the parcel layer. Shade out private land, reserves, parks and leases with cattle (see Maps 3).

- In season changes are posted online. Check them before each trip (99%, synopsis page 7).
```

## Step 4: find the benches

```diagram hf-bench-imagery
```

- South and southwest slopes, flat steps under timber (Tip).
- October band near Heffley Creek: start about 700 to 1,200 m (Tip).
- Snow comes: go lower (85%).

```more step 4 in full
- Switch to satellite imagery over the Crown land you kept.
- Look for **south and southwest facing slopes** (Tip). Interior mule deer winter range sits on south and west aspects in old Douglas fir (85%, Armleder 1994 via Hunt Mentor research notes).
- Look for **benches**: flat steps on the slope. On topo, a wide gap between contour lines (90%, see Maps 1).
- October search band near Heffley Creek: start at about 700 to 1,200 m (Tip, owner's working band). Valley floors here sit around 370 to 450 m (55%, VERIFY).
- A study near Kamloops used winter range below 760 m (80%, Willms 1976). So drop lower as snow comes.
- Snow and cold, not the rut, push mule deer down to winter range (85%, Monteith 2011). Early snow: go lower. No snow: go higher (Tip).
- On imagery, benches look like a lighter, flatter patch with scattered trees, under a darker band of timber (Tip).
```

## Step 5: edges and water

```diagram hf-venn
```

- Edge: open beside cover. Deer feed in the open, bed near cover (60%).
- Mark spots with bench, edge and water close together (Tip).

```more step 5 in full
- **Edges** are where two kinds of cover meet: open grass beside timber, a young cutblock beside old trees, a burn beside green forest (Tip).
- Deer feed in the open and bed close to cover. The edge is the doorway between them (60%, hunter observation).
- A 5 to 15 year old cutblock against timber is classic deer country (60%, hunter observation; see Maps 3).
- **Water:** blue lines on topo, dark green strips on imagery, small ponds and springs on benches (Tip).
- In a dry fall, water near a south bench makes it better (Tip).
- Mark each spot that has all three: bench, edge, water within a short walk (Tip).
```

## Step 6: roads, closures, the walk

```diagram hf-access-route
```

- Trace highway, FSR (Forest Service Road), spur, then on foot (Tip).
- Check every road on the thompsonaccess closure maps (99%).
- Region 3: no motor vehicles above 1,700 m except on existing roads and trails (99%).

```more step 6 in full
- Trace how you reach each spot: highway, FSR, spur road, then on foot (Tip).
- Open the Region 3 closure maps at [gov.bc.ca/thompsonaccess](https://www.gov.bc.ca/thompsonaccess). They are georeferenced PDF (Portable Document Format) maps (99%, synopsis page 33; see Maps 2).
- Region 3: no motor vehicles, except snowmobiles, above 1,700 m, except on existing roads and trails (99%, synopsis page 33).
- Check each road you plan to use with your ATV (all terrain vehicle) against the [[Motor Vehicle Closed Area]] maps (99%, page 33).
- Measure the walk from the last legal parking spot. Count contour lines on the climb (Tip, see Maps 1).
- A spot that is a 40 minute climb in the dark needs a start time on your plan (Tip).
- Leave gates as you find them. Region 3 asks this of every hunter (99%, synopsis page 33).
```

## Step 7: glassing points and the wind

```anim hf-glass-point Glass across the draw. Morning air carries scent up and away.
```

- Pin a knob across the draw, 300 to 800 m (330 to 875 yd) from the bench (Tip).
- Write one good wind and one bad wind per spot (Tip).

```more step 7 in full
- From each bench, look for a **glassing point** across or above it: a knob, a spur, a rock outcrop with a view (Tip).
- Best: you look across a draw at the bench, 300 to 800 m (330 to 875 yd) away, so deer do not see or smell you (Tip).
- Drop a pin on each glassing point, the parking spot and the bench. Name them A1, A2, B1 (Tip).
- Plan the wind for each pin (see Wind and thermals):
  - Mornings, air rises uphill (75%). Come in from above or the side.
  - Evenings, air sinks downhill (75%). Sit below or to the side.
- Write two lines per spot: "Good wind: west. Bad wind: east." (Tip)
- Check the forecast wind the night before and pick the spot that matches it (Tip).
```

## Your scouting checklist

```checklist s2-map-scout
MU picked and confirmed on the Region 3 map
My species rows read on synopsis page 34, with the class written down
In season changes checked online
Crown land layer on, private land, reserves, parks and leases shaded out
South facing benches marked in my search band
Edges and water marked near each bench
Road route traced and checked against the thompsonaccess closure maps
Elevation of each road checked against the 1,700 m rule
Glassing point pinned for each bench
Good wind and bad wind written for each spot
Plan A and plan B chosen, offline maps downloaded
Trip plan left with someone at home
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram hf-thermal-mistake
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Scout with your thumb before you scout with your boots. But believe your boots.

> [!mistake]
> - Loving one bench before checking land, lease and road.
> - Reading the date, missing the class. A 2 point mule deer buck is not legal in November in Region 3.
> - A morning glassing point below the bench.

```more the mistakes in full
- Falling in love with one bench and not checking whether it is Crown land, under a lease, or behind a closed road.
- Reading the date on page 34 and missing the class: a 2 point mule deer buck is not legal in November in Region 3.
- Pinning a glassing point that puts the morning thermal straight from you to the bench.
```
