> [!why]
> The bullet is the only part that touches the deer. Know the round, pick the right bullet, and never load the wrong one.

```diagram ff-ct-cutaway
```

- Four parts: case, primer, powder, bullet (85%).
- Memory: **CPPB**.

```more the four parts, in hunter education words
- **Case:** "The container that holds all the other ammunition components together." Usually brass (85%, hunter education).
- **Primer:** "An explosive chemical compound that ignites the gunpowder when struck by a firing pin" (85%, hunter education).
- **Powder:** "A chemical mixture that burns rapidly and converts to an expanding gas when ignited" (85%, hunter education).
- **Bullet:** "A projectile, usually containing lead, fired through a rifle or handgun barrel" (85%, hunter education).
- A cartridge is one complete round: primer, powder, case and bullet together (80%, Massachusetts hunter education lesson).
- Which load to buy: [ammo for your guns](#/s/ammo-guide/0). How the rifle fires it: [how a rifle works](#/s/rifle-mechanics/0).
```

## Key points

```diagram ff-ct-match
```

- Barrel stamp, box and headstamp must all match (85%).
- Hunting bullets must expand. Full metal jacket is illegal for hunting in BC (99%).
- Tikka: no ammunition over 10 years old (95%).

```more all key points
- Compare "the barrel stamp on the firearm against the description on the ammunition box and the stamp on each cartridge" (85%, hunter education).
- Smokeless powder burns, it does not explode in the open. Gas pressure in the chamber is what drives the bullet (85%, hunter education).
- Centrefire primers sit in the centre of the base. Rimfire priming is inside the rim (85%, hunter education).
- Five hunting bullet designs: cup and core, bonded, partition, all copper, polymer tip (70%).
- BC: "It is unlawful to hunt or trap with a rifle using a full metal jacketed non expanding bullet, or a tracer, incendiary, or explosive bullet" (99%, [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods/0)).
- Tikka: do not fire ammunition over 10 years old, and inspect every round (95%, Tikka manual).
- 7,000 grains make one pound (95%).
```

## Read the headstamp

```diagram ff-ct-case-parts
```

- The headstamp on the base names the cartridge (85%).
- .308 Win in a .308 Win rifle. Nothing else (95%).

```more case parts in full
- **Rim:** the edge at the base. The extractor grabs it (85%).
- **Extractor groove:** the cut in front of the rim on rimless cases like .308 (70%).
- **Body:** the main tube that holds the powder (85%).
- **Shoulder:** the slope down to the neck. On a .308 it sets how far the round goes into the chamber (70%).
- **Neck:** grips the bullet (85%).
- **Headstamp:** maker and calibre stamped on the base (85%).
- Tikka: "Make sure that the ammunition is of the appropriate caliber and loading for the firearm and is clean, dry, and in good condition. The caliber designation for your rifle is marked on the side of the barrel" (95%, Tikka manual).
- Tikka also says use only factory ammunition made to CIP (the European proof commission) or SAAMI (the US standards institute) standards (95%).

> [!warn]
> .308 Winchester and 7.62x51 NATO are close cousins but not identical in every chamber. Use what your barrel stamp says, and ask a gunsmith before mixing (60%, Tip).
```

## What happens when it fires

```steps The shot in 5 steps
ff-ct-fire-1 | Pin dents the primer.
ff-ct-fire-2 | Primer flashes through the flash hole.
ff-ct-fire-3 | Powder burns into hot gas.
ff-ct-fire-4 | Brass swells and seals the chamber.
ff-ct-fire-5 | Gas drives the bullet into the rifling.
```

- All five happen in a few thousandths of a second (Tip).
- The case seal keeps hot gas off your face (70%).

```more the five steps in full
1. The firing pin strikes the primer (85%, hunter education).
2. The primer compound ignites and sends a flash through a small flash hole into the powder (85%).
3. The powder burns very fast and turns into a large volume of hot gas (85%, hunter education).
4. Pressure swells the brass case against the chamber walls. This seals the gas so it can only push forward (70%).
5. The gas pushes the bullet out of the neck, into the throat and rifling, and down the barrel (85%).
- This is why a bulged or split case, a pierced primer or gas spitting back means stop shooting (95%, Tikka manual).
- Ammunition that is wet, oily or dirty can change pressure. Tikka asks for clean, dry rounds and a dry chamber before shooting (95%).
```

## Centrefire and rimfire

```diagram ff-ct-rim-centre
```

- Centrefire: primer in the centre. Your .308 (85%).
- Rimfire: compound in the rim. Your .22 LR (long rifle) (85%).

```more the difference in full
- "Centerfire ammunition is used for rifles, shotguns, and handguns." The primer sits in the centre of the casing base. Most centrefire ammunition can be reloaded (85%, hunter education).
- "Rimfire ammunition has the primer contained in the rim of the ammunition casing." Low pressure only. Cannot be reloaded (85%, hunter education).
- BC legal methods treat them differently. Rimfire for grouse, ptarmigan and turkey only among game birds; no rimfire for deer (99%, [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods/0)).
- The .22 LR in depth: [the .22 rimfire](#/s/rimfire-22/0).
```

## Powder: fuel, not a bomb

```diagram ff-ct-fire-3
```

- Smokeless powder burns slowly in the open air (85%).
- Locked in a chamber, the same burn makes huge pressure (85%).

```more powder in full
- "Modern smokeless powder will burn slowly when ignited in the open (outside of the case)" (85%, hunter education).
- "Black powder is less stable and can be explosive when impacted or ignited in the open" (85%, hunter education).
- In the chamber, the gas has nowhere to go but forward, so pressure climbs fast (70%).
- Heat and cold change how fast powder burns, and so the speed of the bullet (65%). See [ballistics deep dive](#/s/ballistics-deep/0) for what that does to drop.
- Store ammunition cool and dry. Tikka asks you to store it in a separate locked place (95%).
```

## Parts of a bullet

```diagram ff-ct-bullet-parts
```

- Tip, ogive, shank, base (70%).
- Jacket outside, core inside on most hunting bullets (70%).

```more bullet parts in full
- **Tip:** soft point (exposed lead), hollow point, or a plastic tip. All start expansion (70%).
- **Ogive:** the curved nose. A long ogive cuts the air better (65%).
- **Shank:** the full width part that the rifling grips (70%).
- **Base:** flat or boat tail (tapered). A boat tail drags a little less at long range (65%).
- Hunter education bullet types: pointed soft point (flat shooting, excellent mushrooming), round nose soft point (low speed, tube magazines), protected tip (accurate, good expansion), full metal jacket (no expansion) (85%).
- BC (ballistic coefficient) on the box tells you how well the bullet cuts air. Higher drifts less (85%). More in [.308 ballistics made simple](#/s/ballistics-308/0).
```

## Grains: bullet weight

```diagram ff-ct-grains
```

- 150 gr (grain) = 9.7 g. 165 gr = 10.7 g. 180 gr = 11.7 g (95%).
- Heavier: slower, carries energy, more recoil (70%).

```more weights in full
- 7,000 grains = 1 pound = 453.6 g, so 1 grain = 0.0648 g (95%, unit definition).
- 150 gr = 9.7 g. 165 gr = 10.7 g. 168 gr = 10.9 g. 180 gr = 11.7 g. 40 gr .22 LR = 2.6 g (95%, arithmetic).
- Powder charges are also weighed in grains. Do not confuse bullet weight and powder weight on a box (Tip).
- In a .308: 150 gr leaves faster and shoots flatter. 165 to 180 gr leaves slower, keeps more energy, and penetrates more (70%).
- The [ammo session](#/s/ammo-guide/0) gives the picks by animal: 150 gr for deer, 165 to 168 gr all round, 178 to 180 gr for elk and moose (65%).
```

## Five hunting bullet designs

```diagram ff-ct-bullet-types
```

- Cup and core: cheap, opens fast (70%).
- Bonded, partition, copper: hold together for bone and big game (70%).

```more each design in full
| Design | How it is built | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cup and core | Lead core in a copper jacket | Cheap, accurate, opens fast | Core can separate at high impact speed |
| Bonded | Core fused to the jacket | Holds together, deep penetration | Costs more |
| Partition | Copper wall splits front and rear cores | Front opens, rear drives on | Front may shed |
| Monolithic copper | One piece of copper, no lead | Keeps nearly all its weight, lead free | Needs speed to open, costs more |
| Polymer tip | Plastic tip on any of the above | Starts expansion, flatter shooting | Depends on the core design |

- Cup and core: "velocities should be held to a moderate level as high impact velocities can induce core separation" (70%, American Hunter, Max Prasac).
- Bonded: "highly reliable performance as they are able to withstand high impact velocities" (70%, American Hunter).
- Partition style: the partition stops the bullet over expanding; the shank keeps going (70%, American Hunter).
- Copper: one source says they may not fully open at long range or low impact speed (65%). Makers list a minimum impact speed for each bullet. Check your box or the maker's page: VERIFY.
- Federal lists 1,900 fps (feet per second) as the minimum for Fusion to expand (85%, from the [ballistics session](#/s/ballistics-308/0)).
```

## Expansion

```anim ff-ct-expand Speed in, mushroom out.
```

- A hunting bullet widens into a mushroom (85%).
- Each bullet has a speed window. Below it, little expansion (70%).

```more expansion in full
- Hunting bullets often have soft or hollow points that expand on impact. Target bullets usually do not (85%, hunter education).
- Wider frontal area makes a bigger wound channel through heart and lungs (70%).
- Too slow (long range): may not open, pencil hole wound, poor blood trail (70%).
- Too fast (very close, soft bullet): can break up before it reaches the vitals (70%).
- Your 100 to 250 m deer shots with a .308 sit in the middle of most deer bullets' window (65%). Placement still decides the outcome: [shot placement](#/s/shot-placement/0).
```

## Bullet for the animal

| Animal | Bullet | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Deer | 150 gr cup and core or bonded | Opens fast on light bone (65%) |
| Black bear | 165 to 168 gr bonded | Heavy shoulder (65%) |
| Elk, moose | 178 to 180 gr bonded, partition or copper | Deep penetration (65%) |
| Grouse, .22 | 40 gr round nose | Head shots (65%) |

- Pick one load, zero with it, hunt with it (Tip).

```more how to choose in practice
- Start from the [ammo session](#/s/ammo-guide/0) table. Buy one box each of two candidates, group them at 100 m, keep the tighter one (Tip).
- Buy 3 to 4 boxes of the same lot so your zero holds all season (Tip).
- Switching loads means re zeroing (75%).
- Legal: expanding bullets only for hunting with a rifle. No full metal jacket, tracer, incendiary or explosive bullets (99%, Rule Book).
- Bison have a special minimum: 175 grain or more and 2,712 joules at 100 m (99%, Rule Book note 7). Not a beginner hunt, but it shows the law cares about bullets.
```

## Check every round

```diagram ff-ct-inspect
```

- Split, dented, crusty, loose, wrong stamp, too old: set it aside (95%).
- Wipe the chamber dry before shooting (95%).

```more the inspection rules
- Tikka: "Carefully inspect each cartridge before it is loaded in the magazine. Be certain the cartridge cases are not split, deformed, or the cartridges do not possess any other dents or defects (this applies even to factory ammunition)" (95%, Tikka manual).
- "Do not fire old (age over 10 years) ammunition in this firearm" (95%, Tikka manual).
- Tikka warns never to shoot steel jacketed bullets in their rifles (95%).
- Reloaded or handloaded ammunition voids Tikka's warranty (95%).
- Tip: write the purchase date on each box with a marker.
```

## Shotshells, briefly

```diagram ff-ct-shotshell
```

- Primer, powder, wad, shot, crimp (70%).
- Gauge and shell length must match the gun (85%).

```more shotshells in full
- "The shotshells must match exactly the gauge and shell length specified by the manufacturer" (85%, hunter education).
- Shot: "multiple pellets fired through a shotgun barrel." Slug: "a solid projectile, usually of lead, used for hunting big game with a shotgun" (85%, hunter education).
- The wad cushions the shot and carries it down the barrel (70%).
- Ducks and geese: non toxic shot only, no slugs (99%, Rule Book).
- Shot sizes, chokes and patterns: [shotgun mastery](#/s/shotgun-mastery/0).
```

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Loose rounds of mixed brands in a pocket | One load, one box, one pocket |
| Target FMJ on a hunt | Expanding hunting bullets only |
| Old, dented or wet rounds | Inspect, wipe, discard |

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Read the base of the round before you read the deer.

```more the mistakes in full
- Mixing loads in a jacket pocket. Two loads hit two places.
- Bringing range FMJ (full metal jacket) to the field. Illegal for hunting and it does not expand.
- Loading dented, wet or very old rounds. Tikka says inspect each one and do not fire ammunition over 10 years old.
```

> [!field]
> Before the hunt, lay out your rounds on a towel. Read every headstamp, look at every neck, wipe each dry. Five minutes.
