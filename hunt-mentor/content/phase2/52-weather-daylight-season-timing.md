## Big news: no fall back this year

```diagram fl-wx-clock
```

- 1 November 2026: BC clocks stay put (95%).
- So November sunrise is about 8:15 am near Heffley Creek on 15 November, not 7:15 (90%).

```more the time zone change in full
- BC government release, 2 March 2026: "Spring forward" on 8 March 2026 was the last time change (95%).
- New time zone: seven hours behind Coordinated Universal Time (UTC minus 7) (95%).
- On 1 November 2026, clocks "would usually be turned back, but now will remain the same" (95%).
- Northeast BC (Dawson Creek, Fort St. John) stays on UTC minus 7; southeast BC (Golden, Cranbrook, Invermere) keeps switching to match Alberta (95%).
- Older apps, newspapers, printed tables or US websites may still show times one hour earlier for November. If a sunrise time looks an hour early, check it (Tip).
- Washington State still falls back: from November, the border towns south of the Fraser Valley are one hour behind BC (80%, news reports).
```

## Legal hours, every day

> [!warn] Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

```anim rb-sunrise-hours Legal hours: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset.
```

- Work out the start and stop time the night before (Tip).
- In the field, the hunter decides sunrise and sunset by terrain (99%).

```more the rules in full
> [!law] Item 38, page 11
> "To hunt game, except migratory game birds from one hour after sunset to one hour before sunrise. To find out the time of sunrise and sunset, consult a local newspaper or the following website www.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/eng/services/sunrise/index.html. The responsibility remains with the hunter to make the determination of sunrise and sunset in the field based on existing terrain and circumstances."

> [!law] Item 37, page 11
> "To hunt migratory game birds from 1/2 hour after sunset to 1/2 hour before sunrise."

- Plain English: deer, quail and grouse: 1 hour before sunrise to 1 hour after sunset. Ducks and geese: half an hour (99%).
- No lights to hunt (99%, item 38). Tracking after dark: [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing/0).
- Quail are not migratory game birds; the 1 hour rule applies to them (95%, my reading of the Schedule and item 38).
```

## Higher is usually colder

```diagram fl-wx-elev
```

- About 6.5 °C colder per 1,000 m on average (Tip).
- Calm, clear nights: cold air pools in valleys, so benches can be warmer (75%).

```more elevation in full
- The standard atmosphere cools about 6.5 °C per 1,000 m; real days vary a lot (Tip, textbook average).
- Snow lies on high benches long before it reaches the Kamloops valley (Tip). Deer have trouble in snow deeper than about 30 cm (85%, from [Weather, moon and what moves game](#/s/weather-moon/0)).
- Cold air drains downhill at night and pools in hollows (75%, see [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals/0)).
- Forecast tip: look at a mountain or point forecast for your bench, not only the city forecast (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fl-wx-mistakes
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): The sun sets the clock, the mountain sets the light. Be in your spot early and leave with light to spare.

> [!mistake]
> - Using an old sunrise time in November.
> - Trusting the town forecast for the high benches.
> - Starting the walk out at sunset.

```more the mistakes in full
- Old printed or US tables show November times one hour earlier than BC clocks now read (95% for the time zone fact).
- Kamloops valley numbers are not the bench. Expect colder and snowier up high (Tip).
- In November, legal light ends near 6 pm and full dark comes fast. Plan the walk out with a headlamp (Tip and 90% for the times).
```

> [!field]
> Pick one evening this week. At your spot or at home, note the official sunset, the time you can no longer see a deer shape at 100 m with binoculars, and the time you need the headlamp. Write all three in the journal. That gap is your real last light.
