> [!why]
> A deer that smells you is gone before you see it. You cannot hide scent, but you can put it where deer are not.


- Keep the deer **upwind**: the wind blows from the deer to you.

## How scent moves

```anim hf-scent-shift Steady wind is your friend. Shifting wind is the danger.
```

- Your scent drifts with the air, like smoke from a campfire.
- Deer rely heavily on smell to detect danger (70%).

```more how scent moves, in full
- Your scent drifts with the air, like smoke from a campfire.
- Deer rely heavily on smell to detect danger (70%).
- Deer often travel with the wind at their back or side so they can smell behind and look ahead (60%, hunter observation).
- Steady wind is your friend. Shifting, light wind is the danger.
- Rule of thumb: keep the deer **upwind** of you. The wind blows from the deer to you.
```

## Thermals: morning up, evening down

```anim hf-thermal-clock One day on a slope: up in the morning, down in the evening.
```

- Morning sun warms slopes: air rises **uphill** (75%).
- Evening slopes cool: air sinks **downhill** (75%).

```more thermals in full
- Thermals are air currents made by the ground warming and cooling (75%).
- **Morning:** the sun warms slopes and air rises **uphill** (75%).
- **Evening:** slopes cool and air sinks **downhill**, into draws and valley floors (75%).
- Shaded north slopes and timber stay cool longer, so the switch comes later there (Tip).
- On calm days, thermals beat the light breeze. In strong wind, the wind wins (Tip).
```

## Swirls and eddies

```diagram hf-swirl
```

- Wind swirls in draws, saddles, valleys and thick timber (70%).
- In a swirl, no setup is safe. Back out.

```more swirls in full
- Wind swirls in draws, saddles, valleys and thick timber, where air bounces off terrain (70%).
- Swirl also happens around the switch time, mid morning and near sunset, as thermals change direction (Tip).
- Lakes and creeks: cold air often slides down creek bottoms in the evening (Tip).
- In a swirl, no setup is safe. Back out and come back with steadier air.
```

## How to check the wind

```steps Four ways to see the wind
hf-wind-puffer | Puffer bottle: one puff shows drift.
hf-wind-milkweed | Milkweed: floats far, shows thermals and swirls.
hf-wind-lighter | Lighter: quick check at your hand only.
hf-wind-thread | Thread on the sling: always on.
```

- Check every few minutes and at every new terrain feature.

```more wind check tools in full
- **Puffer bottle:** a squeeze bottle of unscented powder. One puff shows drift and direction (70%).
- **Milkweed fluff:** floats far and shows thermals and swirls over terrain (70%).
- **Lighter flame:** quick check, shows only the air right at your hand (Tip). Mind fire risk in dry grass.
- **Thread** on your bow or rifle sling: always on, no hands (Tip).
- Check every few minutes and at every new terrain feature, not just at the truck.
```

## Planning your approach

```diagram hf-plan-ab
```

- Plan an A and B spot for each wind. Wrong wind? Go to B.
- Walk in with the wind in your face or across you.

```more the six planning steps
1. Check the forecast wind direction and speed the night before.
2. On the map, mark likely bedding and feeding areas.
3. Mornings: come in from **above** or from the side so rising air carries scent up and away.
4. Evenings: sit **below** or to the side so sinking air carries scent down and away.
5. Plan an A and B spot for each wind. Wrong wind on the day? Go to B, do not force A.
6. Walk in with the wind in your face or across you, never at your back toward the deer.
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram hf-field-three
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Hunt the wind you have, not the wind you wanted.

> [!mistake]
> - Checking wind once at the truck, then hunting a swirling valley.
> - A morning seat above deer bedded higher up still.
> - Ignoring the evening switch.

```more the mistakes in full
- Checking wind once at the truck, then hunting a valley where it swirls.
- Sitting on a morning ridge above deer that are bedded higher up, so rising air takes your scent right to them.
- Ignoring the evening switch and staying at a midday spot as air begins to sink.
```

> [!field]
> Stand mid slope at 7 am, 12 noon and 5 pm on a scouting day. Puff or release milkweed. Draw the drift in your journal.
