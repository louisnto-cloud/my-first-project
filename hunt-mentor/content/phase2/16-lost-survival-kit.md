## Key points

```diagram fs-lost-priorities
```

- Stop early. Call early. Stay put if in doubt (75%).

```more the 8 key points in full
1. STOP: Stop, Think, Observe, Plan (75%, AdventureSmart via BC Search and Rescue Association).
2. Staying put reduces the time and the area searchers must cover (75%, BC Search and Rescue Association).
3. Without sun or landmarks, people walk in circles without knowing it (75%, Max Planck Institute study, 2009).
4. If you need help, your first action is to call 911 (75%, BC Search and Rescue Association).
5. A phone can reach 911 through any carrier's tower in range (75%).
6. Signal in threes: whistle, mirror, horn, fires or rock piles (75%).
7. For aircraft: a V or SOS on the ground at least 3 m long (75%).
8. Carry the essentials on you, not in the truck (75%, AdventureSmart Three Ts).
```

## STOP the moment you are unsure

```steps Lost? STOP
gr-stop-s | Stop. Sit down. Do not keep walking.
gr-stop-t | Think. Where did you last know where you were?
gr-stop-o | Observe. Map, compass, sun, creeks, roads.
gr-stop-p | Plan. Stay put, or move only if sure.
```

```video
yt-hug-a-tree | Lost? Stop. Stay put. Shelter and signal.
```

- Sit down for 5 minutes. Eat something. Then think (Tip).

```more STOP in full
- BC Search and Rescue Association, emergency steps (75%):
  - Don't panic: stay calm and keep a positive attitude.
  - S.T.O.P.: Stop. Think. Observe. Plan. Then act.
  - Stay put: it reduces time and search area for the people looking for you.
  - Seek shelter: stay warm and dry.
- North Shore Rescue: stay put once lost; wandering usually takes you farther from your last known point (75%).
- North Shore Rescue: avoid going downhill into ravines. They are steep, unstable and hard to climb out of (75%). This matters in the gullies above the Thompson too (Tip).
- Sitting stops the "walk faster" panic loop. A snack and water help you think (Tip).
```

## Why you walk in circles

```anim fs-lost-circle Without sun or landmarks, straight lines bend into loops.
```

- In a 2009 study, forest walkers under cloud crossed their own path (75%).

```more the study in full
- Max Planck Institute researchers tracked walkers by GPS (Global Positioning System) in the Sahara and in a German forest (75%, Max Planck press release, Current Biology, 2009).
- With sun or moon visible, people walked fairly straight. Under cloud in the forest, they curved and some crossed their own path without noticing (75%).
- Blindfolded, most walked in circles, some under 20 m across (75%).
- Cause: small errors in the sense of "straight ahead" add up. A visible landmark resets it (75%).
- Lesson (Tip): in fog, snow or thick timber, use the compass or GPS for every leg, and aim at a tree you can see, then the next one.
```

## Stay or move?

```diagram fs-lost-stay-move
```

- Doubt means stay (Tip).

```more when moving makes sense
- Moving can be right when (Tip):
  - You know the way back to your last known point and can reach it in daylight.
  - You can follow your own fresh tracks in snow back the way you came.
  - Your position is dangerous: avalanche path, rising water, fire.
- If you move: mark your route (broken branches pointing the way, flagging tape, a note with time and direction at your start point) (Tip).
- Do not "follow water downhill" by default in steep country. Creeks drop into canyons and ravines (75%, North Shore Rescue advice on avoiding ravines).
- Moving at night in the bush is how ankles break. Settle in before dark (Tip). See the sunset table in [Cold injuries](#/s/cold-injuries/0).
```

## Every km you wander costs searchers

```diagram fs-lost-search-area
```

- Area grows with the square of the distance (75%, geometry).

```more the math
- A search circle's area is pi times the radius squared. Double the radius, the area is 4 times as big. Triple it: 9 times (95%, geometry).
- That is why staying put helps: your trip plan tells searchers where to start, and every kilometre you walk away grows the area they must cover (Tip).
- Your [[Trip plan]] with the truck location, route and return time is the start point for every search (75%).
```

## Call for help

```steps Call for help in 4 steps
fs-lost-call-1 | Call 911 early. Any tower works.
fs-lost-call-2 | Give your coordinates first.
fs-lost-call-3 | No signal? Satellite SOS from open sky.
fs-lost-call-4 | Then save battery. Check in on the hour.
```

- 911 dispatches police, who call in Search and Rescue (75%).

```more calling in full
- BC Search and Rescue Association: if you need help, your **first action** is to call 911. The operator dispatches the RCMP (Royal Canadian Mounted Police), who request Search and Rescue (75%).
- Your phone can connect to any cell tower in range, even another provider's, for 911 (75%).
- Turn on location services and learn to read your coordinates on your phone (75%).
- North Shore Rescue: call as soon as you know you are lost or injured. Early calls help find you before conditions get worse (75%).
- No signal: iPhone 14 or later can send Emergency SOS via satellite in Canada (85%, see [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0)). A satellite messenger works too.
- Tip: agree on check in times with dispatch or family. Phone off between, on at the hour.
- Search and rescue in BC does not charge the lost person (Tip: North Shore Rescue says rescue has no cost; VERIFY for your area).
```

## Keep the phone alive

```diagram fs-lost-phone
```

- Inside pocket, against your body (70%).

```more phone battery in full
- Apple lists an iPhone operating range of 0 to 35 °C. Outside it, battery life can drop and the phone can shut off (70%, Apple spec via forum and news; Apple page not read directly).
- A cold shutdown is usually temporary. Warm it slowly against your body (65%).
- Battery savers (Tip): low power mode, screen dim, close navigation apps you do not need, airplane mode between check ins (it stops the phone searching for a tower).
- Carry a small power bank in an inside pocket (Tip).
- Keep the phone's map of the area downloaded for offline use before you go (75%, [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0)).
```

## Signal in threes

```diagram fs-lost-threes
```

- 3 of anything means help (75%).

```more signals in full
- BC Search and Rescue Association: whistle blasts times 3, mirror flashes times 3, horn blasts times 3, signal fires times 3, or rock piles times 3 (75%).
- Ground to air: a V or SOS, at least 3 m long, big and high contrast (75%).
- North Shore Rescue: blow 3 blasts for help (75%).
- A whistle carries farther than a voice and costs no energy. Clip one to your jacket zip (Tip).
- Gunshots: 3 shots are a well known signal among hunters (Tip). Keep enough ammunition for the walk out and for any animal. A fire or whistle is better if you are short.
- At night: a headlamp flashed in threes (Tip).
```

## Help them find you

```steps Be found in 3 steps
fs-lost-found-1 | Settle at the edge of an opening.
fs-lost-found-2 | Spread orange and bright gear in the open.
fs-lost-found-3 | Hear searchers? Whistle 3 times. Stay put.
```

- Hidden in thick timber, you are warm but invisible (65%).

```more being found in full
- Dense cover can keep you warm but make you hard to see. Making yourself visible outside the shelter matters as much as the shelter (65%, hiking safety source via search).
- Hug a Tree program: stay put, keep warm and dry, and answer searchers' calls (70%, Canadian Geographic Education).
- Blaze orange is already on you. Hang the vest in the open (Tip).
- A smoky fire by day and a bright fire by night help aircraft (Tip). See [Fire](#/s/fire-wet-weather/0) and the fire rules there.
- Leave a note at your start point if you do move: time, direction, your name (Tip).
```

## Don't get lost: look back

```diagram fs-lost-look-back
```

- Pin the truck before you leave it (Tip).

```more prevention in full
- Drop a GPS pin at the truck, the [[ATV]] (all terrain vehicle) and each junction (Tip). See [Maps 2: navigation](#/s/maps-navigation/0).
- Turn around at each junction and landmark. The way out looks different from the way in (Tip).
- Note a "handrail": a creek, road or ridge you can follow back, and a "backstop" you must not cross (Tip).
- Set a turn back time 1 hour before sunset (Tip, from [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0)).
- Tracking a wounded animal: mark last blood with flagging and a GPS pin, and stop if dark comes with no plan. See [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing/0).
- Carry a paper map and compass as backup to the phone (75%, AdventureSmart essentials).
```

## The survival kit


| Tier | What | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | Lighters, whistle, foil blanket, cord | VERIFY |
| Mid | Plus headlamp, fire starter, bivy bag | VERIFY |
| Premium | Plus satellite messenger, stove | VERIFY |

```more the kit in full, and why
- Carry the essentials and know how to use them (75%, AdventureSmart Three Ts via BC Search and Rescue Association).
- The AdventureSmart 10 essentials: light, fire kit, signalling device, extra food and water, extra clothing, navigation and communication, first aid, emergency shelter, knife, sun protection (75%, see [Pack and car kit](#/s/pack-kit/0)).
- Pocket kit additions (Tip):
  - **2 lighters** in 2 pockets, plus a fire starter (wax cubes or cotton balls with petroleum jelly in a tube).
  - **Whistle** on the zip.
  - **Space blanket** or, better, an emergency bivy bag. Foil blankets reflect radiant heat but do little for conduction from the ground (65%). See [Shelter](#/s/shelter-night-out/0).
  - **Headlamp** with fresh batteries, plus a spare.
  - **Water bag** and purification tabs. See [Water](#/s/water-safe-drinking/0).
  - **10 m of cord** and some flagging tape.
- Prices: VERIFY before buying. Not checked here.
```

## Where the kit lives

```diagram fs-lost-kit-where
```

- You may drop the pack chasing a deer. The pockets go with you (Tip).

```more why three layers
- Pockets (Tip): whistle, lighter, fire starter, phone, tourniquet ([First aid](#/s/field-first-aid/0)), small knife.
- Day pack: the 10 essentials plus shelter and a puffy.
- Truck or ATV: food, water, a sleeping bag, spare clothes, a full first aid kit ([Pack and car kit](#/s/pack-kit/0)).
- Hunters drop packs to stalk. Then they follow a blood trail 1 km. Then it gets dark. The pocket layer is for that night (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fs-lost-stay-move
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): The minute you wonder where you are, sit down. The bush is not going anywhere, and neither should you.

> [!mistake]
> - Walking faster when unsure.
> - Following a creek down into a canyon.
> - Kit in the truck, phone in the pack pocket.

```more the mistakes in full
- Walking faster and farther when unsure. The search area grows with every step (Tip).
- Following water downhill into a ravine (75%, North Shore Rescue).
- Waiting until dark to call. Call early (75%).
- Leaving the essentials in the truck on a "quick" walk (Tip).
- Letting the phone die in a cold pack pocket (70%).
```
