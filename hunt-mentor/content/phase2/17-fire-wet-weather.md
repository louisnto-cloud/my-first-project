> [!why]
> A fire dries you, warms you, signals for you and calms you. In cold October rain, getting one going is a skill you practise before you need it.

```diagram fs-fire-triangle
```

- Heat, fuel, air. Wet steals heat, so prepare twice the small stuff (Tip).

```more what this session covers
- The fire rules first, where dry fuel hides, the fuel ladder, feather sticks, the build, lighting in wind and rain, signal fires, and putting it out cold.
- Fire kit lives in your pockets: [Lost and the survival kit](#/s/lost-survival-kit/0).
- Wildfire danger and smoke: [Wildfire, smoke and heat](#/s/wildfire-smoke-heat/0).
- Assumption: you hunt Interior pine and fir country (Kamloops) and wet coastal forest (Fraser Valley). Both need the same skills; the coast needs more patience.
```

## Key points

```diagram fs-fire-sizes
```

- Check bans before any fire. Every time (80%).

```more the 8 key points in full
1. Fire needs heat, fuel and air. Take away one and it dies (75%, basic science).
2. A Category 1 campfire is no bigger than 0.5 m high by 0.5 m wide (80%, BC Wildfire Service brochure, search preview).
3. Keep at least 8 litres of water or a hand tool on hand the whole time it burns (80%).
4. Fire bans change. Check the BC Wildfire Service before each fire (80%).
5. Dry fuel in rain: dead, off the ground, and split to the inside (Tip).
6. Birch bark and pitch wood (resin soaked wood) burn even when wet (65%).
7. Gather 2 big handfuls of each size before you strike the lighter (Tip).
8. Out means cool to the touch: drown, stir, feel, repeat (80%).
```

## Fire rules first

```diagram fs-fire-cat1
```

- Check the ban status for your fire centre before every fire (80%).
- Kamloops Fire Centre status today: VERIFY.

```more the rules in full
- **Category 1 campfire:** an open fire burning material in one pile no larger than 0.5 m high and 0.5 m wide, lit for recreation (80%, BC Wildfire Service Category 1 campfire brochure, search preview). Bigger is a Category 2 open fire (80%).
- **Water or tool:** at least 8 litres of water or a hand tool on hand at all times while it is lit (80%). One fire centre notice says "a shovel or at least eight litres of water" (75%). The exact wording varies: VERIFY on the current order.
- **Fuel break:** clear the area around the fire of debris and anything that burns (80%). One fire centre advises a pit or ring at least 3 m from trees, shrubs and debris (70%).
- **During a campfire ban:** only a CSA (Canadian Standards Association) or ULC (Underwriters Laboratories of Canada) approved portable campfire apparatus with a flame no higher than 15 cm may be used (75%).
- **Fines:** up to $1,150 for breaking an open fire ban (80%). An escaped fire that causes a wildfire can bring a fine up to $1 million, up to 1 year in jail and the firefighting costs (75%).
- **Kamloops Fire Centre 2026:** Category 1 campfires were allowed again from noon 3 September 2026; Category 2 and 3 open fires stayed banned until noon 9 October 2026 unless rescinded earlier (70%, District of Logan Lake release, 3 September 2026). After 9 October: VERIFY.
- **Survival fire during a ban:** whether a life threatening emergency changes the rule is not confirmed here. VERIFY with BC Wildfire Service. My lean: carry a small stove and an emergency bivy so you rarely need a fire in a ban.
- Not in the Rule Book: the hunting synopsis does not carry fire bans. The [Rule Book](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts/0) covers hunting rules only.
```

## Where dry fuel hides

```diagram fs-fire-dry-spots
```

- Dead, off the ground, and split (Tip).
- Snap test: dry dead wood snaps; wet or green wood bends (Tip).

```more dry fuel in full
- Standing dead wood stays drier than wood lying on wet ground (Tip, common bushcraft advice).
- Dead twigs on the lower trunk of big spruce and fir stay dry under the canopy (Tip).
- Split a wet piece: the core is often dry (65%, wet weather fire guides).
- **Birch bark:** the oily layer burns even when damp (65%). Paper birch grows in the BC Interior (Tip). Take bark from dead or downed trees only (Tip).
- **Pitch wood** (also called fatwood): resin soaked wood from old pine or fir stumps and knots. The resin makes it burn even when the outside is wet (65%).
- Pine needles can still burn when damp (60%).
- Coast tip: in the Fraser Valley rain, bring your own tinder. Cotton balls with petroleum jelly in a pill tube, or wax fire starters (Tip).
```

## The fuel ladder

```diagram fs-fire-sizes
```

- Have 2 big handfuls of every size before you light (Tip).

```more why the ladder matters
- Small pieces have more surface for their weight, so they heat and catch faster (75%, basic science).
- The most common failure: lighting with only tinder and big wood. The tinder burns out before the big wood gets hot (Tip).
- Sizes, smallest first (Tip): tinder fluff, matchstick, pencil, thumb, wrist.
- In rain, double the matchstick and pencil piles. Wet wood needs more heat to dry before it burns (Tip).
- Leave gaps. Fire needs air (65%).
```

## Feather sticks

```steps Make a feather stick
fs-fire-feather-1 | Split to the dry core.
fs-fire-feather-2 | Shave long thin curls, still attached.
fs-fire-feather-3 | Make 3 sticks and a pile of shavings.
```

- Your hunting knife can do this. Cut away from you (Tip).

```more feather sticks in full
- Split the thickest, driest dead wood into quarters, then shave a fine feather stick or a pile of shavings (65%, bushcraft sources via search).
- Feather sticks give a big surface for the flame to grab (65%).
- Use the knife's straight edge, long strokes, thumb behind the spine (Tip). Knife safety: [First aid](#/s/field-first-aid/0).
- Practise at home on dry wood first, then in the rain (Tip).
```

## Build it

```steps Build a fire in 5 steps
fs-fire-build-1 | Build a floor of dry sticks.
fs-fire-build-2 | Loose tinder in the middle.
fs-fire-build-3 | Lean small sticks in a tipi, door upwind.
fs-fire-build-4 | Light low on the upwind side. Shield it.
fs-fire-build-5 | Feed the next size only when this one roars.
```

```anim fs-fire-catch Each size lights the next.
```

```more the build in full
- A platform of dry sticks or bark keeps tinder off wet ground or snow (65%, wet weather fire guides).
- Lay out piles in the order you will need them, within reach (65%).
- Tipi: kindling leaned over tinder with gaps for air and a door on the upwind side (Tip).
- Light the bottom of the tinder. Flame climbs (Tip).
- Your body and pack make a wind wall (Tip).
- Add wood gradually: small to big, never jump straight to logs (65%).
- On snow: a platform of green or wet logs under the fire stops it melting down into a hole (Tip).
```

## What lights it

```diagram fs-fire-ignition
```

- Two ways to light, in two pockets (Tip).

```more lighting tools in full
- **Lighter:** fast. Cold can weaken it and wet fingers slip on the wheel. Keep one inside your jacket (Tip).
- **Matches:** stormproof matches burn in wind and wet. Strike surface must stay dry (Tip).
- **Ferro rod** (ferrocerium rod): throws hot sparks even wet, but needs very fine dry tinder like birch bark shavings or cotton (Tip).
- **Fire starter:** wax cubes, gel or cotton with petroleum jelly. Burns several minutes to dry the kindling (Tip).
- My lean: lighter plus fire starter in one pocket, stormproof matches or a ferro rod in another.
```

## Rain and wind

```diagram fs-fire-shield
```

- A tarp goes high above the fire, never close (Tip).

```more rain and wind in full
- A sheet of birch bark or your hat can shield the first flame from rain (65%).
- Use a rock or log as a wind wall on the windward side (Tip).
- Do not light a fire in strong wind. The BC Wildfire Service advises against campfires in windy conditions (75%).
- Tarps melt and catch fire. Keep it high, and the fire small (Tip).
- Keep spare tinder in a zip bag inside your jacket so body heat dries it (Tip).
```

## Signal fire

```diagram fs-fire-signal
```

- Smoke by day, flame by night. Three fires mean help (75%).

```more signal fires in full
- BC Search and Rescue Association lists signal fires times 3 as a distress signal (75%).
- Green boughs on a hot fire make thick white smoke (Tip).
- Build it in the open where aircraft can see it, and keep it inside the rules and under control (Tip). An escaped signal fire becomes a wildfire.
- More on being found: [Lost](#/s/lost-survival-kit/0).
```

## Put it out cold

```steps Put it out in 4 steps
fs-fire-out-1 | Drown every coal.
fs-fire-out-2 | Stir the ashes, more water.
fs-fire-out-3 | Feel it: cool to the touch.
fs-fire-out-4 | Any warmth? Water and stir again.
```

- Fully out before you leave. Not smoky, not warm (80%).

```more putting it out in full
- Stir water into the ashes until they are cool to the touch. Hot coals can reignite (80%, BC Wildfire Service brochure, search preview).
- The fire must be fully extinguished before you leave the area (80%).
- A fire with water on it, thought to be out, can stay lit underground (75%, Cariboo Fire Centre notice).
- No water? Mineral soil stirred in, then more stirring. Water is far better (Tip).
- See smoke or flames you did not start? Report a wildfire: 1 800 663 5555 toll free, or *5555 on a cell (80%).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fs-fire-sizes
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Gather twice what you think, light once. And drown it till you could sleep on the ashes.

> [!mistake]
> - Lighting before the kindling pile is ready.
> - Smothering the flame with big wet wood.
> - Leaving a fire that is "mostly out".

```more the mistakes in full
- Lighting with only tinder ready, then running for kindling while it dies (Tip).
- Piling on wood too fast and too big: no air, too much water to boil off (Tip).
- Building on wet ground or snow with no platform (Tip).
- Not checking the ban (80%).
- Walking away from warm ashes (80%).
```

## Do this in the field

```diagram fs-fire-build-3
```

> [!field]
> On a rainy scouting day, legal campfire conditions only: light a fire with one lighter and only what you find, in under 15 minutes. Then put it out cold and feel it.

```checklist fs-fire-ready
Ban status checked for this fire centre
Lighter inside jacket, plus a second way to light
Fire starter in a zip bag
8 litres of water or a hand tool ready
2 handfuls of each fuel size before lighting
Ashes cool to the touch before leaving
```
