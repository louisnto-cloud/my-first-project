> [!why]
> Hunters do everything that draws bears: move quietly at dawn and dusk, carry blood, and leave gut piles. Knowing what to do turns a bad moment into a story.

```diagram fs-bc-why
```

- Most encounters end with the bear leaving. Your job: do not make it worse (Tip).

## Key points

```diagram fs-bc-flow
```

- Never run from a bear or a cougar (75%).

```more the 9 key points in full
1. If you meet a bear: stop, stay calm and never run (75%, WildSafeBC).
2. Make noise near running water, thick brush, berry patches and in high wind (75%).
3. Found a carcass you did not kill? Leave and report it (75%).
4. Bear spray: use it when the bear is within about 5 to 10 m (75%, WildSafeBC).
5. Defensive attack, contact made: lie on your stomach, hands over the back of your neck, legs apart (75%).
6. Predatory attack: fight back hard, at the face, eyes and nose (75%).
7. Cougar: never run, look big, back away slowly; if attacked, always fight back (75%).
8. Move meat at least 200 m from the gut pile; hang it 3.5 m up (70%, Alberta bear and hunter guidance).
9. Report aggressive bears and cougars: 1 877 952 7277 (99%, synopsis RAPP line; also the Conservation Officer Service line per WildSafeBC).
```

## Why hunters meet more bears

```diagram fs-bc-why
```

- Calls and decoys can bring in bears as well as game (75%).

```more why in full
- Distress or mating calls, decoys and cover scents can attract bears as well as game (75%, Alberta: Bears and hunters).
- Be extra careful at dusk, at night and at dawn, when it is noisy, or when sight lines are short (75%).
- Gut piles and carcasses draw bears, which then defend them (75%).
- Quiet stalking is good hunting and bad bear safety. Balance it: quiet when hunting, noisy when travelling, packing or working on a kill (Tip).
```

## Avoid the encounter

```steps Avoid bears in 4 steps
fs-bc-avoid-1 | Read fresh sign. Slow down or go around.
fs-bc-avoid-2 | Call out near creeks, brush, wind.
fs-bc-avoid-3 | No headphones. Stay in touch.
fs-bc-avoid-4 | Found a carcass? Leave and report.
```

- WildSafeBC says do not rely on bear bells (75%).

```more avoiding in full
- WildSafeBC (75%):
  - Watch for scat, tracks, freshly torn logs and fresh claw marks.
  - Talk or sing to avoid surprising a bear, especially near running water, in thick brush or high wind. Not bear bells.
  - No headphones, do not hike alone, keep pets leashed.
  - Pack out all food waste.
  - Extra caution where sight lines are poor: berry bushes, salmon streams.
  - Found a carcass? Leave the area and notify the Conservation Officer Service.
- Sign photos: [Black bear](#/s/black-bear/0) and [Reading sign](#/s/sign/0).
```

## You see a bear

```diagram fs-bc-flow
```

- Watch what it does, then match it (75%).

```more each case in full
- WildSafeBC (75%):
  - **Unaware of you:** leave quietly the way you came, keep watching it, check your bear spray.
  - **Aware but not reacting:** speak softly, back away slowly. Do not follow it if it leaves.
  - **Agitated** (jaw popping, moaning, woofing, stomping, bluff charging): speak calmly, back away slowly, do not make direct eye contact but keep it in sight, spray ready.
  - **Approaching steadily:** yield slowly. If it keeps coming, it may be predatory. Yell, move to higher ground, be ready to fight.
- In every case: never run (75%). You cannot outrun a bear (Tip).
- Holding a loaded rifle: a bear is not a target unless it is legal game in season, or a real threat to life. Follow up rules: [Rule Book: unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts/0) (Tip).
```

## A bear saying back off

```diagram fs-bc-warning
```

- These are warnings, not attacks. Give it room (75%).

```more warning signs in full
- Jaw popping, moaning, woofing, stomping and bluff charging are signs of an agitated bear (75%, WildSafeBC).
- Most bluff charges stop short of contact (75%, WildSafeBC).
- A defensive bear wants you gone. Backing away slowly gives it what it wants (Tip).
```

## Bear spray by distance

```diagram fs-bc-spray-ranges
```

```anim gr-bear-spray Aim low, sweep up.
```

```video
yt-bear-spray-parks | Remove the clip, aim low, spray a cloud between you and the bear.
```

- Spray on the chest or hip, never in the pack (75%).

```more spray in full
- WildSafeBC: carry it where you can reach it fast, like a hip holster, not in your pack. Use it when the bear is within about 5 to 10 m (75%).
- Alberta guidance by distance (75%):
  - 9 to 15 m: a half to 1 second warning blast, aimed slightly down.
  - 6 to 9 m: 1 to 2 second bursts, aimed slightly down in front of the head, until it leaves.
  - 0 to 6 m: 1 to 2 second bursts at the head, into the nose and mouth, until it leaves.
  - Keep some in reserve. After spraying, back away while watching the bear. Bears may be drawn to spray residue.
- Do not spray into the wind (70%, [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0)).
- Can range: about 3 to 10 m depending on the can. Read yours (70%).
- Cold weakens spray pressure (Tip: keep it inside your jacket on frozen mornings, VERIFY on your can's label).
```

## Spray or rifle?

```diagram fs-bc-spray-gun
```

- Studies found spray stopped bears more often than guns (70%).

```more the studies in full
- Smith and Herrero (2008): bear spray stopped aggressive bear behaviour in about 92% of cases (70%, news coverage via search; some coverage says 90%).
- Firearms: one report gives 67%; a 2014 article quoting the firearms paper gives 84% for handguns and 76% for rifles (65%, figures differ by source).
- The firearms study found people with guns had about the same injury rate in close encounters whether or not they fired (65%).
- Stopping a charging bear with bullets took on average about 4 hits in that work (60%).
- My lean: spray in hand first, rifle as backup. Your partner must never be in the line of fire (Tip).
```

## Attack: defensive or predatory?

```diagram fs-bc-attack-types
```

```diagram fs-bc-play-dead
```

- Defensive: lie still once it makes contact. Predatory: fight (75%).

```more attacks in full
- **Defensive** (usually a surprise, the bear protecting itself, food or cubs): if knocked down, lie still on your stomach, hands clasped over the back of your head, feet slightly apart. Stay still until the bear has left (75%, WildSafeBC).
- **Predatory** (the attack does not stop and the bear tries to eat you): fight back with everything, at the face, eyes and nose (75%).
- Parks Canada adds: a bear that stalks you or comes at night is predatory; fight (75%, from [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0)).
- Keep your pack on; it protects your back (Tip).
```

## The kill site

```steps Kill site safety in 4 steps
fs-bc-kill-1 | One works and talks, one watches.
fs-bc-kill-2 | Meat at least 200 m from the gut pile.
fs-bc-kill-3 | Hang it 3.5 m up, 1.5 m from trunks, in view.
fs-bc-kill-4 | Return upwind. Glass it first.
```

- Get the meat out fast. Avoid dressing at dusk if you can (75%).

```more kill site in full
- Alberta: Bears and hunters (70% for BC use; same bears, other province's advice):
  - Make noise while field dressing; scent may draw a bear. Remove the carcass as quickly as possible.
  - Move the carcass at least 200 m from the gut pile before you leave, if you can. Bears often go to the gut pile first.
  - Hang it at least 3.5 m (11.5 ft) off the ground and 1.5 m (5 ft) from nearby trees, where you can see it from a distance.
  - Coming back: approach from upwind, check with binoculars, make noise.
  - Bear at the site, or the carcass moved or buried? Do not approach. Leave and report.
  - Do not drag the carcass back to camp: it lays a scent trail to you.
- Montana says at least 100 yards (91 m) from the gut pile and hang 10 ft (3 m) up (65%). Rules differ by place; the bigger number is safer (Tip).
- Idaho: do not try to scare a bear off a carcass (65%).
- BC law: a carcass is an attractant. Do not leave one where people are, or likely to be, in a way that draws dangerous wildlife (99%, see [Rule Book: after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill/0)).
```

## Going back for meat

```anim fs-bc-return Stop, glass and call out before you walk in.
```

- Put something bright on the meat so you can see from afar if it moved (65%).

```more more detail
- Montana State University guide: put something noticeable on the carcass so you can tell if it was disturbed (65%).
- Leave the area if the carcass is moved or buried: a bear has claimed it (70%).
- Plan the pack out so no meat sits overnight if you can. Dusk, night and dawn are the high risk times (75%). See [Packing out](#/s/packing-out/0).
```

## Camp in bear country

```diagram fs-bc-camp
```

- Food, cooking and meat at least 100 m from where you sleep (75%).

```more camp in full
- Alberta guidance (75%): food and toiletries in airtight containers at least 100 m from where you sleep; hang food at least 3.5 m up and 1.5 m from trees; harvested animals at least 100 m from camp; cook and wash up at least 100 m away and downwind; never keep food in the tent.
- It is unlawful in BC to intentionally feed dangerous wildlife, including bears and cougars (99%, see [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods/0)).
- The truck: lock food and coolers inside, not in the box (Tip).
```

## Cougars

```photo cougar
```

```steps Cougar encounter in 4 steps
fs-bc-cougar-1 | Never run. Never turn your back.
fs-bc-cougar-2 | Look big, eye contact, loud voice.
fs-bc-cougar-3 | Back away slowly. Leave it an exit.
fs-bc-cougar-4 | Attacked? Fight. Face and eyes.
```

- Cougars rarely attack people. Never play dead with one (75%).

```more cougars in full
- WildSafeBC (75%):
  - Stay calm, never run. Look as large as possible and back away slowly.
  - Keep it in view and leave it a clear exit. No sudden moves, do not turn your back.
  - Watching you: keep eye contact, speak loud and firm. Bear spray out, safety off.
  - Aggressive or following: be aggressive back. Yell, make noise, arm yourself with sticks or rocks without crouching down.
  - Attacked: fight back, aim for the face and eyes. Spray, rocks, sticks.
  - Found a kill site (often covered with debris)? Leave at once and report it.
- Cougars can sprint over 70 km/h (70%). The Conservation Officer Service gets about 2,500 cougar calls a year, many of them other animals (70%).
- Cougar tracks: big round pads, usually no claw marks (75%, see [Tracks](#/s/tracks/0)).
```

## Report it

```diagram fs-bc-flow
```

- Aggressive bear or cougar, attack, or a kill site near people: call 1 877 952 7277 (99%).

```more who to call
- RAPP (Report All Poachers and Polluters) line: 1 877 952 7277, open 24/7, for wildlife and human conflicts where public safety is at risk (99%, synopsis page 2, from [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0)).
- WildSafeBC lists the same number for the Conservation Officer Service: report aggressive behaviour, kill sites, urban sightings (75%).
- Injury: 911 first (75%).
- Killed a bear or cougar in self defence: report it to an officer promptly (99%, see [Rule Book: unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts/0)).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fs-bc-kill-4
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Quiet when you hunt, loud when you work. And look before you walk back to your meat.

> [!mistake]
> - Spray in the pack.
> - Walking straight back to the hanging meat at dusk.
> - Running from a bear or cougar.

```more the mistakes in full
- Spray buried in the pack, or the safety clip stuck with cold fingers (75%).
- Walking straight in to the kill without glassing or calling out (70%).
- Dragging a carcass back to camp (70%).
- Running (75%).
- Playing dead with a cougar or a predatory bear (75%).
- Calling elk or predators in thick cover with no partner watching your back (Tip).
```

> [!field]
> With an inert practice can, draw and fire in under 2 seconds from the chest holster, in gloves. Then walk a creek bottom and call out every minute.

## Memory hooks

| Hook | Means |
|---|---|
| Never run | Bears and cougars |
| Brown, lie down. Black, fight back | Old rhyme. Too simple: use defensive or predatory |
| Defensive: lie still. Predatory: fight | The real rule |
| Cougar: big, loud, fight | Never play dead |
| 200 and 3.5 | 200 m from guts, 3.5 m up |
| Upwind, glass, shout | Returning to meat |
| 952 7277 | The RAPP line ends |

```more about the old rhyme
- "If it's brown, lie down; if it's black, fight back" is a common rhyme, but colour does not tell the species and the right action depends on the attack type (Tip; colour point 75%).
- Use instead: defensive (surprised, cubs, food): lie still once it makes contact. Predatory (stalks, keeps coming, at night): fight (75%).
```
