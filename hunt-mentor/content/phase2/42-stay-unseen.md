> [!why]
> A deer that sees you first is gone. Expensive camo is not the answer: outline, flash, shadow, skyline and above all movement give you away. Fix those and plain wool works.

```diagram fn-cam-six
```

- Six ways you get seen. Movement is number one (Tip).
- Pattern and stillness beat colour (75%).

```more what this lesson adds
- How deer see and why: [Deer senses and mind](#/s/deer-mind/0). How to move: [Moving quietly](#/s/still-hunting/0) and [Stalking footwork](#/s/stalking-footwork/0).
- The six words come from military concealment teaching: the Australian Army lists shape, shine, shadow and silhouette among its "Seven S's of Tactical Concealment"; older guides add spacing and movement (70%, Australian Army CoVE and older field guides, search preview). Using them for hunting is Tip.
- Scent and sound are the other two ways: see [Wind mastery](#/s/wind-mastery/0) and Moving quietly.
```

## Key points

| Fix | How (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Shape | Brush in front, tree behind, sit low |
| Shine | Mask or paint, matte gear, glasses in shade |
| Shadow | Sit in shade, watch where yours falls |
| Silhouette | Never on a skyline |
| Spacing | Two hunters move one at a time |
| Movement | Move when heads are down, freeze when up |
| Colour | Avoid blue; orange looks dull to deer (75%) |

```more the key points in full
1. Deer have two cone types, not three, and lack the long wavelength "red" cone: likely red green colour blind like some people (75%, National Deer Association on the University of Georgia study, Jacobs and others).
2. They likely tell blue from red, but not green from red or orange from red (75%). Clothing in green, red or orange is equal to them; blue is a disadvantage (75%).
3. "The actual color of the fabric is relatively unimportant as long as the pattern blends with your surroundings." (75%, NDA)
4. Scent and movement matter far more than clothing colour or UV brighteners (75%, NDA).
5. Deer lack the UV filter in the human eye and see UV better than we do, but poorer fine detail (75%, NDA). A 2014 behaviour study found some UV sensitivity (70%, Wildlife Society Bulletin, search preview).
6. More rods than people: better night vision, poorer daytime colour vision (75%, NDA).
```

## Shape, shine, shadow, silhouette, spacing, movement

```steps The six, one at a time
fn-cam-s1 | Shape: break up your outline with brush.
fn-cam-s2 | Shine: kill the flash from face, glasses and gun.
fn-cam-s3 | Shadow: sit in shade, watch where yours falls.
fn-cam-s4 | Silhouette: never on the skyline.
fn-cam-s5 | Spacing: two hunters, one shape at a time.
fn-cam-s6 | Movement: head down, move. Head up, freeze.
```

- Run through the six every time you sit (Tip).

```more the six in full
- **Shape:** a person's outline, head and shoulders above a straight body, is what animals learn to fear (Tip). Brush in front, a tree behind, sit or kneel to lose the vertical line (Tip).
- **Shine:** skin, glasses, rifle bluing, scope lenses and phone screens flash in low sun (Tip). Face mask or paint, gloves, matte finishes, a scope cover, glasses in the shade of a brim (Tip). Binocular glare: see [Glassing](#/s/glassing/0).
- **Shadow:** your shadow moves when you move and stretches long at dawn and dusk (Tip). Sit inside shade, not at its edge (Tip).
- **Silhouette:** a figure on a ridge against the sky is visible a long way (Tip). Cross ridges at a low spot, crawl the last metres, glass from below the crest (Tip).
- **Spacing:** even, regular shapes look man made; nature is random (70%, military concealment teaching). Two hunters side by side are two shapes; move one at a time (Tip).
- **Movement:** deer have "an amazing ability to detect movement" (75%, NDA, hunter consensus). Move when the head is down or behind cover; freeze when it looks (Tip; see Moving quietly).
```

## What a deer likely sees

```diagram fn-cam-colours
```

- Blue pops. Orange, red and green look like dull browns and greys (75%).
- The drawing is an illustration, not a measurement (Tip).

```more colour in full
- Two cone pigments: short wavelength sensitivity around 450 to 460 nm (blue) and middle wavelength around 537 nm in white tailed deer (75%, Jacobs and others, via search previews).
- Missing the long wavelength cone: red and orange fall in a range deer barely separate from green and brown (75%).
- Blue jeans and blue jackets are a bad idea (75%, NDA: "disadvantaged in blue").
- Mule deer were not in these studies. Assumption: similar, as both are deer (Tip).
- Deeper detail: [Deer senses and mind](#/s/deer-mind/0).
```

## Blaze orange: safety first

```diagram fn-cam-orange
```

- Orange looks dull to a deer but bright to other hunters (75%).
- Wear it walking in and out, and when others hunt nearby (Tip).

```more blaze orange in full
- BC rule: the Hunt Mentor Rule Book lists no blaze orange clothing rule for BC hunters (85%, my reading). VERIFY in the synopsis before relying on it.
- Other places do require it (New Brunswick, Washington State, Missouri), so check the rules when you hunt outside BC (85%, those regulations, search preview). See the trip lessons.
- Deer see orange as dull, close to brown or grey; motion and outline give you away, not the colour (75%, see Deer senses and mind).
- Tip: an orange toque and vest for walking, packed away when you sit in a safe spot with no other hunters around. Wrap a carried deer or turkey in orange.
- Firearm safety with your brother: [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety/0).
```

## Shine: one turn of the head

```anim fn-cam-glint Low sun plus glasses equals a flash a deer sees from far away.
```

- Brim low, glasses shaded, turn your head slowly (Tip).

```more shine fixes
- Face: mask, net or paint (Tip).
- Hands: thin gloves, they move the most (Tip).
- Rifle and scope: matte finish or a cover, lens caps on while walking (Tip).
- Phone: screen off and away when deer are close; a lit screen in shade is a flash (Tip).
- Glass: shade the objective lenses of binoculars with your hand or a hood in low sun (Tip). See [Glassing](#/s/glassing/0).
```

## Sit in front of something bigger

```diagram fn-cam-backdrop
```

- Back against a tree, rock or bush wider than you (Tip).
- A little brush in front breaks your middle (Tip).

```more seats in full
- The backdrop hides your outline; the brush in front hides your hands moving (Tip).
- Shade over the seat hides shine and your shadow (Tip).
- Leave a clear shooting lane through the brush in front. Clear it in the off season or quietly, not on the morning (Tip).
- Sit, do not stand. A seated hunter is a smaller, wider, less human shape (Tip).
- Check the wind before you choose a seat: [Wind mastery](#/s/wind-mastery/0).
```

## UV brighteners: a cheap check

```steps Check your hunting clothes
fn-cam-uv-1 | Dark room, small UV flashlight.
fn-cam-uv-2 | A blue white glow means brighteners.
fn-cam-uv-3 | Wash in a detergent without them (Tip).
```

- Evidence is mixed. It costs little to check (Tip).

```more UV in full
- Deer lack the UV filter in the human lens and likely see UV better than people (75%, NDA). The 1990s retina work found little UV response; a 2014 behaviour study with 7 deer found some sensitivity (70%, search previews).
- No direct test found that UV brightened clothing spooks deer (60%: not a fact). Treat it as cheap insurance, labelled Tip.
- Many household detergents add optical brighteners (Tip). Read the label or test with the light.
```

## Two hunters

```diagram fn-cam-s5
```

- One moves, one watches. Then swap (Tip).
- Hand signals, not voices (Tip).

```more two hunter craft
- Moving together doubles the shapes and the noise (Tip).
- The watcher keeps the animal in view and signals when its head goes up (Tip).
- Agree signals at the truck: stop, down, come, deer, buck, shoot lane clear (Tip).
- Safety first: zones of fire and where each of you is at all times. See [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety/0).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram hf-peek
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Camo hides you when you are still. Nothing hides you when you move at the wrong time.

> [!mistake]
> - Standing on the ridge top to glass.
> - Bare face and hands in low sun.
> - Fidgeting while a deer is looking.

```more the mistakes in full
- Standing on the crest to glass, skylined for a kilometre.
- Bare face, bright hands and glasses catching the low October sun.
- Scratching, checking the phone or lifting binoculars while a deer stares your way.
```

> [!field]
> Have your brother sit in three spots: open, against a tree, against a tree in shade with brush in front. Walk away 100 m and glass each one. Then swap. Score which you saw first.

```checklist fn-unseen
Nothing blue in my hunting clothes
Face and hands covered or shaded
Scope and binocular glare checked in low sun
Seat has a backdrop and shade
Never glassed from the skyline today
Froze every time a deer looked up
```
