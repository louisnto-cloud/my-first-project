> The forecast is for the valley. You are on the ridge. Clouds, pressure and wind give you hours of warning about snow, rain, thunder and wind shifts, so you change the plan before the weather changes it for you.

## Key points

| Sign | Usually means |
|---|---|
| Cirrus, then a halo, then grey | Warm front, rain or snow within about a day (70%) |
| Puffy clouds growing tall | Showers or thunder later (85%) |
| Anvil top | Thunderstorm (95%) |
| Pressure falling | Low or front coming (75%) |
| Wind swings to W or NW, colder | Cold front passed (70%) |
| Clear, calm night | Frost, valley fog, cold dawn (75%) |
| Higher up | Colder: rain below, snow above (85%) |

```more the key points in full
1. Ten cloud types in three levels (95%, NOAA).
2. Warm front sequence: cirrus, cirrostratus (halo), altostratus, nimbostratus (70%, Met Office and textbook sources agree on order, not timing).
3. Cumulonimbus is "the thunderstorm cloud", tall with an anvil shaped top (95%, NOAA). It can bring hail (95%).
4. Behind a cold front winds shift from south to west or northwest, the dewpoint drops and pressure rises (70%, NWS text via secondary source).
5. With your back to the wind, low pressure is on your left in the northern hemisphere (75%, Buys Ballot's law, Wikipedia).
6. Cold air drains down slopes at night and pools in valleys (75%, USDA Forest Service Fire Weather handbook via Forest Encyclopedia).
7. Thunder heard? Go to a hard topped vehicle and wait 30 minutes after the last thunder (85%, US National Weather Service).
```

## A warm front, step by step

```steps Four skies before the rain
fn-wx-wf-1 | Cirrus streaks: often the first sign.
fn-wx-wf-2 | A halo round the sun or moon: thin cirrostratus.
fn-wx-wf-3 | Grey sheet, sun like frosted glass: altostratus.
fn-wx-wf-4 | Dark low grey, steady rain or snow: nimbostratus.
```

- The sky lowers and thickens over hours (70%).

```more warm fronts in full
- Order: cirrus, cirrostratus, altostratus, nimbostratus (70%).
- The Met Office says cirrostratus can produce rings, spots or arcs of light around the sun or moon, and that one type means an incoming warm front will bring persistent rain within a day (75%, Met Office cirrostratus page, search preview).
- Timing varies: sources give 12 to 24 hours from halo to rain, some less (60%). Treat it as "within about a day".
- Ahead of a warm front pressure falls steadily and the rain is widespread and steady (70%, secondary source).
- Hunter use (Tip): halo at evening glassing means plan B for tomorrow, rain gear in the pack, meat care plan for wet weather.
```

## Cold fronts: fast and gusty

```diagram fn-wx-coldfront
```

- Tall clouds, gusts, a short burst of rain or snow (70%).
- After: wind W or NW, colder, clearing, pressure rising (70%).

```more cold fronts in full
- Before: winds often from the south, warm and humid (70%).
- At the front: towering clouds, gusty winds, heavy showers or snow squalls (70%).
- After: wind shifts west or northwest, temperature and dewpoint drop, pressure rises, skies clear (70%, NWS text via secondary source).
- In the mountains, terrain channels wind and can hide the classic wind shift (75%, Fire Weather handbook: "the effect of the mountains on the windflow usually overshadows this").
- Hunter use (Tip): the clear, cold morning after a cold front is a classic glassing morning. Snow after it brings fresh tracks; see [Snow tracking](#/s/snow-tracking/0).
```

## Puffy clouds that keep growing

```anim fn-wx-cbuild A cumulus that keeps growing by noon can be a thunderstorm by afternoon.
```

- Flat bottomed puffs that stay small: fair weather (70%).
- Puffs that tower and spread an anvil: thunder, hail, gusts (95%).

```more thunder in full
- Cumulonimbus: heavy, dense, shaped like a mountain or huge tower, with an anvil or plume top and a dark base; can bring hail (95%, NOAA).
- "When thunder roars, go indoors." Shelter in a substantial building or a hard topped vehicle and stay until 30 minutes after the last thunder (85%, US National Weather Service).
- Lightning can strike well away from the rain (70%, US Army safety document).
- Thunderstorms are less common in late October than in summer (Tip). Still: get off ridges and away from lone trees when you hear thunder.
```

## Pressure trend

```diagram fn-wx-pressure
```

- Falling: weather coming. Rising: clearing, often colder (75%).
- The Hunt Map Weather sheet shows the 3 hour trend (99%).

```more pressure in full
- Hunt Map, Weather and wind: "Pressure ... hPa (hectopascals), rising, falling or steady over the last 3 hours", from a weather model (99%, app; a change of more than 1 hPa counts as rising or falling).
- Phone or watch barometer: sitting still, if your altitude reading climbs, the pressure is falling (85%, physics: about 8 m of altitude per hPa near sea level).
- Pressure trends are general rules. Mountains and local winds can fool them (Tip).
- Do deer care about pressure? The evidence is weak and mixed (65 to 80%). See [Weather, moon and what moves game](#/s/weather-moon/0).
```

## Back to the wind

```diagram fn-wx-buys
```

- Back to the wind: low pressure on your left (75%).
- The low is where the bad weather lives.

```more Buys Ballot's law in full
- In the northern hemisphere, with your back to the wind, low pressure is to the left and high pressure to the right (75%, Wikipedia, Buys Ballot's law, 1857).
- Near the ground and in mountains it is approximate (75%). Use the upper clouds' direction for a better read (Tip).
- Use (Tip): if the low is to your west, weather is usually on its way, since systems here mostly move from the Pacific eastward. VERIFY with the forecast.
```

## Cold air pools at night

```diagram fn-wx-inversion
```

- Clear calm nights: cold air drains down into valleys (75%).
- Morning fog or frost below, milder and clear above (Tip).

```more inversions in full
- Downslope flow starts first along the valley floor in the evening, deepens through the night and becomes the down valley wind; as cold air pools, pressure rises in the valley (75%, Fire Weather handbook via Forest Encyclopedia).
- Down valley flow lasts through the night and weakens after sunrise (75%).
- Kamloops sits in a deep valley. Fall mornings often have valley fog or haze (Tip).
- Hunter use (Tip): climb above the fog to glass; the sun hits upper slopes first and deer move to it on cold mornings. See [Sun and shade in the Hunt Map](#/s/hunt-map-pro/0).
```

## Higher is colder

```diagram fn-wx-lapse
```

- About 6.5°C colder per 1,000 m up in standard air (85%).
- Rain at the truck can be snow on the ridge (85%).

```more temperature and height in full
- Standard air cools about 6.5°C per 1,000 m of height (85%, International Standard Atmosphere). Real days vary, and inversions flip it at night.
- Example: 8°C at a 400 m valley floor is about 1.5°C on a 1,400 m ridge (85%, maths).
- Wind makes it feel colder still. See hypothermia in [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0).
- Pack for the top of the hunt, not the truck (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fn-wx-wf-2
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Ring around the moon, rain or snow soon. Pack the wet gear and hunt the morning.

> [!mistake]
> - Reading the valley forecast and dressing for the valley.
> - Ignoring a halo and a falling barometer the night before.
> - Staying on a ridge while a cloud grows an anvil.

```more the mistakes in full
- Dressing for the truck's temperature, not the ridge 1,000 m higher.
- Ignoring the halo at evening glassing and the falling pressure: no rain gear the next day, a soaked deer and wet meat.
- Staying on an open ridge while a cloud grows tall and dark overhead.
```
