> [!why]
> A rusty bore, a frozen firing pin or a snow plugged barrel can cost you the deer or your face. Ten minutes after each hunt prevents all three.

```diagram ff-gc-rust
```

- Tikka: corrosion can begin within 24 hours (95%).
- Clean after every use, even if you never fired (95%).

```more what the manuals say
- Tikka: "Routine cleaning should always take place right after use of the rifle (even without shooting). Maintenance should not be neglected even in case of the 'All weather' or 'Stainless' models. Corrosion can begin within 24 hours if the rifle is not cleaned after use" (95%, Tikka manual).
- Your Arctic is stainless with a laminated stock (95%, Sako). Stainless resists rust; it is not rust proof (Tip).
- CZ: clean each time you use it, if it gets wet, as soon as possible after shooting, at least once a year (95%, CZ manual).
- Shotgun: your model is not chosen yet. Follow its manual; the steps here are general (Tip).
```

## Key points

```diagram ff-gc-kit
```

- Rod from the breech, through a guide (95%).
- Wet: dry it warm, out of the case (95%).
- Deep cold: less oil, or a cold rated lube (95%).

```more all key points
- Use a straight, smooth one piece rod with a bore guide, and clean only in the direction the bullet travels (95%, Tikka manual).
- Leave a thin coat of oil in the chamber for storage, and wipe it dry before shooting (95%, Tikka manual).
- Use a CLP oil (clean, lubricate, protect) (95%, Tikka manual).
- Grease lightly: locking lugs, cocking cam, cocking piece head (95%, Tikka manual).
- Check action screws after cleaning: 7 Nm (newton metres) with a metal trigger guard (95%, Tikka manual).
- After any fall in snow or mud: unload and look through the bore before firing (95%, Tikka obstruction check).
- Transport: unloaded in any vehicle or boat, by BC and federal rules. See the transport screen (99%).
```

## Routine clean, step by step

```steps Clean the Tikka in 6 steps
ff-gc-clean-1 | Unload. Bolt and magazine out.
ff-gc-clean-2 | Wet? Dry it warm, out of the case.
ff-gc-clean-3 | Solvent brush, breech to muzzle, 10 to 15 strokes.
ff-gc-clean-4 | Patches one way until clean.
ff-gc-clean-5 | Bolt face, chamber, receiver.
ff-gc-clean-6 | Thin oil. Check the action screws.
```

- About 10 minutes once you know it (Tip).

```more Tikka's 11 steps in full
1. Ensure the rifle is unloaded; remove bolt and magazine (95%).
2. If used in wet conditions, dry it with compressed air or let it dry in a warm, dry place without a protective cover (95%).
3. Straight, sturdy, smooth rod, bronze brush soaked in bore solvent, push pull about 10 to 15 times. Use a rod guide (95%).
4. Clean felt pellets wet with solvent, one after another, until they come out clean, about 5 to 10. Only in the bullet direction (95%).
5. Clean the inside of the receiver with pellets or a brush and solvent (95%).
6. Small solvent wet brush on the bolt (95%).
7. Nylon brush or chamber pellet with solvent for the chamber (95%).
8. Pellet wet with rust preventive oil through the bore. Thin oil in the chamber, and wipe it dry before shooting (95%).
9. Very light rust preventive oil on touching surfaces of receiver and bolt (95%).
10. Wipe the stock dry. Wooden stocks: light stock oil (95%).
11. Check the receiver screws (95%).
- Source: Tikka T1x and T3x manual v5.3, routine cleaning and maintenance (95%).
```

## Breech in, bullet direction

```diagram ff-gc-breech
```

- Never push a rod in from the muzzle on the Tikka or CZ (95%).
- A bore guide keeps the rod off the throat (95%).

```more why the direction matters
- Tikka: "Clean only in the direction of bullet movement" and use a guide so the rod does not drag on the bore and receiver (95%).
- CZ: insert rod and brush from the chamber "to prevent wear or damage to the muzzle." Push the brush all the way through before reversing (95%).
- The crown is the last thing the bullet touches. A nick there opens groups (70%, see [how a rifle works](#/s/rifle-mechanics/0)).
```

## Grease and oil

```diagram ff-gc-grease
```

- Grease: lugs, cocking cam, cocking piece head (95%).
- Oil: a thin film everywhere else (95%).

```more lubrication in full
- Tikka asks that the locking lug surfaces, cocking cam and head of the cocking piece be lightly greased (95%).
- New rifle: wipe the factory oil from the bore with a dry patch from the breech, and clean extra oil from bolt and chamber before first use (95%, Tikka manual).
- More oil is not better: oil in the bolt collects grit and thickens in cold (Tip; see the cold screen for the manuals' words).
- CZ: never put bore cleaner on other parts; it can stay in joints and cause corrosion (95%).
```

## Copper fouling and the yearly service

```diagram ff-rm-bedding
```

- Once a year, or more with heavy use (95%).
- Remove copper fouling from the bore (95%).

```more Tikka periodic maintenance in full
At least once a year, more often with heavy use or severe conditions, and before long storage (95%, Tikka manual):
1. Do the routine clean.
2. Remove copper fouling from the bore with a copper solvent, following its maker's instructions. "Badly fouled barrels can shoot inaccurately and will corrode much easier" (95%).
3. Lubricate all metal surfaces with a lightly oiled rag.
4. Lubricate sling swivel mounts and check them.
5. Strip the bolt as the manual shows, clean, lightly oil, grease lugs, cam and cocking piece head.
6. Reassemble the bolt and check extractor and ejector.
7. Check sights and scope mounts.
8. Clean accessories.
9. Check all screws, including stock and receiver screws.
10. Reassemble and check function.
11. Wooden stocks: stock oil.
- Spare parts: original factory parts only (95%).
```

## Torque card

```diagram ff-gc-torque
```

- Tikka action: 7 Nm metal guard, max 5 Nm plastic (95%).
- CZ 457 stock screws: 3.5 Nm (95%).

```more torque notes
- Scope rings: use your ring maker's number. Examples are in [choosing and mounting your scope](#/s/scope-guide/0) (60 to 70%).
- Tikka length of pull spacers: do not over tighten recoil pad screws or the stock can be ruined (95%).
- A paint pen line across each screw head shows movement at a glance (Tip).
```

## The CZ 457

```diagram ff-rf-clean
```

- Patches until clean. Very dirty: solvent, wait about 10 min, scrub (95%).
- Lots of rounds fired: a gunsmith cleans the bolt (95%).

```more CZ care in full
- Strip for cleaning: magazine out, safety off, open the bolt, press the bolt catch, pull the bolt out. "No further disassembly is needed for routine maintenance" (95%).
- "When a great number of cartridges has been fired it is advisable to hand the gun to a competent gunsmith for cleaning of the bolt" (95%).
- Other parts: dry cloth, old toothbrush, copper bristle brush or wooden scraper; preserving or paraffin oil (95%).
- Preserve: wipe clean, dry parts with gun oil, remove excess, and run a dry patch before shooting (95%).
- More on the CZ: [the .22 rimfire](#/s/rimfire-22/0).
```

## The shotgun

```steps Clean a shotgun in 4 steps
ff-gc-sg-1 | Unload. Action open. Prove it.
ff-gc-sg-2 | Choke out. Clean and oil the threads.
ff-gc-sg-3 | Brush, patches, light oil in the bore.
ff-gc-sg-4 | Choke back in, snug.
```

- A seized choke is a gunsmith job. Pull it often (85%).

```more shotgun care in full
- Trulock warns that steel shot can cause "choke creep" and that over time a choke can lock in the barrel (85%). Removing, cleaning and lightly oiling the threads regularly is common advice (Tip).
- Duck hunting means wet guns: dry, then the routine clean the same day (Tip; Tikka's 24 hour corrosion warning shows how fast rust starts on rifles).
- Plug: make sure it is back in after cleaning, before any duck or upland hunt (99%, plug rule in the [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods/0)).
- Semi autos and pumps need their own action cleaning. Follow your gun's manual (Tip).
```

## Wet weather

```diagram ff-gc-wet
```

- Never leave a wet gun in a case (95%).
- Leather and fabric cases attract moisture (95%).

```more wet care in full
- Tikka: dry a wet rifle with compressed air or in a warm, dry place "without any protective cover i.e. drag bag" (95%).
- Tikka storage: "Do not store the rifle in a leather or fabric case. These materials attract moisture, even though they may appear to be perfectly dry" (95%).
- In the field: a muzzle cover or tape over the muzzle keeps rain out (Tip). Firing through thin tape is common practice; VERIFY with your rifle's maker before relying on it.
- Scope: keep caps on until you need them. Wipe lenses with a lens cloth, not your shirt (Tip). See [optics](#/s/optics-mastery/0).
```

## Cold weather

```diagram ff-gc-cold
```

- CZ: in very low temperatures, remove oil or use a cold lube (95%).
- Tikka: clean oil out of the bolt for cold hunts (95%).

```more cold care in full
- CZ: "At very low temperatures remove the oil from all accessible parts or apply a lubricant especially for these conditions" (95%).
- Tikka: "If you have reason to believe that oil may have penetrated the bolt, you should clean it in order to retain its smooth operation. This is particularly important if you use the rifle in cold or harsh weather" (95%).
- Thick grease for long storage only, removed before use (95%, CZ).
- Gloves: practise the safety and trigger with your winter gloves on (Tip).
- Batteries in rangefinders and lighted reticles fade in cold. Keep spares in an inside pocket (Tip).
```

## Condensation (Tip)

```anim ff-gc-condense Cold metal into a warm room beads with water.
```

- Cold steel into a warm room sweats (Tip).
- Dry it fully before it goes in the safe (Tip).

```more condensation tips
- Warm, moist air meets cold metal and leaves water on it (Tip, basic physics; no firearm manual found on this).
- Two common approaches: leave the rifle cased in a cold, secure place overnight; or bring it in, uncase it, and dry and oil it once it warms (Tip). Storage law still applies.
- Scope fog: warm breath on a cold eyepiece fogs it. Breathe down and away (Tip).
```

## Snow in the barrel

```diagram ff-gc-snow
```

- Any fall or muzzle dip: unload and look (95%).
- An obstruction is a gunsmith job (95%).

```more obstruction check in full
- Tikka: check the bore for obstructions before use; "serious injury can result" if fired with an obstruction (95%).
- Check: magazine out, bolt out, look through from the rear. Any obstruction, even minor: a gunsmith removes it, then inspect again before firing (95%).
- Field reality: snow you can see at the muzzle can often be cleared with a cleaning rod pushed from the breech, rifle unloaded. Mud or anything stuck: do not shoot (Tip).
- A take down rod or pull through in the pack makes this possible on the hill (Tip).
```

## Transport and storage

```diagram gr-gun-transport
```

- No loaded firearm in or on a vehicle or boat (99%).
- Unattended vehicle: locked trunk, or out of sight with the car locked (99%).

```more the rules, as the app's safety and Rule Book lessons state them
- BC: you must not transport, carry or store a loaded firearm in or on a vehicle or boat (99%, Firearm Violence Prevention Act section 4(1), via the [firearm safety refresher](#/s/firearm-safety/0)).
- Rule Book item 28: no live ammunition in the breech or an attached magazine in or on a motor vehicle and other conveyances (99%, [Rule Book: unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts/0)).
- Boats: a BC exemption allows a loaded firearm only in a boat with no motor, or one the motor is not moving, when hunting or retrieving wildlife (95%, firearm safety refresher).
- Unloaded means no cartridge in the chamber and none in a magazine attached to or inserted in the firearm (99%, federal transport regulations section 1).
- Leaving the vehicle: non restricted firearms locked in the trunk or a lockable compartment; no trunk, out of sight and the vehicle locked (99%, federal transport regulations section 10(2)).
- Home storage law is not covered in this app yet: VERIFY with the RCMP Canadian Firearms Program. Tikka's advice: lock the firearm away and store ammunition in a separate locked place (95%, manufacturer advice).
- After a bumpy ATV or truck ride: check scope screws and confirm zero if anything moved (Tip).
```

```diagram ff-rm-stop
```

- Never ship a loaded firearm (95%).


## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Wet gun left in its case overnight | Out of the case, dry, then clean |
| Rod in from the muzzle | Breech, guide, bullet direction |
| Oil soaked bolt in deep cold | Clean it, use little or cold rated lube |

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Clean it tonight, and it shoots tomorrow.

```more the mistakes in full
- Leaving a wet rifle zipped in a soft case after a rainy hunt.
- Pushing a rod in from the muzzle and nicking the crown.
- Drowning the bolt in oil before a cold hunt.
```

> [!field]
> After every hunt, before supper: unload check, wipe down, bore patch, light oil, screws checked. Log it in the journal.

```checklist ff-gc-after-hunt
Unloaded and proved
Out of the case to dry if wet
Bore: brush, patches, light oil
Bolt face and chamber wiped
Action and ring screws checked
Chamber wiped dry before the next hunt
Shotgun: choke out, threads clean, plug in
```
