> [!why]
> An unregistered quad, a loaded rifle on the rack or a ride into a closed area each cost more than the hunt. The rules are short. Learn them once.

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Most lines here were read directly in the official synopsis, the Off Road Vehicle Act and its regulation, and ICBC (Insurance Corporation of British Columbia) pages. Those lines show 95% or more.
- [[ATV]] (all terrain vehicle) means a quad here. [[ORV]] (Off Road Vehicle) is the law's wider word: quads, dirt bikes, side by sides and snowmobiles (98%).
- Your ATV's registration status is unknown. The checklist at the end sorts that out.

## Three sets of rules

| Law | What it covers | Where it applies |
|---|---|---|
| Off Road Vehicle Act and Regulation | Registration, plate, helmet, lights, photo ID (identification), kids (98%) | Crown land, including resource roads (98%) |
| Motor Vehicle Act, through ICBC | Licence, insurance and permits for any ride on or across a public road (95%) | Public roads and highways |
| Wildlife Act, in the [[Synopsis]] | Loaded firearms on a conveyance, shooting from a vehicle, Motor Vehicle Closed Areas (98%) | Everywhere you hunt |

- The ORV Act does not apply on a highway as defined in the Motor Vehicle Act (98%). Section 3.
- A Forest Service Road ([[FSR]]) is Crown land, not a highway, so the ORV Act applies there (90%). Most gravel roads are FSRs. A few gravel roads are highways. Ask the district office if unsure (90%).
- No ORV on private land without the consent of the owner, lessee or occupant (98%). Section 17.

## Registration and plate

- Since 1 November 2015 an ORV used on Crown land must be registered with ICBC and display its plate or sticker (98%).
- Registration is a one time fee of $48 for plate and registration combined (95%). The regulation lists $30 registration plus $18 plate or sticker (98%).
- Register at any ICBC Autoplan broker (95%). Buying a used quad: register the transfer within 10 days, $28 (98%).
- Plate or sticker goes on the front, back or left side, horizontal, clean and readable (98%).
- Carry a copy of the certificate of registration on the quad or on you. Show it to an officer on request (98%).
- Fine for an unregistered ORV on Crown land: $230 (95%).

## Insurance and driver's licence

- On an FSR: a valid driver's licence and at least $200,000 third party liability insurance, from ICBC or another insurer (98%).
"While travelling on a Forest Service Road, operators of ATVs are required to hold a valid driver's licence and carry a minimum of $200,000 third-party liability insurance." Synopsis page 9 (98%).
- On other Crown land: liability insurance is voluntary but recommended (95%).
- On or across a public road: the quad needs ICBC Basic insurance and a licence decal, and you need a driver's licence (95%).
- ICBC rate class 036 Basic covers highway operation only. It does not cover you on Crown land or FSRs (95%).
- Crossing a highway at a stop sign or traffic light, or loading in a parking lot: no police permit needed if the quad is licensed and insured (95%).
- Any other road riding needs a free police operation permit from the nearest detachment (95%).

> [!lean]
> Buy the $200,000 liability cover before the first ride on an FSR. Almost every hunt starts on one.

## Helmet, lights, ID, kids

- Helmet on every rider and passenger on a quad, dirt bike or snowmobile on Crown land (98%). Standards: Snell M2005 or M2010, FMVSS 218, or ECE 22 (98%).
"Mandatory safety laws apply to all ORVs; this includes helmets for ride-astride ORVs and seatbelts (if installed). People under 16 must be supervised by an adult at least 19 years." Synopsis page 9 (98%).
- Lights on from half an hour after sunset to half an hour before sunrise, and any time weather cuts visibility (98%). Lights on for the ride out after legal light.
- Everyone 12 and older carries government photo ID with a birthdate (98%).
- Under 16: supervised by an adult 19 or older who holds a driver's licence or a rider course certificate, and no passengers (98%).
- Careless operation, or harassing, running over or killing wildlife or livestock with an ORV: an offence, $368 ticket (95%).
- Spark arrestor required during wildfire conditions under the Wildfire Regulation. Ticket $460 (95%).

## Firearms on the quad

> [!law]
> Synopsis page 11, number 27, exact words (98%): "To discharge, carry or have in possession a firearm containing live ammunition in its breech or in its magazine attached to the firearm, in or on a railway car, motor vehicle, sleigh, aircraft, bicycle or other conveyance."

- An ATV is a motor vehicle under the synopsis definition (98%). Page 4.
- [[Loaded]] in BC includes a loaded magazine clipped to the rifle (98%). Page 3. So: magazine out, chamber empty, before you touch the quad.
- Number 22, page 11 (98%): "To shoot wildlife from a motor vehicle or a boat propelled by a motor." Never from the seat, never from the rack.
- Number 24, page 11 (98%): no using a motor vehicle to herd or harass wildlife.
- Get off, step away from the quad, then load. Engine off is not enough (85%). My reading of "in or on".
- Tip: a padded rifle boot on the rack keeps dust out and the muzzle fixed. Bolt in your pocket on rough trails.

## Closed areas

- Two kinds under the Wildlife Act, each seasonal or year round (98%). Page 10.
"Motor Vehicle Closed Area: Prohibits the use or operation of a motor vehicle and e-bike. These prohibitions can be for all motor vehicles, or specific to ATVs/e-bikes or snowmobiles." Page 10 (98%).
- Motor Vehicle for Hunting Closed Area: no vehicle to hunt, carry game, carry hunting gear or carry hunters to or from wildlife (98%).
- Closures include private land unless exempted. They do not include highways unless included (98%).
- Signs may be posted. Where sign and regulation differ, the regulation wins (98%). No sign is not a defence (98%). Page 33.
- The old name was Access Management Area. You will still hear it (95%).

## Region 3 closures you will meet

- Region 3 maps: [gov.bc.ca/thompsonaccess](https://www.gov.bc.ca/thompsonaccess), a table of georeferenced PDF (Portable Document Format) maps by [[MU]] (Management Unit) (98%).
- Region wide: "The operation of all motor vehicles, excluding snowmobiles is prohibited in region 3 above 1,700 m elevation, except on existing roads and trails." Page 33 (98%).
- Existing road or trail includes a logging road, and a trail bare enough that a 4 wheel vehicle can straddle any strip of plants (98%). Page 3. No new tracks across the alpine.
- ATV for Hunting Closed: "from Sept 1 - Dec 10 in MUs 3-28, 3-29, 3-30, and that portion of MU 3-17 shown on Map C3." Page 33 (98%). The regulation calls it Elephant Hills (98%).
- Skull Mountain (MU 3-28): closed 1 September to 10 December except roads shown open on Map C13 (98%).
- Lac du Bois and Dewdrop (MUs 3-28, 3-29) and Dewdrop Rosseau Creek Wildlife Management Area (MU 3-29): closed year round except roads marked open (98%). Maps C15 and C16.
- Greenstone Mountain Burn (MU 3-19): no vehicle for hunting except Duffy Lake Road, Beaton Lake Road and Greenstone Mountain Road (98%). Map C5.
- Stake Lake cross country ski trails: closed to unauthorized vehicles, motorbikes, ATVs and snowmobiles all year (98%).

## Fire season bans

- BC Wildfire Service can ban recreational ORV use on Crown land across a whole fire centre (95%).
- It happened in 2026: an ORV ban covered the Kamloops Fire Centre from noon on 10 August 2026 (98%). News reports say it was lifted on 28 August (70%).
- On 25 September 2026 the Kamloops Fire Centre page listed no ORV ban and no area restrictions (95%).
- Area restriction orders close ground near active fires under Wildfire Act section 11 (95%).
- Check the [Kamloops Fire Centre page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/safety/wildfire-status/prevention/fire-bans-and-restrictions/kamloops-fire-centre-bans) the night before every trip. Mission falls under a different fire centre: VERIFY which.

## Riding safety basics

- Take a rider course. Ride with someone. Ride within your skill (90%). Canada Safety Council code.
- Hills: low gear, weight forward going up, weight back going down, never sideways across a steep slope (65%). Rider course practice.
- Stalled on a climb: brake, dismount on the uphill side, never try to turn around on the slope (65%). Rider course practice.
- Loads: rack limits are in the owner's manual and they are low. Heavy or high loads tip quads (Tip).
- A deer on the rear rack: quarter it, strap it low and centred, head forward, and ride slower than you want to (Tip).
- Quad on the truck: ramp straps on, then 4 ratchet straps to frame points, one per corner. Recheck after 10 minutes (Tip).
- No alcohol. Lights on in dust. Slow for blind corners on FSRs. Logging trucks own the road (Tip).

## What to carry

```checklist s2-atv-carry
ICBC registration certificate copy on the quad
Driver's licence and photo ID in my jacket
Proof of $200,000 liability insurance
Approved helmet, eye protection, gloves, boots over the ankle
Tow strap and a small tool kit
Tire plug kit and pump
Spare fuel if the loop is over half a tank
First aid kit, fire kit, headlamp, whistle
Water, food, extra warm layer
Spark arrestor fitted and checked
Region 3 closure maps for my MU downloaded offline
Is my ATV registered with ICBC? Plate on? If not, book the broker this week
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): The quad gets you to the hunt. It is not the hunt. Park it, unload it, walk.

> [!mistake]
> - Rifle in the rack with a loaded magazine clipped in. That is a loaded firearm on a conveyance.
> - Riding past a closure sign because "the road was there". Above 1,700 m you stay on existing roads and trails.
> - No liability insurance on the FSR, then a collision with a logging truck.

> [!field]
> At the truck: magazine out, chamber empty, rifle in the boot. Helmet on. Open the closure map for this MU and say the closure out loud before you turn the key.
