## "Loaded" in BC

```diagram rb-loaded-firearm
```

```more the BC and federal definitions
- BC meaning: live ammunition in the **breech (chamber) or the magazine**. A clip with live rounds attached to the firearm counts as the magazine (99%).
- So a full magazine in the rifle with an empty chamber **is loaded** in BC (99%).
- Federal "unloaded": no cartridge in the chamber and none in a magazine attached to or inserted in the firearm (99%).
- Loose rounds in your pocket or a detached magazine: not loaded (95%, my reading of both definitions).

> [!law]
> No loaded firearm in or on a motor vehicle, railway car, sleigh, aircraft, bicycle or other conveyance. No shooting from one either (99%).
```

## Truck, ATV and boat

```anim rb-loaded-truck Magazine out, chamber empty, then the truck or ATV (All Terrain Vehicle).
```

```diagram rb-boat-rules
```

```more every vehicle and boat rule
- **Truck:** magazine out or empty, chamber empty before the firearm goes in the truck (99%).
- **ATV (all terrain vehicle):** same rule. In the synopsis an ATV is a motor vehicle. Unload before you get on (99%).
- Engine off is not enough: the rule covers a loaded firearm in or on the vehicle, running or not (95%). Get off the [[ATV]], step away, then load. Tip.
- Federal rules also say load, or handle a loaded firearm, only where you may lawfully shoot (99%).
- **No hunting wildlife from a motor vehicle** (99%, page 11 item 22). **No hunting game from a boat unless it is not being propelled by a motor** (99%, item 23, 2 October 2026 edition).
- The 10 ft boat with motor: no shooting wildlife while the motor drives it (99%). Since 1 October 2026, shooting while the motor is not driving it is exempt, when hunting or retrieving game (99%).
- Never shoot a deer while it swims, unless it is already wounded (99%). Tip: no deer from the boat.
- Kayak: no motor, so the motorboat rule does not apply (95%). Ducks: see the duck screen.
- Deer from a kayak: since 1 October 2026, shooting from a boat with no motor is exempt from the [[FVPA]] (Firearm Violence Prevention Act) boat rule when hunting (99%). Tip: do not.
```

## Roads

```diagram rb-road-allowance
```

```anim rb-road-walk Tip: walk 15 m off the road, then load.
```

```more the road rules in full
- No shooting **across** the road allowance of a numbered highway, or of a 2 lane or wider public road maintained by the Ministry of Transportation and Infrastructure (99%).
- No hunting or shooting **on** that road allowance either (99%).
- Road allowance: 15 m (16 yd) each side of the middle of a road with under 3 lanes. Or 15 m from the pavement edge on 3 lanes or more. Or to private or cultivated land, if closer (99%).
- Which roads count: every numbered highway, plus 2 lane or wider public roads maintained by that Ministry, the federal government or another province (99%).
- A [[FSR]] (Forest Service Road) is not named in this rule (95%). Tip: never shoot from or across any road.
- Safe habit: walk at least 15 m off the road, then load. Tip.
```

## The 100 m rule

```diagram rb-100m-ring
```

- Your brother's house counts, even with his OK (95%, my reading).

```more the rule and the field job
> [!law]
> No hunting or shooting within 100 m (109 yd) of a church, school building, school yard, playground, regional district park, or a dwelling house, farm or ranch building occupied by people or domestic animals (99%).

- "Occupied" includes animals in a barn (99%).
- 100 m is close. A rifle bullet travels kilometres. Use your rangefinder on buildings. Tip.
- Owners can slaughter their own livestock near their buildings (99%). That exemption is not for hunting (95%, my reading).
- **Your brother's house on the 20 acre property counts.** No shot within 100 m of it, even with his permission (95%, my reading: the only owner exemption is livestock slaughter).
- Barns or sheds with animals inside count too (99%).
- Field job: rangefind the house from each likely stand. Mark a 100 m ring on your phone map. Tip.
```

## Private land and the Trespass Act

```diagram rb-enclosed
```

```more the Trespass Act in full
- Fenced, posted or cultivated private land: you need the owner's consent (99%). Get it **in writing**. Tip.
- On any other private land, ask first anyway. Tip.
- Under the Trespass Act, "enclosed land" includes land with a lawful fence, a natural boundary, or signs (99%).
- Signs must be clearly visible in daylight from each ordinary access point (99%).
- On enclosed land you are presumed to have **no** permission (99%). You must be able to show you do (95%).
- Cultivated land (crops, hay, orchard: my examples): no hunting over it without the consent of the owner, lessee or occupier (99%).
- A posted "No Hunting" sign is legal notice. Hunting there after you see it is an offence (95%, my reading of the Trespass Act).
- The owner may still hunt there, and may let others hunt (99%).
```

## Ducks and geese (federal)

```diagram rb-plug
```

```anim rb-boat-glide A boat still gliding is still moving.
```

```more every federal duck rule
- Non toxic shot only for ducks, geese, coots and snipe. No lead (99%).
- Shotgun no larger than 10 gauge (99%).
- Holds **3 shells max**, chamber plus magazine. Plug must need the gun taken apart to remove (99%).
- No hunting from a **moving** boat with a motor or sail (99%).
- OK once the boat has stopped moving under motor or sail, for example beached, anchored or tied off (95%).
- Retrieving: the BC synopsis (page 12, 2 October 2026 edition) says retrieving a bird with a boat is lawful when the boat is not being propelled by a motor or sail (99%). Federal section 41 allows retrieval from a moving boat (99%). The rules differ, so the safe way is to paddle or drift to the bird with every gun unloaded (Tip). No shooting while the motor drives the boat (99%).
- Possession limit: 3 times the daily limit, at all times, **including at home** (99%). The federal limit counts birds not yet preserved (99%).
- Daily limits: ducks 8, Canada and cackling geese 10 (99%). Other species: the Rules tab.
```

## Ducks: the bird in your cooler

```diagram rb-evidence-birds
```

- Tip: leave a wing on until the duck is in your home freezer.

```more preserved birds and labels
- Unpreserved birds keep **one fully feathered wing or the fully feathered head** attached (99%).
- Store unpreserved birds so each can be counted and its species identified (99%).
- "Preserved" means gutted and plucked, then frozen, made into sausage, cooked, dried, canned or smoked away from the hunting area (99%).
- It also means the edible meat taken off away from the hunting area and then frozen or cooked that way, or a bird mounted for taxidermy (99%).
- Handing unpreserved birds to anyone, even your brother: label them with your name, contact information, the date and your permit number, and sign (99%).
- Tip: leave a wing on until the duck is in your home freezer.
```

## Deer: tag and evidence

```steps Cancel the licence in 5 steps
rb-notch-1 | Animal down. Do not touch it yet.
rb-notch-2 | Take out the species licence for it.
rb-notch-3 | Cancel it as the licence itself shows.
rb-notch-4 | Now you may handle the animal.
rb-notch-5 | Keep the licence with the meat.
```

```diagram rb-evidence-deer
```

```more tag and evidence rules in full
- Notch the species licence right after the kill, before you handle the animal (99%). See Phase 1 Session 13.
- Evidence of sex stays **naturally attached** until the meat is home and butchered there, at a meat cutter or cold storage plant, or inspected (99%).
- Buck: the unskinned tail **and** a testicle or part of the penis (99%).
- Doe: the unskinned tail **and** part of the udder and teats (99%).
- Antler restricted season (like 4 point): keep antlers naturally attached to part of the upper skull, with the species licence (99%).
- No hide patch option for deer: the unskinned tail is required. The 6 square cm hide patch is for caribou, elk and moose only (99%).
```

## Meat wastage

```diagram rb-edible
```

```more the meat rules in full
- You must remove the **edible portions** to your home, a meat cutter or a cold storage plant (99%).
- Deer edible portions: neck, ribs, all four quarters and the loins (99%).
- Game birds (ducks, quail, grouse): both breasts (99%).
- Guts and bones can stay at the kill site, but not where people are likely to be: a carcass is a wildlife attractant (95%).
- Never dump a carcass inside town limits or where the public goes (99%).
- You may give the animal to someone who then takes the edible portions out (99%).
```

## CWD (Chronic Wasting Disease)

```diagram rb-cwd-leave
```

```diagram parts-home-bc
```

```more CWD rules in full
- [[CWD]] is confirmed in the Kootenay Region: 9 cases by January 2026 (99%).
- CWD Management Zone: [[MU]] (Management Unit) 4-1 to 4-8 and 4-20 to 4-25 (99%).
- In that zone: mandatory CWD testing for every deer, elk and moose. Samples go to a designated freezer before you leave the zone (99%).
- No brain or spinal column (tail OK) taken out of that zone (99%).
- Region 3 and Region 8 are **not** in the zone (99%).
- From outside BC, bring in only the meat of the four quarters, loins, neck and ribs, or a hide, antlers and skull plate cleaned of all tissue (99%).
- Deer, elk, moose or caribou parts or products from outside BC, like urine lures, are banned for hunting (95%). Tip: use none.
```

## Transporting firearms (federal)

| Firearm in transport | Rule |
|---|---|
| Always | Unloaded (99%) |
| Left in the vehicle | Locked trunk or locked compartment, or out of sight in the locked vehicle (99%) |
| ATV (All Terrain Vehicle), remote area | Out of sight, trigger or cable lock on (95%) |

```more federal transport rules in full
- Unloaded whenever you transport it (99%).
- Non restricted rifle or shotgun: no case or trigger lock needed while you travel (95%).
- Leaving it in the vehicle: lock it in the trunk or a locked compartment. No trunk: out of sight in the **locked** vehicle (99%).
- Remote wilderness and the vehicle cannot be locked, like an ATV (all terrain vehicle): out of sight, with a trigger or cable lock on (95%).
- No special permit to carry a non restricted firearm. The Authorization to Transport is only for restricted and prohibited firearms (95%).
- Ammunition can travel with it, just not in it (95%, my reading).
```

## Giving away meat, and helpers

```diagram rb-transport-papers
```

- Your brother needs his own licences. Each hunter cancels only his own (99%).

```more Record of Receipt and helpers in full
- Anyone carrying your game without you needs a **Record of Receipt** (99%).
- Record of Receipt shows: date and place, your name and address, who receives it, your FWID (Fish and Wildlife ID), the species licence number, species and sex (99%).
- Your brother hunts too. He needs his **own** hunting licence, species licences and federal bird permit (99%).
- Each hunter notches only his own licence, for the animal he killed (99%).
- Shooting a deer for the other person's tag: not allowed. The hunter who kills must cancel his own licence, and using another person's licence is an offence (95%).
- Ducks: each hunter has his own daily limit. Do not pool birds. Tip.
- Carrying his deer while he is not with you: you need his Record of Receipt (99%).
- Friends you give meat to at home: write a short Record of Receipt anyway. Tip.
- Selling or trading game meat: an offence (99%).
```

## Wounded animal on closed or private land

| Animal went onto | Do this |
|---|---|
| Posted or fenced land | Get the owner's permission first (99%) |
| Owner says no, or absent | Call a conservation officer (Tip) |
| Park closed to hunting, No Shooting Area | No shooting. Call an officer (99%) |

```more the rules in full
- The Trespass Act has no exception for tracking wounded game (99%).
- Posted or fenced land: get the owner's permission first. Written permission is recommended (99%).
- Owner says no, or can't be found: call a conservation officer. Tip.
- Animal ran into a park closed to hunting or a No Shooting Area: no shooting there (99%). Call a conservation officer. Tip.
- You must make all reasonable effort to find, finish and retrieve wounded game, and count it in your bag limit (99%).
```

> [!rule]
> If you have to ask "is this legal?", the answer today is no. Find out at home, then come back.

> [!mistake]
> - A full magazine in the rifle in the truck. That is loaded in BC.
> - A quick shot from the road or the ATV seat.
> - Freezing ducks with no wing or head attached.

> [!field]
> Before each trip: open the synopsis map for your MU. Mark No Shooting Areas, closures and buildings on your phone map.
