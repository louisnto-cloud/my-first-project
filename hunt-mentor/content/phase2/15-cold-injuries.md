## Key points

```diagram fs-cold-stages
```

- Shivering is good: the body fighting. Shivering that stops is an emergency (75%).

```more the 8 key points in full
1. You lose heat 4 ways: conduction (cold ground), convection (wind), radiation (open sky), evaporation (sweat and wet clothes) (75%, physics, Wilderness Medical Society treatment logic).
2. Insulation and a barrier from the ground and weather are the core of all hypothermia care (75%, Wilderness Medical Society via American Family Physician).
3. Mild: alert, shivering strongly. Moderate: drowsy, shivering slows or stops. Severe: unconscious, no shivering (75%).
4. Mild hypothermia can be treated in the field with shelter, insulation, heat to the trunk, and food if alert (75%).
5. Moderate and severe: gentle, horizontal, no food or drink, wrap, warm the upper trunk, evacuate (75%).
6. Frostbite: do not rub, do not thaw if it may refreeze, rewarm in 37 to 39 °C water (75%).
7. Wind makes skin freeze much faster (70%, US National Weather Service chart).
8. The trap: sweating on the climb, then sitting still (Tip).
```

## The hunter's cold trap

```anim fs-cold-sweat-sit Sweat on the climb, then sit still in wet clothes.
```

- Damp base layer plus a long sit is how October hunters get hypothermic (Tip).

```more why it happens
- Wet clothing loses much of its insulation, and evaporation pulls heat from you (70%).
- A long glassing sit in a breeze adds convection, and a cold rock or wet ground adds conduction (75%, physics).
- Classic sequence (Tip): fast climb in a heavy jacket, soaked back, then 2 hours still on a cold ridge as the sun drops.
- Also see the round 1 animation on layers: [What to wear](#/s/what-to-wear/0).
```

## Stay dry: 4 habits

```steps Stay dry in 4 habits
fs-cold-dry-1 | Start a little cold at the truck.
fs-cold-dry-2 | Vent before you sweat on the climb.
fs-cold-dry-3 | Soaked base layer? Swap to a dry spare.
fs-cold-dry-4 | Puffy on and pad under the moment you stop.
```

```anim gr-layer-swap Shed on the climb, add when you stop.
```

```more the habits in full
- All 4 habits are Tip (hunter and mountaineer practice, not a study).
- Start a little cold: you warm up within minutes of walking.
- Vent early: zips open, toque in the pocket, gloves off on the climb.
- Carry a spare dry base layer top in a dry bag. Changing at the top takes 2 minutes and pays for hours.
- Puffy (insulated jacket) on before you feel cold. Keep it at the top of the pack.
- Sit on a closed cell foam pad. Cold ground pulls heat by conduction (75%).
- Wool or synthetic, never cotton next to the skin: see [What to wear](#/s/what-to-wear/0).
```

## Hypothermia: the 3 stages

```diagram fs-cold-stages
```

- Judge by what you see, not a thermometer (75%).

```more the stages in full
- Wilderness Medical Society 2019 guidelines, summarized by American Family Physician (75%):
  - **Mild:** core 35 to 32 °C. Movement impaired, but alert and shivering.
  - **Moderate:** core 32 to 28 °C. Shivering slows or stops. Not alert, but conscious.
  - **Severe:** under 28 °C. No movement, no shivering, not conscious.
- Shivering slows around 32 °C and stops by about 30 °C (75%).
- In the field you cannot measure core temperature. Stage by what the person does (75%).
- Bush safety covers the same scale in short: [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0).
```

## The umbles

```diagram fs-cold-umbles
```

- Your partner sees the umbles before you feel them (Tip).

```more the umbles in full
- Early signs often taught as the "umbles": stumbles, mumbles, fumbles, grumbles (75%, Red Cross material via [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety/0)).
- Fumbles test: can they work the zip and load a cartridge? If not, stop (Tip).
- Hypothermia dulls judgment, so the cold person often says "I'm fine". Trust the signs, not the words (Tip).
- Alcohol: hypothermia is common with alcohol or other intoxicants (75%, American Family Physician). Leave the flask at camp (Tip).
```

## Treat mild hypothermia

```steps Mild hypothermia in 4 steps
fs-cold-mild-1 | Get out of the wind and wet.
fs-cold-mild-2 | Pad under, dry layers, head covered.
fs-cold-mild-3 | Heat packs on armpits, chest, back.
fs-cold-mild-4 | Warm sweet food if alert. Rest 30 minutes.
```

- Alert and shivering? You can fix this in the field (75%).

```more mild treatment in full
- Wilderness Medical Society advice, via American Family Physician (75%):
  - Insulate with a vapour barrier (a layer that stops moisture and wind, like a tarp or plastic), especially head, neck and the ground contact area.
  - Wet clothes: keep them on until you are in a warm place, then change. In wind and rain, a wrap over wet clothes beats stripping (75%).
  - Warm, high calorie food or drink only if alert, shivering well and able to swallow safely.
  - Sit or lie down at least 30 minutes, then watch.
  - Active heat to armpits, chest and back. Check the skin every 20 to 30 minutes so the heat packs do not burn.
  - Exercise can come back slowly after warming and food.
- Shivering burns fuel. Food is heat (Tip).
```

## Moderate or severe: the wrap

```steps The hypothermia wrap
fs-cold-wrap-1 | Gentle and flat. No walking.
fs-cold-wrap-2 | Thick pad under, insulation all round.
fs-cold-wrap-3 | Big heat on the upper trunk.
fs-cold-wrap-4 | Tarp outside. Call 911 or SOS.
```

- No food or drink. Handle like glass (75%).

```more moderate and severe in full
- Wilderness Medical Society advice, via American Family Physician (75%):
  - Handle gently. Keep the person horizontal.
  - No food or drink.
  - Insulate and actively warm the upper trunk. This is essential.
  - Use **large** heat sources. Small chemical hand warmers are not enough for the core; use them only on hands and feet in transport.
  - Evacuate carefully. Call 911 or send SOS.
- Severe (unconscious): check breathing and pulse for up to 60 seconds; pulses are hard to feel through cold skin. Start CPR (cardiopulmonary resuscitation) if there are no signs of life (75%). Take a CPR course (Tip).
- The wrap order, from inside out (Tip, common wilderness first aid practice): pad, sleeping bag or all spare layers, heat on the chest, then a tarp or emergency blanket outside.
```

## Afterdrop

```diagram fs-cold-afterdrop
```

- Rubbing limbs or warming hands first can make it worse (75%).

```more afterdrop in full
- Warming or moving the arms and legs can send cold blood back to the core, dropping core temperature and raising the risk of a dangerous heart rhythm (75%, American Family Physician summary of the Wilderness Medical Society).
- Keep moderate hypothermia patients horizontal to prevent it (75%).
- After a cold water rescue, a person can collapse soon after being pulled out ("circumrescue collapse") (75%). Handle gently, keep them flat. See [Ice and cold water](#/s/ice-cold-water/0).
```

## Frostnip or frostbite?

| | Frostnip | Frostbite |
|---|---|---|
| What | Skin surface only | Tissue freezes |
| Looks | Pale, numb | White, waxy, hard |
| After warming | Back to normal fast | Red, swollen, blisters |
| Action | Warm it now | Protect, plan rewarm |

```more the difference in full
- Frostnip: superficial, no freezing inside the tissue. Numbness and paleness go away quickly after warming (75%, American Family Physician summary of the Wilderness Medical Society).
- Frostbite: tissue freezes. Mild cases are numb, red, swollen; the deepest reach muscle and bone (75%).
- Common spots: fingers, toes, ears, nose, cheeks (70%, US National Weather Service).
- Frostnip is your warning. Warm it now: hands in armpits, a warm hand on the cheek (Tip).
```

## Frostbite: what to do

```steps Frostbite in 4 steps
fs-cold-fb-1 | Spot it: numb, white, waxy. Check each other.
fs-cold-fb-2 | Do not rub it or put snow on it.
fs-cold-fb-3 | Could it refreeze? Keep it frozen.
fs-cold-fb-4 | Rewarm in 37 to 39 °C water, about 30 minutes.
```

- Thaw then refreeze is the worst outcome (75%).

```more frostbite care in full
- Wilderness Medical Society advice, via American Family Physician (75%):
  - Keep a frozen part frozen if it could refreeze. Field rewarming only if care is more than 2 hours away and refreezing can be prevented.
  - Water at 37 to 39 °C. No thermometer: put an uninjured hand in for 30 seconds; it should feel comfortable.
  - Done when the area is red or purple and soft, usually within 30 minutes.
  - Air dry or blot. Do not rub.
  - Ibuprofen early: up to 600 mg four times a day in the field (check your own health first).
  - Bulky gauze dressing, raise the part above the heart if you can.
  - Do not walk or climb on a frozen foot until care is reached, if you can avoid it.
- Prevention: keep skin dry and out of wind, mitts instead of gloves, check hands and feet often (75%).
- Skin creams may raise the risk (75%).
```

## Wind chill

```diagram fs-cold-windchill
```

- The same air feels far colder in wind. Faces and fingers freeze first (70%).

```more wind chill in full
- US National Weather Service chart examples (70%, search preview; my conversion to metric):
  - 0 °F (minus 18 °C) with a 20 mph (32 km/h) wind: wind chill about minus 30 °C; exposed skin can freeze in about 30 minutes.
  - Minus 10 °F (minus 23 °C) with 5 mph (8 km/h) wind: about 30 minutes.
  - Same temperature with 30 mph (48 km/h) wind: about 10 minutes.
- The chart has 3 bands: 30, 10 and 5 minutes to frostbite (70%).
- Riding the [[ATV]] (all terrain vehicle) or a boat makes your own wind. A 40 km/h ride in November adds wind chill to your face and hands (Tip).
- Canadian wind chill guidance exists from Environment and Climate Change Canada, but canada.ca was not available here. VERIFY against it.
```

## Cold kit for a long sit

```diagram fs-cold-kit
```

- Mitts are warmer than gloves (75%).

```more the kit in full
- Mitts beat gloves; add layers as needed (75%, Wilderness Medical Society prevention advice).
- Toque and buff: cover head and neck, big heat loss areas when the body is covered (Tip).
- Puffy jacket at the top of the pack (Tip).
- Dry socks in a zip bag (Tip).
- Closed cell foam sit pad: stops conduction into a cold rock or log (75%, physics).
- Chemical heat packs: good for hands, feet, or trunk care in mild hypothermia (75%).
- Thermos of hot sweet drink and extra food: fuel for shivering (Tip).
- Shooting mitts: a mitt with a fold back finger flap keeps the trigger hand warm (Tip).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

```diagram fs-cold-dry-4
```

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Be bold, start cold. Stop, then puffy on before your teeth tell you to.

> [!mistake]
> - Climbing in the warm jacket and soaking it.
> - "I'm fine" from a partner who is fumbling.
> - Rubbing a white frozen cheek with snow.

```more the mistakes in full
- Climbing in the heavy jacket: it gets wet from the inside, then cannot keep you warm at the top (Tip).
- Believing the cold person's words over their actions. Hypothermia dulls judgment (Tip).
- Rubbing frostbite or rubbing snow on it damages tissue (75%).
- Thawing feet at camp, then walking out on them. Thaw then refreeze, or walking on thawed feet, adds damage (75%).
- No foam pad: hours of conduction into a frozen log (Tip).
```

| Hook | Means |
|---|---|
| Be bold, start cold | Do not overdress at the truck |
| Wet plus wind plus still | The hunter's cold |

```more number tricks
- 37 to 39 °C: about body temperature, plus a little. Your good hand should find it comfortable for 30 seconds (75%).
```
