> [!why]
> A shotgun throws a cloud, not a bullet. Match choke, shell and range, swing ahead of the bird, and the cloud does the work.

## Key points

```diagram ff-sg-circle
```

- Cylinder, IC, Modified, Full: about 40, 50, 60, 70% at 40 yd (85%).
- Steel needs chokes rated for steel (85%).
- Pattern at your real distance, then stop changing (Tip).

```more all key points
- Choke is a slight narrowing at the muzzle that controls spread (85%).
- One maker's lead shot chart, 76 cm (30 in) circle at 40 yd (37 m): cylinder 40%, IC (improved cylinder) 50%, modified 60%, improved modified 65%, full 70% (85%, Trulock). "This chart should be used as a starting point only" (85%).
- Big steel through tight chokes can cause "choke creep" and lock a choke in the barrel (85%, Trulock).
- Pattern at least 5 shots per choke and shell (85%, Trulock).
- Lead (forward allowance) at a 30 m crossing duck: roughly 1 to 2 m (65%).
- Most misses are behind (70%, shotgun skills session).
- Aim with the dominant eye (85%, hunter education).
- Ducks and geese: non toxic shot, plug, 3 shells total (99%, [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods/0)).
```

## Constriction: the squeeze

```diagram ff-sg-constriction
```

- Choke = bore minus a few thousandths of an inch (85%).
- Markings differ by maker: read yours (Tip).

```more constriction in full
| Choke | Constriction | Pattern at 40 yd |
|---|---|---|
| Cylinder | .000 in | 40% |
| Skeet 1 | .005 in | 45% |
| Improved cylinder | .010 in | 50% |
| Skeet 2, light modified | .015 in | 55% |
| Modified | .020 in | 60% |
| Improved modified | .025 in | 65% |
| Full | .030 in | 70% |
| Extra full | .040 in | 73% |

- Source: Trulock choke chart, lead shot (85%).
- Makers do not agree exactly: one lists full at .035 in, others .030 in. The stamp on the tube is a name, not a measurement (70%).
- Cylinder and Skeet 1 are normally patterned at 25 yd, the rest at 40 yd (85%, Trulock).
- Notches or stamps on choke tubes vary by brand: VERIFY in your gun's manual.
```

## Steel and chokes

```diagram ff-sg-creep
```

- Steel is hard and does not squeeze like lead (70%).
- Use only chokes your maker rates for steel (85%).

```more steel and chokes in full
- Steel is about 100 times harder than lead and two thirds as dense (70%, Wikipedia shot article).
- Trulock: hard steel puts more force on the choke cone and can deform it, "choke creep," which can lock the choke in the barrel (85%).
- Trulock rates most of its chokes for steel from cylinder through improved modified (85%). Your gun maker's rating is what counts for your gun (Tip).
- Shotgun session rule of thumb: nothing tighter than modified with steel unless the choke says steel safe (75%).
- Trulock's steel pairings, number size shot: about 30 yd Skeet 1, about 35 yd Skeet 2 (light modified), 35 yd plus improved modified. For BB and BBB: cylinder, IC, modified for close, mid, long (85%).
- Steel at faster speeds patterning poorly? Trulock suggests backing off constriction by .005 in (85%).
```

## Pattern your gun, step by step

```steps Pattern a shotgun in 5 steps
ff-sg-pat-1 | Big sheet at your real distance.
ff-sg-pat-2 | Padded rest, head down like in the field.
ff-sg-pat-3 | Circle the densest 76 cm.
ff-sg-pat-4 | Count, divide, average 5 sheets.
ff-sg-pat-5 | Hunt for bird sized holes.
```

- Pattern where you will shoot: 35 m for ducks, 25 m upland (Tip).

```more Trulock's method in full
1. Target about 4 ft (1.2 m) square. Butcher paper or flooring paper works (85%).
2. Mark the centre. Shoot from a padded bench with your head and eye where they are when you shoot offhand (85%).
3. At least 5 shots, each on its own sheet, for each shell and choke (85%).
4. Draw a 30 in (76 cm) circle around the densest part of each pattern. Count pellets inside, divide by total pellets in the shell (85%).
5. Average all sheets (85%).
6. Look for holes big enough for a target to slip through. "The goal is multiple hits" (85%).
- Pellet count in your shell: shot weight × pellets per ounce (next screen), or cut one shell open and count (Tip).
- Your first pattern session is in [sighting in and patterning](#/s/sighting-in/0).
```

## Read the sheet

```diagram ff-sg-read
```

- Pattern centre versus aim mark: where your gun shoots (70%).
- Dense core kills. Thin fringe cripples (70%).

```more reading in full
- If the pattern centre sits well away from the aim mark, check gun fit and mount first; the gun may shoot off centre (70%).
- Fringe hits are few pellets at the edge. Those are the wounded birds that fly off (70%).
- Big ducks need about 100 pellets in the 76 cm circle at your longest range (70%, shotgun skills session).
- Example: 1 1/8 oz of steel #2 is about 141 pellets. At 65% that is about 92 in the circle (70%, arithmetic from Wikipedia's 125 per oz).
```

| Bird | Shell | Choke | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teal over decoys | Steel #4 | IC | 30 m (Tip) |
| Mallard, wigeon | Steel #2 or #3 | IC or light modified | 35 m (Tip) |
| Canada goose | Steel BB | Modified, steel rated | 35 m (Tip) |
| Ruffed grouse | Lead or steel #6 | IC | 25 m (Tip) |
| California quail | Lead #7.5 | IC or Skeet | 25 m (Tip) |

- Ducks: non toxic only (99%). Lead is legal for upland birds (99%).

```more notes on the table
- Shot and choke picks: ammo session and Trulock pairings (65 to 85%). Ranges are Hunt Mentor's beginner limits (opinion), matching the [shotgun skills](#/s/shotgun-skills/0) lean of 35 m on ducks and 30 m upland.
- Hunting quail and ducks the same day: carry only steel (Tip).
- Lead is banned in National Wildlife Areas (95%, ammo session).
```

## Lead: shoot where it will be

```anim ff-sg-lead The bird flies on while the shot travels.
```

- At 30 m the shot takes about a tenth of a second (65%).
- A duck covers 1 to 2 m in that time (65%).

```more why lead exists
- Shot slows fast. Steel leaves at about 1,400 to 1,550 fps (feet per second) and loses about half its speed by 40 yd (60 to 70%, ammo and skills sessions).
- Hunt Mentor physics model (sphere drag, steel #2 at 1,450 fps): 0.06 s to 20 m, 0.10 s to 30 m, 0.15 s to 40 m (60%).
- Duck speeds: most waterfowl fly 40 to 60 mph (64 to 97 km/h); a GPS study put mallards' median flight speed just over 51 mph (82 km/h); Ducks Unlimited says teal typically fly about 30 mph (48 km/h) (65%, Ducks Unlimited, search preview).
- Birds slowing over decoys are slower than migrating birds (Tip).
- Lead = bird speed × flight time of the shot. The swing adds your own timing on top (65%).
```

## How much lead

```diagram ff-sg-lead-table
```

- 30 m, 48 km/h: about 1.4 m. 40 m: about 2 m (60%).
- Miss? Next shot, more lead (70%).

```more the model table in full
| Range | 40 km/h | 48 km/h | 80 km/h |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 m | 0.7 m | 0.8 m | 1.3 m |
| 25 m | 0.9 m | 1.1 m | 1.8 m |
| 30 m | 1.1 m | 1.4 m | 2.3 m |
| 35 m | 1.4 m | 1.7 m | 2.8 m |
| 40 m | 1.7 m | 2.0 m | 3.3 m |

- Hunt Mentor physics model, steel #2, full crossing bird (60%). Lead #6 at upland speed needs about 10% more, because it starts slower (60%).
- This agrees with the shotgun skills estimate of roughly 1 to 2 m at 30 m (65%).
- Do not calculate in the blind. Learn the picture on clays, then let the swing do it (70%).
```

## Angles change the lead

```diagram ff-sg-angles
```

- Crossing: most lead (70%).
- Quartering: about half. Incoming: cover it (Tip).

```more angles in full
- The lead needed is the bird's sideways movement across your line of fire. Straight away or straight in: little sideways movement (70%).
- Quartering at 45 degrees: about 70% of the crossing lead (70%, sine of 45 degrees).
- Incoming ducks: swing up through the bird, blot it out with the barrel, fire as it disappears (Tip).
- Going away birds: small target, vitals shielded. Inside 30 m only (70%, shotgun skills).
```

## Swing methods

```anim sk-leads Three ways to lead. The gun never stops.
```

- Pull away, swing through, sustained (70%).
- Pick one, practise it on clays (Tip).

```more the three swings
- **Swing through:** start behind, swing through the bird, fire as you pass ahead. "Butt, belly, beak, bang" (70%).
- **Pull away:** start on the bird, pull ahead, fire (70%).
- **Sustained:** start ahead and stay ahead at the same distance (70%).
- In every method the gun keeps moving after the shot. Stopping the gun is the classic miss behind (70%).
- Videos and details: [shotgun skills](#/s/shotgun-skills/0).
```

## Find your dominant eye

```steps Eye dominance in 3 steps
ff-sg-eye-1 | Triangle with your hands, arms out.
ff-sg-eye-2 | Frame a far object, both eyes open.
ff-sg-eye-3 | Pull back. It lands on your master eye.
```

- Aim with your dominant eye (85%).
- Usually it matches your strong hand. Not always (85%).

```more eye dominance in full
- Hunter education: make a triangle with thumbs and forefingers, arms out, focus on a far object with both eyes, bring your hands to your face; the opening lines up with your dominant eye (85%).
- Unsure? Close one eye at a time. The weak eye sees the back of your hand (85%).
- Right handed but left eye dominant (cross dominant): the barrel can look aligned when it is not. Common fixes are squinting or a dot of tape on the shooting glasses over the dominant eye (Tip). VERIFY with a coach.
```

## Fit and mount

```diagram gr-fit
```

- The gun comes to your face, not your face to the gun (70%).
- Your eye is the rear sight (70%).

```more fit and mount in full
- A shotgun has no rear sight. Your eye on the rib does that job, so fit decides where it shoots (70%).
- Mount: eyes on the bird, push the gun out, bring it up to the cheek, then into the shoulder (70%, [shotgun skills](#/s/shotgun-skills/0)).
- Wear your hunting coat when you check fit: it changes length of pull (Tip).
- 20 dry mounts a night for a week makes the mount automatic (Tip). Unloaded, proved, no shells in the room.
```

## Upland versus waterfowl setups

| | Upland: grouse, quail | Waterfowl: ducks |
|---|---|---|
| Shot | Lead or steel #6 to #7.5 | Steel #2 to #4, non toxic only |
| Choke | IC or Skeet | IC to light modified, steel rated |
| Range | Flush close: 20 to 30 m | Over decoys: 25 to 35 m |
| Mount | Low gun, fast | Often seated, then stand and mount |

- Legal: the plug stays in for both (99%).

```more notes
- The plug: pump, repeating or auto loading shotguns must be plugged so the magazine holds no more than 2 shells (99%, [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods/0)).
- Migratory birds: no slugs, no bigger than 10 gauge, one shotgun unless others are cased or unloaded (99%, Rule Book note 5).
- Ranges and chokes: Hunt Mentor opinion from the sources above.
```

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Stopping the swing | Follow through past the bird |
| Never patterning | 5 sheets at your real distance |
| Tight choke, big steel | Choke rated for steel, more open |

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Look at the beak, not the barrel. The gun goes where the eyes go.

```more the mistakes in full
- Stopping the gun at the shot: the cloud passes behind the bird.
- Hunting with a choke and shell combination you have never seen on paper.
- Shooting big steel through a tight, non rated choke and seizing it in the barrel.
```

> [!field]
> Before your first duck morning, set a decoy at 35 m. Birds inside it are shootable. Past it, watch them.
