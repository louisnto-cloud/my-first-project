## Key points

```diagram ff-op-exit-pupil
```

- Exit pupil = objective lens ÷ power (85%).
- 8x42 or 10x42 binoculars suit BC hunting (Tip).
- Set the diopter once for your eyes (85%).

```more all key points
- "10x50": 10 is the magnification, 50 is the objective lens diameter in mm (85%, Vortex).
- Exit pupil: objective ÷ magnification. 10x50 = 5 mm (85%).
- Your pupil: about 2 to 3 mm in bright light, 4 to 5 mm at dawn or dusk, 7 to 8 mm in the dark (85%, Vortex).
- More magnification: narrower view, more shake, shallower depth of field (85%, Vortex).
- "7x or 8x magnification is considered adequate for woodland settings, while 10x is preferred for viewing at greater distances" (85%, Vortex).
- Fully multi coated optics have every air to glass surface coated with multiple films: the best image (85%).
- Spotting scopes must sit on a tripod (85%).
- Infrared optics, such as thermal scopes and monoculars, are banned on the hunt (99%, [Rule Book: unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts/0)).
```

## Your eye sets the limit

```diagram ff-op-pupil
```

- At dawn and dusk your pupil opens to about 4 to 5 mm (85%).
- An exit pupil bigger than your pupil adds little (70%).

```more exit pupil in practice
- "A larger exit pupil will deliver brighter images, especially under low light conditions" (85%, Vortex).
- In daylight your pupil shrinks to 2 to 3 mm, so almost any binocular is bright enough (85%).
- At legal light at dawn, an 8x42 (5.25 mm) or 10x50 (5 mm) matches your eye. A 10x42 (4.2 mm) is a touch dimmer (70%, arithmetic plus Vortex pupil sizes).
- Pupil size shrinks with age. Older hunters gain less from huge exit pupils (60%, Tip).
- Twilight factor (the square root of power × objective) is often quoted, but it is a maths number, not real brightness. Glass quality matters more (65%).
```

## 8x or 10x

```diagram ff-op-fov
```

- 8x: wider, steadier, brighter (85%).
- 10x: more detail at long range, more shake (85%).

```more choosing power in full
| | 8x42 | 10x42 |
|---|---|---|
| Exit pupil | 5.25 mm | 4.2 mm |
| Field of view | Wider | Narrower |
| Shake | Less | More, use a tripod |
| Best for | Timber, quail, grouse, close deer | Open slopes, cutblocks, long glassing |

- Field of view is given in feet at 1,000 yd for binoculars, or in degrees; one degree is 52.5 ft at 1,000 yd (85%, Vortex).
- Heffley Creek has open benches and cutblocks; the Fraser Valley is closer cover (Assumption from the local plan lessons). A 10x42 on a tripod covers the open ground; 8x is easier in timber.

> [!lean]
> One 10x42 for both of you this season, with a tripod adapter. Add an 8x later if you hunt mostly timber.
```

## Glass and coatings

```diagram ff-op-coatings
```

- Each bare glass surface reflects 4 to 5% of the light (85%).
- Look for "fully multi coated" (85%).

```more quality in full
- "Without lens coatings, almost 50 percent of the light could be lost" through a binocular's many surfaces (85%, Vortex).
- Coating levels, best first: fully multi coated, multi coated, fully coated, coated (85%).
- ED or HD glass (extra low dispersion) cuts colour fringing and sharpens the image (85%).
- Waterproof and fogproof: sealed with o rings and purged with dry nitrogen or argon gas so the inside does not fog (85%).
- A good warranty is part of the product for a tool that lives outdoors (85%).
```

## Roof or Porro

```diagram ff-op-prisms
```

- Roof: straight, slim, durable (85%).
- Porro: offset, rich depth, good value (85%).

```more prisms in full
- Porro prism binoculars have the objective lenses spaced wider than the eyepieces, giving depth of field, a wide view and a three dimensional image at a reasonable cost (85%, Vortex).
- Roof prism binoculars have lenses in a straight line: streamlined and durable. Phase correction coatings keep them sharp (85%).
- Full size binoculars have objectives over 35 mm and weigh about 20 oz to 2 lb (85%).
- Most hunting binoculars sold today are roof prism (Tip).
```

## Set up your binoculars

```steps Fit binoculars to your eyes in 4 steps
ff-op-bino-1 | Hinge until you see one circle.
ff-op-bino-2 | Eyecups up, or down with glasses.
ff-op-bino-3 | Right eye shut: centre focus for the left eye.
ff-op-bino-4 | Left eye shut: diopter for the right eye.
```

- Note your diopter number. Then only use the centre wheel (85%).

```more Vortex's steps in full
1. Interpupillary distance: rotate the barrels in or out until you see a single image with no shading (85%).
2. Eyecups: extend them without glasses; twist or fold them down with glasses, or you see a "tunnel" (85%).
3. Pick an object about 20 yd (18 m) away. Close the right eye or cover the right lens; turn the centre focus until the left eye sees it sharp (85%).
4. Close the left eye; turn the diopter ring, usually on the right eyepiece, until sharp. Note the setting. From then on, use only the centre wheel (85%).
- Some diopters lock: lift to unlock, push down to lock (85%).
- Your brother's eyes differ. Write both diopter numbers on tape on the strap (Tip).
```

## Spotting scopes

```diagram ff-op-spotter
```

- 20 to 60x on a tripod, always (85%).
- 50 to 65 mm packs light. 80 mm plus is brighter, heavier (85%).

```more spotting scopes in full
- A configuration like 20 to 60x85 means 20 to 60 power, 85 mm objective (85%, Vortex).
- High power and big lenses mean spotting scopes "must be mounted on a tripod" (85%).
- Angled eyepiece (45 degrees): sits lower on the tripod, more stable, easy to share between different heights. Straight: natural line of sight, works on a car window mount (85%).
- As magnification goes up, the image gets dimmer (85%). Start low, find the animal, then zoom (Tip).
- Use a fast focus dial to get close, a fine dial for detail (85%).
- When it earns its weight: judging a 4 point buck on a far slope before a long stalk (Tip). Antler rules: [Rule Book](#/s/rb-definitions/0).
```

## Rifle scope: focus it for your eye

```steps Set up a rifle scope in 3 steps
ff-op-scope-1 | Quick looks at the sky: crosshair sharp at once.
ff-op-scope-2 | The eyepiece is for the reticle, not the deer.
ff-op-scope-3 | Move your head: if the crosshair slides, fix parallax.
```

- Set the eyepiece once. Then leave it (85%).

```more reticle focus and parallax in full
- Ocular focus: back it out until the reticle is fuzzy, then turn in "while taking short, quick looks through the scope" until it is sharp immediately. "Do NOT use this focus to adjust the target image" (85%, Vortex).
- Parallax: the target image is not on the same plane as the reticle, so the reticle seems to move if your eye is off centre (85%, Vortex).
- Scopes with a side focus or adjustable objective: set it to the distance, then nudge it until moving your head does not shift the crosshair (85%).
- Fixed parallax hunting scopes (often set at 100 yd) are fine to about 300 m if your eye is centred (65%, [scope session](#/s/scope-guide/0)).
- Eye relief: at least about 3 in (7.6 cm) on a rifle scope for safety; it often shrinks as power goes up (85%, Vortex).
```

## Rifle scope power in the field

```diagram ff-op-power
```

- Walk with the scope on low power (Tip).
- Lower power is brighter with a wider view (85%).

```more power in full
- "Lower magnifications will provide brighter images and wider fields of view which can be helpful in low light and/or close range shooting and with moving targets" (85%, Vortex).
- Higher power: narrower and dimmer, but better on small targets far away (85%).
- A deer jumping at 40 m in timber is a blur of hair on 9x. Set 3 or 4x when moving (Tip).
- Second focal plane hash marks are only true at one power (70%, scope session).
```

## Never glass with the rifle scope

```diagram ff-op-no-scope
```

- Your muzzle points wherever the scope looks (95%).
- Binoculars to look, rifle to shoot (Tip).

```more why it matters
- First rule of firearm safety: never point a firearm at anything you do not intend to shoot (95%, [firearm safety refresher](#/s/firearm-safety/0)).
- Glassing a hillside with the rifle scope means sweeping the muzzle across everything, including other hunters (95% follows from that rule).
- Identify the animal and confirm it is legal with binoculars or a spotting scope first (Tip). Then the rifle comes up.
```

## Thermal and night optics: banned

```diagram ff-op-no-ir
```

- Infrared optics, like thermal scopes and monoculars, are banned on the hunt (99%).
- No light to hunt by (99%).

```more the rules as the Rule Book states them
- Rule Book definitions: infrared optics means any optic that can detect infrared radiation: thermal scopes and thermal monoculars (99%, page 3, via [Rule Book: definitions](#/s/rb-definitions/0)).
- Rule Book: no thermal scope or thermal monocular on the trip, not even in the pack (99%, page 11, via [Rule Book: unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts/0)).
- No light or illuminating device to hunt by (99%, page 11, [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods/0)).
- Illuminated reticles light the crosshair inside the scope. Whether that counts as a light is not addressed in these lessons: VERIFY with a conservation officer before relying on one (55%).
```

## Care in the field

```diagram ff-gc-wet
```

- Caps on, lens cloth or pen, never your shirt (Tip).
- Dry optics out of the case after rain (Tip).

```more care in full
- Blow off grit first, then a lens brush, then a lens cloth or pen. Grit wiped with a shirt scratches coatings (Tip).
- Fogging on the outside lens in cold: breathe away from the eyepiece, warm it slowly (Tip). Inside fog means a broken seal: warranty (85%, waterproofing explained by Vortex).
- A binocular harness keeps glass dry, quiet and at hand (Tip).
- Check scope ring screws after a fall or a rough ATV ride; see [gun care](#/s/firearm-care/0).
```

## Grandpa's rule and common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Glassing with the rifle scope | Binoculars first, always |
| Never setting the diopter | 2 minutes, once, write the number |
| Scope on 9x while walking timber | Low power when moving |

> [!rule]
> Grandpa's rule (wisdom): Buy the best binoculars you can afford, then sit behind them twice as long.

```more the mistakes in full
- Sweeping hillsides with a loaded rifle's scope.
- Blaming the binoculars for a blurry image when the diopter was never set.
- Leaving the rifle scope on high power and fumbling a close shot.
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram ff-op-hooks
```

- **"Lens over power, exit pupil hour."** 42 ÷ 8 = 5.25 mm.
- **"Find, judge, aim."** Binocular, spotter, scope.
- **"Eight in the trees, ten on the hills."**

```more more memory tricks
- **"Sky, then set, then forget."** Reticle focus once.
- **"Head moves, cross stays."** No parallax.
- **"No heat, no light."** Thermal optics and lights are banned on the hunt.
```
