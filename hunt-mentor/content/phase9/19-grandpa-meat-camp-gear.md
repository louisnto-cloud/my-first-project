## 1. Cold, clean, dry

```diagram fw-grandpa-cool
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "Cold meat, clean meat, good meat."

- **Means:** cool it fast, keep dirt and hair off, keep it dry.
- **Label:** opinion (app). Penn State Extension: cool the carcass to 3 to 4 °C quickly (90%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Penn State Extension: "To prevent bacterial growth, quickly cool the carcass to 35 to 40 °F (3 to 4 °C)"; keep it out of direct sun and let air move around it (90%, read directly).
- Alaska Department of Fish and Game's three words: clean, cool and dry ([Skills masterclass 9](#/s/mc-skills-09)).
- **When it is wrong:** never.
- **Where it comes from:** meat hunters and food safety extension services.
```

## 2. Get the hide off to get the heat out

```diagram sk-cool-1
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "A hide is a winter coat. Get it off the meat."

- **Means:** gut fast, then skin as soon as you can, so the meat cools.
- **Label:** opinion (app). Ohio: removing the hide quickly lets meat cool faster (90%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Ohio Department of Natural Resources: field dress promptly, "a five minute job after a little practice"; removing the hide as soon as possible lets the meat cool more rapidly (90%, read directly).
- App: "A bear's hide is a winter coat. Get it off the meat" ([Black bear masterclass 2](#/s/mc-black-bear-02)).
- **When it is wrong:** in fly season or dirty conditions, the hide keeps meat clean until you can bag it (Tip).
- Evidence of sex or species may need to stay attached: [Rule Book: after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill).
- **Where it comes from:** meat hunters.
```

## 3. Let it breathe

```diagram gr-cooler
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Never seal warm meat in plastic."

- **Means:** use cloth game bags, not plastic or tarps, while meat is still warm.
- **Label:** tradition. Plastic traps heat and moisture (65%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- A food safety article quoting extension advice: avoid plastic bags or tarps, which trap moisture and heat; use breathable game bags (65%, Cullman Tribune, search preview).
- Penn State Extension uses sealed plastic only for ice or snow packed in the cavity, and for organ meat on ice (90%).
- **When it is wrong:** once meat is cold, sealed bags in a cooler or freezer are fine (Tip). See [Meat care](#/s/meat-care).
- **Where it comes from:** meat hunters and extension services.
```

## 4. Hang it days, not weeks

```diagram fl-camp-meat
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "Hang it cold and short."

- **Means:** a few days at fridge temperature is plenty for home hanging.
- **Label:** opinion. Penn State Extension: 4 °C or below, 2 to 3 days at most (90%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Penn State Extension: hold the carcass at 4 °C (40 °F) or below "for two to three days, at most"; for sausage meat, aging is not necessary (90%, read directly).
- Michigan State University Extension: aging venison is not necessary and can increase gamey flavour (65%, search preview).
- **When it is wrong:** a commercial cooler with steady temperature can age longer (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** extension services; old hanging traditions vary.
```

## 5. Cotton kills

```diagram fs-cold-heat-loss
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Cotton kills."

- **Means:** wet cotton stops insulating and dries slowly. Wear wool or synthetics.
- **Label:** tradition. Wet cotton loses its warmth (70%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Outdoor writers: cotton soaks up water, loses its insulating air pockets and dries slowly; wool keeps some warmth when wet (70%, search preview).
- It is the cold and wet that kill, through [[Hypothermia]], not the fibre itself (70%).
- **When it is wrong:** short, warm, dry trips. The saying is a broad brush (65%).
- **Where it comes from:** said to come from search and rescue teams; no source confirms that (50%).
```

## 6. Be bold, start cold

```diagram fs-cold-kit
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Be bold, start cold."

- **Means:** start the walk in a bit too little, so you do not soak your layers with sweat. Add layers when you stop.
- **Label:** tradition. A common saying among walkers and runners (70%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Outdoor writers: start an active outing in fewer layers than feels comfortable, because you warm up; sweat chills you when you stop (70%, Outside, ADVNTURE, search preview).
- This is the app's Grandpa's rule in [What to wear](#/s/what-to-wear) and [Cold injuries](#/s/cold-injuries).
- **When it is wrong:** for a long sit, put the puffy on before you get cold (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** hikers and mountain walkers.
```

## 7. Two is one, one is none

```diagram fs-fire-ignition
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Two is one, and one is none."

- **Means:** carry a backup for anything your life depends on: fire, light, navigation, knife.
- **Label:** tradition. A military saying about planning for failure (70%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- A former Navy SEAL on CNN in 2011: "In the SEAL teams we say, two is one; one is none" (70%, American Dialect Society discussion, search preview).
- **When it is wrong:** do not double everything; weight matters. Double only the critical items (Tip).
- App: [Lost and survival kit](#/s/lost-survival-kit), [Fire in wet weather](#/s/fire-wet-weather).
- **Where it comes from:** military and survival writers.
```

## 8. A sharp knife is a safe knife

```diagram fs-fa-knife-safe
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "A dull knife cuts the hunter."

- **Means:** a sharp blade needs less force and slips less.
- **Label:** tradition. University extension services agree (80%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Iowa State University Extension: sharp knives are safer; cuts happen when knives slip (80%, search preview).
- Washington State University Extension: dull knives are harder to use, resulting in more injuries (80%).
- **When it is wrong:** a sharp knife still cuts you if your fingers are in front of the blade (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** butchers and cooks.
```

## 9. Keep a clean camp

```anim fl-camp-scent Cooking smell drifts downwind. Bears follow it.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "A clean camp is a quiet camp."

- **Means:** food, meat and anything bloody well away from where you sleep.
- **Label:** tradition. Alberta's BearSmart: at least 100 m from your sleeping area (80%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Alberta BearSmart for hunters: food in airtight containers at least 100 m from your sleeping area; anything with blood or cooking smell outside the camp area (80%, search preview).
- Yukon caribou camp guide: tents at least 100 m upwind from cooking, food storage and latrines (75%). Wyoming: meat at least 100 yards from where you sleep, downwind if possible (75%).
- This is the app's Grandpa's rule in [Hunting camp](#/s/hunting-camp).
- **When it is wrong:** never in bear country.
- **Where it comes from:** bear safety programs.
```

## 10. Check your zero

```anim sk-range-test Three cold shots on a plate tell the truth.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "Shoot it before you hunt it."

- **Means:** confirm the rifle's [[Zero]] before the season, after a fall or bump, and after air travel.
- **Label:** opinion. Writers agree on checking after travel; not before every outing (65%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Buckmasters: the best defence against travel induced zero changes is to shoot before you go afield (65%, search preview).
- Some shooters say checking before every outing is impractical; most check at season start and after anything odd (60%).
- App: "Three shots tell the truth. One shot tells a story" ([Sighting in](#/s/sighting-in)).
- **When it is wrong:** never wrong after a knock.
- **Where it comes from:** riflemen.
```

## Myth check

```diagram fw-grandpa-myth-meter
```

| Saying | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Cut the throat to bleed it out" | Myth (90%) |
| "The longer it hangs, the better" | Myth for home hanging (80%) |
| "Never let water touch the meat" | Mixed (85%) |

```more the evidence for each
- **Throat:** Ohio's field dressing guide calls it a popular misconception; removing heart and lungs does a better job than cutting the throat (90%, read directly).
- **Hanging:** Penn State Extension says 2 to 3 days at 4 °C or below, at most, for home processing; Michigan State says aging is not necessary and can add gamey flavour (65 to 90%).
- **Water:** Ohio says rinse the cavity with clean cold water if you have it; Penn State says if you wash it, dry it quickly (85 to 90%). Wet meat that stays wet spoils (Tip).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram fw-grandpa-cool
```

- **Rhyme:** "Cold meat, clean meat, good meat."
- **Number trick:** 3 to 4: degrees Celsius to aim for, fast (90%).
- **Number trick:** 100: metres from your tent to food and meat (80%).

```more more hooks
- **Hide off, heat out.**
- **Two is one** for fire, light and knife.
- **2 to 3 days** of home hanging, at most (90%).

> [!mistake]
> - Leaving a warm carcass in a closed truck or plastic tarp.
> - Wearing cotton jeans on a wet November hunt.
> - Hanging meat beside the tent.

> [!field]
> Before the season: pack two fire starters, two lights, a sharpener and three game bags. Shoot three cold shots to confirm your zero.
```
