## 1. Never between a cow and her calf

```diagram sa-moose-family
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Never get between a cow moose and her calf."

- **Means:** give a cow with a calf a wide berth, and back away if she faces you.
- **Label:** tradition. In Alaska, moose injure more people each year than bears (65%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Evidence:** Alaska Department of Fish and Game is widely quoted: more people are injured by moose than by bears each year (65%, news reports, search preview). Attacks peak in the fall rut and in spring when cows guard new calves (60%).
- App: [Moose masterclass 4](#/s/mc-moose-04): "A moose walking slowly toward you is not your friend."
- **When it is wrong:** never.
- **Where it comes from:** northern hunters and wildlife agencies.
```

## 2. A moose in the water stays in the water

```diagram fw-grandpa-moose-water
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "A moose at the water's edge is a moose in the water. Wait."

- **Means:** hold the shot until the moose stands on dry ground you can reach.
- **Label:** opinion. Guide advice; some hunters disagree (60%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **For:** a moose is too heavy to drag; you would be butchering a floating animal (60%, Ron Spomer, outdoor writer, search preview).
- **Against:** some experienced hunters quarter moose in shallow water without trouble (50%, forum posts).
- App: [Moose masterclass 9](#/s/mc-moose-09).
- Shooting a swimming moose or from a moving boat is a legal question: [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods).
- **Where it comes from:** moose guides.
```

## 3. Plan the pack before the shot

```diagram mc-moose-meat-math
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Plan the pack before the shot."

- **Means:** know how you will get every quarter out before you pull the trigger.
- **Label:** tradition (app). A moose carcass is hundreds of kilograms (90%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** BC meat cutter records show moose carcass weights in the hundreds of kilograms (90%, the app's [Moose masterclass 10](#/s/mc-moose-10)).
- Elk version: "One elk, two men, three trips" ([Elk masterclass 10](#/s/mc-elk-10)).
- App: "Never shoot a moose you cannot get a truck or a boat within a kilometre of" (opinion).
- **When it is wrong:** never. Wasting meat is also a legal issue: [Rule Book: unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts).
- **Where it comes from:** moose and elk hunters.
```

## 4. Call, then wait

```diagram mc-moose-call-1
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "Call, then wait longer than you think."

- **Means:** a bull moose may come in slowly and silently. Stay ready long after you call.
- **Label:** opinion. Forum and writer advice only (50%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- A cow call imitates a cow asking for a bull; young bulls come in cautiously (60%, Outdoor Canada, search preview).
- One Alberta hunter's advice: give each calling spot at least 45 minutes; impatient hunters bump bulls that were coming (50%, forum post).
- App: [Moose masterclass 9](#/s/mc-moose-09).
- **When it is wrong:** some bulls come fast and grunting. Be ready from the first call (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** moose callers.
```

## 5. Bump elk and they leave the country

```diagram fw-grandpa-elk-flee
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "Never spook the herd you want to hunt tomorrow."

- **Means:** a spooked elk herd may leave the drainage. Back out quietly instead.
- **Label:** opinion (app). Some Montana elk moved over 10 km in the first days of a season (65%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Evidence:** in a Lolo National Forest study, some elk traveled more than 10 km (6 miles) back into the mountains in the first days of the hunting season (65%, American Hunter summary, search preview).
- App: [Elk masterclass 4](#/s/mc-elk-04).
- **When it is wrong:** elk with nowhere else to go may circle back in days (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** elk hunters and Montana research.
```

## 6. Call less than you want to

```anim mc-elk-call-arc A bull circles to get downwind of the call.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "Call less than you want to."

- **Means:** set up right first, then use few, meaningful calls.
- **Label:** opinion (app). Champion callers stress setup over volume (65%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- A Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation article with a champion caller: bugle to locate, then use breeding calls; do not call unless your setup is right; volume does not matter (65%, search preview).
- Colorado Parks and Wildlife: basic chirps and mews are contact calls that "don't actually say anything" (75%, search preview).
- App: [Elk masterclass 9](#/s/mc-elk-09).
- **When it is wrong:** a silent bull that is hung up may need one more call (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** elk callers.
```

## 7. Food next to dark timber

| Time | Where elk tend to be (Tip) |
|---|---|
| Dawn | Meadow and cutblock edges |
| Midday | Beds in dark timber |
| Dusk | Back out to feed |

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Find the food next to the dark timber, and wait for the evening."

- **Means:** elk feed in openings and bed in big, thick timber close by.
- **Label:** tradition (app). Elk chose large forest patches with hiding cover (80%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Western Montana: elk chose large patches of forest with good hiding cover, away from open roads (80%, Weber and others).
- App: [Elk masterclass 7](#/s/mc-elk-07), and "Never walk through the beds at noon" ([masterclass 6](#/s/mc-elk-06)).
- **When it is wrong:** early season heat can keep elk near water; late season snow pushes them to wind blown slopes (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** elk hunters.
```

## 8. Watch a bear for cubs

```diagram mc-black-bear-judge-2
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "Watch a bear five minutes. Cubs show up late."

- **Means:** a bear that looks alone may have cubs trailing or in a tree.
- **Label:** opinion (app). Agencies say spring cubs lag behind their mother (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife: females may have cubs in spring "that tend to lag behind when traveling" (85%, search preview).
- New Hampshire Fish and Game: take your time to confirm the bear is solitary; fall groups of two or more are families (85%, search preview).
- In BC, a cub or yearling, and the sow or any bear with it, is off limits (99%, [Rule Book: unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts)).
- App: [Black bear masterclass 4](#/s/mc-black-bear-04).
- **When it is wrong:** never wrong to wait.
- **Where it comes from:** wildlife agencies and bear hunters.
```

## 9. Small ears, big bear

```diagram mc-black-bear-judge-3
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "If the ears look big, the bear is small."

- **Means:** a mature boar's head and body dwarf its ears; a young bear looks all ears and legs.
- **Label:** opinion. Field judging advice from the Boone and Crockett Club and guides (70%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Boone and Crockett Club: look at where the bear is first (big bears hold the best food), then ears and head; get close, because distance fools size (70%, search preview).
- Outfitter advice: big boars have thick, straight legs to the feet; sows and small boars taper to a thin wrist (60%, Outdoor Life).
- **When it is wrong:** big sows exist. Check sex and cubs too (Tip). See [Black bear masterclass 1](#/s/mc-black-bear-01).
- **Where it comes from:** bear guides.
```

## 10. Bear meat, well done

```diagram mc-black-bear-trich
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "Bear meat is good meat, cooked well done."

- **Means:** cook every bit of bear to 71 °C (160 °F) inside. Freezing is not enough.
- **Label:** opinion (app). The food safety part is fact (90%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention: cook wild game to 71 °C (160 °F) to prevent [[Trichinosis]]; the northern parasite in bears survives freezing for months or years (90%, report via search).
- Bear meat is dark, so colour is not a test. Use a thermometer (90%).
- App: [Black bear masterclass 10](#/s/mc-black-bear-10).
- **When it is wrong:** never for bear.
- **Where it comes from:** public health agencies and bear hunters.
```

## Myth check

```diagram fw-grandpa-myth-meter
```

| Saying | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Freeze bear meat and it is safe" | Myth (90%) |
| "A brown bear is a grizzly" | Myth (85%) |
| "Bugle loud and long" | Myth (65%) |

```more the evidence for each
- **Freezing:** the northern Trichinella in bears survives freezing for months or years; only cooking to 71 °C kills it (90%, US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention).
- **Colour:** black bears come in brown and cinnamon. Use the shoulder hump, face profile and ears, never colour (85%, [Black bear masterclass 1](#/s/mc-black-bear-01)).
- **Loud bugling:** champion caller advice says volume does not matter and calling only works from the right setup (65%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram mc-black-bear-trich
```

- **Rhyme:** "Cow and calf, give them half: half the meadow, at least."
- **Number trick:** 71: degrees Celsius inside bear meat (90%).
- **Number trick:** 10: kilometres some bumped elk moved in the first days (65%).

```more more hooks
- **Water? Wait.** Dry ground first.
- **Big ears, small bear.**
- **Five minutes** of watching before any bear decision.

> [!mistake]
> - Shooting a moose standing in a lake.
> - Taking the first bear you see without watching for cubs.
> - Freezing bear sausage instead of cooking it through.

> [!field]
> Before your next moose or bear hunt, write the pack out plan: who, which route, how many trips, where the meat cools.
```
