## Rule 61: Non toxic shot only, and none other on you

```diagram rb-shot-ban
```

- **Rule:** ducks, geese, coots and snipe: non toxic shot only, and no other shot in your possession in the hunting area (99%).
- **Slip:** a box of lead grouse loads in the vest or the boat.
- **Fix:** empty the vest before a duck hunt. Steel, bismuth, tungsten or tin only.

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] s. 38(1)
> "A person must not hunt a migratory game bird (a) while having in their possession in the hunting area shot other than non-toxic shot; or (b) using shot other than non-toxic shot."

- BC note 4, page 13, says the same (99%). Lead is still legal for grouse, quail, pheasant, partridge, turkey, pigeons and doves (99%, page 13).
- Delta and Mud Bay dykes: no lead shot for any purpose (99%, page 27).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** grouse and ducks share one season and one vest.
- **How common:** using or possessing toxic shot averages 285 waterfowl citations a year in a Mississippi Flyway warden tally (14 US states and 3 Canadian provinces) (70%, Wisconsin Waterfowl Association, read directly). US flyway data.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods), page 13; [ducks and geese](#/s/rb-migratory-birds), s. 38 (99%).
```

## Rule 62: Three shells, plug only out with tools

```diagram re-mb-gun
```

- **Rule:** duck gun: 10 gauge or smaller, 3 shells in magazine and chamber together, with a one piece filler you cannot remove without taking the gun apart (99%).
- **Slip:** a plug you can thumb out, or a borrowed unplugged gun.
- **Fix:** check the plug every season. No slugs (99%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] s. 37(1)(c)
> "a shotgun not larger than number 10 gauge that either (i) is designed to be capable of holding no more than three cartridges, or (ii) has had its capacity reduced to three cartridges in the magazine and chamber combined, by means of the cutting off or the altering or plugging of the magazine with a one-piece metal, plastic or wood filler that cannot be removed unless the gun is disassembled."

- One shotgun at a time. Any second gun unloaded and taken apart or cased (99%, s. 37(3)(a); note 5, page 13).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** plugs come out for cleaning and stay out.
- **How common:** Wisconsin wardens average 44 citations a year for a gun holding more than 3 shells (70%, Wisconsin Waterfowl Association, read directly). US data.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: ducks and geese](#/s/rb-migratory-birds), s. 37; [legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods), page 12 (99%).
```

## Rule 63: Hit the daily limit, stop hunting that bird

```anim rb-duck-count At 8 ducks, stop hunting ducks for the day.
```

- **Rule:** once you have the daily limit of a species, you may not keep hunting it that day (99%).
- **Slip:** 8 ducks in the bag, still shooting "for my brother".
- **Fix:** at 8 ducks, unload or switch to geese if they are open (95%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 12
> "It is unlawful to continue to hunt game species on a day in which the daily bag limit of that species has been taken"

- Federal s. 44 says the same for migratory birds (99%). A found dead or wounded bird you keep counts in your bag (99%, s. 43(3)).
- Shooting more ducks "for a partner" after your limit is still you hunting ducks that day (90%, my reading). Ask a conservation officer (VERIFY).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** birds keep coming and the group feels like one bag.
- **How common:** shooting or possessing over the daily limit averages 249 waterfowl citations a year in the Mississippi Flyway tally (70%, Wisconsin Waterfowl Association, read directly). US flyway data.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: bag limits](#/s/rb-bag-limits), page 12; [ducks and geese](#/s/rb-migratory-birds), s. 43 and 44 (99%).
```

## Rule 64: Pintails and goldeneyes count inside the 8

```diagram rb-duck-bag
```

- **Rule:** 8 ducks a day in all. Inside the 8: at most 4 pintails, 4 canvasbacks, 2 goldeneyes, 2 harlequins (99%).
- **Slip:** 8 mallards, then "pintails have their own 4".
- **Fix:** know your ducks on the wing, or pass.

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 12
> "The daily bag limit for Canvasback is 4, Northern Pintail is 4, Goldeneye (Barrow's and Common in aggregate) is 2 and Harlequin is 2."

- These [[Restricted species]] count inside the [[Aggregate bag limit]] of 8, never on top. The federal Table 1 prints them inside the 8: "8 (not more than 4 may be Northern Pintails ...)" (99%).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** the separate numbers look like separate limits.
- **How common:** a US writer lists exceeding bag limits and misidentifying waterfowl among the top 5 waterfowl violations (60%, Split Reed, read directly). US source.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: bag limits](#/s/rb-bag-limits), page 12; [ducks and geese](#/s/rb-migratory-birds), Table 1 (99%).
```

## Rule 65: The freezer counts

```diagram re-mb-preserved
```

- **Rule:** possession limit is 3 times the daily limit at all times, home included, for every bird not yet preserved (99%). Ducks: 24.
- **Slip:** three weekends of unplucked ducks in the fridge.
- **Fix:** pluck and freeze, or cook, and the bird is preserved (99%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 12
> "The possession limit for all migratory game birds at all times (including while hunting, returning from hunting or at a residence) is three times the daily limit."

- [[Preserved]]: plucked and gutted then frozen, sausage, cooked, dried, canned or smoked away from the hunting area, or mounted (99%, s. 1).
- Canada geese: 10 a day, 30 in possession (99%).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** "possession" sounds like what is in the truck.
- **How common:** see rule 63.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: bag limits](#/s/rb-bag-limits), page 12; [ducks and geese](#/s/rb-migratory-birds), s. 1 and 46 (99%).
```

## Rule 66: Leave a wing or the head on

```diagram rb-evidence-birds
```

- **Rule:** a duck or goose keeps one fully feathered wing or the feathered head attached until it is preserved (99%).
- **Slip:** breasting birds at the marsh and bagging bare meat.
- **Fix:** breast it if you like, but keep a wing on the piece you carry (85%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] s. 52(1)
> "A person must not have in their possession or transport a migratory game bird that is not preserved ... unless at least one fully feathered wing or the fully feathered head is attached to the bird to allow its species to be identified."

- BC page 15 says the same (99%). Grouse and quail: rule 74.
- Keeping the wing on a field breast meets "attached to the carcass" (85%, my reading). Ask a conservation officer (VERIFY).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** breasting at the marsh saves weight.
- **How common:** road checks often find carcasses without required parts attached (rule 72). No bird specific count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: ducks and geese](#/s/rb-migratory-birds), s. 52; [after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill), page 15 (99%).
```

## Rule 67: Label birds before you hand them over

```diagram re-mb-label
```

- **Rule:** a duck you shot may not pass to anyone, even for a moment, unless it is labelled or preserved (99%).
- **Slip:** handing your brother 4 ducks at the truck.
- **Fix:** a label with your name, contact, date, permit number and signature (99%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] s. 50(1)
> "A person who kills or takes a migratory game bird must not allow it to enter the possession, including the temporary possession, of another person unless it is labelled or preserved."

- One label in a package covers the birds in it (99%, s. 50(6)).
- Riding home in a private car with the hunter who shot them needs no label (99%, s. 56).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** it feels like sharing lunch.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: ducks and geese](#/s/rb-migratory-birds), s. 50 and 56 (99%).
```

## Rule 68: Stay 400 m from bait for 7 days

```diagram re-mb-bait
```

- **Rule:** no hunting migratory birds within 400 m (437 yd) of bait until the place has been bait free for 7 days (99%).
- **Slip:** a blind beside grain someone spread for geese.
- **Fix:** ask the farmer what was spread and when. Normal farming spill is not bait (99%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Item 43, page 12
> "To hunt migratory game birds within 400 m of any place where bait has been deposited unless that place has been free of bait for at least 7 days."

- Not bait: standing crops, flooded harvested fields, sheaves drying, grain spilled by normal farming (99%, s. 36(2)).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** you may not know someone else baited.
- **How common:** taking waterfowl with the aid of bait averages 113 citations a year in the Mississippi Flyway tally (70%, Wisconsin Waterfowl Association, read directly). US flyway data.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: it is unlawful](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts), item 43; [ducks and geese](#/s/rb-migratory-birds), s. 36 (99%).
```

## Rule 69: Mission and Heffley Creek open on different days

```anim re-mb-two-towns Same day, two towns, two answers.
```

- **Rule:** duck and goose seasons are set by federal District. Heffley Creek is District 3, Mission District 2 (99%).
- **Slip:** Mission ducks on 5 October 2026 "because ducks are open".
- **Fix:** District 2 ducks open 10 October 2026; District 3 opened 8 September (99%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- District 3, Heffley Creek: ducks 8 September to 23 December; Canada geese closed 21 to 30 September (99%, Table 1 item 3).
- District 2, Mission: ducks 10 October 2026 to 24 January 2027; Canada geese closed 23 November to 18 December (99%, Table 1 item 2, page 28).
- District 8, Okanagan: ducks 23 September to 7 January (99%, Table 1 item 8).
- MUs 2-1, 3-45, 3-46, 5-16 and 7-1 sit in no district: no federal season there, and BC's Hunting Regulation duck and goose rows skip them too (95%, BC Laws and the ECCC, Environment and Climate Change Canada, BC summary, checked 2026-10-17).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** one province, but bird dates follow the [[District]].
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: ducks and geese](#/s/rb-migratory-birds), Schedule 3 Part 10 (99%).
```

## Rule 70: Dead still before you shoot from a boat

```anim rb-boat-glide Gliding after the motor is cut still counts as moving.
```

- **Rule:** no hunting migratory birds from or using a moving boat with a motor or sail. Still gliding after the motor is cut counts as moving (99%).
- **Slip:** cutting the outboard and shooting as you drift into the flock.
- **Fix:** wait until the boat is fully stopped, or hunt from a blind or the kayak.

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Hunting Regulation s. 17(1.2)
> "a boat is considered to be moving if it continues to move because of motion that was imparted by its motor or sail."

- Federal s. 41(4) says the same (99%). Item 24 on page 11 is the BC rule (99%).
- A kayak or paddled boat with no motor or sail is not covered (95%, my reading).
- Picking up downed birds: under paddle, or motor off and gun unloaded, until confirmed (80%, my reading). Ask a conservation officer (VERIFY).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** the old tilted motor exception is gone since 1 October 2026 (99%).
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). New wording, no count yet.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: it is unlawful](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts), item 24; [ducks and geese](#/s/rb-migratory-birds), s. 41 (99%).
```
