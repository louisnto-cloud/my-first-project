## Rule 21: No hunting from a motor vehicle

```diagram rb-unlawful-1
```

- **Rule:** no hunting wildlife from a motor vehicle: truck, ATV (All Terrain Vehicle), snowmobile (99%).
- **Slip:** resting the rifle on the open truck window, engine off.
- **Fix:** get out, walk off the road allowance, then hunt (rule 22).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Item 22, page 11
> "To hunt wildlife from a motor vehicle."

- Parked or moving, it is still a motor vehicle (95%, my reading).
- Hunting includes searching and lying in wait with a weapon (99%, page 3), so road cruising with a loaded gun is a problem twice over (95%, my reading; see rule 13).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** the truck feels like a blind.
- **How common:** a Williams Lake case involved a firearm discharged from a maintained roadway and hunting with a light (60%, CTV News, search preview).
- **Source:** [Rule Book: it is unlawful](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts), item 22 (99%).
```

## Rule 22: The road allowance is closed

```anim rb-road-walk Walk out of the road allowance before you load.
```

- **Rule:** no hunting or shooting within the road allowance of numbered highways and 2 lane or bigger public roads, and no shooting across them (99%).
- **Slip:** a shot from the gravel shoulder.
- **Fix:** under 3 lanes, walk past 15 m (16 yd) from the middle of the road (99%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 10
> "It is unlawful to discharge a firearm across or to discharge a firearm or hunt within the road allowances of all numbered highways and any two lane or greater public road in B.C. that is maintained by the Ministry of Transportation and Infrastructure (or their contractors), the federal government or another province or territory."

- Width, whichever comes first: 15 m each side of the middle (under 3 lanes); 15 m from the pavement edge (3 lanes or more); or the line of private or cultivated land (99%).
- Forest roads: the law, the Closed Areas Regulation s. 12(1), covers only public roads of two lanes or more maintained by the ministry responsible for the Transportation Act, or another province, a territory or Canada (99%, BC Laws, checked 2026-10-17). An FSR kept by the Ministry of Forests likely does not count (85%, my reading).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** the deer is in the ditch, and the shoulder is open ground.
- **How common:** a Fort St. John hunter was fined $5,000 and banned a year for an elk shot in the road allowance of Upper Cache Road. The Conservation Officer Service said: "Highways and publicly maintained roads are defined as no-shooting areas under the Wildlife Act" (70%, CJDC, March 2026, read directly).
- **Source:** [Rule Book: where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt), page 10 (99%).
```

## Rule 23: Coquihalla: no bullets within 400 m

```diagram rb-road-allowance
```

- **Rule:** Highway 5 from Hope to the Highway 1 junction at Kamloops: no bullet or slug within 400 m (437 yd) of either side of the road allowance (99%).
- **Slip:** a rifle shot 150 m off the Coquihalla.
- **Fix:** walk past 400 m with the rifle. Shot for grouse is allowed in the band (99%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 10
> "The discharge of a firearm using a single projectile is prohibited within 400 m (1/4 mile) on either side of the road allowance of following portions of the highways and roads in British Columbia listed below:"

- Highway 5 north of Kamloops, past Heffley Creek, is not on that list (95%, my reading).
- Highway 97C, Peachland to Aspen Grove, is stricter: no hunting and no shooting at all within 400 m (99%, page 10).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** the 15 m rule is the one everyone knows.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt), page 10 (99%).
```

## Rule 24: In a hunting closure, hauling counts

```diagram fw-rules-03-haul
```

- **Rule:** in a Motor Vehicle for Hunting Closed Area, no vehicle to hunt, carry hunters, carry gear or haul game (99%).
- **Slip:** "I only used the quad to drag the deer out."
- **Fix:** in MU (Management Unit) 3-28, 1 September to 10 December, no ATV or e bike for any hunting use (99%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 10
> "The operation of motor vehicles and e-bikes to hunt, transport wildlife, transport equipment and supplies which are intended for or in support of hunting, or transport hunters to and from the location of wildlife is prohibited."

- Region 3, page 33: ATV for hunting closed 1 September to 10 December in MUs 3-28, 3-29, 3-30 and part of 3-17 (99%). MU 3-27 has no such closure (99%).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** the hunt feels over once the animal is down.
- **How common:** near Revelstoke, three tickets went to people hunting in a motor vehicle closure, which also covers electric bicycles (60%, Revelstoke Review, search preview).
- **Source:** [Rule Book: where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt), page 10; [Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3), page 33 (99%).
```

## Rule 25: No sign is no excuse

```diagram rb-mv-types
```

- **Rule:** a closure covers private land inside it unless exempted. If a sign and the regulation disagree, the regulation wins (99%).
- **Slip:** "There was no gate sign, so it was open."
- **Fix:** check the closure list for your MU before the trip.

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 10
> "Where there are inconsistencies with signs and the regulation, the regulation is the final authority."

> [!law] Page 33
> "it is the hunter's responsibility to recognize closures whether a sign is in place or not."

- Closures leave out highways unless they name them (99%, page 10).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** signs get shot up, stolen or never posted.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt), page 10; [Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3), page 33 (99%).
```

## Rule 26: Above 1,700 m, stay on the road

```diagram gr-1700
```

- **Rule:** in Regions 3 and 8, above 1,700 m (5,577 ft), motor vehicles stay on existing roads and trails, all year. Snowmobiles are not covered (99%).
- **Slip:** cutting a new quad track across an alpine meadow.
- **Fix:** paved, logging, mining or service roads, or a bare two track only.

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 33
> "The operation of all motor vehicles, excluding snowmobiles is prohibited in region 3 above 1,700 m elevation, except on existing roads and trails."

- Region 8 has the same 1,700 m rule, electric bicycles named (99%, page 67).
- An [[Existing road or trail]] is defined on page 3. A game trail or an overgrown track does not count (90%, my reading).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** no fence or sign marks a height line.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: Region 3](#/s/rb-region-3), page 33; [Region 8](#/s/rb-region-8), page 67 (99%).
```

## Rule 27: ATV on a Forest Service Road: licence and insurance

```diagram rb-mv-types
```

- **Rule:** riding an ATV on a Forest Service Road needs a valid driver's licence and at least $200,000 third party liability insurance (99%).
- **Slip:** your brother rides the second quad with no driver's licence on him.
- **Fix:** both riders carry a driver's licence; insure the quads.

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 9
> "While travelling on a Forest Service Road, operators of ATVs are required to hold a valid driver's licence and carry a minimum of $200,000 third-party liability insurance."

- Damage to soil and plants from off road riding can mean a $575 fine, and serious cases up to $100,000 and a year in jail (99%, page 9).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** it feels like a trail, not a road.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt), page 9 (99%).
```

## Rule 28: Register the quad, wear the helmet

```diagram rb-mv-types
```

- **Rule:** ORVs (Off Road Vehicles) on Crown land must be registered with the plate or sticker shown. Helmet on any ORV you sit astride; seatbelt if fitted (99%).
- **Slip:** no plate on the quad, helmet left at camp.
- **Fix:** plate front, back or left side; helmet on every ride.

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 9
> "Mandatory registration applies to ORVs. The plate or sticker must be clearly displayed on the front, back or left side of the ORV."

- [[ORV]] includes ATVs, motorcycles, snowmobiles, side by sides and on highway vehicles under 4,536 kg (99%).
- Riders under 16 must be supervised by an adult 19 or older (99%).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** it is not a hunting rule, so hunters do not read it.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt), page 9 (99%).
```

## Rule 29: Never herd game with a boat or vehicle

```diagram rb-boat-rules
```

- **Rule:** no using a power boat, aircraft, motor vehicle or other machine to herd or harass wildlife (99%).
- **Slip:** running the boat at a raft of ducks to push them to your brother.
- **Fix:** let birds and deer move on their own.

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Item 25, page 11
> "To use a power boat, aircraft, or motor vehicle or other mechanical device to herd or harass wildlife."

- Chasing deer with the truck or the quad counts too (99%).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** "I was just driving the boat back."
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: it is unlawful](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts), item 25 (99%).
```

## Rule 30: No hunting from a boat the motor is pushing

```diagram rb-boat-rules
```

- **Rule:** deer, grouse and other game that is not a migratory bird: never hunt from a boat while its motor propels it (99%).
- **Slip:** shooting a grouse on the bank from the moving boat.
- **Fix:** motor off, or beach the boat, before any shot. Ducks: rule 70.

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Item 23, page 11
> "To hunt wildlife, other than migratory game birds, from a boat, unless the boat is not being propelled by a motor, or is held stationary at a fixed position in current, tide or wind by the power of the motor."

- Holding still on the motor is allowed by item 23, but a loaded firearm may not be (rule 14) (85%, my reading). Ask a conservation officer (VERIFY).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** new wording since 1 October 2026.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: it is unlawful](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts), item 23 (99%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram fw-rules-03-haul
```

- **Rhyme:** "Out of the truck, off the road, then load."
- **Number trick:** 15 m off the road, 400 m off the Coquihalla, 1,700 m stay on the track.
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "If wheels or a motor are moving, the gun is not."

```more mistakes and field drill
> [!rule]
> Old hand's rule (wisdom, not law): read the closure list at the kitchen table, not at the gate.

> [!mistake]
> - Hauling a deer out on the quad in MU 3-28 in October.
> - Shooting from the road shoulder.
> - Pushing ducks with the boat.

> [!field]
> Pace 20 steps off any road before you load. On the Coquihalla, carry the rifle 400 m in.
```
