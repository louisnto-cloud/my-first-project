## 1. Find the food

```anim rl-ed-dusk At dusk, deer walk from timber to the feed along the edge.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Find the food, find the animal."

- **Means:** learn what the animal eats this month. Hunt near it at feeding time.
- **Label:** tradition. Forage best explains where mule deer feed (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** a study of mule deer day beds and feeding sites found forage best described feeding sites, while temperature and canopy cover drove bed sites (85%, Germaine and others 2004, abstract via search).
- The app says it for every species: mule deer ([masterclass 7](#/s/mc-mule-deer-07)), moose ([masterclass 7](#/s/mc-moose-07)), black bear ([masterclass 7](#/s/mc-black-bear-07)).
- **When it is wrong:** at midday the animal is in its bed, not the food. Under heavy pressure, cover beats food (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** the oldest saying in hunting.
```

## 2. Get off the road

```diagram rq-pr-road-bands
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Walk past where the others stop."

- **Means:** most hunters stay close to the road. Walk farther and you have the land to yourself.
- **Label:** tradition, with data: 73% of hunters stayed within 0.5 km of a road (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Penn State, 45,907 ha of public land:** 73% of hunters were within 0.5 km of a road; the average farthest point from a road was 0.84 km (85%, Penn State final report 2004, read in the app's research).
- Hunters **said** they went 2.23 km from a road. GPS (Global Positioning System) said 0.84 km (80%, Penn State, search preview).
- Hunters did best on 10 to 20 degree slopes and 460 to 910 m (500 to 1,000 yd) from the nearest road (70%, Penn State page, search preview).
- **When it is wrong:** small patches right by the road that nobody bothers to hunt. App: "The spot everybody drives past is the spot nobody hunts" ([Habitat islands](#/s/habitat-islands)).
- Shooting near roads is restricted: [Rule Book: unlawful acts](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts).
- **Where it comes from:** old hands everywhere; Penn State put numbers on it.
```

## 3. Glass more, walk less

```diagram glassing-grid
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Glass more, walk less."

- **Means:** sit high, scan with binoculars, and walk only when you have found an animal.
- **Label:** tradition. The Mule Deer Foundation teaches it (80%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** the Mule Deer Foundation: glass from afar and "let the spotting scope do the walking" until you find a buck worth the stalk (80%, Mickey Luby, Mule Deer Foundation, search preview).
- See [[Glassing]] and [Mule deer masterclass 9](#/s/mc-mule-deer-09).
- **When it is wrong:** in thick timber or coast forest you cannot see far. Still hunt instead ([lesson 4](#/s/fw-grandpa-04)).
- **Where it comes from:** western mule deer and elk hunters.
```

## 4. Look for the flat line

```diagram fw-grandpa-flat-line
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "Look for a piece, not the whole animal."

- **Means:** search for a flat back line among upright trunks, an ear flick, a tail, a patch of white.
- **Label:** opinion. Widely taught (70%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** most things in the bush are vertical. A deer's back is a horizontal line that does not fit (70%, Pennsylvania Game News and hunting retailers, search preview).
- Ear flicks, tail flicks and white hair give deer away (70%).
- App: "The deer you find is part of a deer" ([Skills masterclass 1](#/s/mc-skills-01)).
- **When it is wrong:** in open grass you may see the whole animal. Then look for the colour that does not fit (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** Pennsylvania Game Commission writers, hunting retailers, the UK National Trust deer spotting tips.
```

## 5. Hunt the edges

```anim rp-grass-draw On the grass: walk up the hidden draw.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Hunt the edges, not the middle."

- **Means:** the line where cover meets food is where animals feel safe and eat.
- **Label:** tradition. Edges hold deer in habitat studies (75%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** white tailed deer use the meeting line of cover and food (75%, the app's habitat sources).
- In the app: [Edges](#/s/edges), and "Hunt the edges, not the middles" ([Mule deer masterclass 7](#/s/mc-mule-deer-07)).
- **When it is wrong:** on open grassland deer hide in folds and draws, not along a tree line. App: "On the grass, the deer is in the fold you are not looking at" ([Grasslands](#/s/grassland-country)).
- **Where it comes from:** a saying for deer, grouse and quail alike.
```

## 6. Go where the hunters are not

```anim hf-pressure-shift Opening day: trucks arrive, deer slide into the timber.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Pressure pushes them. Go where the pressure is not."

- **Means:** hunted animals move to thick cover far from open roads.
- **Label:** tradition. Montana elk data back it (80%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Western Montana elk:** elk chose areas away from open roads, with large patches of forest and good hiding cover. Elk were killed where roads were dense and cover thin (80%, Weber and others, Intermountain Journal of Sciences, abstract via search).
- Another Montana summary: density of open roads was the strongest predictor of where elk were (65%, secondary).
- App: [Hunting pressure](#/s/pressure-push).
- **When it is wrong:** private land and no hunting zones can soak up the animals, and you may not hunt there (Tip). Check the [Rule Book](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt).
- **Where it comes from:** elk and deer hunters across the West.
```

## 7. Ask the farmer

```anim rp-orchard-deer A deer slips through the orchard fence at dusk.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "The farmer is the best scouting report in the valley."

- **Means:** landowners know where the animals cross, eat and bed. Ask them.
- **Label:** tradition. 88% of landowners in one survey say yes when asked first (80%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Evidence:** Idaho Fish and Game reports 88% of surveyed rural landowners allow hunting if hunters ask permission first (80%, Idaho Fish and Game, search preview). That is Idaho, not BC (Tip).
- This is the app's Grandpa's rule in [Farmland and orchards](#/s/farm-country).
- **When it is wrong:** never wrong to ask. Where and how you may hunt private land in BC: [Rule Book: where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt).
- **Where it comes from:** farm country hunters everywhere.
```

## 8. Be on the glass before the light

```anim rl-sa-sun-arc The sun's path over a knob: first light lands east.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Be on the glass before the light."

- **Means:** walk in dark, sit down, and be glassing when shooting light arrives.
- **Label:** tradition. The Mule Deer Foundation says set up before first light (80%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** bucks often bed early in the morning; spot them before they do (80%, Mule Deer Foundation, search preview).
- Dawn is a main activity time for deer in collar studies (85%, Penn State).
- App: [Mule deer masterclass 6](#/s/mc-mule-deer-06), and "Be there before the deer, not with the deer" ([White tailed deer masterclass 6](#/s/mc-white-tailed-deer-06)).
- **When it is wrong:** walking in blind through the bedding area spooks what you came for. Plan the route in daylight (Tip).
- Legal shooting hours: [Rule Book](#/s/rb-legal-methods).
- **Where it comes from:** western glassers.
```

## 9. Cold morning, sun; hot noon, shade

```diagram sa-bed-warm-cold
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "Cold morning, look to the sun. Hot noon, look to the shade."

- **Means:** in the cold, animals bed in the first sun. In the heat, under thick shade.
- **Label:** opinion. Temperature and canopy drove mule deer bed choice (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** in a study of 236 mule deer day beds, site temperature and canopy closure were the strongest factors (85%, Germaine and others 2004, abstract via search).
- This is the app's Grandpa's rule in [Slope and aspect](#/s/slope-aspect).
- **When it is wrong:** wind and pressure can override temperature (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** the app's land lessons, built on bedding research.
```

## 10. Big country, small spots

```anim rl-pp-hourglass Trails squeeze through a narrow gap between lake and cliff.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "Big country, small spots."

- **Means:** in huge country the animals use a few small places: a brushy draw, a bench, a pinch.
- **Label:** opinion. Remote brushy draws protected Montana elk (70%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Evidence:** a Montana Fish, Wildlife and Parks study in the Missouri Breaks found remote brushy draws and small no hunting zones helped elk avoid hunters (70%, news report, search preview).
- App: "Big country, small spots" ([Elk masterclass 7](#/s/mc-elk-07)), and "Don't hunt where the animals are. Hunt where they have to go" ([Pinch points](#/s/pinch-points)).
- **When it is wrong:** early season, before pressure, animals may be spread across the open (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** elk hunters.
```

## Myth check

```diagram fw-grandpa-myth-meter
```

| Saying | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Big bucks go nocturnal" | Mixed (60%) |
| "I walk way back in" | Myth for most hunters (80%) |
| "The farmer will just say no" | Myth (80%) |

```more the evidence for each
- **Nocturnal bucks:** in one South Carolina GPS (Global Positioning System) study, buck daylight visits to stand areas fell from about 1 in 3 to about 1 in 20 by week 13 (60%, secondary reports). The National Deer Association's Kip Adams says bucks are rarely truly nocturnal; they shift to cover they feel safe in (65%, Field and Stream interview).
- **"Way back in":** hunters said 2.23 km from the road; GPS said 0.84 km (80%, Penn State).
- **"They'll say no":** 88% of surveyed Idaho landowners allow hunting when asked first (80%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram fw-grandpa-flat-line
```

- **Rhyme:** "Food at dusk, beds at noon, edges in between."
- **Number trick:** 73: % of hunters within 0.5 km of a road (85%).
- **Number trick:** 2.23 versus 0.84: how far hunters think they walk, and how far they do (80%).

```more more hooks
- **Glass more, walk less.**
- **A flat line in a vertical forest.**
- **88:** % of surveyed landowners who say yes when asked first (80%, Idaho).

> [!mistake]
> - Hunting the same roadside strip as everybody else.
> - Walking through the bedding area to get to the glassing knob.
> - Looking for a whole deer instead of an ear or a back line.

> [!field]
> On the [Hunt Map](#/map), mark one spot 1 km or more from any open road, on a 10 to 20 degree slope, with food next to thick cover.
```
