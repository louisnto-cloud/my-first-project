## 1. Aim small, miss small

```diagram fw-grandpa-aim-small
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Aim small, miss small."

- **Means:** pick one hair behind the shoulder, not the whole animal.
- **Label:** tradition. Origin unknown (Tip).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** a small aiming point keeps your misses close to it (Tip, shooting instructors).
- App: [Mule deer masterclass 2](#/s/mc-mule-deer-02).
- **When it is wrong:** never as an aiming habit.
- **Where it comes from:** unsettled. Buckmasters credits golf teacher Harvey Penick in the 1930s; others tie it to the film "The Patriot". No solid source (50%, Tip).
```

## 2. If you can get closer, get closer

```anim ff-ps-get-down Stop, choose a position, get steady, then shoot.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "If you can get lower, get lower. If you can get closer, get closer."

- **Means:** every metre you close and every bit steadier you get makes the shot safer.
- **Label:** tradition (app). Close shots forgive bad range guesses (75%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** wind drift and drop grow with distance; close shots forgive errors (75%, the app's ballistics lessons).
- App: Grandpa's rule in [Field positions](#/s/field-positions); [Skills masterclass 6](#/s/mc-skills-06).
- **When it is wrong:** when moving closer would break your wind or cover and blow the stalk. Then shoot from where you are, if it is inside your limit (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** riflemen and the app.
```

## 3. Your range is your limit

```anim sk-range-test Cold shots on a plate: your range ends where the plate is missed.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "Your range is your limit, not the rifle's."

- **Means:** shoot only as far as you can hit a small target, cold, from field positions.
- **Label:** opinion. Ducks Unlimited says the same for shotguns (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Ducks Unlimited: shooting beyond your maximum competent range risks crippling (85%, read directly).
- App: "The cold first shot is the one that counts" ([Skills masterclass 6](#/s/mc-skills-06)); test plan in [Season practice](#/s/season-practice).
- **When it is wrong:** never.
- **Where it comes from:** shooting instructors and hunting groups.
```

## 4. Broadside or quartering away

```diagram vitals-deer
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Wait for the near leg to step forward."

- **Means:** shoot a still animal broadside or quartering away, with the front leg forward to open the lungs.
- **Label:** tradition. Hunter education vital zone advice (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Hunter education: the heart and lung area is the target, best from broadside or quartering away (85%, Texas Parks and Wildlife).
- App: "Wait for the near leg to step forward" ([Black bear masterclass 2](#/s/mc-black-bear-02)); [Shot placement](#/s/shot-placement).
- **When it is wrong:** facing straight away is never a good rifle shot (85%).
- **Where it comes from:** hunter education.
```

## 5. Mark the spot before you move

```anim sa-wt-mark Two marks: where it stood, where it vanished.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion, widely taught)
> "Mark it, then wait it."

- **Means:** note where the animal stood and where you last saw it, using landmarks.
- **Label:** opinion. The National Deer Association teaches landmarks and flagging (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- National Deer Association: note the direction of travel and use landmarks to mark the last place you saw or heard the deer; carry flagging tape and a good light (85%, read directly, 2023).
- App: [Mule deer masterclass 10](#/s/mc-mule-deer-10).
- **When it is wrong:** never.
- **Where it comes from:** deer hunters and trackers.
```

## 6. Wait it out

```diagram fw-grandpa-wait
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion, widely taught)
> "Pushed early, lost forever."

- **Means:** after a poor hit, wait hours before tracking. A bedded animal pushed early runs far.
- **Label:** opinion. National Deer Association waits: liver 8 to 10 h, guts 10 to 12 h (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- National Deer Association (2023): heart or lungs with a firearm, wait a few minutes; liver 8 to 10 hours; guts 10 to 12 hours; stomach hit deer often bed quickly; if you bump one, back out (85%, read directly).
- Other sources give shorter waits (liver from about 3 h, guts from about 6 h). The app's chart in [Skills masterclass 8](#/s/mc-skills-08) uses shorter ones. When unsure, wait longer (Tip).
- **When it is wrong:** a leg or bone hit: the National Deer Association says track at once and look for a follow up shot (85%).
- Hot weather can spoil meat during a long wait: a hard choice. See [Blood trailing](#/s/blood-trailing).
- **Where it comes from:** deer hunters and blood tracking dog handlers.
```

## 7. Down is not dead

```diagram fw-grandpa-dropped
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "A deer that drops like a stone can get up like a deer."

- **Means:** reload and watch. Approach from the back side, ready to shoot again.
- **Label:** opinion. Spine shock can wear off in about a minute (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Bowhunter education: a hit that stuns the spine without cutting the cord can drop a deer; "within a minute or so, the animal may get up and run away" (85%, Hunter Ed bowhunter course, search preview).
- Approaching from behind the back and checking the eye: hunter tradition, no study found (Tip). App: [After the shot](#/s/after-the-shot).
- **When it is wrong:** never wrong to be ready.
- **Where it comes from:** hunter education and old hands.
```

## 8. Read the hit site

```anim rs-bl-drops Blood drop tails point the way it went.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "The deer writes its own report at the hit site. Read it slowly."

- **Means:** before you follow, look for hair, blood colour and bone where it stood.
- **Label:** opinion (app). No blood does not mean a miss (75%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- National Deer Association: bone fragments suggest a leg hit; watch whether tracks show running or walking (85%).
- Hunting writers list "no blood means a miss" among blood trailing myths (65%, MeatEater, search preview).
- App: Grandpa's rule in [Blood, hair and tissue](#/s/rs-blood-hair-tissue).
- **When it is wrong:** never wrong to look.
- **Where it comes from:** trackers.
```

## 9. Walk beside the trail

```anim sk-trail-beside Walk beside the blood, flag each spot.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "Walk beside the trail. You may need it twice."

- **Means:** never walk on the blood line. Flag the last blood and search ahead from it.
- **Label:** opinion (app, from BC Outdoors Magazine). The National Deer Association also flags the trail (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- National Deer Association: one person marks the last blood while others search ahead (85%).
- App: [Skills masterclass 8](#/s/mc-skills-08).
- **When it is wrong:** never.
- **Where it comes from:** trackers.
```

## 10. Stay on the same animal

| Situation | Old hands say (opinion) |
|---|---|
| Missed a duck | Stay on the same bird |
| Deer still standing after a hit | Shoot again if safe and clear |
| Leg or bone hit | Track now, ready to shoot |

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "Finish the one you started."

- **Means:** if the animal is still up and the shot is safe, a quick second shot beats a long track.
- **Label:** opinion. Ducks Unlimited and the National Deer Association both say it (75%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Ducks Unlimited: if you miss, stick with the same bird and shoot again until it falls (75%).
- National Deer Association: on a leg hit, start tracking at once and look for a follow up shot (85%).
- **When it is wrong:** never shoot again without a clear view and a safe backstop. Rules on wounded game and legal hours: [Rule Book: after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill).
- **Where it comes from:** hunting groups.
```

## Myth check

```diagram fw-grandpa-myth-meter
```

| Saying | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Hit deer always run downhill to water" | Mixed (60%) |
| "No blood means a clean miss" | Myth (75%) |
| "It dropped, so it is dead" | Myth (85%) |

```more the evidence for each
- **Downhill and water:** a state wildlife agency says hit deer may seek water as blood volume drops; others report hard hit animals going uphill to bedding cover (60%, NC Wildlife and hunting writers, search preview). Follow the sign, not the rule. App: "Downhill, water, thick cover. Look there first" ([Skills masterclass 8](#/s/mc-skills-08)).
- **No blood:** many hits bleed little at first; check for hair and bone and grid search (75%).
- **Dropped:** spine shock can wear off in about a minute (85%, bowhunter education).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram fw-grandpa-wait
```

- **Rhyme:** "Aim small, miss small. Mark it, then wait it."
- **Number trick:** 8 and 10: hours to wait for liver and guts, at least (85%, National Deer Association).
- **Number trick:** 1: minute for a stunned deer to get back up (85%).

```more more hooks
- **Down is not dead.**
- **Walk beside, not on.**
- **Leg hit: go now.**

> [!mistake]
> - Running to the deer the moment it vanishes.
> - Walking on the blood trail.
> - Assuming no blood means a miss.

> [!field]
> Carry flagging tape and a headlamp on every hunt. After any shot: note the time, mark where it stood, and sit for 30 minutes before deciding.
```
