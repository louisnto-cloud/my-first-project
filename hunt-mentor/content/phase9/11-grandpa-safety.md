## 1. Every gun is loaded

```diagram rb-loaded-firearm
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Treat every firearm as if it is loaded."

- **Means:** handle every gun, every time, as if a round is in the chamber.
- **Label:** tradition. Rule 1 of [[ACTS]] in the Canadian course (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** the four rules overlap. If you break one, the others still keep the muzzle and the finger safe (Tip).
- **ACTS** (the Canadian Firearms Safety Course rules): Assume every firearm is loaded. Control the muzzle. Trigger finger off the trigger and out of the guard. See that it is unloaded: [[PROVE]] it safe (85%, course summaries by the BC Wildlife Federation and training schools, search preview).
- US hunter education teaches the same four rules. Georgia packs them as "ACTT" and says they are covered in all hunter education courses (85%, Georgia Department of Natural Resources, search preview).
- **When it is wrong:** never. "I just checked it" is the classic last words. PROVE it again each time it changes hands (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** the Canadian Firearms Safety Course and every state hunter education course.
- In the app: [Firearm safety](#/s/firearm-safety).
```

## 2. Watch the muzzle

```diagram zones-of-fire
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Never point a gun at anything you do not want to shoot."

- **Means:** know where the muzzle points every second: walking, sitting, crossing, in the truck.
- **Label:** tradition. The C in ACTS (Assume, Control, Trigger, See) (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** if the muzzle is safe, a slip or a mistake fires into the dirt, not into a person (Tip).
- Washington hunter education words it: keep the muzzle pointed in a safe direction, never at another person, yourself, or any part of your body (85%, Hunter Ed course video, search preview).
- With your brother: agree on zones of fire before you walk or sit (Tip). See [Hunting with a partner](#/s/hunting-partner).
- **When it is wrong:** never.
- **Where it comes from:** hunter education in every province and state.
```

## 3. Look past the animal

```anim hf-look-past Look past the deer before you shoot.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Know your target and what is beyond it."

- **Means:** name the animal, then check where the bullet goes if it misses or passes through.
- **Label:** tradition. The third rule of hunter education (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** a rifle bullet that misses or passes through keeps going a long way. Only earth stops it safely (Tip; see [[Backstop]]).
- Georgia's version: be certain of the target and what is in front of it and beyond it (85%, search preview).
- The app's own Grandpa's rule in [First deer hunt](#/s/first-deer-hunt): "Look at the deer, then look past it."
- **When it is wrong:** never. The sky is never a backstop.
- **Where it comes from:** hunter education.
```

## 4. Finger off the trigger

```diagram fw-grandpa-trigger
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Finger off the trigger until the sights are on and you have decided."

- **Means:** finger straight along the stock, outside the guard, until the moment you shoot.
- **Label:** tradition. The T in ACTS (Assume, Control, Trigger, See) (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** a slip, a branch or a startle can close a finger that sits on the trigger (Tip).
- Georgia: keep your finger outside of the trigger guard until you are ready to shoot (85%, search preview).
- **When it is wrong:** never.
- **Where it comes from:** hunter education and the Canadian course.
```

## 5. Not sure? No shot

```diagram fw-grandpa-sure
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion, widely taught)
> "If you are not sure, you are not shooting."

- **Means:** any doubt about species, sex, points, backstop or range means no shot.
- **Label:** opinion. Never shoot at a sound or a movement (85%, Texas hunter education).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** "mistaken for game" shootings happen when someone shoots at a shape, a sound or a flash of colour (Tip).
- Texas Parks and Wildlife: never shoot at a sound or movement; in low light at dawn or dusk, pass the shot and use binoculars (85%, search preview).
- Already in the app: "If you are not sure, you are not shooting" ([Mule deer masterclass 1](#/s/mc-mule-deer-01)) and "When in doubt, don't" ([Legal to shoot](#/s/legal-to-shoot)).
- **When it is wrong:** never. Passing costs you an animal. A bad shot can cost a life.
- **Where it comes from:** hunter education.
```

## 6. Wear orange

```diagram fw-grandpa-orange
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Wear orange so the next hunter sees a person."

- **Means:** blaze orange makes you stand out to people.
- **Label:** tradition with data: 94% of New York "mistaken for game" victims wore none (90%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Evidence:** New York, 1989 to 1995: 125 hunters were shot after being mistaken for game. 117 (94%) were not wearing hunter orange; 6 (5%) were (90%, US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention report, read directly).
- North Carolina: after a hunter orange law, deaths from "mistaken for game" fell, and the drop was statistically significant (75%, Southern Medical Journal, search preview).
- These are before and after or case counts, not controlled trials (Tip).
- **When it is wrong:** orange does not replace identification. A hunter in orange can still be shot by someone who shoots at movement.
- Deer see orange poorly: see the myth check below.
- Whether orange is required for your hunt in BC: [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods). See also [[Blaze orange]].
- **Where it comes from:** state agencies and hunter education.
```

## 7. Unload to cross

```diagram fw-grandpa-fence
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Unload before you cross, climb or ride."

- **Means:** fence, log, creek, tree stand, truck, boat or quad: empty the gun first.
- **Label:** tradition. Taught in hunter education (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** people trip while crossing. An empty gun cannot fire.
- Alone: unload, lay the gun across or under with the muzzle away, cross away from it, then check the barrel for mud or snow (85%, Pennsylvania hunter education on the Hunter Ed course site and a Minnesota lesson plan, search preview).
- With a partner: one holds both unloaded guns while the other crosses, then hands them over (85%).
- Tree stands: pull an unloaded gun up with a rope (80%, a city hunting safety checklist, search preview).
- Boats: I found no boat specific source. Same idea, unload before you step in or out (Tip).
- What counts as "loaded" in BC and the vehicle rules: [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods).
- **When it is wrong:** never.
- **Where it comes from:** hunter education.
```

## 8. No alcohol

| Alcohol or drugs | What it does to a hunter |
|---|---|
| Body | Slows your hands and balance (85%) |
| Mind | Clouds judgement (85%) |
| Mood | Makes it easier to lose control (85%) |

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Gunpowder and alcohol do not mix."

- **Means:** no alcohol or drugs before or while you handle a gun.
- **Label:** tradition. In every hunter safety handout found (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Texas Parks and Wildlife: alcohol and drugs impair physical and mental functions and affect emotions (85%, search preview).
- Massachusetts handout: never use alcohol or drugs that can impair judgement before or while shooting (85%, search preview).
- Kansas hunter education: never handle a firearm or drive while affected by alcohol or drugs (85%, search preview).
- Being exhausted works the same way: rest before you decide (Tip).
- **When it is wrong:** never. The beer is for after the gun is unloaded and cased.
- **Where it comes from:** hunter education.
```

## 9. Leave a trip plan

```diagram fw-grandpa-search-area
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Tell someone where you are going and when you will be back."

- **Means:** leave a written trip plan with someone who will call for help if you are late.
- **Label:** tradition. Part of the AdventureSmart "3 Ts" (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- AdventureSmart's 3 Ts: trip planning, training, taking the essentials (85%, AdventureSmart, search preview).
- A trip plan lists where you go, your route, your gear and your return time. Your contact calls search and rescue if you are overdue (85%).
- Search area grows fast: 3 km off your route is about 28 km2 to search; 10 km is about 314 km2 (85%, Whistler search and rescue figures via search preview; matches the area of a circle).
- The app's [Bush safety](#/s/bush-safety) Grandpa's rule says the same.
- **When it is wrong:** a plan only works if you stick to it, or text an update when you change it (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** AdventureSmart and BC search and rescue.
```

## 10. Wear the lifejacket

```diagram hf-cold-water
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "The PFD in the bow locker is a cushion. The PFD on your chest is a lifejacket."

- **Means:** in the boat or kayak, wear the [[PFD]] (personal flotation device) zipped, every trip.
- **Label:** opinion, from the app's [Water safety](#/s/water-safety). Built on the "1, 10, 1" rule (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Cold Water Boot Camp, "1, 10, 1":** about 1 minute of [[Cold shock]] to get your breathing under control; about 10 minutes of useful movement; up to about 1 hour before [[Hypothermia]] knocks you out (85%, Canadian Safe Boating Council and Dr. Gordon Giesbrecht, search preview).
- The 1 hour is an estimate, not a promise (80%).
- A 2007 US Coast Guard study: 90% of drowning victims were not wearing lifejackets (70%, secondary source).
- **When it is wrong:** never in fall water. Duck season water is cold.
- Rules for hunting from a boat: [Rule Book: legal methods](#/s/rb-legal-methods). More: [Ice and cold water](#/s/ice-cold-water).
- **Where it comes from:** the Canadian Safe Boating Council's Cold Water Boot Camp.
```

## Myth check

```diagram fw-grandpa-myth-meter
```

| Saying | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Orange spooks deer" | Mostly myth (85%) |
| "In orange, nobody will shoot me" | Mixed (90%) |
| "You have an hour to swim to shore" | Myth (85%) |

```more the evidence for each
- **"Orange spooks deer."** Deer have two kinds of colour cones. To them orange looks much like green; blue stands out. Scent and movement matter far more than colour (85%, National Deer Association, "The Hunter's Guide to Deer Vision", read directly). See [[Blaze orange]].
- **"In orange, nobody will shoot me."** Orange helps a lot, but 6 of 125 New York "mistaken for game" victims were wearing it (90%, US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention). Identification is still the shooter's job.
- **"You have an hour to swim."** The hour is until you pass out. Useful movement lasts about 10 minutes (85%, Cold Water Boot Camp). Get out or get on top of the boat fast.
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram fw-grandpa-sure
```

- **Rhyme:** "Every gun is loaded, every time, every hand."
- **Number trick:** 1, 10, 1: breathe, move, survive (85%).
- **Number trick:** 94: the % of New York "mistaken for game" victims without orange (90%).

```more more hooks
- **ACTS** (Assume, Control, Trigger, See) on every gun; **PROVE** when it changes hands.
- **Four questions** before the shot: species, legal, backstop, range. Any "no": no shot.
- **28 and 314:** square kilometres to search when you are 3 or 10 km off a plan nobody has (85%).

> [!mistake]
> - Trusting "I already checked it" instead of proving it again.
> - Crossing a fence with a round in the chamber.
> - Leaving the PFD under the seat because the lake looks calm.

> [!field]
> Next outing: say the four rules out loud at the truck, and text your trip plan before you lose cell signal.
```
