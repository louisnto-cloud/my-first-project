## 1. Ducks land into the wind

```anim hf-duck-landing Ducks circle and land into the wind.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Ducks land into the wind. Sit with the wind at your back."

- **Means:** set the blind so the wind blows from behind you toward the decoys.
- **Label:** tradition. Ducks Unlimited teaches the same (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** birds use the wind to slow down on the final approach (80%). A Ducks Unlimited article picks the blind so the wind blows from the hunter's back, directly or quartering (85%, search preview).
- This is the app's Grandpa's rule in [Habitat tour: ducks](#/s/habitat-ducks).
- **When it is wrong:** a wind straight from behind puts the sun in your eyes some mornings; set a little crosswise for crossing shots (75%, Ducks Unlimited).
- **Where it comes from:** waterfowlers everywhere.
```

## 2. Leave them a hole

```diagram decoy-spread
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Put decoys where you do not want them to land."

- **Means:** leave an open [[Landing zone]] about 20 m in front of the blind.
- **Label:** tradition. Ducks Unlimited calls about 18 m (20 yd) the sweet spot (80%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Ducks Unlimited: twenty yards is the sweet spot for a landing zone; farther can put birds out of range when shooting starts (80%, search preview).
- See [[Decoy spread]] and [[J hook]].
- **When it is wrong:** in a crosswind ducks set down short; move the spread upwind (70%, Outdoor Life).
- **Where it comes from:** Ducks Unlimited and decoy carvers' tradition.
```

## 3. Call to the tail, not the face

| The duck is | You do (opinion) |
|---|---|
| Coming at you | Stay quiet, stay still |
| Crossing or leaving | A call to turn it |
| Wings slow, rear dropping | It is committing: quiet |

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Call to the tail, not to the face."

- **Means:** call when birds turn away; go quiet when they come toward you.
- **Label:** tradition. Field and Stream and Ducks Unlimited callers teach it (70%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Field and Stream: the most basic advice is to call only at wingtips and tails, not at ducks flying toward you (70%, search preview).
- Ducks Unlimited caller Jim Ronquest: good practice, but read wing speed and body; slow easy wingbeats with the rear dropping mean they are committing (70%).
- Ducks Unlimited: in heavily hunted areas ducks learn calling fast; the common mistake is calling too much (70%).
- **When it is wrong:** some days birds want more calling. Read the birds (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** duck callers.
```

## 4. Hide your face

```anim mdk-flare The hunter looks up; the ducks flare.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion, widely taught)
> "If you can see the duck's eye, it can see yours."

- **Means:** face mask or paint, gloves, and look up with your eyes, not your head.
- **Label:** opinion. A bare face or hands flare ducks (70%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Outdoor Life: the flash of a bare face or hands will flare ducks; wear a face mask and light gloves (70%, search preview).
- App: [Ducks masterclass 3](#/s/mc-ducks-03) and the goose version in [Geese masterclass 3](#/s/mc-geese-03).
- **When it is wrong:** never wrong to hide better.
- **Where it comes from:** waterfowlers.
```

## 5. Let them commit

```anim fw-grandpa-commit Wings cupped, feet down: now.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Wait for the feet."

- **Means:** let birds finish into the hole before you stand and shoot.
- **Label:** tradition. Shooting too early means longer shots (70%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Realtree: hunters rush the shot when birds drop their feet and end up shooting farther; let them commit (65%, search preview).
- Ducks Unlimited: birds that decoy well will be hanging when you stand, giving time for a smooth mount; shoot at the bill, not the feet (75%, search preview).
- **When it is wrong:** birds that circle and will not finish may leave; take a good shot in range when you get it (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** decoy hunters.
```

## 6. Know your range

```diagram mdk-shot-zones
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "If you have to wonder if it is in range, it is not."

- **Means:** shoot birds only inside the distance you can kill cleanly. Do not "sky bust".
- **Label:** opinion. Many self taught hunters struggle past about 27 m (30 yd) (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Ducks Unlimited (Phil Bourjaily, 2025): shooting beyond your maximum competent range risks crippling birds; misjudging by even 5 yards on a long shot can cripple; many self taught hunters struggle with 30 yard crossing shots (85%, read directly).
- Spend time at the pattern board to find your choke and load (85%).
- App: "Hide better, call less, shoot closer" ([Ducks masterclass 9](#/s/mc-ducks-09)).
- **When it is wrong:** a skilled shooter with a patterned gun has a longer range than a beginner. Your limit is yours (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** Ducks Unlimited and every good waterfowler.
```

## 7. Pick one bird

```anim hf-covey-flush The covey bursts. Pick one bird.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Pick one bird. The flock never falls."

- **Means:** on a covey rise or a flock of ducks, choose one bird and shoot it, then the next.
- **Label:** tradition. Kentucky's wildlife agency teaches it (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Kentucky Department of Fish and Wildlife Resources: note how many flush, pick a single bird, shoot, pick a second, shoot, mark the down birds (85%, search preview).
- Shooting Sportsman: resist "flock shooting"; pick one bird on a hittable line (70%).
- See [[Covey]] and [Quail masterclass 9](#/s/mc-quail-09).
- **When it is wrong:** never.
- **Where it comes from:** quail and duck hunters.
```

## 8. Mark the fall, fetch it first

```anim mql-mark-fall Eyes on the spot. Hat down. Walk straight there.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion, widely taught)
> "Fetch the bird before you shoot the next."

- **Means:** mark where it fell, then go get it before you shoot again.
- **Label:** opinion. A lost bird is wasted (Tip).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- The app's line in [Ducks masterclass 10](#/s/mc-ducks-10) and [Quail masterclass 10](#/s/mc-quail-10).
- Ducks Unlimited: if you miss, stay on the same bird and shoot again until it falls (75%).
- App: "If you would not swim for it, do not shoot it" ([First duck hunt](#/s/first-duck-hunt)).
- **When it is wrong:** a crippled diver may need a quick follow up shot on the water first (opinion, [Ducks masterclass 2](#/s/mc-ducks-02)).
- **Where it comes from:** waterfowl and upland hunters.
```

## 9. Never shoot the roost

```anim mdk-sanctuary-day Ducks rest in the refuge by day and feed at night.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Watch them at dusk, hunt them at dawn, and let them sleep where they sleep."

- **Means:** do not hunt or disturb the water where birds roost. Hunt where they feed or fly.
- **Label:** tradition (app). Setting up near a roost can push birds off it (65%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Goose scouting advice: find the roost, but keep your distance, because hunting too close can make birds leave it (65%, onX and other hunting sites, search preview).
- App: Grandpa's rule in [Waterfowl daily flights](#/s/waterfowl-daily-flights), "Never shoot the roost" ([Ducks masterclass 4](#/s/mc-ducks-04)).
- **When it is wrong:** big rivers or lakes where birds simply raft farther out (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** waterfowlers.
```

## 10. Hunt the field they fed in yesterday

```anim sb-goose-day Evening: geese feed in a field. Mark it. Hunt it at dawn.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Find the field, not the goose."

- **Means:** watch where geese feed in the evening, then set up there before dawn.
- **Label:** tradition. Scouts set decoys where the birds fed the evening before (65%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- Mississippi goose scouts find the field geese use late in the afternoon, then set decoys there next morning (65%, Mississippi Sportsman, search preview).
- Set decoys in the exact part of the field the birds used (60%).
- App: Grandpa's rule in [Geese](#/s/geese) and [Geese masterclass 9](#/s/mc-geese-09).
- **When it is wrong:** if you scout from the field and bump them, they may switch fields (Tip).
- Permission for farm fields: [Rule Book: where you can hunt](#/s/rb-where-you-can-hunt).
- **Where it comes from:** goose hunters.
```

## Myth check

```diagram fw-grandpa-myth-meter
```

| Saying | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Shoot only drakes to save ducks" | Mixed (75%) |
| "Grouse are just fool hens" | Mixed (70%) |
| "Steel kills like lead, same size" | Myth (80%) |

```more the evidence for each
- **Drakes only:** Delta Waterfowl's scientist Todd Arnold says drake harvest has little or no effect on mallard numbers, and hunting takes only a small share of hens compared with nest predators (75%, Delta Waterfowl, read directly). Sparing hens does no harm, but it is not the big lever many believe. Bag limits by sex: [Rule Book: migratory birds](#/s/rb-migratory-birds).
- **Fool hens:** spruce grouse and some ruffed grouse are tame where they see few people; hunted birds "become educated" (70%, Maine Audubon and Alaska Magazine, search preview).
- **Steel:** steel is lighter than lead, so hunter education suggests 1 to 2 sizes larger; some writers say 3 (80%, see next screen).
```

## Myth check: steel shot

```diagram fw-grandpa-steel
```

- Steel is lighter than lead for its size. Go about 2 sizes larger (75%).
- Pattern your gun at the range to check.
- Non toxic shot rules for waterfowl: [Rule Book: migratory birds](#/s/rb-migratory-birds).

```more the steel shot details
- The common rule of thumb: use steel 2 sizes larger than the lead you would use (75%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game "Shoot Steel" booklet 1991 and a Texas Parks and Wildlife quote, search preview).
- Hunter education: 1 to 2 sizes larger (80%, Nevada course on the Hunter Ed site). Randy Wakeman argues at least 3 sizes for pheasants (55%, Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** the switch to non toxic shot for waterfowl.
```

## Memory hooks

```anim fw-grandpa-commit Wait for the feet.
```

- **Rhyme:** "Wind at my back, ducks in my face."
- **Rhyme:** "Tail, call. Face, hush."
- **Number trick:** 20: yards to the landing hole, about 18 m (80%).

```more more hooks
- **One bird, then the next.**
- **Dusk tells you, dawn pays you.**
- **2 sizes up** for steel (75%).

> [!mistake]
> - Calling hard at birds already coming straight in.
> - Looking up with a bare face as they set their wings.
> - Shooting into the middle of the flock.

> [!field]
> Next scouting evening: sit with binoculars, mark the field where geese land, and note the wind. Plan tomorrow's blind with the wind at your back.
```
