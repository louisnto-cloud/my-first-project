## Rule 91: Shared LEH: the Harvest Report goes with you

```diagram rb-licence-path
```

- **Rule:** on a shared LEH (Limited Entry Hunting) authorization, carry the Harvest Report section while hunting, fill it in at the kill, and tell every hunter in the group at once (99%).
- **Slip:** the Harvest Report sits in one partner's truck.
- **Fix:** whoever hunts carries it; text the group the moment an animal is down (Tip).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 5
> "If you win a shared limited entry hunting authorization, you are required to carry the Harvest Report section of your authorization at all times while hunting. You must complete the Harvest Report and communicate the harvest to all hunters in your group immediately upon a kill."

- **Why it trips people (Tip):** one paper, several hunters, spread over a hillside.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: licences](#/s/rb-licences-fees), page 5 (99%).
```

## Rule 92: Paid help is guiding

```diagram fw-rules-10-help
```

- **Rule:** helping someone hunt for pay or a promised reward is guiding, and needs a guide licence. Using an unlicensed guide can make you a party to the offence (99%).
- **Slip:** taking a co worker out "for gas money and a cut of the meat".
- **Fix:** help for free, or not at all. Free help for your brother is not guiding (90%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Illegal Guiding, page 11
> "It is unlawful to act as, or offer to act as, a guide for wildlife for compensation or reward unless licensed to do so."

> [!law] Page 11
> "A person who uses the services of an illegal guide may, as a party to the offence, be charged and convicted of the same offence."

- Guide: "a person who, for compensation or reward, received or promised, accompanies and assists another person to hunt wildlife" (99%, page 11).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** splitting costs feels like friendship, not payment. Whether gas money counts: ask a conservation officer (VERIFY).
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found for unlicensed guiding.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: it is unlawful](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts), page 11 (99%).
```

## Rule 93: A paid boat ride to the hunt needs a licence

```diagram fw-rules-10-help
```

- **Rule:** carrying hunters to, from or between hunting spots for money or other pay makes you a transporter, and needs a transport or guide outfitter licence (99%).
- **Slip:** charging hunters to boat them across the lake.
- **Fix:** ferry friends for free, never for a fee (90%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 11
> "A person must not act as a transporter unless the person holds a transport licence or a guide outfitter licence issued under section 15 of the Wildlife Act."

- Transporter: "a person who, for money or other compensation, transports a hunter to, from or between locations so that the hunter can hunt" (99%, page 11).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** owning the boat makes it feel like your call.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: it is unlawful](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts), page 11 (99%).
```

## Rule 94: Nobody under 10 hunts

```diagram rb-supervise
```

- **Rule:** no one under 10 may hunt. A person who causes or allows a child under 10 to hunt commits an offence (99%).
- **Slip:** letting a 9 year old take a grouse "with help".
- **Fix:** under 10, they watch and learn; they do not shoot game.

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 6
> "No one under the age of 10 may hunt. A person who causes or allows a person under the age of 10 to hunt commits an offence."

- Hunting includes searching for or stalking wildlife with a weapon (99%, page 3). A child carrying the gun on the stalk is hunting (90%, my reading).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** family tradition says start them young.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: licences](#/s/rb-licences-fees), page 6 (99%).
```

## Rule 95: Youth hunters: always beside you, and their deer is yours

```diagram rb-supervise
```

- **Rule:** a youth hunter, 10 to under 18, is always accompanied and closely supervised by a qualified adult. Game taken on a youth or initiation licence counts in the supervisor's bag (99%).
- **Slip:** the youth shoots a buck, then you shoot one too, over your limit.
- **Fix:** count the youth's deer as yours before you shoot.

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Page 6: a [[Youth Hunting Licence]] gives no personal bag limit; wildlife taken under it counts in the supervising adult's bag (99%).
- No species licences may be bought with a youth or initiation licence, except a resident youth hunting with an adult in an LEH (Limited Entry Hunting) season (99%).
- Youth only seasons are for hunters under 18 (99%, page 34).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** it feels like the youth's own deer.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: licences](#/s/rb-licences-fees), page 6 (99%).
```

## Rule 96: Not every adult may supervise

```diagram rb-supervise
```

- **Rule:** a supervising hunter needs an FWID (Fish and Wildlife ID) with residency and hunting credentials, a hunting licence in at least 3 earlier licence years, no firearms prohibition, and supervises at most two (99%).
- **Slip:** a first year hunter supervising a nephew.
- **Fix:** check the list before you offer. Your first seasons: you cannot supervise (95%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Page 6 list: FWID with active residency and hunting credentials; held a licence other than an initiation licence in at least 3 earlier licence years (not needed if a guide outfitter also accompanies an initiation hunter); not prohibited from possessing a firearm anywhere; at most two youth licence hunters, two initiation hunters, or one of each (99%).
- Youth who hold a basic hunting licence do not count toward the two (99%).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** any adult with a licence feels qualified.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: licences](#/s/rb-licences-fees), page 6 (99%).
```

## Rule 97: Initiation licence: sign the form every day

```diagram rb-supervise
```

- **Rule:** an initiation licence hunter, 18 or older and never licensed, is accompanied at all times. The supervisor signs a form before each day's hunt (99%).
- **Slip:** signing once for the whole weekend.
- **Fix:** a fresh signed form every morning.

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Page 6: the [[Initiation Hunting Licence]] is once in a lifetime; no hunter safety training needed; you must be accompanied by a supervising hunter at all times; before starting to hunt on each day, the supervisor signs a form acknowledging the conditions (99%).
- Forms are at www.gov.bc.ca/hunting (99%).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** one form feels like enough for one trip.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: licences](#/s/rb-licences-fees), page 6 (99%).
```

## Rule 98: Accompany means eyes and voice, not radios

```diagram rb-accompany
```

- **Rule:** to accompany is to stay in the person's company, able to see them without binoculars and to talk without a radio or amplifier (99%).
- **Slip:** "supervising" a youth on the next ridge by radio.
- **Fix:** stay close enough to talk in a normal voice.

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Definition, page 3
> "ACCOMPANY - means to remain in the company of the other person, able to see the other person without the aid of any device other than ordinary corrective lenses and able to communicate by unamplified voice with that person."

- This is the test for supervising a youth or initiation hunter (95%, my reading).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** splitting up covers more ground.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: definitions](#/s/rb-definitions), page 3 (99%).
```

## Rule 99: A youth duck hunter needs a seasoned adult

```diagram re-mb-youth
```

- **Rule:** a minor on the free federal permit hunts only beside an adult who holds a permit now and held one in an earlier year. One adult, two minors at most (99%).
- **Slip:** your first year duck permit, and you take two nephews.
- **Fix:** you qualify from your second permit year on (95%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] s. 32(2)
> "A minor who holds a permit referred to in subsection (1) must not hunt migratory game birds unless they are accompanied by an individual who (a) is the holder of a migratory game bird hunting permit; (b) has held such a permit in a previous year; and (c) is not a minor."

- s. 32(3): no more than two minors at a time (99%).
- BC adds: the youth and the supervisor carry the right BC licences too (99%, page 7).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** the federal rule adds an earlier year that BC does not.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: ducks and geese](#/s/rb-migratory-birds), s. 32; [licences](#/s/rb-licences-fees), page 7 (99%).
```

## Rule 100: Never block another legal hunter

```diagram fw-rules-10-help
```

- **Rule:** no interfering with or obstructing a person licensed to hunt, guide or trap while they do it lawfully (99%).
- **Slip:** setting up in front of another group's decoys, or walking through their stalk on purpose.
- **Fix:** first there keeps the spot; move on (Tip).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Item 45, page 12
> "To interfere with or obstruct a person licensed or permitted to hunt, guide or trap while that person is lawfully so engaged."

- Related: never damage or interfere with a lawfully set trap (99%, item 12).
- Whether crowding a spot counts as interfering depends on the facts: ask a conservation officer (VERIFY).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** public land feels first come, any way you like.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: it is unlawful](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts), items 12 and 45 (99%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram rb-accompany
```

- **Rhyme:** "Free help is a friend; paid help is a guide."
- **Number trick:** 10 to hunt, 3 years to supervise, 2 kids per adult.
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "If you can't hear them, you aren't with them."

```more mistakes and field drill
> [!rule]
> Old hand's rule (wisdom, not law): the youth's deer is your deer. Count it before you lift your own rifle.

> [!mistake]
> - Taking money to help someone hunt.
> - Supervising by radio.
> - A youth's buck plus your buck, over your limit.

> [!field]
> Before a youth hunt: your licences, their licence, both FWID numbers, the plan to stay within talking distance all day.
```
