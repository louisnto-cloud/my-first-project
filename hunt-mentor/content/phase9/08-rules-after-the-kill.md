## Rule 71: Cancel the licence before you touch it

```steps Cancel the licence in 5 steps
rb-notch-1 | Animal down. Do not touch it yet.
rb-notch-2 | Take out the species licence for it.
rb-notch-3 | Cancel it as the licence itself shows.
rb-notch-4 | Now you may handle the animal.
rb-notch-5 | Keep the licence with the meat.
```

- **Rule:** after a big game kill, cancel the species licence at once, before handling the animal (99%).
- **Slip:** gutting first, tagging at the truck.
- **Fix:** knife stays sheathed until the licence is cancelled.

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Item 1, page 11
> "Any person who kills any big game species must immediately after the kill and before handling the big game killed, cancel the appropriate species licence in accordance with the instructions on that licence."

- How to cancel is printed on the licence itself (99%).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** excitement, cold hands, and a deer to get cool.
- **How common:** a Lower Mainland hunter was fined $3,000; the investigation found he had not cancelled his species licence (60%, Prince George Citizen, search preview).
- **Source:** [Rule Book: it is unlawful](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts), item 1; [bag limits](#/s/rb-bag-limits) (99%).
```

## Rule 72: Deer: tail and evidence of sex stay on

```diagram rb-evidence-deer
```

- **Rule:** a deer carcass you carry keeps the unskinned tail and the evidence of sex naturally attached (99%).
- **Slip:** skinning the tail off, or cutting away the testicles, in the field.
- **Fix:** leave both on the hind quarter you keep with you (90%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Page 15: deer, the unskinned tail AND for a male a testicle or part of the penis, for a female a portion of the udder and teats (99%).
- Moose and elk: the tail or a hide piece of at least 6 cm², plus [[Evidence of sex]] (99%).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** butchering habits from TV, and wanting a clean carcass.
- **How common:** at a late October 2023 Boston Bar road check of over 400 hunters and anglers, conservation officers called possessing a carcass without parts attached the biggest issue of non compliance (70%, Q101 News, read directly). An earlier Boston Bar check wrote 9 tickets and 8 warnings for it (60%, Abbotsford News, search preview). Quesnel officers seized 9 deer lacking sex and species evidence (60%, Quesnel Observer, search preview).
- **Source:** [Rule Book: after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill), page 15 (99%).
```

## Rule 73: The evidence comes off only at 3 places

```diagram rb-evidence-off
```

- **Rule:** evidence of species and sex may come off only at home once butchered and stored, at a meat cutter or cold storage plant, or after a compulsory inspection (99%).
- **Slip:** butchering at a friend's cabin on the way home.
- **Fix:** keep it whole, evidence on, until your own freezer.

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 15
> "Evidence of species and sex may be removed from the carcass or the hide of game: after it arrives at a person's normal dwelling place and is butchered and stored there for consumption on the premises, after it is taken to a meat cutter or the owner or operator of a cold storage plant, or after it has been inspected by a qualified Compulsory Inspector."

- A friend's house on the way home is not one of the three (90%, my reading).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** a long drive, and a shop with a cooler halfway.
- **How common:** see rule 72.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill), page 15 (99%).
```

## Rule 74: Grouse and quail keep one wing

```diagram rb-evidence-birds
```

- **Rule:** grouse, quail and other upland birds keep one feathered wing attached while you possess or carry them. A bearded only season turkey keeps the beard (99%).
- **Slip:** breasting grouse at the truck and bagging only meat.
- **Fix:** one wing stays on every bird until home and butchered.

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 15
> "Anyone who possesses or transports a game bird, other than a migratory game bird, must leave attached to the carcass one feathered wing"

- Ducks and geese: a wing or the head (rule 66).
- Upland possession: 3 times the daily limit while hunting or coming home; 2 times for sharp tailed grouse in Region 3 (99%, page 12).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** quick breasting is a common grouse habit.
- **How common:** see rule 72.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill), page 15 (99%).
```

## Rule 75: Bring the meat home

```diagram rb-edible
```

- **Rule:** take the edible portions to your home, a meat cutter or cold storage: big game neck, ribs, all four quarters and loins; birds both breasts (99%).
- **Slip:** quartering a deer and leaving the neck and ribs.
- **Fix:** plan the pack out before the shot. Black bear meat counts too (99%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Definition, page 3
> "EDIBLE PORTIONS - with respect to big game, excluding wolf, lynx, bobcat and wolverine, means the edible portions of the neck, ribs, four quarters and the loins of the animal and with respect to game birds, means the edible portions of both breasts of the bird."

- Item 44, page 12 makes it an offence (99%). Hand it to someone who brings it home and you are covered (99%).
- Gut piles are attractants: never leave them where people are or are likely to be (99%, page 15).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** neck and rib meat look like scraps.
- **How common:** two Vernon men were fined a combined $13,000; one count was failing to remove the [[Edible portions]] of a moose (60%, Global News, search preview).
- **Source:** [Rule Book: it is unlawful](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts), item 44; [definitions](#/s/rb-definitions), page 3 (99%).
```

## Rule 76: Look for every animal you hit

```diagram rb-self-report
```

- **Rule:** wound or kill game and you must make every reasonable effort to find it, finish it, retrieve it and count it in your bag (99%).
- **Slip:** "No blood, must have missed," and walking away.
- **Fix:** mark the spot, search properly. Ducks: have a dog, waders or boat ready (99%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 12
> "No person shall kill, cripple or wound game without making all reasonable effort to locate, dispatch, retrieve and include it in their bag limit."

- Federal s. 42: no hunting migratory birds without adequate means to retrieve them (99%).
- Wounded black bear, cougar or wolf: a guide, youth supervisor or permit to accompany holder may finish it; the hunter still cancels their own licence (99%, page 11).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** a poor blood trail feels like a miss.
- **How common:** a Lower Mainland hunter's conviction included failing to retrieve wildlife (60%, Prince George Citizen, search preview). A Burns Lake officer said complaints about animals shot and left wounded are not unusual (60%, Lakes District News, search preview).
- **Source:** [Rule Book: it is unlawful](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts), retrieval lines page 12; [ducks and geese](#/s/rb-migratory-birds), s. 42 (99%).
```

## Rule 77: Moose or elk licence: report by 31 March

```diagram rb-mhr
```

- **Rule:** everyone who buys an elk, moose or caribou licence files the Mandatory Hunter Report by 31 March, hunt or no hunt (99%).
- **Slip:** no moose, so no report.
- **Fix:** file in WILD (Wildlife Information and Licensing Data) the week after the season (Tip).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 16
> "All individuals who purchase an elk, moose or caribou species licence are required to submit a report on their hunting activity by March 31."

- Miss it: an offence, and no licence or LEH (Limited Entry Hunting) application next season until you file (99%).
- Elk is new on the list this edition (99%).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** it feels like a survey for successful hunters.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found. The Rule Book lists it as a top mistake.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill), page 16 (99%).
```

## Rule 78: A road kill is not yours to keep

```diagram rb-self-report
```

- **Rule:** you may not possess or transport big game killed by accident or collision. Report any accidental kill to an officer promptly (99%).
- **Slip:** loading the deer your truck hit.
- **Fix:** call RAPP (Report All Poachers and Polluters), 1 877 952 7277, and leave it (99%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 15
> "It is unlawful to possess or transport a big game animal that was killed unintentionally, by accident or collision, or was illegally killed."

- Item 18: killing or wounding wildlife by accident, or to protect life or property, must be reported promptly (99%, page 11).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** wasting the meat feels wrong.
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill), page 15; [it is unlawful](#/s/rb-unlawful-acts), item 18 (99%).
```

## Rule 79: Wrong animal? Self report, at once

```diagram rb-self-report
```

- **Rule:** shot the wrong animal? Cancel your species licence at once, write in ink on it that you intend to self report, then call RAPP (99%).
- **Slip:** hiding it, or tagging it with someone else's licence.
- **Fix:** cancel, mark in ink, call. Field dress it so the meat keeps (99%).

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Page 18
> "Immediately cancel your species licence and mark in ink on the species licence that your intention is to self-report."

- No phone nearby: field dress it, leave it, go and call. Remote: field dress and care for it until you can call (99%).
- Every self report is investigated; the officer decides what fits (99%).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** fear. The page says the true test of a hunter is how a mistake is handled (99%).
- **How common:** Tip (opinion). No count found.
- **Source:** [Rule Book: after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill), page 18 (99%).
```

## Rule 80: Stop at every game check

```anim rb-meat-trip The licence, tail and evidence ride with the meat all the way home.
```

- **Rule:** all hunters, with or without game, must stop and report at a temporary check station run by an officer (99%).
- **Slip:** driving past with an empty truck.
- **Fix:** pull in every time; have licences and ID ready.

```more why, how common, and the source
> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

> [!law] Game Checks, page 15
> "All hunters, with or without game, when encountering temporary checking stations operated by an officer, are required by law to stop and report."

- A [[Game check]] is also where officers find most of the slips in this lesson (Tip).
- **Why it trips people (Tip):** "I have nothing, so it is not for me."
- **How common:** the late October 2023 Boston Bar check stopped over 400 hunters and anglers (70%, Q101 News, read directly).
- **Source:** [Rule Book: after the kill](#/s/rb-after-the-kill), page 15 (99%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram fw-rules-08-after
```

- **Rhyme:** "Tag it, leave the tail, bring it all home, then report."
- **Number trick:** 3 places the evidence may come off; 31 March for the moose report.
- **Old hand's rule** (opinion): "The true test is how you handle the mistake." (Echoes page 18.)

```more mistakes and field drill
> [!rule]
> Old hand's rule (wisdom, not law): the tail rides home on the deer, not in the gut pile.

> [!mistake]
> - Skinning the tail off in the field.
> - No Mandatory Hunter Report on an unfilled moose licence.
> - Hiding a mistake instead of self reporting.

> [!field]
> At the kill: cancel, photo of the licence beside the animal, tail and evidence on, every edible portion bagged.
```
