## 1. Hunt the wind

```diagram hf-upwind-rule
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Hunt the wind, or hunt somewhere else."

- **Means:** keep the wind blowing from the animal to you. Wrong wind: change spots.
- **Label:** tradition. Scent and movement beat camouflage colour (85%, National Deer Association).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** deer, elk, moose and bears check danger with their noses. The National Deer Association says scent and movement are far more important than clothing colour (85%, read directly).
- The app says it many ways: "Hunt the wind, or hunt somewhere else" ([White tailed deer masterclass 3](#/s/mc-white-tailed-deer-03)), "Hunt the wind you have, not the wind you wanted" ([Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals)).
- **When it is wrong:** a steady crosswind works too, as long as your scent does not reach where the animal will be (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** every hunting educator and the app's wind lessons.
```

## 2. Every ridge, a new wind

```anim hf-milkweed One milkweed tuft shows what the air really does.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "Every new ridge is a new wind."

- **Means:** check the wind again at every ridge, draw, saddle and creek.
- **Label:** opinion. Terrain bends wind (80%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** wind tumbles behind crests, squeezes through saddles and pools where draws meet (80%, the app's terrain wind lesson; standard mountain weather).
- Tools: a puff of powder, a thread on the barrel, milkweed fluff. See [Skills masterclass: wind](#/s/mc-skills-03).
- **When it is wrong:** on flat open fields with a strong steady wind, one check lasts longer (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** the app's skills lessons, widely repeated by mountain hunters (Realtree, onX).
```

## 3. Cool air sinks, warm air climbs

```anim sg-thermal-day Morning sun: air climbs. Evening cool: air sinks.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Cool air sinks, warm air climbs."

- **Means:** on calm days air slides downhill in the cold, and uphill once the sun warms the slope.
- **Label:** tradition, backed by physics (80%). See [[Thermals]].

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** cold air is heavier and drains downhill. Sun warmed air rises up the slope (80%).
- Hunting sources agree on the daily cycle: upslope as the sun warms the slopes, downslope as shady canyons cool in the evening (75%, onX and Realtree, search preview).
- **When it is wrong:** the first hour of the morning. Sources disagree, because air often keeps sliding downhill until the sun actually hits the slope (75%).
- A passing front or a strong wind wipes thermals out (70%).
- **Where it comes from:** mountain hunters everywhere. App: [Wind and thermals](#/s/wind-thermals).
```

## 4. A swirl is a no

```diagram mc-skills-terrain-wind
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "A swirl is a no. Back out, come back."

- **Means:** if your wind check curls and changes, every animal around may smell you.
- **Label:** opinion, from the app's [wind skills](#/s/mc-skills-03) (Tip).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** a swirl spreads your scent in all directions, so no approach is safe (Tip).
- Elk version in the app: "When the wind swirls, go home and come back tomorrow" ([Elk masterclass 3](#/s/mc-elk-03)).
- **When it is wrong:** if the animal is already walking to you and in range, a swirl may not reach it in time (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** widely repeated mountain hunting advice. No study.
```

## 5. Red sky at night

```diagram fw-grandpa-red-sky
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Red sky at night, shepherd's delight. Red sky at morning, take warning."

- **Means:** a red sunset often means dry weather tomorrow; a red sunrise, weather coming.
- **Label:** tradition, partly true where weather moves west to east (75%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** high pressure traps dust that scatters blue light and leaves red. A red sunset means the high is coming from the west. A red sunrise means it has passed east and a low may follow (75%, UK Met Office explainer, search preview).
- Most BC weather arrives off the Pacific, from the west (70%).
- **When it is wrong:** where weather does not move west to east, and in mountains where local weather builds fast. A rough sign, not a forecast (75%).
- **Where it comes from:** the Bible, Gospel of Matthew, and shepherds (75%, Met Office).
```

## 6. Ring around the moon

```diagram fw-grandpa-halo
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Ring around the moon, rain or snow soon."

- **Means:** a halo round the moon can mean wet weather in a day or two.
- **Label:** tradition, partly true (65%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** the halo comes from ice crystals in thin high cloud. That cloud often runs ahead of a warm front, before the rain clouds (65%, Live Science and TV meteorologists, search preview).
- **When it is wrong:** the same thin cloud can pass with no change. A rough sign only (65%).
- Already in the app as a Grandpa's rule: [Field weather](#/s/field-weather).
- **Where it comes from:** sailors and farmers.
```

## 7. Hunt ahead of the front

```anim rq-wx-front-pass A front passes: wind swings, rain, then clearing.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "Hunt the day before the storm."

- **Means:** many hunters believe deer feed hard before a front arrives.
- **Label:** opinion. Studies disagree (60%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **For:** a North Carolina study reported in Buckmasters caught more deer under a falling barometer (60%, search preview).
- **Against:** Penn State collared deer moved a little less during storms, but found no meaningful effect of oncoming winter storms (75%, search preview). Quality Deer Management staff said half a dozen projects testing pressure and fronts found nothing (60%).
- **When it is wrong:** often. Go when you can; do not wait for the perfect front (Tip).
- App: [Weather fronts](#/s/fronts-and-movement): "The front tells you where to sit, not whether to go."
- **Where it comes from:** hunting magazines and camp talk.
```

## 8. Deer lie up in a gale

```diagram rq-rw-wind-evidence
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (tradition)
> "Stay home on a windy day. The deer do."

- **Means:** the old belief that deer bed down when it blows.
- **Label:** tradition. Evidence mixed (75%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Penn State, GPS (Global Positioning System) collars:** deer moved MORE on windy days and LESS on windy nights, even at light wind (80%, Penn State Deer Forest blog, read directly).
- **Oklahoma, 7 years of GPS collars:** very little change in distance moved with wind speed (70%, search preview).
- **South Texas, older radio collars:** movement dipped at 24 to 31 km/h (15 to 19 mph), then rose again above 32 km/h (20 mph) (65%, search preview).
- **When it is wrong:** on most windy days. Wind also covers your noise (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** camp talk.
```

## 9. Cold snaps move deer

```diagram fn-wx-coldfront
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion)
> "When the cold snaps, the deer get up."

- **Means:** a sudden drop in temperature gets deer feeding in daylight.
- **Label:** opinion. Some support for temperature (65%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **For:** a Mississippi State study found temperature influenced deer movement more than any other weather factor (65%, secondary).
- **Against:** a Texas study found no link between buck activity and temperature (60%).
- **When it is wrong:** a warm spell in the rut can still have bucks on their feet (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** hunting magazines and camp talk.
```

## 10. Hunt the day you can go

```anim sa-deer-day A deer day: beds at noon, feeds at dawn and dusk.
```

> [!rule] Grandpa's rule (opinion, widely repeated)
> "The best day to hunt is the day you can go."

- **Means:** do not wait for the perfect moon or weather. Be out at dawn and dusk.
- **Label:** opinion. Dawn and dusk beat moon and weather in studies (85%).

```more why, when it is wrong, where it comes from
- **Why it works:** in Penn State collar data, dawn and dusk activity was the main pattern in every moon phase (85%, Penn State Deer Forest blog, 2017).
- A South Carolina GPS thesis: weather and moon effects were smaller than the daily rhythm (75%, Auburn University, search preview).
- Same idea in the app: "Hunt the weather you have" ([Mule deer masterclass 6](#/s/mc-mule-deer-06)).
- **When it is wrong:** dangerous weather. Lightning, ice storms and gales on the lake: stay home (Tip).
- **Where it comes from:** widely repeated hunting advice.
```

## Myth check

```diagram fw-grandpa-myth-meter
```

| Saying | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Hunt by the moon phase" | Myth (85%) |
| "The moon sets the rut" | Myth (70%) |
| "Watch the barometer" | Mixed (60%) |

```more the evidence for each
- **Moon phase:** GPS collared does in three Pennsylvania state forests, October 2015 and 2016: "Moon phase has an insignificant effect on deer movement" (85%, Penn State Deer Forest blog, read directly). Bucks were not in that study. App: [Moon and daylight](#/s/moon-and-daylight).
- **Moon and rut:** University of Georgia researchers used fetal aging from agency data and found moon phase has virtually no bearing on breeding dates. Day length sets the rut (70%, secondary reports).
- **Barometer:** a few studies find an effect, many do not; temperature looks more important (60%).
```

## Memory hooks

```diagram fw-grandpa-red-sky
```

- **Rhyme:** "Red at night, dry and bright. Red at dawn, rain comes on."
- **Rhyme:** "Cool air sinks, warm air climbs."
- **Number trick:** 0: the moon's meaningful effect on doe movement in the Penn State study (85%).

```more more hooks
- **Wind first:** "Hunt the wind, or hunt somewhere else."
- **New ridge, new check.**
- **Dawn and dusk** beat any moon table.

> [!mistake]
> - Skipping a hunt because a moon chart says "poor".
> - Checking the wind once at the truck, then never again.
> - Trusting morning thermals before the sun reaches the slope.

> [!field]
> Carry a puffer or milkweed. Check it at the truck, at every ridge, and when you sit.
```
