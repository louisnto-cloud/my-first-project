## The numbers

```diagram sk-temp-chart
```

- Danger zone 4 to 60°C. Freezer minus 18°C. Cook to 71°C (80 to 95%).
- Best: in a cooler the same day as the kill (80%).

```more the numbers table and fridge times
| Number | What it means |
|---|---|
| 4°C to 60°C (40°F to 140°F) | The danger zone. Bacteria can double every 20 minutes (80%, Penn State Extension) |
| Below 4°C (40°F) | Target for the carcass. Slows bacteria, does not kill them (80%) |
| 1°C to 3°C (34°F to 37°F) | Aging range, 7 to 14 days max (80%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife) |
| Minus 18°C (0°F) | Freezer. Game keeps about a year (80%, Penn State Extension) |
| 71°C (160°F) | Minimum internal cooking temperature for all wild game (95%, Canadian Food Inspection Agency) |

- Fridge life once home: about 5 days for cuts, 2 days for ground meat and birds (80%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife).
- Cool it "ideally" by "taking the carcass to a cooler on the day of the kill" (80%).
```

## Cooling in the field

```steps Cool it in 5 steps
sk-cool-1 | Guts out or meat off within the hour.
sk-gut-7 | Prop the chest open so air moves.
sk-gut-8 | Shade, off the ground, never direct sun.
sk-cool-4 | Above 4°C: ice bags in the cavity.
sk-cooler | Into a cooler the same day.
```

- Above freezing: skin elk, moose and bear (80%).
- No plastic, no tarp: "Plastic will hold heat in" (80%).

```more cooling details
- Guts out or meat off within the hour. The cavity is the hottest part and stays hot (80%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game).
- Air must move. Prop the chest open. Hang it, or lay it on logs. Off the ground (80%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife).
- Shade. Never direct sun (80%).
- Above freezing: skin big animals. Elk, moose and bear hold heat under the hide (80%).
- Above 4°C: bags of ice or snow in the cavity, tied shut (80%, Penn State Extension).
- No plastic, no tarp on a warm carcass. "Plastic will hold heat in" (80%).
```

## Why never a tarp

```anim sk-tarp-heat Open air lets heat out. A tarp holds it in.
```

- Plastic and tarps trap heat on a warm carcass (80%).

## Game bags

```diagram sk-gutless-8
```

- Breathable cloth, one per quarter. Hang apart, never stacked (80%).
- Never inside garbage bags (80%).

```more game bag details and prices
- Breathable cloth bags. One per quarter, one for loins and neck, one for rib and trim. Tip.
- Bags keep dirt, hair and flies off and let the surface dry (80%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game).
- Hang bags apart. Do not stack them, do not put them in garbage bags (80%).
- Entry: cheesecloth style, about $20 a set. Mid: synthetic reusable, about $60 to $90. Premium: heavy synthetic moose sets. Prices not checked, VERIFY before buying.
- Wash and reuse. Dry fully before storing. Tip.
```

## Hanging and aging

```diagram sk-aging
```

| Do not age these | Sure? |
|---|---|
| Young animal | 80% |
| Warm day, not chilled fast | 80% |
| Ran hard or trailed for hours | 80% |
| Heavy wound damage or dirty | 80% |

```more aging details
- Aging: holding the carcass at 1°C to 3°C (34°F to 37°F) "for 7 to 14 days" so enzymes tenderise the meat (80%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife).
- North Dakota State University says 10 to 14 days in the same range (80%).
- "No longer than two weeks" (80%).
- Hide on while aging protects the meat from drying (80%, Penn State Extension, aging at 0°C to 3°C with hide on).
- Do not age: a young animal, one shot in warm weather and not chilled fast, one that ran hard or was trailed for hours, one with heavy wound damage, or a dirty carcass (80%).
- Slime, mould or off smell: stop, trim, freeze at once (80%).
- No cold room? Skip aging. Cut and freeze within a few days at fridge temperature. Tip.
```

## Flies, rain, warm October days

```diagram sk-rain-roof
```

- Rain: a tarp as a roof, with an air gap. Never wrapped (80%).
- Flies: bags, plus pepper or citric acid on the surface (80%).

```more flies, rain and warm days in detail
- Flies: a skinned carcass in cheesecloth or a game bag. Black pepper or food grade citric acid on the surface helps (80%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife).
- A dry crust on the meat "deters egg laying insects" (80%, Alaska Department of Fish and Game).
- Rain: tarp as a roof above, with an air gap. Never a tarp wrapped around (80%).
- Wet meat spoils faster. Dry it with paper towel and keep it in bags under cover. Tip.
- Warm day plan: skin, quarter, bag, shade by noon. Cooler by evening. Tip.
- Night cold, day warm: hang it out at night, cooler by day. Tip.
```

## Cooler and ice plan

```diagram sk-cooler
```

- Ice on the bottom, bagged meat on top. Drain melt daily (Tip).
- Thermometer inside: under 4°C or add ice (80%).

```more cooler details
- Two coolers: one for quarters, one for loins, neck and trim. A 70 litre cooler holds a boned deer with ice. Tip.
- Frozen water jugs or bagged ice on the bottom. Meat in game bags on top. Drain the melt water daily. Meat never sits in water. Tip.
- Keep the lid closed and the cooler in the shade of the truck, not the cab. Tip.
- A cheap thermometer inside. Under 4°C or add ice (80%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife target).
- Transport: carcass "clean and dry", "out of direct sunlight", with air flow. Chill fully before any plastic (80%).
- Long drive from Heffley Creek to Mission in October: boned meat in coolers with ice, not a whole deer on the roof. Tip.
```

## What spoils meat

| Cause | Example |
|---|---|
| Heat | Cavity, hide, sun, truck (80%) |
| Moisture | Rain, washing, plastic (80%) |
| Dirt and hair | Meat on the ground (80%) |
| Gut contents | Cut paunch or bladder (80%) |
| Delay | Slow gutting, slow cooling (80%) |
| Stress | Animal run hard (80%) |

```more what spoils meat in detail
- Heat: the cavity, the hide, the sun, the truck (80%).
- Moisture: rain, washing, condensation inside plastic (80%).
- Dirt and hair: wipe off at once, keep meat on the hide or a tarp (80%).
- Gut contents: a cut paunch or bladder. Wash the spot, dry it, trim it (80%).
- Delay: slow gutting and "failure to cool the carcass promptly" are the usual causes of strong flavours (80%, North Dakota State University).
- Stress: an animal run hard before it died is tougher and ages badly (80%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife).
```

## Bear meat and trichinosis

| Method | Kills wild Trichinella? |
|---|---|
| Freezing | No (95%) |
| Salting, drying, smoking, microwave | Not reliably (95%) |
| Cooking to 71°C or more | Yes, federal advice (95%) |

- Cook all bear meat to at least 71°C (95%).

```more the federal advice in full
> [!law]
> Not a BC hunting rule, but federal food safety advice: "All wild game meat, pork and horse meat should be cooked to an internal temperature of at least 71°C" (95%, Canadian Food Inspection Agency).

- In Canada "the primary risk for acquiring trichinellosis is through the consumption of undercooked or raw meat from wildlife species such as bears and walruses" (95%).
- "Most species of Trichinella found in wildlife are resistant to freezing" (95%). Freezing a bear roast does not make it safe.
- "Curing (salting), drying, smoking or microwaving the meat does not consistently kill infective larvae" (95%). No bear jerky unless cooked to 71°C first.
- Washington goes higher: 74°C (165°F) for all game, 77°C (170°F) for bear (80%).
- Skin a bear fast. The coat and fat hold heat (80%).
- Bear fat renders well. Trim it off the meat for storage. Tip.
- Bear meat is a legal edible portion in BC like any big game (95%, synopsis p. 3).
```

## Bird care

| Step | Do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Gut, remove the crop (80%) |
| 2 | Cool apart, 2 to 4°C (80%) |
| 3 | Leave a wing or head on until home (95%) |
| 4 | Cook to 74°C (80%) |

```more bird care details
- Gut and remove the crop as soon as you can. It opens the cavity for cooling (80%, North Dakota State University).
- Cool to 2°C to 4°C (35°F to 40°F) fast. Do not pile warm birds. Keep them apart in the cooler (80%, Penn State Extension).
- Keep one fully feathered wing on grouse and quail, a wing or the feathered head on ducks and geese, until home (95%, synopsis p. 15).
- Pluck ducks if you will roast them. The skin keeps the meat moist (80%, Penn State Extension).
- Grouse and quail: soak 1 to 2 hours in cold water to pull blood out, then fridge (80%).
- Age birds no more than 2 to 3 days at 4°C or less (80%, Penn State Extension).
- Cook birds to 74°C (165°F) (80%, North Dakota State University).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Warm quarters in a tarp | Game bags, open air |
| Aging a gut shot deer trailed 5 hours | Cut and freeze |
| Frozen bear called safe | Cook to 71°C |

> [!rule]
> If you would not leave a steak on the dashboard, do not leave a deer in the truck.

```more 3 common mistakes and the field habit
- Wrapping warm quarters in a tarp "to keep them clean" for the drive.
- Aging a gut shot deer that was trailed for 5 hours in the sun.
- Freezing bear meat and calling it safe. Freezing does not kill wild Trichinella.

> [!field]
> Thermometer in the cooler. Check it when you check the ice. Under 4°C, every time you look.
```

```checklist p5s8-meat
Guts out or meat off within the hour
Carcass hung or propped open, in shade, off the ground
Skinned if above freezing
Game bags on, no plastic, no tarp wrap
Ice in the cavity or the cooler if above 4°C
Cooler thermometer under 4°C
Birds gutted, cooled apart, wing or head left on
Bear meat marked: cook to 71°C or more
```
