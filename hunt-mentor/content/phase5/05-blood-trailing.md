> [!why]
> Most lost deer are lost in the first 10 minutes. The hunter walks in too soon and pushes a dying animal out of the country.

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

## Watch and listen

```diagram sk-reactions
```

- Rifle on the animal until it is gone. Reload, safety on (Tip).
- Fix the last spot you saw it. Pin it, note the time (Tip).

```more what to watch for
- Keep the rifle on the animal until it is out of sight. Then reload, safety on. Tip.
- Mule kick, then a hard low run: often heart or lungs (70%, National Deer Association).
- Hunched back, short run, then a walk: often a gut hit (70%).
- Hard run with a limp: often a leg hit (70%).
- Listen for a crash. Fix the exact last spot you saw it: a tree, a rock, a stump. Tip.
- Phone pin for where you shot from and where the animal stood. Note the time. Tip.
```

## Wait times by hit type

```diagram sk-wait-chart
```

- Heart or lungs out of sight: 30 minutes minimum (70%).
- Liver or gut: back out for hours (70%). Opinion, not law.

```more wait table with sources
| What you saw | Wait | Where this comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Heart or lungs, went down in sight | Go when it stops moving | Tip |
| Heart or lungs, ran out of sight | 30 minutes minimum (70%) | Hunter Ed, National Deer Association |
| Liver: dark blood, no bubbles | 4 to 6 hours (70%) | Hunter Ed. National Deer Association says 2 to 4 hours |
| Gut: green or brown, smells | 6 to 8 hours, or overnight if it is cool (70%) | Hunter Ed. National Deer Association says 4 to 6 hours |
| Muscle or leg | 30 to 60 minutes, then expect thin blood (70%) | Hunter Ed |
| Not sure where it hit | Several hours (70%) | Hunter Ed |

- These are opinions from hunter education material and experienced trackers, not law (70%).
- A rifle hit in the heart or lungs: the deer is usually dead within 150 yards (137 m) (70%, National Deer Association).
- Warm afternoon and a gut hit: the meat clock and the waiting clock fight. Wait less, trim more. Tip.
- Rain or snow coming: go sooner. Rain erases blood in an hour. Tip.
```

## Mark the spot

```diagram sk-flag-line
```

- Flag where it stood and where you last saw it (70%).
- Paper or tape on every blood spot. Collect the tape (Tip).

```more marking details
- Walk to where the animal stood. Flag it with tape (70%, Hunter Ed).
- Look for blood, hair, bone chips, scuffed ground, a bullet furrow. Tip.
- Flag the last place you saw it too. Now you have a line. Tip.
- Every blood spot after that: a square of toilet paper or a strip of tape. Paper rots, tape must be collected. Tip.
- Phone pins are good. Flags you can see from the last flag are better. They show the line of travel. Tip.
```

## Reading blood

```diagram sk-blood-chart
```

- Pink and bubbly: lungs. Dark, no bubbles: liver (70%).
- Peroxide fizzes on blood you cannot see in leaves (70%).

```more blood reading details
| Blood | Likely hit |
|---|---|
| Bright pink or crimson, frothy, bubbles | Lungs (70%) |
| Bright red, lots of it | Heart, a big artery, or a leg (70%) |
| Dark red, no bubbles | Liver (70%) |
| Thin, greenish brown, bits of food, smells | Gut (70%) |
| Thin and patchy, animal keeps a normal pace | Muscle (70%) |

- Blood sprayed on brush both sides of the trail: a pass through, bleeding well. Tip.
- Blood only low and on one side: maybe no exit hole. Expect less blood. Tip.
- Spray bottle of hydrogen peroxide: it fizzes on blood you cannot see in leaves (70%, National Deer Association).
```

## Hair at the hit site

```diagram sk-hair
```

- Hair tells you how high you hit. Hunter lore, so Tip.
- Hair but no blood: back out, give it more time (70%).

```more hair types
- Hair tells you how high you hit. Hunter lore, so Tip:
  - Long, white, fine: belly or brisket. Low hit.
  - Short, brown or grey with dark tips: side of the chest. Good.
  - Long, coarse, hollow, grey brown: top of the back. High hit, maybe not fatal.
  - White with a dark band: tail or rump.
- Hair but no blood at all: back out and give it more time (70%, Hunter Ed).
```

## Trail it

```anim sk-trail-beside Walk beside the line. Lost it? Back to the last flag.
```

- Walk beside the blood, not on it (70%).
- Wounded deer head downhill and to water (70%).

```more trailing details
- Walk beside the blood line, not on it. You may need it again (70%, BC Outdoors Magazine).
- Move slowly. Look ahead for the animal, not only down for blood. Tip.
- A bedded animal ahead? Stop. If it is legal hours and the backstop is safe, finish it. If not, back out again. Tip.
- Lost the blood? Go back to the last flag. Never wander ahead making a second set of tracks (70%).
- Wounded animals head downhill and to water, often on a game trail. Check creeks (70%, National Deer Association).
```

## Grid search

```anim sk-grid-search Circles at 20 m and 40 m, then lanes 10 m apart.
```

- Lanes about 10 m apart, each person in sight of the next (70%).
- Nothing? Come back at first light. Ravens show you (Tip).

```more grid search details
- Last blood is the centre. Circle it at 20 m, then 40 m. Tip.
- Nothing? Lanes. Walk parallel lines about 10 m apart, each person in sight of the next (70%, National Deer Association).
- Brother on one lane, you on the next. Talk low so you stay even. Tip.
- Look under deadfall, in thickets, in creek bottoms. A dead deer is brown and low. Tip.
- Still nothing: come back at first light. Ravens and magpies will show you. Tip.
```

## The law on hours and lights

```diagram sk-hours
```

- Following a trail with a firearm is hunting (95%).
- No hunting after hours or with a light (95%).
- Lost the light on a lung hit? Call RAPP (Report All Poachers and Polluters), 1 877 952 7277.

```more the law in full
> [!lean]
> Lost the light and sure of a lung hit? Call the Conservation Officer Service on the [[RAPP]] line (Report All Poachers and Polluters, 1 877 952 7277) and ask for direction. Go with a headlamp and no rifle if they say yes. Doubtful hit: back out until legal light.

> [!law]
> Unlawful: "To hunt game, except migratory game birds from one hour after sunset to one hour before sunrise" (95%). Unlawful: "To hunt wildlife by the use of, or with the aid of a light or illuminating device" (95%).

- "Hunt" includes "following after or on the trail of" wildlife "while in possession of a firearm or other weapon" (95%).
- My reading: tracking with your rifle after legal hours, or with a headlamp, is hunting outside hours and with a light. Do not (85%).
- Tracking with no firearm, only to find a dead animal: the [[Synopsis]] (BC Hunting and Trapping Regulations Synopsis) does not say. BC Outdoors Magazine says tracking "can still meet the legal definition of hunting" (70%). VERIFY with a conservation officer.
- You cannot shoot a wounded animal after legal hours. No exception (95%).
- Infrared optics on a hunting trip are banned (95%).
- "The responsibility remains with the hunter to make the determination of sunrise and sunset in the field" (95%).
```

## You must try to recover it

| Situation | What the law says |
|---|---|
| Wounded animal | All reasonable effort to find it (95%) |
| Boat retrieval | No loaded firearm aboard, or motor off (99%) |
| Wrong animal or accident | Cancel licence, report (95%) |

- Walking away after 10 minutes is not reasonable effort (95%).

```more the law in full
> [!law]
> "No person shall kill, cripple or wound game without making all reasonable effort to locate, dispatch, retrieve and include it in their bag limit" (95%).

- The effort is the law. Walking away after 10 minutes is not reasonable effort (95%).
- You may retrieve a dead or injured animal with a boat if nobody aboard has a loaded firearm, or if the boat is not being propelled by a motor (99%, synopsis page 12, 2 October 2026 edition). Since 1 October 2026 the Firearm Violence Prevention Act (FVPA) also bans a loaded firearm in a boat while the motor pushes it (95%).
- Killed the wrong animal, or one by accident? Cancel the licence, write on it in ink that you intend to self report, and call RAPP (95%).
- Unlawful: to kill or wound wildlife by accident "and fail to promptly report the killing to an Officer" (95%).
```

## Private land, parks, closed areas

```diagram sk-fence
```

- The Trespass Act has no tracking exception (95%).
- Find the owner and ask. No answer: call a conservation officer (Tip).

```more trespass, parks and No Shooting Areas
- The Trespass Act has no hunting or tracking exception. Entering "premises that are enclosed land" is an offence (95%, Trespass Act s. 2).
- "A person found on or in premises that are enclosed land is presumed to be on or in the premises without the consent of an occupier" (95%, s. 2).
- [[Enclosed land]]: a lawful fence, a natural boundary, or trespass signs at each ordinary access point (95%, s. 1 and s. 4).
- The only defences: consent of the occupier, other lawful authority, or colour of right (95%, s. 3). A dying deer is not one of them (85%, my reading).
- Wildlife "taken while trespassing on private property" is an unlawful kill (95%, synopsis p. 11).
- So: find the owner, ask. Written consent is best. No owner, no answer: call a [[Conservation officer]]. Tip.
- A conservation officer can arrest a trespasser without a warrant (95%, s. 7).
- Park closed to hunting: you may not even possess a firearm there except inside a vehicle or with a park officer's say so (95%, synopsis p. 9).
- [[No Shooting Area]]: no discharge, so no finishing shot. Retrieve without the rifle, or call a conservation officer (90%).
```

## When to get help

| Situation | Call |
|---|---|
| Thick timber, snow coming | Your brother, now |
| Gut hit | More eyes, not more hours |
| Private land, closed area | Conservation officer |

- Dogs on deer in BC must be on a leash (95%).

```more help details
- Call your brother before you start trailing, not after dark. Tip.
- Thick timber, snow coming, or a gut hit: more eyes beat more hours. Tip.
- Dogs on deer in BC must be on a leash (95%, synopsis p. 12). A leashed dog with a nose can help. Tip.
- No shame in a conservation officer call. They would rather help than find a wasted deer. Tip.
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| In after 5 minutes on a gut hit | Wait 6 to 8 hours |
| Rifle and headlamp after dark | That is hunting with a light |
| Over the posted fence | Stop and ask |

> [!rule]
> The deer is not lost until you have walked every lane twice. Then go home, and come back at dawn.

```more 3 common mistakes and the trailing kit
> [!field]
> Trailing kit in the pack: flagging tape, headlamp, spare batteries, spray bottle of hydrogen peroxide, 10 m of rope, phone with map pins, RAPP number saved.

- Walking in 5 minutes after the shot on a gut hit.
- Tracking after dark with the rifle and a headlamp. That is hunting with a light.
- Crossing a fence onto posted land because "the deer is right there".
```
