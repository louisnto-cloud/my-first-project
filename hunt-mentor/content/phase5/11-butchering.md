> [!why]
> A deer is about 25 kg of meat. Cut well, that is 60 meals. Cut badly, it is 10 kg of freezer burnt grind.

> [!warn]
> Study aid only. The official regulations are the law.

- Two paths: a kitchen table and a sharp knife, or a cut and wrap shop.
- The law follows the meat either way: licence or Record of Receipt with it, evidence of sex on until it is butchered, butcher receipt with your [[FWID]] (Fish and Wildlife ID) (95%).
- Business names below are confirmed only where stated. Phone before you drive.

## Cuts from a deer

- Front quarters: shoulder roasts, or bone out for stew and grind. The shank goes to grind or slow braise (80%, North Dakota State University Extension).
- Backstraps (loins): the long muscles either side of the spine. Loin chops or whole roasts. The best steaks (80%).
- Tenderloins: two small muscles inside the cavity. Eat them first (80%).
- Hind quarters: follow the seams into sirloin tip, inside round, outside round and eye. Steaks and roasts (80%).
- Neck: a roast or grind. Great braised whole (80%).
- Ribs and flank: trim the meat off for grind, or keep a rack for the smoker (Tip).
- Trim, shank, neck: burger and sausage. About a third of the deer by weight (Tip).
- Heart and liver: fresh within two days (80%, Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife fridge life).

- Prices are guesses for budgeting, not checked. VERIFY before buying.
- Muscle boning on a table needs only a sharp boning knife. No saw (80%, North Dakota State University).
- Keep the meat cold while you work. Cut one quarter, fridge the rest (80%, Penn State Extension).
- Clean boards and knives in hot soapy water, then a weak bleach rinse, and let them air dry (80%, North Dakota State University).

## Grinding and sausage

- Trim silver skin, fat, hair and bloodshot before the grinder. Venison fat tastes waxy and goes rancid (Tip, common practice).
- Chill meat and the grinder parts near freezing. Warm meat smears (Tip).
- Burger: add 10 to 20% pork or beef fat for juiciness, or none for lean (Tip).
- Shops: the Maple Ridge shop runs a 25 lb (11 kg) minimum sausage batch: 19 lb game plus 6 lb of 50/50 pork (85%, read on their site 2026-10-01).
- Sausage prices there: $4 to $7.50 per lb of finished product, plus $1 per lb for vacuum packing (85%, price checked 2026-10-01).
- Cook ground game to 71°C (160°F) (95%, Canadian Food Inspection Agency; North Dakota State University).

## Wrapping and labelling

- Freezer paper with interleaving paper between steaks, or vacuum bags (80%, North Dakota State University).
- Label every package: species, cut, weight, date, which animal. Example: "Mule deer buck, backstrap, 600 g, 18 Oct 2026, Heffley" (Tip).
- Keep a freezer list on the door. Oldest first (Tip).
- Minus 18°C (0°F). About a year of quality (80%, Penn State Extension).
- Keep the notched licence "until the animal has been consumed" (95%, synopsis p. 7). Staple a photo of it to the freezer list. Tip.
- The butcher receipt with FWID, licence number, species and sex goes in the same folder (95%, p. 15).

## Questions for any shop

- Do you cut wild game, and when do you start taking it? Tip.
- Does my animal stay separate, or is grind pooled? Tip.
- Price per lb on hanging weight or finished weight? Minimum charge? Sausage minimum batch? Tip.
- Deliver skinned, quartered, or whole? Bone in or boned? Tip.
- What do you need from me? They must record your FWID, licence number, species, sex and the MU (95%, synopsis p. 14, p. 16).
- Get the receipt. "Obtain from the butcher a receipt" with FWID, licence number, species and sex (95%, p. 15).

> [!rule]
> Label like a stranger will cook it. In February, you are the stranger.

> [!mistake]
> - Grinding the backstraps because "it is all going to burger anyway".
> - Leaving fat and silver skin in the grind. That is the gamey taste.
> - Dropping a deer at a shop with no notched licence and no Record of Receipt.

> [!field]
> Phone two shops in September. Write down the price, the minimum and the drop off rules in your journal.
