> [!why]
> The rifle is zeroed from a bench. Deer are shot from dirt, snow and sticks. The gap between the two is the shot you miss.

## Key points

- Four standard rifle positions: prone, sitting, kneeling, standing. Prone is the steadiest. Standing is the hardest (85%).
- Get closer if you can. Get steadier if you can. A rest beats a position (Tip).
- Sticks or a tripod turn a standing shot into a real option in tall grass and brush (70%).
- Five fundamentals: aiming, breath control, movement control, trigger control, follow through (85%).
- Dry fire builds the trigger press for free. Tikka says use a snap cap (95%).
- Your max ethical range is a number you tested, not a feeling. The 20 cm test is on a later screen (Hunt Mentor method, opinion).
- Four weeks and about 60 rounds gets a beginner there (Tip).

## The four positions

```diagram positions
```

- **Prone.** Flat on the ground, both elbows down. The steadiest of the four. Best for learning the fundamentals because it is easiest to hold (85%).
  - Needs flat, open ground. Grass and sage block the view at deer height (Tip).
- **Sitting.** Both arms supported by your legs. Next to prone, the steadiest (85%).
  - Elbows go inside the knees, bone against the soft inside of the knee, not elbow point on kneecap (Tip).
  - Face downhill on a slope and sitting becomes almost a bench (Tip).
- **Kneeling.** Only one arm braced. Less steady than prone and sitting (85%).
  - Fast to get into. Gets your scope above low brush (Tip).
  - Sit on the heel of your rear foot. Rest the support elbow on the raised knee (Tip).
- **Standing (offhand).** Neither arm supported. The most difficult position for an accurate shot (85%).
  - Do not try to stop the wobble. Keep it small and smooth (85%).
  - Beginner rule: offhand only when the animal is close and there is no time to sit (Tip).

## Sticks and tripods

- Standing is the least stable position. Sticks improve stability and increase your effective range while standing (70%).
- Three leg sticks: two legs toward you, spread, third leg toward the target. Height about your armpit or a little lower (70%).
- Set your feet nearly square to the rifle, not bladed like a pistol stance. Your legs are the rear bipod (70%).
- Hold the forend as normal and rest the back of your hand on the sticks. Most control, fastest second shot (70%).
- A rifle mounted bipod works prone and sitting, or braced against a tree (70%).
- Natural rests: a tree, a pack, a rock. Hard rests can shift the shot high, soft rests low. Learn yours on paper (70%).
- Carry the rest you practised with. A tripod you have never dry fired from is a surprise at the worst time (Tip).

> [!lean]
> Buy a 3 leg trigger style shooting stick for the first season. It works standing and sitting, and doubles as a walking stick on the hill. Price checked 2026-10-01: VERIFY in the gear session.

## Trigger and breath

- Draw a deep breath. Let about half out. Hold it while you squeeze (85%).
- Held too long? Your pulse rises and the rifle moves. Take another breath and start over (85%).
- Trigger finger: the trigger rests on the pad, between the first joint and the fingertip (85%).
- Slow, steady pressure until the rifle fires. Jerking the trigger or clenching the hand moves the gun enough to miss (85%).
- Follow through: keep the squeeze and the sight picture until the bullet has left the barrel (85%).
- Hold your aim: put any movement on the target and keep the area of movement small (85%).
- B.R.A.S.S.: Breathe, Relax, Aim, Squeeze, Squeeze more. A common memory aid (85%).
- Call your shot. Say where the crosshair was when the shot broke. If you cannot, you blinked (Tip).

## Dry fire plan

> [!warn]
> Dry fire is only safe with zero ammunition in the room. Every session. No exceptions.

1. Take every round out of the room. Magazine empty. Pockets empty (Tip).
2. [[PROVE]] the rifle safe: Point, Remove, Observe, Verify, Examine (85%).
3. Put a snap cap in the chamber. Tikka's manual says long term dry firing may damage the bolt and tells you to use a snap cap. Spring loaded "primers" are ideal (95%).
4. Pick an aim point with a real [[Backstop]]: a basement wall, never a window or a door (Tip).
5. Ten slow presses per position: prone, sitting, kneeling, sticks. Watch the crosshair at the click. It must not jump (Tip).
6. Say "finished" out loud. Snap cap out. Rifle locked away before ammunition comes back in (Tip).

- Ten minutes, three evenings a week, is plenty (Tip).
- Target: a 2 cm dot at 5 m. From sitting it should sit still through the click within two weeks (Tip).
- Dry fire from the sticks too. One writer's wife dry fired from sticks for a week of evenings, then made a clean 200 yd shot from them on her hunt (70%).

## Four week plan

| Week | At the range (live) | At home (dry) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Zero at 100 m (109 yd) from the bench, 10 rounds. Then 5 rounds prone | Learn each position. 10 min, 3 nights |
| 2 | 15 rounds at 100 m: 5 prone, 5 sitting, 5 from sticks. Aim at a 20 cm circle | Sticks every night. Mount from carry |
| 3 | 15 rounds: sitting and sticks at 150 m (164 yd), kneeling at 100 m. One 3 shot group at 200 m (219 yd) if the range allows | 10 min, 3 nights |
| 4 | Max range test, next screen. 9 to 12 rounds cold. Finish with 3 rounds to confirm zero | 2 nights only. Rest before the hunt |

- Total: about 60 rounds, 3 boxes of your hunting load (Tip). Prices are in the ammo session.
- Every live session: the first 3 shots are cold and count. Write the result in the journal (Tip).
- Shoot with the clothes you will hunt in. A thick jacket changes the stock fit (Tip).

## My max ethical range test

> [!lean]
> This is Hunt Mentor's method, an opinion, not a law. It is stricter than most hunters' habits on purpose.

- Target: a **20 cm circle**. A paper plate. Deer vitals are about 20 to 25 cm (8 to 10 in) across, so the plate gives margin (70%).
- **Cold.** First shots of the day. No warm up, no sighters. A deer gives you none (Tip).
- **Field position.** Sitting or sticks, whichever you will really use. Not the bench (Tip).
- **Timed.** From standing with the rifle slung: get down, 3 shots, 90 seconds (Tip).
- Start at 100 m. All 3 inside the circle? Move to 150 m. Repeat. Then 200 m, then 250 m.
- The last distance where all 3 hit is your max ethical range. One miss and your max is the previous distance (Tip).
- Cap it at 250 m (273 yd) for your first season, even if you pass (Tip).
- Wind day? Take 50 m off. At 250 m a 20 km/h crosswind moves a 150 gr .308 bullet about 27 cm (75%). The ballistics session has the table.
- Write the number on the tape inside your scope cap. Test again each season and after any change of scope, rings or load (Tip).

```checklist s5-range-test
20 cm circle targets, 4 of them
Timer on phone, 90 seconds
Sticks or the rest I will carry
12 rounds of my hunting load, cold barrel
Result written in the journal: distance, position, wind
```

> [!rule]
> If you can't hit a paper plate, you can't hit a deer. The plate doesn't walk away.

> [!mistake]
> - Practising only from the bench, then meeting a deer with no bench in sight.
> - Dry firing with a box of ammunition on the same table.
> - Shooting offhand when sitting was one second away.

> [!field]
> Before the shot, say the position out loud: "Sitting. Sticks." Get into it. Then find the animal again in the scope.
