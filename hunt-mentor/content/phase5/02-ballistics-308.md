> [!why]
> Your bullet drops a hand's width by 200 m and a forearm by 300 m. A 20 km/h wind moves it a hand's width at 200 m. Know the numbers or stay inside 150 m.

## Key points

- Two common loads with published tables: Federal Power Shok 150 gr (grain) and Federal Fusion 165 gr (85%).
- With a 100 m (109 yd) [[Zero]], the 150 gr bullet lands about 12 cm low at 200 m and 47 cm low at 300 m (75%).
- With a 200 m (219 yd) zero it is about 6 cm high at 100 m, 11 cm low at 250 m and 29 cm low at 300 m (75%).
- A 20 km/h full crosswind pushes the 150 gr bullet about 17 cm at 200 m and 40 cm at 300 m (75%).
- The heavier 165 gr Fusion drops the same but drifts about 30% less in wind (75%).
- Your 20 inch barrel is roughly 70 to 100 fps (feet per second) slower than the test barrel, about 3% (70%).
- Both loads carry over 1,000 ft lb past 300 m. Energy is not your limit inside 300 m. Placement is (85%).

## The two loads

| Load | Bullet | Muzzle velocity | BC (ballistic coefficient) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Federal Power Shok .308, 150 gr, part 308A | Jacketed soft point | 2,820 fps (860 m/s) | .313 |
| Federal Fusion .308, 165 gr, part F308FS2 | Bonded soft point | 2,700 fps (823 m/s) | .446 |

- Both from Federal's own product pages, read directly 2026-10-01 (85%).
- Federal's tables assume sights 1.5 inches (3.8 cm) above the bore (85%).
- BC: how well the bullet cuts air. Higher is flatter and drifts less. The 165 gr Fusion has the better BC (85%).
- Cross check: Hornady American Whitetail 150 gr, item 8090, 2,820 fps from a 24 inch test barrel, 200 yd zero: 1.9 in high at 100 yd, 8.4 in low at 300 yd. Same shape as the Federal 150 gr (85%).
- Your bullet may differ. These tables tell you the shape of the curve. Your 200 m target tells you the truth (Tip).

## Drop with a 100 m zero

| Distance | 150 gr Power Shok | 165 gr Fusion |
|---|---|---|
| 100 m (109 yd) | 0 | 0 |
| 150 m (164 yd) | 4 cm low | 4 cm low |
| 200 m (219 yd) | 12 cm low | 13 cm low |
| 250 m (273 yd) | 27 cm low | 27 cm low |
| 300 m (328 yd) | 47 cm low | 47 cm low |

- Federal publishes drops at 50, 100, 200, 300, 400 and 500 yd. Hunt Mentor fitted a curve through those points and read it off at metric distances. Worst fit error: 0.5 cm (75%).
- Direct from Federal, 100 yd zero: 150 gr is 3.9 in low at 200 yd and 14.7 in low at 300 yd. 165 gr is 4.1 in and 14.7 in low (85%).
- Hold centre to 150 m. At 200 m hold on the top third of the vitals. Past 250 m the hold is a guess: do not shoot (Tip).

## Drop with a 200 m zero

| Distance | 150 gr Power Shok | 165 gr Fusion |
|---|---|---|
| 100 m (109 yd) | 6 cm high | 7 cm high |
| 150 m (164 yd) | 6 cm high | 6 cm high |
| 200 m (219 yd) | 0 | 0 |
| 250 m (273 yd) | 11 cm low | 11 cm low |

- Direct from Federal, 200 yd zero: 150 gr is 2.0 in high at 100 yd and 8.8 in low at 300 yd. 165 gr is 2.0 in high and 8.6 in low (85%).
- The bullet never leaves a 15 cm (6 in) band around the crosshair out to about 240 m (75%).
- Cost: on a close shot at a small animal you are 6 cm high. Aim low on the chest inside 150 m (Tip).

## Wind drift

Full crosswind, blowing straight across the shot, in cm.

| Distance | 150 gr, 10 km/h | 150 gr, 20 km/h | 165 gr, 10 km/h | 165 gr, 20 km/h |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 m (109 yd) | 2 | 4 | 1.5 | 3 |
| 150 m (164 yd) | 5 | 9 | 3 | 6.5 |
| 200 m (219 yd) | 8 | 17 | 6 | 12 |
| 250 m (273 yd) | 13 | 27 | 10 | 19 |
| 300 m (328 yd) | 20 | 40 | 14 | 28 |

- Federal lists drift for a 10 mph (16 km/h) crosswind: 150 gr 4.4 in at 200 yd, 10.4 in at 300 yd. 165 gr 3.1 in and 7.5 in (85%).
- Drift grows in a straight line with wind speed, so Hunt Mentor scaled to 10 and 20 km/h and read off metric distances (75%).
- 10 km/h: you feel it on your face, leaves rustle. 20 km/h: small branches move, dust lifts (65%).
- Wind at 45 degrees: use about 70% of the table (85%).
- Wind from the front or behind: ignore at these ranges (Tip).
- Drift grows faster than drop. Doubling the distance from 150 m to 300 m makes the drift 4 times bigger (75%).

> [!lean]
> No shot past 200 m in a real wind this season. Inside 150 m, hold centre and forget the wind.

## Your 20 inch barrel

- Rifleshooter.com cut a .308 barrel from 28 to 16.5 inches, one inch at a time, and chronographed four loads at each length (70%).
- 24 inch versus 20 inch: a 150 gr load went 2,766 to 2,679 fps (87 fps slower). A 168 gr load 2,635 to 2,565 fps (70 fps). A 180 gr load 2,553 to 2,478 fps (75 fps) (70%).
- Average loss across the whole test: 21 to 25 fps per inch of barrel, depending on the load (70%).
- The Berger reloading manual says about 20 fps per inch from a 24 inch barrel, as quoted in that test (70%).
- So expect roughly 80 to 100 fps less than the box says. About 3% (70%).
- Effect: about 1 cm more drop at 200 m and about 3 cm more at 300 m, a touch more wind drift. Hunt Mentor estimate: drop grows with the square of the speed loss (65%).
- Only a chronograph or your own 200 m group knows your real number. Shoot the group (Tip).

## MPBR: maximum point blank range

- MPBR (maximum point blank range): the farthest distance where you can hold on the centre of the vitals and still hit inside the vital zone, with no hold over (85%).
- Picture a long pipe as wide as the vital zone. Set the zero so the bullet just misses the top of the pipe. Where it hits the bottom is your MPBR (85%).
- Deer hunters often use a 20 cm (8 in) circle. Cautious hunters use 15 cm (6 in) (75%).
- 150 gr, 15 cm zone: zero at about 210 m, bullet 7 cm high at 100 m, MPBR about 245 m (70%).
- 150 gr, 20 cm zone: zero at about 230 m, 9 cm high at 100 m, MPBR about 270 m (70%).
- The 165 gr numbers are within 5 m of those (70%).
- Real world: rifle error, shooter wobble, altitude and wind all eat into MPBR. Spomer advises using a smaller zone than the real vitals (75%).

> [!lean]
> Zero at 100 m this season, as the sighting in session says. MPBR is a tool for after you pass the 250 m range test. A 9 cm high bullet at 100 m is a bad trade for a beginner in timber.

## Energy and the 1,000 ft lb rule

| Distance | 150 gr Power Shok | 165 gr Fusion |
|---|---|---|
| Muzzle | 2,648 ft lb (3,590 J) | 2,671 ft lb (3,620 J) |
| 100 m (109 yd) | 2,090 ft lb (2,840 J) | 2,260 ft lb (3,060 J) |
| 200 m (219 yd) | 1,630 ft lb (2,210 J) | 1,900 ft lb (2,570 J) |
| 300 m (328 yd) | 1,250 ft lb (1,700 J) | 1,580 ft lb (2,140 J) |

- Muzzle energy direct from Federal. Metric distances read from a curve through Federal's velocities (75%).
- Direct from Federal at 300 yd: 150 gr 1,341 ft lb, 165 gr 1,660 ft lb (85%).
- **Rule of thumb:** "1,000 ft lb (1,356 J) at the animal for deer". Often credited to Townsend Whelen. Writer Richard Mann calls it an urban legend with no clear origin (70%).
- By that rule both loads are fine past 300 m. It is not your limit (85%).
- Federal lists a minimum velocity of 1,900 fps for Fusion to expand. The 165 gr is still above 1,900 fps at 400 yd (366 m) (85%). Power Shok minimum: not listed, VERIFY.
- What matters more: a bullet built for the job, put through both lungs (70%).

> [!rule]
> The bullet knows the wind and the distance. You have to know them first.

> [!mistake]
> - Memorising a drop table from the box, never shooting at 200 m to check it.
> - Forgetting the wind. Drop is easy to see on paper, drift is not.
> - Using a 200 m zero and shooting over a deer's back at 120 m.

> [!field]
> Tape inside the scope cap: "100 m zero. 200 m: 12 low. 250 m: 27 low. Wind 20 km/h at 200 m: 17." Read it before every stalk.
