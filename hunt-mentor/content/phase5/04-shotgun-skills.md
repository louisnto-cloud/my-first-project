> [!why]
> A shotgun is pointed, not aimed. The bird is where it will be, not where it is. Most misses are behind the bird and over the top of it.

## Key points

```photo mallards-flying
```

- Eyes on the bird's head, both eyes open, gun keeps moving (70 to 85%).
- Swing through is the beginner's lead (85%).
- With steel, 35 m (38 yd) is a long shot on ducks (70%).

```more all key points
- Stance: feet shoulder width, knees soft, front foot toward the target. You must swing freely over a wide arc (85%).
- Mount: stock to the cheek first, then back into the shoulder. Head stays up and still (85%).
- Eyes on the bird's head, never on the bead. Both eyes open (70%).
- Three ways to lead: swing through, sustained lead, pull away. Swing through is the beginner's method (85%).
- A duck crossing at 30 m (33 yd) needs roughly 1 to 2 m (4 to 6 ft) of lead (65%).
- With [[Steel shot]], 35 m (38 yd) is a long shot and 40 yd is the edge for ducks (70%).
- Two misses cause most of the rest: stopping the swing and lifting your head (85%).
```

## Stance and mount

```steps Stance and mount in 3 steps
sk-mount-1 | Feet shoulder width, front foot toward the bird.
sk-mount-2 | Eyes on the bird, muzzle pushes toward it.
sk-mount-3 | Stock to your cheek, then your shoulder.
```

- Bring the gun to your face, not your face to the gun (85%).
- Practise the mount at home, gun proven empty (70%).

```more stance, mount and home drills
- Feet about shoulder width apart, knees slightly bent, balanced. Front foot a little forward, toes about 45 degrees toward the target (85%).
- The bent rear leg lets your hips turn. That is the swing (85%).
- Mount: bring the stock to your cheek first, then back to the shoulder. The butt lands on the same spot every time (85%).
- Common error: dropping your head and cheek down to the stock instead of bringing the stock up to your face (85%).
- Your eye is the rear sight. It must sit above the centre of the rib. If it does not, the gun does not fit (85%).
- Practise the mount at home, gun proven empty: push the muzzle toward the target, stock to the face, butt to the shoulder (70%).
- Flashlight drill: tape a small torch in the muzzle, lights down, mount and swing the beam along the join of the wall and ceiling without wobble (70%).
```

## Swing and the three leads

```anim sk-leads Three ways to lead. The gun never stops.
```

- Swing through: start behind, pass the head, fire, keep swinging (85%).
- Long duck crossers: sustained lead or pull away (70%).

```more the three leads in detail
- **Swing through** (beginner's method): start behind the bird, swing faster than it, fire as the muzzle passes its head, keep swinging (85%).
- **Sustained lead:** start ahead of the bird, match its speed, hold the gap, fire, keep swinging. Needs experience to judge the gap (85%).
- **Pull away:** start on the bird, match its speed, then pull ahead and fire. The skeet shooter's favourite (70%).
- Swing through handles flushing grouse and quail well. For longer duck crossings, sustained lead or pull away wins (70%).
- Whatever the method, the gun must still be moving when the shot leaves. Then keep moving (85%).
- Read the line. A crossing duck is often also climbing or dropping. Lead along the real line, not level (70%).
```

## How much lead

```diagram lead
```

- Crossing duck at 30 m: roughly 1 to 2 m of lead (65%).
- Missed? Next time, more lead (70%).

```more the lead math
- Shot takes time to get there. A steel pellet leaves at about 1,400 fps (feet per second) and has lost about half its speed by 40 yd (60%).
- Hunt Mentor estimate: about 0.1 second to reach 30 m (65%).
- A mallard cruises at about 40 to 60 km/h. Cornell says migrating flocks have been estimated at 55 mph, about 90 km/h (70%).
- At 50 km/h a duck moves about 1.4 m in 0.1 second. So the lead at 30 m is roughly 1 to 2 m (4 to 6 ft) depending on speed (65%).
- Published tables that ignore pellet slow down give 0.7 m at 40 km/h and 1.2 m at 64 km/h at 30 yd. Real leads are longer (60%).
- At 40 yd and beyond, hold more lead than feels right. Nearly all misses there are behind (70%).
- Do not compute lead in the blind. Keep the eyes on the head, keep the gun moving, and the lead comes from the swing (70%).
- Miss? Change something on the next shot. Usually that means more lead (70%).
```

## Range with steel

```diagram sk-steel-range
```

- Pattern density runs out before pellet energy (70%).
- No dog? Shorter range. A sailed bird over big water is lost (70%).

```more range with steel in detail
- Steel is lighter than lead. It loses energy faster and patterns thinner at distance (70%).
- Light modified or modified chokes are easy to hit with at 25 yd and still effective to 40 yd (70%).
- Big ducks need about 100 pellets inside a 76 cm (30 in) circle at your longest range. That is the real limit, not pellet energy (70%).
- On paper, steel #2 carries enough energy for a mallard to about 55 yd. Pattern density and your skill run out first (70%).
- Many self taught hunters struggle with 30 yd crossing shots. For them, anything past 30 yd is too far (70%).
- Birds flying straight away: small target, vitals shielded. Inside 30 yd only (70%).
- No dog? Your ethical range is shorter. A sailed bird over big water is a lost bird (70%).
- Upland with steel: grouse and quail flush close. 20 to 30 m with Improved Cylinder and #6 steel is the shot (Tip).
- Set a decoy at 35 m. Birds inside it are shootable. Birds past it are for looking at (Tip).

> [!lean]
> Your line this season: 35 m (38 yd) on ducks, 30 m on upland. A rangefinder on the farthest decoy makes it honest.
```

## Pattern it first

```diagram pattern-target
```

- Pattern at the range you will shoot: 35 m (70%).
- Find the choke and load that print even. Then stop changing (Tip).

```more patterning details
- Your pattern at 35 m decides your range. The [sighting in and patterning session](#/s/sighting-in) shows the 76 cm circle test (85%).
- Pattern at the distance you will shoot, not only 40 yd. A 75% pattern at your real range is the goal (70%).
- Life size duck drawn on the paper at 35 m shows you how small a duck is at that range (70%).
- Match the [[Choke]] and load that print the even pattern. Then stop changing things (Tip).
```

## Common misses

```diagram sk-misses
```

- Stopped swing: behind. Lifted head: high (85%).
- Fix: tip the cap back, lighter loads, eyes on the bird's eye (85%).

```more all common misses
- **Stopping the swing.** Your eye goes to the bead to check the lead, the gun stops, the shot goes behind (85%).
- **Lifting your head.** Like raising a rifle's rear sight: you shoot over the top. Lifting to watch the bird fall guarantees it will not (85%).
- **Swinging too fast.** Mystery misses. The gun pulls your eyes off the bird and then stops (70%).
- **Aiming like a rifle.** One eye shut, bead on the bird. Shotguns are pointed with both eyes open and the eyes on the bird (70%).
- **Shooting the flock.** Pick one bird. A double starts with the first one (70%).
- **Misjudging range.** Underestimating by 5 yd on a long shot risks a cripple. Use the decoys as markers (70%).
- **Flinching.** Heavy loads build it. Shoot the lightest load that patterns, and dry fire the gun (70%).
- Fixes: tip the cap back, drop to lighter loads, shoot doubles to keep your head down, focus on the bird's eye (85%).
```

## Grandpa's rule and mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Checking the bead | Eyes on the head |
| 45 m shot after a 30 m kill | Decoy at 35 m is the line |
| No practice between seasons | Six clay trips |

> [!rule]
> Shoot the head, not the duck. The body follows the head. The pattern follows your eyes.

> [!field]
> Bird coming? Feet first, then eyes on its head, then the mount. The shot is the last thing, not the first.

```more 3 common mistakes
- Checking the bead for lead. The gun stops and the bird flies on.
- Shooting at 45 m because the last bird at 30 m fell.
- Never touching the shotgun between seasons, then blaming the shells.
```
