## Biology sources
- S1 Hinterland Who's Who, Ruffed grouse, https://www.hww.ca/en/wildlife/birds/ruffed-grouse.html (read directly) 85%: male about 500 g; drum made by cupped wings beating air, not the log; drum log often big mossy log at forest edge; males drum again in autumn; clutch 7 to 12; incubation 23 to 24 days; hatch usually early June; hen leaves nest within a day; about half of hens lose all chicks within 1 to 2 weeks; yearling plumage by 16 to 17 weeks; adults moult from June; winter buds and catkins of poplar, birch, alder; chicks insects then flowers, leaves, berries, seeds by August, clover; toe "snowshoes"; burrow in deep snow; crust or cold forces birds into conifer clumps, weight loss; no migration, a few hectares; males hold territories for life; hens range wider; young displaced in autumn, more die in winter; hunting has little effect, most hunted birds are young near roads and clearings; predators fox, goshawk, great horned owl; droppings curved cylinders pencil thick.
- S2 Pennsylvania Game Commission, Ruffed grouse profile, https://www.pa.gov/agencies/pgc/wildlife/discover-pa-wildlife/ruffed-grouse (read directly) 75% (US agency): about 1.5 lb; 15.5 to 19 in long; wingspread 22 to 25 in; hens shorter tails, adult male band uniform; moult July to September; flight 20 to 35 mph; glide usually under 100 yards; adults rarely range more than a few hundred yards a day; walk more than fly; drumming March and April, slow then faster into a whir; 6 to 16 eggs; incubation about 24 days; chicks leave nest within 24 h; low buzzy flights in first week, fly well by 3 weeks; fall shuffle collisions; over three quarters of chicks die within 35 days, most in first week; annual adult survival about 50%; few live to age 2; Jan to Mar monthly survival 80 to 90%; young forest under 20 years; winter roosting under conifers; mammals take chicks, raptors take adults; 98% of hunters never reach the daily limit; adults 34% of harvest in October, 51% November, 57% December; harvest 48% near open roads vs 19% farther; male survival 36% beyond 201 m from roads vs 23% within; 22% of late season birds tested 2015 had West Nile virus antibodies.
- S3 Alaska Department of Fish and Game, A Guide to Aging and Sexing Grouse and Ptarmigan, https://adfg.alaska.gov/static/hunting/smallgamehunting/pdfs/guide_aging_sexing_grouse_ptarmigan.pdf (read directly) 85%: primaries moult P1 outward to P10 midsummer; juveniles moult P1 to P8 only, adults all 10; P8 not grown = juvenile; P9 or P10 not grown = adult; juvenile P9 and P10 frayed or pointed; ptarmigan pigment P9 vs P8; spruce grouse P1 calamus under 2.4 mm = juvenile; ruffed male unbroken band, tail feathers about 6 in; female band washed out in centre, under 6 in; sooty male black tail with grey tip, female black tail with mottled rump; sharp tailed male central feathers long vertical white bars, female horizontal barring; spruce male black tail, female mottled tan; ptarmigan turn white by mid October; male rock ptarmigan black eye stripe.
- S4 Connecticut DEEP, Ruffed Grouse Hunter Log, https://portal.ct.gov/deep/hunting/ruffed-grouse-hunter-log (search preview) 70%: rump feather dots: 1 dot female, 2 dots male; central tail feather over 5 7/8 in male, under 5 1/2 in female; juvenile P9 and P10 pointed and worn.
- S5 BC Identified Wildlife Management Strategy account, Columbian Sharp tailed Grouse, 2004 (retained for historical context 03 2026), https://www.env.gov.bc.ca/wld/frpa/iwms/documents/Birds/b_columbiansharptailedgrouse.pdf (read directly) 85%: 41 to 48 cm, 596 to 1,031 g; pink neck air sacs in display; Columbian range near Vanderhoof south to Merritt; breeding elevation 275 to 1,190 m; spring forbs, grasses, insects; fall greens then berries; winter buds and catkins; key foods snowberry, rose, dandelion, water birch, aspen, saskatoon, chokecherry; clearcut birds kinnikinnick, juniper, prickly rose, scrub birch; lek calls heard up to 1.5 km; first clutch at 11 months; mean clutch 12.8; one brood; leks traditional; BC year round home range 4.9 km2; most nests within 1.6 km of lek; residual grass 25 cm for nests; roadsides and landings with clover, dandelion, yarrow heavily used in fall; snow roosting; Blue List; about 10,000 breeding birds in BC (2001); central Interior 4,000 to 8,200; southern Interior 600 to 1,200; Thompson Okanagan grassland leks: numbers down close to 50% from 1990; 43% of 23 leks still active in 1998.
- S6 Hinterland Who's Who, Ptarmigan factsheet, https://www.hww.ca/assets/pdfs/factsheets/ptarmigan-en.pdf (read directly) 85%: willow 450 to 800 g, white tailed about 350 g, rock in between; feathered feet; white wings all year; three plumages a year; white tailed tail white all year, willow and rock black; strictly alpine white tailed; winter willow, alder, birch buds; white tailed also conifer needles; clutch 5 to 14, about 3 weeks; chicks fly about 1 week; small flocks in autumn; cycles.
- S7 Idaho Fish and Game, Field and forest grouse, https://idfg.idaho.gov/press/field-forest-grouse (read directly) 75%: dusky about 20 in; yellow orange comb, reddish purple air sacs; hoot audible up to a quarter mile; uphill to conifers in winter, fir needles; spruce grouse low hoots then glide ending in two wing claps; "fool hen"; toe bristles; snow roosts 20 to 30 F warmer than open air.
- S8 Wild About Utah, Western forest grouse, https://wildaboututah.org/western-forest-grouse/ (read directly) 60%: dusky hoot at the edge of human hearing, felt before heard; dusky travel 20 mi or more a year.
- S9 Animal Diversity Web (University of Michigan), Dendragapus obscurus and Bonasa umbellus (read directly) 65% (some internal conflicts): dusky 0.91 to 1.36 kg; hooting late March to mid July; about 50% die first year; summer groups 6 to 7, winter groups 15 to 20; up to 30 mi between ranges; ruffed average 644 g, 18 tail feathers, clutch about 11, incubation 23 to 26 days, fruit over a quarter of adult diet.
- S10 Wikipedia, Ruffed grouse (read directly) 60%: 450 to 750 g, 40 to 50 cm, drum heard a quarter mile (400 m) or more; average lifespan about 1 year; BC subspecies.
- S11 Archibald 1974, Auk 91: 517 to 521 and related Auk paper (search preview) 70%: drum heard roughly quarter to three quarter mile, varies with direction faced.
- S12 Small and Rusch 1989, The natal dispersal of ruffed grouse, Auk 106: 72 to 79, https://sora.unm.edu/sites/default/files/journals/auk/v106n01/p0072-p0079.pdf (abstract via search) 80%: juvenile females 4.82 km net, males 2.14 km; 17 vs 21 days.
- S13 Guglielmo and Karasov 1995, Journal of Wildlife Management (abstract via search) 80%: grouse preferred aspen flower buds, avoided birch and alder catkins.
- S14 Northern Woodlands, Why ruffed grouse take winter in stride, https://northernwoodlands.org/outside_story/article/why-ruffed-grouse-take-winter-in-stride (search preview) 60%: crop filled with a day of buds in about 20 minutes; gizzard grit.
- S15 Pendergast and Boag 1973 (Condor), via search summaries 75%: needles 79% of annual diet, 99% when snow covered (Alberta spruce grouse); winter gut length about 40% longer; gizzard +75% (single secondary, 60%).
- S16 Wilson Bulletin 99(3): 488 to 490 (search preview) 75%: under 20 cm of soft snow, roost temperature -3 to -12 C with air as low as -35 C. Heinrich (Northeastern Naturalist) 70%: grouse plunge into snow once about 40 cm deep. Shipley et al 2019 70%: stress hormone falls when snow deeper than 20 cm.
- S17 Captive ruffed grouse cloacal temperature, Condor 80: 243 to 245, https://sora.unm.edu/sites/default/files/journals/condor/v080n02/p0243-p0245.pdf (search preview) 75%: mean 41.5 C, range 40.4 to 42.6 C.
- S18 Bird respiration: PMC review 2024 (Unidirectional airflow, air sacs or the horizontal septum), https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11864838/ and Life textbook excerpt (search preview) 80%: rigid lungs, one way flow, air sacs as bellows, no gas exchange in sacs; most birds 9 air sacs (70%).
- S19 Avian heart scaling review, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7151090 (search preview) 80%: four chambers, full separation, aorta curves right, larger relative heart linked to flight.
- S20 White breast muscle: fast glycolytic fibres, quick fatigue in galliform birds (turkey and chicken sources via search) 70%.
- S21 Chicken vision: Kram, Mantey and Corbo (PLOS One) and Martin (visual fields review) via search 75% for galliforms: four single cones (violet, blue, green, red) plus a double cone linked to motion; visual field about 300 degrees with about 30 degrees binocular.
- S22 Bird hearing reviews (search preview) 70%: most sensitive 1 to 4 kHz; galliform papilla tuned below 1 kHz; ruffed drum under 100 Hz.
- S23 Ruffed grouse survival: Maine (UMaine thesis, Davis) adults 0.32, juveniles 0.16, lowest Jan to Mar; Maryland 53%; Appalachian monograph harvest 12% of known deaths, compensatory (search previews) 75%. Ten year cycles in parts of the range (seafwa Jones) 75%.
- S24 Spruce grouse: Idaho (S7); clutch 5.6 New Brunswick vs 4.8 Alberta (Franklin's); incubation about 21 to 24 days; median hatch 29 June Alberta; young fly short distances by 6 days (Condor 77: 91 to 96 and reviews, search preview) 75%; displays strut, tail fan, flutter flight, wing clap (Flathead Audubon, Birdweb via search) 65%; Herzog and Keppie 1980 moves up to 9.5 km (earlier log) 70%.
- S25 Dusky and sooty split: AOU 47th supplement 2006 (search preview, Sibley, Wikipedia Dendragapus) 85%; sooty hoots high in conifers, about six hoots, carries half a mile (Birdweb via search) 65%; sooty winters higher in dense conifers (earlier log, 65%).
- S26 Colorado Parks and Wildlife, dusky grouse (earlier log, 70%): leave high country late March or early April; males leave breeding areas by mid July for high forest with huckleberry.
- S27 Audubon, dusky grouse (earlier log 70%): autumn move uphill to dense conifers, maximum known travel about 30 miles.
- S28 BC Breeding Bird Atlas (earlier direct reads 2026-10-01 to 2026-10-03, 85%): ruffed most abundant 750 to 1,250 m; spruce to 2,500 m, conifer specialist; dusky many 1,500 to 2,250 m, breeding 500 to 1,250 m not uncommon; white tailed ptarmigan breeds 1,830 to 2,650 m in the Interior; willow ptarmigan mostly northern half of BC.
- S29 Clemson Cooperative Extension, Safe handling of wild game birds, https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/safe-handling-of-wild-game-birds/ (read directly) 80%: remove entrails and crop soon; cool fast, above 4 C (40 F) is the main threat; wipe cavity with a clean cloth, not grass or snow; air circulation; do not pile warm birds; cooler on ice; fridge use within 3 days; freeze at -18 C (0 F), use within 6 months; cook poultry to 74 C (165 F) checked with a thermometer.

- S31 Macaulay Library recording 2381 notes (search preview) 70%: ruffed hen spread tail and ruff, hissed and "meowed" near caught chicks; distraction display (farm column) 60%.
