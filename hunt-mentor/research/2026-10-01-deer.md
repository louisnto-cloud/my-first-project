# Research notes: deer, southern BC Interior (2026-10-01)

Method: search previews only (gov.bc.ca and most journals blocked by network policy). Capped at 85 to 90%.

## ID
- Mule deer: forked antlers, big ears, rope tail with black tip, big white rump, metatarsal gland 10 to 15 cm. Stots (all 4 feet). AIWC https://www.aiwc.ca/blog/spotting-the-difference-a-guide-to-albertas-mule-deer-and-whitetails/ ; UNL https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1015&context=nebgamepubs (85%)
- White tail: one main beam with single tines, wide tail flagged white, small metatarsal gland. Gallops to cover. (85%)
- Mule deer stopping to look back at 150 to 300 m: hunter lore (65%).

## Where near Heffley Creek
- Mule deer dominant in southern Interior. BC muledeer.pdf https://www.env.gov.bc.ca/wld/documents/muledeer.pdf (85%)
- Mule deer: steeper, higher, open broken ground. White tails: valley bottoms, riparian, farm fields. Staudenmaier 2021 https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ecs2.3813 (85%)
- Local reading (75%): mule deer on grassland and open Douglas fir benches up toward Heffley Lake, Paul Lake, Sun Peaks. White tails in North Thompson hayfields and cottonwood bottoms.

## Migration
- SIMDeer (BCWF, Cache Creek closest study area): 74% migrate, avg 47 to 55 km one way, max ~100 km. Back to winter range Oct to Nov; one year nearly all back by mid Oct. Same route each year. https://bcwf.bc.ca/initiatives/mule-deer-project/ (85%)
- Trigger: snow and cold, not the rut. Monteith 2011 https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1890/ES10-00096.1 (85%)
- BC account: interior deer often stay high until December (80%). Conflicts with SIMDeer. Present as a range.
- Snow: deer avoid > ~50 cm. UBC thesis (80%)
- Winter range: south and west aspects, old Douglas fir. Armleder 1994 (85%)
- Elevations (VERIFY, 55%): valley ~370 to 450 m, Heffley Lake ~950 m, Sun Peaks village ~1,250 m, Tod Mountain ~2,150 m.
- Map: BC dataset "Ungulate Winter Range, Approved" WHSE_WILDLIFE_MANAGEMENT.WCP_UNGULATE_WINTER_RANGE_SP https://catalogue.data.gov.bc.ca/dataset/ungulate-winter-range-approved ; view in iMapBC https://maps.gov.bc.ca/ess/hm/imap4m/ (85%). Kamloops TSA may have no approved mule deer UWR polygons (55%, VERIFY).
- Kamloops winter range study area "Dewdrop" below 760 m north of Kamloops + Jamieson Creek. Willms 1976 https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/pdf/10.4141/cjas76-065 (80%)

## Rut
- Mule deer: mid Nov to mid Dec, peak mid Nov. BC MoTI guidance (80%)
- White tail: peak Nov; conception ~Nov 13 to 17 (Penn State). (80%)
- Late October = early pre rut. Bucks sparring, bachelor groups break up. (70%)

## Daily pattern
- Peaks ~04:00 to 09:00 and 17:00 to 21:00 (Starkey, Oregon). Stewart 2002 https://academic.oup.com/jmammal/article/84/3/1076/905056 (85%)
- Day beds: hiding and thermal cover, north timber, benches, draws; cold weather sunny south and west, out of wind. Germaine 2004 (80%)
- Bucks bed high, top third of slope: lore (60%)
- Thermals: up in morning warming, down in evening cooling (80% physics).

## Diet near Kamloops
- Willms 1976, 67 rumens Sept to Apr near Kamloops: grass < 10% until spring; more forbs in fall; shrubs early fall and late winter; Douglas fir peaks Dec to Jan. (85%)
- Browse: Douglas fir, saskatoon, Oregon grape, red osier dogwood, willow, rose, bitterbrush, rabbitbrush, sagebrush, snowbrush. BC RN113 (80%)
- White tails use alfalfa and hay regrowth. (80%)

## Senses
- Vision: dichromatic, cones ~455 and 537 nm, no red cone. Jacobs 1994 (85%). More UV sensitivity (75%). Blaze orange not vivid to deer (75%). Motion and outline matter most.
- UV brighteners in detergent: tip (65%).
- Hearing: ~115 Hz to 54 kHz, best ~4 to 8 kHz. D'Angelo 2007 https://wildlife.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.2193/2006-326 (85%). High pitch: zippers, Velcro, nylon, metal clicks.
- Smell: strongest sense. "297 million receptors" is folklore (45%).
- Rattling: ~65% response in Texas telemetry study, best morning, cool, calm, overcast (70%). Mule deer less dependable (50%).
- Natural deer urine lures: BC restricts cervid derived attractants due to CWD. VERIFY current rule (65%). CWD confirmed in BC Kootenay 2024.

## Social
- Doe groups matrilineal. Lingle 2003 (85%). Bachelor groups of 2 to 4 break up in Oct (80%). Mule deer groups mostly 5 or fewer; winter 5 to 8, up to 15 to 30 (75%). Strong home range fidelity year to year (85%).

## Sign
- Tracks: heart shape, mule deer front 5.7 to 10 cm long; overlap too much to ID species by one track (60%). Stotting = groups of 4 prints 2.4 to 4.6 m apart (60%).
- Pellets 1 to 1.5 cm; firm in fall/winter; ~13 to 34 groups/day (70%).
- Browse: deer tear (no upper incisors); hares cut clean 45 degrees (75%).
- Rubs: white tails many, mule deer fewer, small conifers and shrubs (60%). Scrapes: white tails late Oct to Nov; mule deer rarely (60 to 70%).

## Tactics and odds
- Mule deer: spot and stalk, glass from first light, tripod, play wind and thermals (60%, hunter consensus).
- White tail: ambush between bedding and fields; rattle and grunt in early Nov (65%).
- Region 3 hunter survey 2017: 41.9% of committed mule deer hunters got a buck in 2015 (70%, self reported). http://lwdrodgun.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/Region-3-Mule-Deer-Hunter-Survey-Final-Report-December-2017.pdf
- Region 3 bag: 3 deer, mule deer 1, white tail 2; antlerless mule deer LEH only (75%, VERIFY). 4 point rule in many general mule deer seasons (VERIFY).
- White tail antlerless Oct 10 to 31 in MUs 3-12 to 3-20 etc. (70%, VERIFY)

## Predators
- Cougar = top cause of mule deer death in SIMDeer; coyote second (85%). Wolves recovered in Thompson (85%).
- Deer pick sightlines vs cougar, rugged slopes vs wolves (80%).
