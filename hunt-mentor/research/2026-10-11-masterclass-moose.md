## Primary sources read directly (full text)

1. BC Ministry of Environment, Lands and Parks (2000), *Moose in British Columbia: Ecology, Conservation and Management* (text D. A. Blood). https://www.env.gov.bc.ca/wld/documents/moose.pdf . Read directly, including the distribution map (page 3 rendered). Use 90%.
   - Largest deer family member; bulls nearly 2 m at shoulder, most massive antlers. Autumn cows 340 to 420 kg, bulls 450 to 500 kg, max recorded 595 kg.
   - Long legs for fallen timber, muskeg, deep snow. Winter coat of guard hairs and wool. No rump patch; bell; overhanging upper lip; dark brown to blackish.
   - Three subspecies: andersoni (Northwestern), gigas (Alaskan), shirasi (Shiras). Southeast BC smallest bodies and antlers; biggest in the north. Map: Shiras east of a dashed line in the far southeast; Alaskan in the far northwest; Kamloops and Okanagan in Northwestern range (my reading, 80%).
   - Antlers begin late April or early May; full size by early September; velvet dries and is rubbed off. Yearlings 2 or 3 points a side, no significant palm. Antlers not a reliable age guide; grow to about age 10; split palm or butterfly, spread to 1.8 m; brow palm protects face in fights. Shed mid November to March; older first; by March only yearlings may have antlers.
   - Distribution: most abundant in central and sub boreal interior, northern boreal mountains, northeast boreal plains; common in mountain valleys except dry southern valleys like the Thompson and Okanagan; usually absent from coast. About 170,000 moose; over 70% in the north. Winter density 0.3 to 1.5 per km².
   - Solitary; seasonal home range seldom over 5 to 10 km²; no defended range, no year round hierarchy; rut groups of 8 to 10 in northern shrublands; groups at licks lack social basis. Only lasting bond mother and calf, about 1 year.
   - Mate September to November; over 80% of calves conceived in first estrus, two weeks late September to early October; second or third estrus about 25 days apart. Cow moan audible up to 3 km. Cows gather around prime bull and wallow in urine soaked rutting pits; no harem; equal bulls lock antlers; cows aggressive to each other and choose mates.
   - Gestation 8 months; birth late May and June; pregnant cows chase off last year's young; single calves the rule, 10 to 20% twins; newborn 11 to 16 kg, stand day one; cows give birth lying down; at least 150 L of milk June to September; plants from 2 weeks; weaned by autumn; 135 to 180 kg by early winter.
   - Cows usually first conceive at 2; yearling bulls can breed but older bulls do most breeding.
   - Death: starvation, predators; wolves in severe winters; black bears take newborn calves; grizzlies spring to fall; cougars occasionally in the southern interior. Parasites: winter ticks, liver flukes, tapeworm cysts; most BC moose do not die from parasites.
   - Snow: little or no difficulty to 40 cm; slight 40 to 70 cm; commonly winter in 50 cm plus. Mountains: valley winter ranges, higher summer ranges, some stay low; stay high longer than elk, deer, sheep; return gradually. Plateaus: summer near lake shores, swamps, beaver ponds; winter in river valleys, burns, logged areas, wetland complexes.
   - Food: up to 20 kg a day in winter; depend primarily on willows; red osier dogwood, cottonwood, paper birch, aspen, high bush cranberry, false box, subalpine fir; strip bark from willows and poplars; summer horsetail, burweed, submerged pondweeds, new leaves. Reach 2.5 m; break branches down with jaws.
   - Harvest 8,000 to 14,000 a year; more meat than all other ungulates combined. Hundreds die on highways and rail lines, Yellowhead corridor. Logging helps browse but too little cover hurts: cover to hide from predators and hunters, shade, shelter in blizzards.
2. BC Ministry of Forests (2023), *Factors affecting moose population declines in British Columbia: May 2022 progress report* (Anderson and others). https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/environment/plants-animals-and-ecosystems/wildlife-wildlife-habitat/moose/2022_moose_research_progress_report.pdf . Read directly. 90%.
   - Five study areas including Bonaparte (MUs 3-29, 3-30). Landscape change hypothesis: MPB salvage logging and roads raised vulnerability to hunters and predators.
   - 548 cows, 180 calves collared. Cow deaths: 58% predation (mostly wolves; bear 7%, cougar 3%), 17% health, 11% hunting. Calf deaths: 65% wolf, 9% bear, 7% cougar; 17% health.
   - Average age at death 10.4 years (1 to 18). Malnutrition deaths mostly April to June. Body fat entering winter about 8 to 10%.
   - Cow survival generally above 85% (Bonaparte 83% in 2021/22). Late winter calf survival 45 to 85%.
   - 8 month calf weight 172 kg (Bonaparte and Prince George South). Tick transects: Bonaparte cows 4.3 ticks (2021/22), calves 3.4.
   - Bonaparte density 0.30 per km² (2012), 0.25 (2017). Wildfires in Bonaparte 2017 and 2021 (Sparks Lake).
3. BC (2022), *Provincial ungulate population estimates*. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/environment/plants-animals-and-ecosystems/wildlife-wildlife-habitat/ungulates/2022_provincial_ungulate_population_estimates.pdf . Read directly. 90%.
   - Moose: Region 1 10 to 20; 2 70 to 100; 3 7,000 to 9,000 stable; 4 4,000 to 6,000; 5 15,000 to 24,000; 6 25,500 to 48,000; 7 Omineca 23,500 to 39,000; 7 Peace 37,500 to 60,500; 8 4,000 to 5,000 stable to increasing. Province 115,000 to 192,000. Aerial surveys by MU, each key MU every 5 years.
4. BC Ministry of Forests, Lands, Natural Resource Operations and Rural Development (K. Watt), *Provincial Moose Winter Tick Surveillance Program 2021*. https://www.bcsf.org/s/Latest_moose_winter_tick_program_report.pdf (redirects to a Squarespace PDF). Read directly. 85%.
   - Dermacentor albipictus, single host. Larvae attach mid September to mid October; blood meal October; dormant to January; second meal; females engorge March to April then drop. Tens to 10,000s, extreme over 100,000.
   - Effects: anaemia, lower growth and fat, grooming damages coat, heat loss, less feeding; can be fatal with hard winters. Elk, deer, caribou, bison less affected.
   - Hair loss on neck, shoulders, upper mane, withers, hind quarters; five classes up to over 80% ("ghost").
   - 2021: 323 observations, 19% with hair loss (31% in 2020). BC moose high 190,000 (2011), low 148,000 (2017), citing Kuzyk.
5. BC Government news release 2018FLNR0002-000024 (9 January 2018). https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2018FLNR0002-000024 . Fetched. 85%. Tens of thousands of ticks; seen January to April; anaemia; "no health risk to humans"; BC 120,000 to 200,000 moose; Provincial Moose Research Program since 2013.
6. Alaska Department of Fish and Game, Moose notebook (revised 2025), https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/static/education/wns/moose.pdf (read directly), and species profile https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/index.cfm?adfg=moose.printerfriendly (fetched). 85%.
   - Alaska bulls 542 to 725 kg, cows 364 to 591 kg. Calves 13 to 16 kg, 10 times by 5 months. Gestation about 230 days; twinning under 10% to 75% by habitat. Rut peak late September early October. By late October bulls have exhausted fat and begin feeding. Mature bulls shed from November, mostly December and January; young bulls to April. Browse line 6 to 8 ft. Rarely over 16 years. Antlers fastest growing tissue; largest at 10 to 12. Seasonal moves few miles to 60 miles. Swim across Clarence Strait (6 miles). In Europe called elk.
7. Alaska Department of Fish and Game, Aggressive moose. https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/index.cfm?adfg=livewith.aggressivemoose . Fetched. 85%. Cows with calves; bulls in the rut; hungry tired in deep snow; dogs as enemies; warning signs: hump hairs raised, ears laid back, lip licking; many bluff charges; run, they won't chase far; run around a tree; knocked down: curl up, protect head, hold still; more people injured by moose than bears in Alaska.
8. Minnesota Department of Natural Resources, Moose. https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/mammals/moose.html . Fetched. 75%. Poor eyesight, acute smell and hearing; 35 mph; swim 10 miles; rut mid September to mid October; calves 25 to 35 lb; stay with mother 12 to 18 months; seldom aggressive except cow with calf or rutting bull.
9. The Canadian Encyclopedia, Moose. https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/moose . Fetched. 75%. Hump bones with muscle and tendons support head and antlers; bulls' dewlap bigger; four Canadian subspecies incl. americana; antlers 80% complete by mid July, velvet off early September; calves 12 to 16 kg; gestation about 230 days.
10. Ken Bailey, Outdoor Canada, "Make like a cow moose", 5 September 2012. https://www.outdoorcanada.ca/?p=3913 . Fetched. 65%, tactics as Tip. Cow bawl nearly 10 seconds plus 2 or 3 grunts; start soft; rake with stick or shoulder blade; grunts for hung up bulls; call every 20 to 30 min, sequence up to a minute; some move after 3 hours; cool clear calm days; lake points, beaver dams, cutblocks, burns, ridges; rut mid September to late October by location; works other times if other tactics fail; bulls come silent or noisy.
11. BC Ministry of Transportation news release 2020TRAN0163-001934. https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2020TRAN0163-001934 . Fetched. 85%. Dusk, dawn, night; tree lines, water; signs; clean lights; high beams; horn.

## Secondary sources (search previews or abstracts)
- Child and others 2010, *Alces* 46:113 to 121, central BC, 1,886 bulls: spike fork put 15.9% of bulls and about 46% of yearlings at risk; tripalm 11.1%, 10 point 12.0%. https://alcesjournal.org/index.php/alces/article/view/62 (80%, abstract via search).
- Aitken and others 2021, *Alces* 57: about 43% of yearlings and 10% of 2 year olds vulnerable to spike fork rule (75%, search preview).
- Aitken and others 2012, *Alces*: Omineca carcass weights 1995 to 2007 from meat cutter records; carcass = four quarters without head, hide, lower legs, organs; calves 82 and 81 kg; spike fork 162 kg; adult cows 199 kg; adult bulls 249 kg; bulls heavier pre rut. (75%)
- Renecker and Hudson 1986 (2 captive moose, Alberta): summer 14°C faster breathing, 20°C panting; winter about minus 5°C. McCann and others 2013: 17°C no wind, 24°C wind. Critics: no static threshold. (70%)
- Renecker and Hudson 1989: feeding about 10 h a day, 5 bouts; rumination 12.6 h midwinter to 2.8 h May. Kenai collars: rest 105 min summer, 171 min winter. Minnesota thesis: crepuscular peaks about 2 h after sunrise and sunset; 20 minute bouts. (70 to 75%)
- *Alces* digestive system review: seasonally adaptable concentrate selectors, small omasum, wide ostia, fast passage; twig diameter reduces value. (70%)
- Wildlife Biology 2016: moose bed sites as thermal refuges (65%, title and abstract).
- Wildlife collision advice (moose eyes above headlights; look for silhouette), via search of news and program pages (70%).
- Moose calling forum and guide pages (Outdoor Life, Guidefitter, Rokslide): bulls circle downwind; over calling lets a close bull pinpoint you (60%, opinion).
- State hunter education pages (Nebraska, Colorado, Pennsylvania) on little reaction to a hit, waiting and two person tracking (70%).
