## Primary sources read directly

1. BC Ministry of Environment, Lands and Parks (2001), *Black Bears in British Columbia: Ecology, Conservation and Management* (text D. A. Blood after M. Paquet). https://www.env.gov.bc.ca/wld/documents/blackbear.pdf . Read directly (PDF text). Use 90%.
   - Black bears in all of BC except most urban cores; most commonly met large carnivore.
   - Colour phases: black, cinnamon, brown, blonde; white Kermode (north central coast; about 10% on Princess Royal and Gribbel Islands); blue glacier bear (far northwest).
   - Six races: cinnamomum (east of Coast Range, larger, brown phase common); altifrontalis (Oregon to Bella Coola, east to Manning Park and Lillooet, mostly black); vancouveri; carlottae (Haida Gwaii, black only, massive skull); kermodei (Burke Channel to Nass); emmonsii (Tatshenshini). BC has more races than any other part of Canada.
   - Chunky body, broad head, rounded ears, short tail; uniformly black typical with tan muzzle, sometimes white chest V; plantigrade, five toes, short curved claws.
   - Adult males 60 to 90 cm shoulder, 130 to 190 cm long, 80 to 300 kg; females 40 to 140 kg.
   - Grizzlies: prominent shoulder hump, dish shaped face, much longer digging claws; black bears straight profile, shorter curved climbing claws.
   - BC population estimate 120,000 to 160,000, about a quarter of Canada's; higher in wet zones and on the coast (salmon); not rare, threatened or endangered.
   - Females mature about 4, breed every 2 to 3 years (6 or 7 where food is poor); males mostly mature 5 or 6. Captive 25 to 30 years, wild much shorter.
   - Mate early June to mid July; delayed implantation October or November; cubs born January or February, usually 2 (1 to 5), hairless, blind, about 400 g; 3 to 5 kg at spring den exit; weaned July to September; den with mother first winter; on own by middle or end of second spring; daughters may overlap ranges, sons disperse.
   - Hibernation 3 to 5 months south coast, probably 5 to 7 interior and north; pregnant females longest. Coast dens in or under big trees, snags, logs, stumps, up to 25 m up. Interior: tree cavities, rock cavities, brush piles, dug holes.
   - Den: heart about 50 to about 10 per minute; oxygen intake halves; body temperature down about 3°C; no eating, drinking, urinating, defecating; up to a quarter of body weight lost.
   - About six months to fatten; prefer abundant, high protein and energy foods with little effort.
   - Home ranges: males 25 to 150 km2, females 5 to 25 km2; feeding areas linked by travel routes in wooded cover; females with cubs avoid open areas.
   - Mostly solitary; social groups only sow with cubs and the brief mating pair.
   - Omnivore; seek low fibre, digestible foods; vegetable matter the bulk. Spring: meadows, estuaries, riparian, skunk cabbage swamps, avalanche chutes, grassy south facing slopes, burns; newborn deer, elk, moose, caribou. Summer: insects and larvae, fruits, berries, salmon, carrion. Autumn: berries and spawning fish. Small mammals; rarely adult moose and elk. Cambium feeding.
   - Sign: claw and bite marks, rubbing trees, trails where bears step in the same prints year after year, scats, ripped logs.
   - Up to 1,000 conflict bears killed a year by conservation officers; garbage the main cause.
   - Clearcuts give short term berries; dense second growth little food; coastal dens in old growth.
   - Hunting rules mentioned are 2001 era; not used as rules (Rule Book only).
2. Alaska Department of Fish and Game (2022), *Bear Hides: Skinning and Field Care*. https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/static/hunting/pdfs/bear_hides_skining_fieldcare_2022.pdf . Read directly (PDF text). 85%.
   - Skin immediately to dissipate heat; leave head and paws in if unsure and get it to a taxidermist; cool, clean, dry; tarp under the bear; never store a hide in plastic, use game bags or burlap.
   - Cut lines; skin toes to last joint, claws stay on; ears cut close to skull; eyes; lips; nose cartilage to about a 1 inch stump; turn ears, split lips and eyelids; salt face and paws first.
   - Wet hide: hang nose up, tail down. Fleshing: all fat and flesh off. Average black bear hide 10 to 15 lb non iodized salt; brown bear 30 to 40 lb; hides can slip within one day; do not freeze a salted hide; warm days, cool nights: spread at night, roll skin to skin by day; freeze a hide spread flat first.

## Secondary sources (fetched or search preview)

3. Alaska Department of Fish and Game, Black bear species profile. https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/index.cfm?adfg=blackbear.main . Fetched. 85%. About 20% heavier in fall; claws rarely exceed 1.5 inches; straight facial profile; mating June to July; gestation about 7 months; newborns under 1 lb; litters 1 to 4, twins common; maturity 3 to 6 years; females with cubs emerge later; dens sea level to alpine; spring low south slopes, summer mid elevation and high openings, fall salmon rivers and alpine berries; sight and hearing adequate, smell outstanding.
4. WildSafeBC, Black bear. https://wildsafebc.com/species/black-bear/ . Fetched. 75%. 120,000 to 150,000; 14,000 to 25,000 COS calls a year; active April to November; vegetation about 80% of diet; up to 20,000 calories a day in fall; dens December to April or November to April; about 30% weight lost; heart about 10 per minute; temperature down about 3 degrees; smell food over 1 km; eyes and hearing as good or better than ours; fast up or downhill, excellent climbers; mating late June, delayed implantation if fat enough; up to 5 cubs, twins typical; born late January 250 to 500 g; out late April 2 to 5 kg; care about 2 years; cub mortality about 50%; over 30 years possible, 15 to 20 typical; mostly solitary; mainly diurnal, night feeding near people; fatal attacks under one every 3 years in BC; dogs in over 50% of injuries (Hristienko and Herrero 2014); agitated signs jaw popping, moaning, woofing, stomping, bluff charge; spray at 5 to 10 m; defensive and predatory responses; bear bells not recommended; some males do not den if food is available.
5. ScienceDaily (17 Feb 2011) on Tøien and others, Science 331:906. https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2011/02/110217141311.htm . Fetched. 80%. Metabolism down about 75% (to about 25%); body temperature 30 to 36°C in 2 to 7 day cycles, only 5 to 6 degrees lower; heart about 55 to about 14 per minute; spring metabolism about half of summer, 2 to 3 weeks to recover.
6. North American Bear Center, Senses and abilities. https://bear.org/?p=53 . Fetched. 70%. Colour vision, sharp close up, distance over 200 yd untested; hearing over twice human sensitivity and higher range, first line of defence, all directions; smell extremely good, nasal mucosa about 100 times human; lean bears over 30 mph; swim at least 1.5 miles; large brain, excellent long term memory.
7. Smith, Herrero, DeBruyn and Wilder (2008), Efficacy of Bear Deterrent Spray in Alaska, J. Wildl. Manage. 72(3):640 to 645. https://bioone.org/journals/journal-of-wildlife-management/volume-72/issue-3/2006-452/Efficacy-of-Bear-Deterrent-Spray-in-Alaska/10.2193/2006-452.full . Search preview of abstract. 80%. 83 incidents 1985 to 2006 (61 brown, 20 black, 2 polar); stopped undesirable behaviour 92% brown, 90% black, 100% polar; 98% of spray carriers uninjured; wind interfered 5 of 71; side effects 10 of 71.

11. Skull scoring minimums and drying: Idaho Fish and Game records page, Pope and Young entry requirements, Pennsylvania Game News (search preview). 70 to 75%. B&C black bear minimum 20 awards, 21 all time; length plus width of cleaned skull, lower jaw off, inches and sixteenths; 60 day drying, starting when removed from freezer or boiling.
12. Maryland DNR, Bear hunt: care of your bear. https://dnr.maryland.gov/huntersguide/Pages/BearHunt_Care.aspx . Fetched. 75%. Skin promptly, fat and hide insulate; quarter if delayed; ice in all weather, essential above freezing; cloth not plastic; 15 to 20 lb salt, 3/8 inch deep; never store or transport hide in plastic; trim fat; Trichinella and Toxoplasma; cook to 160°F and hold at least 3 minutes; freezing does not always kill parasites.
13. CDC MMWR (2004), Trichinellosis associated with bear meat. https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm5327a2.htm . Search preview. 85%. T. nativa freeze resistant for months or years; cook wild game to 160°F (71°C); colour unreliable on dark meat.
