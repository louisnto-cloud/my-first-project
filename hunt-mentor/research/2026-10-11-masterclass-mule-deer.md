## Primary sources read directly (full text)

1. BC Ministry of Environment, Lands and Parks (2000), *Mule and Black-tailed Deer in British Columbia: Ecology, Conservation and Management* (text D. A. Blood). https://www.env.gov.bc.ca/wld/documents/muledeer.pdf . Read directly (PDF text). Use 90% for biology lines.
   - Three subspecies in BC: O. h. hemionus (interior), columbianus (Columbian black tailed, coast), sitkensis (Sitka, north coast; introduced to Queen Charlotte Islands, now Haida Gwaii, early 1900s).
   - Mule deer 90 to 95 cm at shoulder. Bucks 68 to 113 kg, up to 180 kg in peak condition. Does 50 to 75 kg. Black tail bucks 48 to 90 kg, does 40 to 65 kg.
   - Ears about two thirds the length of the head. Coat tawny brown in summer, dark or grizzled brown in winter. Dark forehead, whitish face, black muzzle, white throat patch.
   - Antlers: two main beams each forking again (dichotomous). Most yearling mule deer have 2 points a side; 2 year olds large forks or 3 points; older bucks 4, 5 or more plus a small eye guard. Point count not a reliable age guide.
   - Interior about 165,000 mule deer; 20,000 to 25,000 on northern ranges. Black tails 150,000 to 250,000.
   - Coast Range separates the two, especially in winter; they interbreed where ranges meet, hybrids in some intermediate valleys.
   - Social: clans of related females; bucks unrelated. Dominance displays: circling with deliberate steps, back arched, head low, tail flicking; thrashing bushes. Sparring of unequal bucks is social bonding, not fights. Serious fights between equal mature bucks in the rut, November and early December.
   - Glands: metatarsal (alarm scent), tarsal (mutual recognition), interdigital (scent trail).
   - Bucks can breed as yearlings; older dominant bucks do most mating. Most does breed as yearlings, first fawn near second birthday. Does cluster around the largest bucks. Tending bond.
   - After rut bucks have lost weight, hide and rest. Antlers drop January to March, older bucks first. Regrow April through August.
   - Gestation 6 to 7 months; fawns born late May through June. Twins the rule; young does often one; triplets occasionally. Birth weight 2.7 to 4 kg. Over 90% of does give birth each year. 45 to 70% of fawns normally die. Few live more than 8 to 10 years. Predation, starvation and hunting main causes of death.
   - Difficulty in snow deeper than 30 cm. Most migrate up in summer, some stay low all year. Interior winter range: shrublands in the dry forest zone and steep south and west facing broken terrain. "They often remain at high elevations until December."
   - Winter foods: big sagebrush, pasture sage, bitterbrush, rabbitbrush, snowbrush, saskatoon, rose, Douglas fir foliage, grasses and herbs. Spring and summer: grasses, balsamroot, clover, wild strawberry, fireweed, shrub leaves.
   - April: deer on low south facing grassy slopes; Dewdrop Range near Kamloops named.
   - Late 1960s harvest about 75,000 deer; by 2000 about 20,000 a year. Several thousand deer die on BC roads each year. Cougar depends on deer.
2. BC Ministry, Species Habitat Model for Mule Deer, Central Okanagan TEM project (10 June 2003). https://a100.gov.bc.ca/pub/acat/documents/r1757/whr_4002_modhe_1224094596301_8e248a8d30d8a9320898638a4b82840986e6405f8898.pdf . Read directly. Use 85%.
   - Table 1 highest abundance (over 1 deer per 0.2 km2) includes North Thompson River, City of Kamloops, Copper Creek (Thompson Basin), Kelowna, Okanagan River.
   - Mule deer are an edge (ecotonal) species: high contrast forest edges with forage near escape and thermal cover.
   - Gestation about 203 days (Robinette 1977). Fawning late May or early June; birthing on gentle terraces and benches between winter and summer ranges.
   - Diet: browse is the bulk of the annual diet; winter diet 79% browse, 15% graminoids, 6% forbs (Cowan 1947). Central interior winter: 45 to 76% conifers, Douglas fir 24 to 73%.
   - Security cover: hides 90% of a deer at 60 m or less; patches 180 m or more across (Thomas 1979).
   - Winter range stand: long crowns intercept snow; canopy about 65 to 70% closure with small openings; layers with Douglas fir.
   - Snow: over 50 cm essentially precludes use; 25 to 30 cm hinders. Energy +50% walking in 25 cm, +150% in 41 cm (Parker 1984).
   - Thermal habitat: warm aspects, gentle to moderate slopes, low elevations, Douglas fir dominant. Primary mode of thermoregulation is habitat selection. Little summer thermoregulation need.
   - Migration: up to 120 km between ranges in the Cariboo (Stevens and Lofts 1988); 2 to 5 km typical elsewhere (McNay and Doyle 1987).
   - Predators listed: coyote, wolf, cougar, bobcat, lynx, golden eagle.
3. WAFWA (2020), *Range-wide Status of Black-tailed and Mule Deer*. https://wafwa.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/2020_MuleDeer-and-BTD_Status-Update.pdf . Read directly. 85 to 90%.
   - BC mule deer population estimate 100,000 to 170,000; harvest 9,986; 87% males; 58,230 hunters (tags).
   - Provincial objective 20 bucks per 100 does post hunt; recent composition near or below it.
   - SIMDeer: Thompson study area almost entirely burned in 2017; adult doe survival 82% and overwinter fawn survival 63% over two years.
   - Buck harvest managed with 4 point and any buck general seasons; antlerless by LEH.
4. Mule Deer Working Group (WAFWA) fact sheets, via Utah Division of Wildlife Resources, PDFs read directly. 85%.
   - #26 Encountering newborn fawns (2018): hiders; over 95% of time hiding; doe a few hundred metres away; days 1 to 3 freeze; days 4 to 6 hide then bolt; resting heart rate 177 bpm drops to 60 when approached; milk nearly 3 times the fat and protein of cow milk; fawns gain 5 to 10% of birth weight a day; most fawns in western provinces born in June. https://wildlife.utah.gov/pdf/deer/mdwg-26_newborn_deer_fawns.pdf
   - #27 Antler development (2018): antlers are bone grown in velvet; daylight drives hormones; testosterone rise in autumn hardens antlers; velvet shed in 24 to 48 hours; antlers fork once 15 to 25 cm (6 to 10 in) above the base then fork again; brow tines small or absent. https://wildlife.utah.gov/pdf/deer/mdwg-27_antler_development.pdf
   - #35 Using teeth to age (2020): 6 lower incisors, no upper incisors; 1.5 yr: P4 with 3 cusps, M3 not fully up; 2.5: P4 2 cusps, M3 up; 3 to 5: dentine line on M3 back cusp; 6 to 8: M1 worn flat; 8+: M1 to the gum line. https://wildlife.utah.gov/pdf/deer/mdwg-35_using_teeth_to_age.pdf
   - #12 Migration (2014): same route year after year; 2 to 4 months migrating, mostly at stopovers following green up; some move over 240 km (150 mi). https://wildlife.utah.gov/pdf/deer/mdwg-12_migration.pdf
   - #17 Winter range disturbance (2016): deer eat less and lose weight in winter even in good conditions; off trail people and dogs cause more avoidance; peak activity within 3 hours of sunrise and sunset; "if deer are moving away from you, you are too close". https://wildlife.utah.gov/pdf/deer/mdwg-17_winter_range_disturbance.pdf
   - #2 Winter feeding (2013): selective foragers; rumen bacteria specific to food; fed deer can die with full stomachs; learned feeding sites passed to offspring. https://wildlife.utah.gov/pdf/deer/mdwg-2_winter_feeding.pdf
   - #24 Managing bucks: by 4 years antlers about 80 to 90% of peak size. https://wildlife.utah.gov/pdf/deer/mdwg-24_managing_bucks.pdf
   - #1 Predators: main predators cougar, coyote, bobcat; black bear, wolf, grizzly also. https://wildlife.utah.gov/pdf/deer/mdwg-1_predators.pdf

## Search previews (secondary, capped)
- Lingle (McIntyre Ranch, Alberta): mule deer move up slopes when confronted by coyotes, white tails move down and away (2002); mule deer does vigorously defend fawns (American Naturalist 2008); deer respond to fawn distress calls (2007), including other species' calls in the right pitch (Lingle and Riede 2014). Search previews of ScienceDaily, UWinnipeg, McIntyre Ranch, AGRIS. 70%.
- Stankowich 2008, Biological Conservation 141(9): hunted ungulate populations flee humans at greater distances; predictable disturbance gives smaller response. Via citing papers. 65%.
- Arizona 1988 (Journal of Range Management) and Montana 1982: hunted deer shift to better escape cover and timber, not out of home range. Search preview. 65%.
- Idaho Fish and Game buck vulnerability: open road density the biggest driver; less hiding cover, gentle terrain, rut hunting also raise vulnerability. Search preview (page 403 on fetch). 65%.
- Montana FWP 1998 policy: mule deer more vulnerable to harvest than white tails (open habitat). Search preview. 65%.
- Webb and others 2010, International Journal of Ecology: 32 GPS collared white tails, Oklahoma, 7 years; no moon phase effect; weather inconsistent. Search preview. 70% (white tails).
- Winter hair: mule deer winter hair air pockets about 26 micrometres vs 13 in summer (Utah Tech conference poster). 60%.
- Fat: Wyoming mule deer does used fat 2.5 times faster in November and December than late winter (Dryad dataset description, preview). 65%. Fat peaks late autumn, lowest in May (Minnesota white tail study). 60%. Starved deer marrow under 10% fat, dark red; healthy winter marrow over 80% fat (Massachusetts, preview). 65%.
- Vomeronasal organ and flehmen in deer (University of Georgia researcher via fishgame.com; Outdoor Life). 65%.
- Estrus about 24 to 36 hours, recycle about 28 days (white tail sources: Mississippi State Deer Lab, Kentucky). 60%.
- Sex segregation: King and Smith 1980, Great Basin Naturalist: bucks on south facing sites with less forb and hiding cover, does on north facing with more. 65%. USGS 2021 Ecosphere: males took about twice as long on spring migration and 44% longer in autumn. 70%.
- Home ranges: wide variation, several to about 30 km2; mature deer faithful to seasonal ranges, yearling males not (Montana). 60%.
- Mule Deer Foundation, M. Luby, "Early season mule deer tactics: spot and stalk success in velvet": glass first, set up before first light and glass into shadows; use terrain, wind and sun. https://muledeer.org/early-season-mule-deer-tactics-spot-and-stalk-success-in-velvet/ Preview. Tip. 10x42 binoculars as first optic (MDF optics article) Tip.
- Look back habit: Rinella; Outdoor Life "50 great hunting tips"; mammal account panoptesv. Hunter consensus 65%.
- Tranquille ecological reserve description: PP and IDF zones 610 to 1,160 m, Douglas fir and ponderosa pine with bluebunch wheatgrass above about 760 m. Preview. 70%.
