# Research: wait times before following a hit deer (2026-10-11)

Why: the app had three sets of numbers, and two of them were both credited to the National Deer Association (NDA).
Owner decision 2026-10-11: show all sources side by side, labelled "Your call". The app makes no pick.

## Verdict on the conflict
- Both NDA claims are real. They come from two different articles on deerassociation.com, 11 years apart (95%, read directly).
  - 2012 article by Bob Westerfield: liver 2 h minimum (sometimes 3 or 4), gut 4 to 6 h. This is the app's "NDA says 2 to 4 h, 4 to 6 h" note.
  - 2023 article by NDA Staff: liver 8 to 10 h, guts 10 to 12 h. This is the Grandpa lesson's number.
- The app's chart (sk-wait-chart: liver 4 to 6 h, gut 6 to 8 h or overnight, muscle 30 to 60 min, not sure several hours) matches the Hunter Ed blog word for word (95%, read directly). Attribution "Hunter Ed" is right, but it is a blog post on hunter-ed.com, not the hunter education course itself. Labels now say "Hunter Ed blog".
- The hunter education course study guide on hunter-ed.com gives only a general wait (half an hour to an hour for deer), not per hit type (95%, read directly).
- So no number in sk-wait-chart is wrong. Only the labels needed to name the article. Chart numbers unchanged.

## Exact quotes and links

### A. Hunter Ed blog (Kalkomey, hunter-ed.com)
- Jared Braddock, "Deer Anatomy for Hunters: Where to Shoot a Deer for a Clean, Ethical Kill", hunter-ed.com blog, 11 September 2026.
- https://www.hunter-ed.com/blog/hunting-basics-understanding-deer-anatomy-for-a-successful-hunt/
- "Thirty minutes is a reasonable minimum for a well-placed heart or lung hit."
- Liver: "Back out quietly and wait four to six hours before tracking."
- Gut: "Wait six to eight hours, or overnight if the shot happened late in the day, before starting the trail."
- Muscle: "Wait 30 minutes to an hour, then track carefully since blood may be sparse."
- Unsure: "Back out immediately and wait several hours before returning."
- Certainty that the page says this: 95% (read directly). As advice: 70% (opinion, course provider blog).

### B. NDA 2012
- Bob Westerfield, "How to Blood-Trail a Deer", National Deer Association, 21 September 2012.
- https://deerassociation.com/blood-trail-deer/
- Heart or lungs, rifle: found dead within 150 yards, "can usually be recovered quickly after the initial 30-minute wait". Arrow: he trails after one hour if confident.
- Liver: "I normally wait a minimum of 2 hours and sometimes 3 or 4 hours depending on the situation."
- Gut: "I normally wait 4 to 6 hours before picking up the trail." Cool night: leave it overnight, search next morning.
- Any shot: wait at least 30 minutes unless the deer is visibly dead.
- 95% that the page says this; 70% as advice (one author's practice).

### C. NDA 2023
- NDA Staff, "You just shot a deer. Here's how to find it" (video and article), National Deer Association, 25 October 2023.
- https://deerassociation.com/watch-you-just-shot-a-deer-heres-how-to-find-it/
- Heart or lungs, firearm: "simply wait a few minutes and then take up the trail". Archery: "waiting 15 to 30 minutes is advised."
- "Wait 8 to 10 hours for liver" and "10-12 hours for guts". Stomach region in general: "wait at least eight hours before pursuing."
- Bone fragments, leg hit: "start tracking these deer immediately", look for a follow up shot.
- Unsure: subtract the time already passed from the gut wait.
- 95% that the page says this; 70% as advice.

### D. American Hunter (NRA, National Rifle Association)
- Jeff Johnston, "Bowhunting: How Long to Wait Before Tracking a Deer", 8 August 2019.
- https://www.americanhunter.org/content/bowhunting-how-long-to-wait-before-tracking-a-deer/
- Heart, lungs, arteries: 30 min. Liver: "three to six hours". Gut: "six to 12 hours". Muscle or limb: after the initial 30 minutes.
- 95% read directly; 70% as advice. Bow hits, not rifle.

### E. MeatEater
- Mark Kenyon, "How Long Should You Wait to Blood Trail a Deer?", 23 November 2019.
- https://www.themeateater.com/hunt/whitetail-deer/how-long-should-you-wait-to-blood-trail-a-deer
- Heart: "can be recovered right away". Double lung: "30 to 90 minutes". Single lung or liver: "closer to 4 to 6 hours". Gut: "8 to 12 or more hours".
- 95% read directly; 65% as advice (hunting media).

### F. Hunter education course study guide (Kalkomey, hunter-ed.com, Iowa course)
- "Trailing Wounded Game": https://www.hunter-ed.com/iowa/studyGuide/Trailing-Wounded-Game/202013_12978/
- "You should wait for at least a half-hour to an hour before trailing a deer, unless the downed deer is in sight." No per hit type numbers.
- 95% read directly; 75% as advice (state approved course).

### G. Bowhunter education study guide (Kalkomey, Pennsylvania)
- "When to Start Tracking": https://www.bowhunter-ed.com/pennsylvania-archery/studyGuide/When-to-Start-Tracking/303039_239388/
- Vital hit: "wait 30 minutes to 1 hour". Non vital area: "wait at least 6 hours".
- 95% read directly; 75% as advice.

### Also in the app already (not rechecked today)
- Pennsylvania Game Commission: gut hit, at least 8 hours (70%, app research for Blood, hair and tissue).

## What every source agrees on (use this for quizzes)
- Heart or lungs: a short wait, minutes up to about an hour (70%).
- Liver: hours, longer than for a lung hit (70%).
- Gut: the longest wait of all, many hours, overnight is common advice (70%).
- Bumping a liver or gut hit deer from its bed early loses it (70%).
- Leg hit: sources differ (30 to 60 min vs track at once).
