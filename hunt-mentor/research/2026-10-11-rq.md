## Weather and movement (lessons 43, 52)
- Penn State Deer Forest Study, Spidey sense (read 2026-10-02): 30 storms, collared does, no effect before storms, slower during (80%). https://www.deer.psu.edu/spidey-sense/
- Penn State, Wandering in the moonlight, Hepner, 9 August 2017, read directly: October does about 60 m/h at night, under 50 m/h by day, 120 to 140 m/h at dawn and dusk; about 6 m/h more at new than full moon; not meaningful (75%). https://deer.psu.edu/wandering-in-the-moonlight
- Rodgers, Sawyer, Mong, Stephens, Kauffman 2021, Journal of Wildlife Management 85(2): 215 to 227, south central Wyoming GPS 2016 to 2018. Males chose security habitat away from motorized routes; temperature and snowfall timed migration; rifle season raised migration likelihood about 2% (does), under 0.01% (bucks). Abstract read directly (85%). https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70229038
- Schummer and others 2010, Journal of Wildlife Management 74(1), Missouri 1995 to 2005: cumulative weather severity index best explained dabbling duck abundance (temperature, consecutive days at or below 0 °C, snow depth, consecutive snow cover days). Abstract read (75%). Formula symbols garbled in extraction: not reproduced.
- Raveling, Crews and Klimstra, Wilson Bulletin 84(3): Crab Orchard, Illinois, about 40,000 Canada geese: morning and late afternoon feeding flights, more flight through the day when stormy (70%, search preview).
- Minnesota DNR pheasant tips, read directly: roost in short to medium grass; grit at first light; feeding about 8 am; densest cover by mid morning; back to feed late afternoon; worse weather pushes birds deeper (70% applied to BC).
- Wind and deer: Outdoor Life summary of a 7 year Noble Foundation Oklahoma GPS study (little change with wind speed) (60%, search preview); older South Texas radio collar study, 25 bucks, drop at 15 to 19 mph and rebound above 20 mph (55%, magazine summary, Tip level); Goethlich thesis, Auburn, South Carolina GPS (65%, search preview).
- Cold front definition: US Weather Prediction Center glossary (80%, search preview); wind shift south to west or northwest and rising pressure behind: NWS forecast office page via search preview (70%). Falling pressure ahead: general meteorology (70%).
- Hypothermia: Fraser Health handout: no need to be below freezing; wind chill and rain raise odds (75%, search preview). https://patienteduc.fraserhealth.ca/file/hypothermia-782885.pdf AdventureSmart page returned 403; not used.

## Hunting pressure (lesson 44)
- Luloff, Finley, Diefenbach and others, Penn State final report, August 2004, PDF read directly (pdftotext): 45,907 ha public land; 73% of hunters within 0.5 km of a road; odds ratio about 2.97 per km closer; 1.25 times per 10 degrees flatter; average maximum distance from road 0.84 km; walked 5.48 km a day; 72% stationary in the morning; 16% of area over 1 km from a road (90% for Pennsylvania). https://ecosystems.psu.edu/research/pacfwru/reports/deer-movement/@@download/file/FinalReportNFWF.pdf
- Little, Webb, Demarais, Gee, Riffell, Gaskamp 2016, Basic and Applied Ecology: Oklahoma 1,861 ha; reduced movement, smaller areas; displacement about 3 times lower after the season (65%, search preview; publisher PDF 403).
- Curtis and Du Toit 2017, Wildlife Society Bulletin 41: 309 to 312, via Conservation Evidence (read directly): Oregon mule deer, GPS, August to October 2015: 190 m from closed roads versus 1,250 m from open roads (70%).
- The Wildlife Society news, March 2022 (read directly): Sergeyev and others, JWM, 445 GPS elk, central Utah, 2015 to 2017; public land use up to 64% before, 29% in rifle season, about 45% after; 41% in 2016 when private land hunting opened (80%).
- Burcham, Edge and Marcum, Wildlife Society Bulletin: Montana elk private land refuges (75%, search preview).
- BHA literature review on roads and elk: Starkey traffic as low as 1 vehicle per 12 hours changed elk distribution; Oregon elk used habitat within 500 m of open roads half as much (60%, search preview).
- Bucks avoiding a stand about 3 days after it is hunted (McCoy, Brosnan Forest): secondary source only (50%), used as Tip.
