## New sources read this session

### Glassing (chapter 1)
1. Josh Kirchner, "Effective Glassing 101", onX Hunt, 14 July 2023. https://www.onxmaps.com/hunt/blog/effective-glassing-techniques . Read (WebFetch). 65%.
   - "The first thing I do when I arrive at a glassing location is not glass, but actually look around with my naked eye."
   - "I'll look close and in obvious areas first." Edge habitat "the low-hanging fruit".
   - Tripod, fluid pan head grid; lock the head and grid with the eyes for dense cover.
   - "one hour at a time at each spot before moving"; warns against thinking you saw everything in 10 minutes. 10x42 most versatile; scopes 50 to 65 mm.
2. Remi Warren, "Advice on long distance glassing for elk", Western Hunter. https://westernhunter.net/tactics/advice-for-pursuing-long-range-bulls/ . Read. No date shown. 65%.
   - "start by quickly glassing all the obvious openings, meadows, or feeding areas."
   - "Pick the furthest points to look first before the heat waves distort your vision."
   - "I generally start out looking east if I can"; west later, animals stand out once the sun hits them.
3. DuWane Adams' 10 glassing secrets, NSSF (National Shooting Sports Foundation) Let's Go Hunting. https://www.letsgohunting.org/resources/articles/big-game/duwane-adams-10-glassing-secrets . Read. 65%.
   - At least 15x binoculars for long distance glassing, sturdy tripod; reach the spot before dawn; southeast faces early, northeast from about 9 a.m. when animals bed; top to bottom, side to side; horizontal backlines against vertical trees; inspect anything out of place; watch where it beds; buddy up, one keeps eyes on it.
   - Does not state near or far first ("I start at the top of the ridge and move my glasses slowly left to right").
4. Search previews (60%): goHUNT mule deer glassing tips (tripods, tiny flick); Tract Optics (west facing slopes at sunrise); Petersen's (nearest surroundings then grid); Field and Stream (15x too heavy to hold steady offhand); a mule deer guide preferring closer areas first; an elk forum post glassing far or high openings first (55%, forum).
5. OPEN DECISION (owner): near first vs far first. Shown side by side, labelled "Your call". The older Glassing lesson diagram shows near first; flagged in chapter 1 as one option.

### Scent (chapter 3)
6. Natalie Krebs, "Here's why a deer's sense of smell is so much better than yours", Outdoor Life, 29 September 2026. https://www.outdoorlife.com/conservation/heres-why-a-deers-sense-of-smell-is-so-much-better-than-yours/ . Read. 65%.
   - "nearly 300 million" receptors is an "oft cited stat from the early 90s"; experts now think it is low; no one is sure.
   - Dogs 125 to 300 million; people "somewhere between 5 and 50 million".
   - Kip Adams (National Deer Association): about 100,000 times more sensory olfactory receptor cells than humans; "probably at least on that order of magnitude better"; nasal passage "runs from the tip all the way up through the head into the brain".
   - No detection distance given. No measured smell distance found anywhere (60%).

### Tree stands (chapter 5)
7. Wisconsin DNR (Department of Natural Resources), Treestand safety. https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/education/OutdoorSkills/TreestandSafety . Read. 80%.
   - "Always wear a full-body harness also known as a fall-arrest system." "Always have three points of contact while climbing into and out of the treestand." "Always use a haul line to raise and lower your unloaded firearm or bow."
   - About 25% of bowhunters have fallen or nearly fallen; fewer than 50% of Wisconsin deer hunters wear a harness every time; 2003 survey: two thirds owned a harness, under one third used it; suspension trauma "can occur in under 20 minutes and can be fatal".
8. Iowa DNR, Tree stand safety focus of awareness campaign (with Tree Stand Safety Awareness). https://iowadnr.gov/About-DNR/DNR-News-Releases/ArticleID/2044/Tree-stand-safety-focus-of-awareness-campaign . Page returned 404 on fetch; search preview only. 70%.
   - ABC's: Always inspect, Buckle on a full body harness, Connect to the tree before your feet leave the ground.
   - About 60% of falls happen climbing up or down or in the transition into the stand; 13 state database: 79% of fallers wore no harness, the other 21% were not attached.
9. Wisconsin Public Radio, UW study: falls from tree stands, 17 January 2018. https://www.wpr.org/environment/uw-study-falls-tree-stands-most-likely-result-spinal-injuries . Read. 75%.
   - 1999 to 2013; 117 patients; 4 reported any harness use; 55% of injuries involved the spine.
- No BC tree stand rule in the app's Rule Book (85%, my reading). Crown land stands and blinds: ask a conservation officer (VERIFY).

### Coyote calling wind (chapter 5)
10. Kevin Wilson, Outdoor Canada, 3 March 2025. https://www.outdoorcanada.ca/coyotemistakes/2/ . Read. 65%. "Whenever possible, I try to call from a downwind position." Crosswind "may be the next best play". "with either a crosswind or the wind directly in your face, your chances are good."
   - Search preview attributes "at least 50 yards (or ideally 200 yards or more)" of downwind visibility to the same piece (60%).
11. Geoff Nemnich, "Wind, the worst four letter word in coyote hunting", Grand View Outdoors, 20 June 2018. https://www.grandviewoutdoors.com/predator-hunting/coyote/the-worst-four-letter-word-in-coyote-hunting . Read. 65%.
   - Headwind "an ideal scenario" for most callers: muffles your approach, keeps scent out of the calling area; cons: call doesn't travel as far, hard to cover directly downwind.
   - Crosswind "my preferred wind direction while on stand": call carries farther, downwind side off to one side.
   - Over 20 mph is high wind; e caller 20 to 40 yd in front.
12. Heath Wood, Bass Pro 1Source, posted 4 January 2019, updated 3 April 2026. https://1source.basspro.com/news-tips/predator/7612/hunters-are-coyotes-not-responding-maybe-its-your-set . Read. 65%. "I prefer a crosswind"; "Always sit where the downwind side is visible for the shot"; caller within 50 yd.
13. Brodie Swisher, Mossberg journal. https://resources.mossberg.com/journal/beat-the-wind-to-kill-more-predators . Read. 60%. Caller upwind 95% of the time, 40 to 50 yd from the stand.
14. OPEN DECISION (owner): wind in your face vs crosswind. Shown side by side, labelled "Your call". The Predators lesson anim shows a crosswind; its text says wind in your face. Both left as options.
