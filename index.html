How this works — and what every number means

Short version: Margin answers "what share of the sale do I keep?" and Markup answers "how much did I add on top of cost?" They are not the same number, and mixing them up is the most common pricing mistake.

The core formulas

Unit profit = MSRP − CP

profit/unit = price − cost

Gross margin — share of the selling price you keep.

margin % = (price − cost) ÷ price

Markup — the uplift relative to your cost.

markup % = (price − cost) ÷ cost

Revenue / COGS / Gross profit across your volume.

revenue = price × volume cogs = cost × volume gross = revenue − cogs

Net profit after fixed costs.

net = gross − fixed costs

Break-even volume with fixed costs.

break-even units = fixed ÷ (price − cost)

Margin and markup are linked

For the same price and cost, markup is always the larger number. Convert between them with:

markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin) margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup)

Beverage-specific cost reality

  • Landed cost ≠ supplier price. Freight, duty/excise, packaging, deposits and breakage all sit between the invoice and the shelf. Build the real CP in the Cost Builder tab.
  • Three-tier world. For alcohol especially, value splits across supplier → distributor → retailer, each taking a margin. Model each tier as its own row in the Portfolio with the prior tier's price as the next tier's cost.
  • Case packs. You buy in cases but sell in units — set units/case so the studio shows both.
  • Excise & deposits are per-unit costs that quietly crush thin margins; the Cost Builder keeps them visible.

Using each tab

  • Calculator — one product, live. Watch the gauge and the revenue→profit waterfall.
  • Cost Builder — assemble a defensible CP, then send it straight to the calculator.
  • Portfolio — every SKU side by side with a profit-weighted blended margin.
  • Sensitivity — a heatmap of how margin/markup/profit react when price and cost move together.
  • Solver — work backwards: price-to-target, max-cost, converter, and break-even.
