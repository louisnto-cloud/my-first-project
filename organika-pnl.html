How this model works

A real CPG P&L is a waterfall: you start from gross sales (list price × volume) and subtract progressively — trade, COGS, channel costs, then corporate overhead — to reach EBITDA. The art is knowing which subtraction is worth fighting.

The waterfall

Gross Sales − Trade & promo − Returns = NET SALES NET SALES − COGS = GROSS PROFIT (Gross Margin %) GROSS PROFIT − fulfillment − platform fees − channel ad − slotting = CONTRIBUTION MARGIN (CM %) CONTRIBUTION − brand marketing − sales − G&A − R&D = EBITDA (EBITDA %) EBITDA − D&A = EBIT

Why contribution margin is the number to watch

Gross margin flatters DTC and Amazon because their shelf prices are high — but those channels bleed it back through referral fees, fulfillment and customer acquisition. Contribution margin is what each channel actually leaves on the table to cover corporate overhead. A channel can have 75% gross margin and still lose money. Manage the mix on contribution, not gross.

Where the levers are

  • COGS — every cent off a can flows straight to EBITDA at full volume. With actives like magnesium bisglycinate and Fibersol, the formula is the richest target; cans are the next (aluminium + tariffs).
  • Price & trade — list price and trade rate are the highest-leverage revenue dials, but trade is sticky once retailers expect it.
  • Mix — shifting volume toward higher-contribution channels can move EBITDA without touching a single cost.
  • Volume vs fixed cost — corporate overhead is fixed, so scale is its own lever: the break-even tab shows the volume that turns the model black.

Glossary

Gross sales
List/invoice price × units, before any deductions.
Trade spend
Promotional money paid to retailers/distributors — scan-downs, off-invoice allowances, coupons, TPRs. Typically 10–20% of gross in food & beverage.
Net sales
Gross sales − trade − returns. The top line everyone benchmarks margins against.
COGS
Landed cost to produce a sellable unit: ingredients + packaging + co-pack conversion + inbound freight, grossed up for yield loss.
Slotting
Up-front fees retailers charge to list a SKU; amortized per unit here.
Contribution margin
Gross profit − all variable channel costs (fulfillment, platform fees, channel ad, slotting). What's left to cover fixed overhead.
EBITDA
Contribution − corporate marketing, sales, G&A and R&D. The headline profitability of the operating business.
Velocity
Units sold per store per week — the metric retailers use to decide whether you keep the shelf.
Three-tier / DSD
Supplier → distributor → retailer. You invoice the distributor at a low price; their margin is already baked into that price.
